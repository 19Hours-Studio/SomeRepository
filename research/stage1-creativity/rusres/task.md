Russia: how to develop and extract its mineral and energy resources in permafrost regions as fast, as cheaply and as
effectively as possible.

Scope. Metals in hard rock and placers (nickel, copper and platinum-group metals of Norilsk and Taimyr; gold of
Yakutia, Magadan and Chukotka; copper of Udokan and Baimskaya; rare earths of Tomtor), diamonds (Yakutia and
Arkhangelsk), coal (Taimyr, Elga and the rest of Yakutia), and oil and gas (Yamal, Gydan and Taimyr). The whole chain
counts: choosing and exploring deposits, building and powering sites, mining or drilling, processing, staffing, and
delivering the product to the buyer.

Defining conditions. Permafrost and its thaw under a warming climate; extreme cold and short building and shipping
seasons; long distances without roads or railways; the ice season of the Northern Sea Route and of the rivers; a
shortage of workers; expensive energy at remote sites; and limited access to imported mining, drilling and LNG
equipment and software since 2022.

How answers are measured, per unit of product delivered to the buyer:
- full cost (annualised capital plus operating plus logistics);
- time from the investment decision to first production;
- effectiveness: recovery of the resource, working time across the year, and how long the infrastructure lasts on
  thawing ground.
Compare with the best current practice in Russia and in comparable cold regions (northern Canada, Alaska, Greenland,
northern Scandinavia).

An answer may change any stage or the order in which deposits are developed. It must work with known physics, be
deployable within 3-7 years, and use equipment and technology that Russian operators can actually obtain from
domestic makers or from suppliers not restricted for them. It must not depend on ways around sanctions.
