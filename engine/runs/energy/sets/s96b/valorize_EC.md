=== VALUED C01 ===
ANCHOR: Rods in a closed coal-ash pond
NAME: Capped Ash-Pond Iron-Air Cell
MECHANISM: A closed coal-ash pond runs as one very large iron-air cell. Steel rod grids reduce the ash's own iron-oxide fraction to iron, with the wet, lime-alkaline pore water as the electrolyte and the unburnt carbon as the conductive network. Regulations already require a closure cap, and here its drainage gravel carries a steel-mesh layer that serves as the air-breathing positive electrode. Each zone's charge in is metered against its charge out, zones below a set efficiency are disconnected, and power flows through the retired plant's switchyard and grid connection, which stay in service.
VALUE: Retired coal plants turn ash-pond closures they must carry out anyway, and their idle grid connections, into multi-gigawatt-hour stores that last for weeks, where the only new hardware is rods, a cap mesh and an inverter, and the low efficiency is paid for with surplus power that would otherwise be curtailed.

=== VALUED C02 ===
ANCHOR: Gas-main wall as iron electrode
NAME: Gas-Main Feeder Battery
MECHANISM: A retired steel or cast-iron gas main is relined with a porous separator sleeve. Its wall becomes the iron-plating negative electrode and its own current collector, and a carbon-fibre cable down the bore is the positive. A cheap complexing agent holds the iron-salt electrolyte near neutral pH, so the plated iron neither gives off hydrogen nor eats away the wall during weeks at rest. Each segment connects at the street transformer it passes through an inverter that also does daily peak-shaving and voltage support for that feeder, so the converter earns its keep every day, while night-time open-circuit checks valve out segments that leak or drift.
VALUE: Distribution utilities get weeks of storage at every street transformer with no new land, tank or stack, using an inverter that its everyday work on the feeder already pays for.

=== VALUED C03 ===
ANCHOR: Immiscible layers replace membranes
NAME: State-Stratified Biphasic Cavern
MECHANISM: A salt cavern holds a dense brine carrying one cheap redox couple beneath an immiscible organic liquid carrying the other. At the surface the two flow through stacked shallow trays, where they settle into millimetre layers between plain carbon plates, so there is no membrane anywhere. The cavern is layered by state of charge as well as by phase: charged brine is drawn from the floor and charged organic from the roof, while spent liquid from the trays returns right at the interface, so metres of spent liquid always shield the interface and the charged bulks lose only what diffuses through that buffer over weeks. Liquid stays in the trays for only a few minutes, less than freshly charged molecules need to diffuse across a millimetre layer, and any crossed molecules that collect at the interface are skimmed off for cleaning.
VALUE: Grid operators get gigawatt-hour storage lasting several weeks in which neither the store nor the converter needs a membrane or tank, and the cavern's own spent-liquid buffer holds off the self-discharge that normally ruins membraneless designs.

=== VALUED C04 ===
ANCHOR: Forecast-set flat trickle rate
NAME: Always-On Trickle Storage Tier
MECHANISM: The long-duration tier gets a converter rated at about its yearly mean flow, roughly one five-hundredth of its stored energy per hour, and that converter runs nearly all year instead of waiting for rare lulls. Each forecast cycle, ensemble weather forecasts reset one flat rate: slow charging through weeks of surplus, and discharge that starts days before a forecast lull at a high-percentile deficit spread over the lead time plus the lull, so later forecast revisions change the rate only slightly. The fast daily batteries absorb the hour-by-hour mismatch, and at such a tiny, steady current density even catalyst-free electrodes and slow, cheap chemistries reach high round-trip efficiency.
VALUE: Grid planners get a long-duration tier whose converter is sized to the mean deficit rather than the peak and stays busy most hours of the year, which removes both the idle-converter cost and most of the round-trip loss that make multi-week storage expensive.

=== VALUED C05 ===
ANCHOR: Deuterium-mapped lignin bed electrodes
NAME: Self-Mediated Lignin Bed
MECHANISM: A water-saturated bed of waste lignin is charged in place through sparse buried carbon electrodes, against a cheap counter-electrode layer laid over the bed. Kept mildly alkaline, the bed dissolves its own small lignin fragments, which carry charge between the electrodes and the solid lignin, so the bed mediates itself and its insulating bulk becomes active with no added chemical. Occasional heavy-water charges tag the cycling quinones with deuterium, NMR on cores measures how far this native shuttle really carries charge, and electrode spacing is set to that measured reach, with electrodes added to dead zones and removed from saturated ones.
VALUE: Pulp and biorefinery sites turn a nearly free byproduct into storage that lasts for weeks, in which most of the bed's mass stores charge and electrodes sit only as close together as the bed's own measured shuttle range requires.

=== VALUED C06 ===
ANCHOR: Salt gradient separates and traps heat
NAME: Hot-Bottom Gradient Pond Battery
MECHANISM: A lined solar pond keeps a standard metre-thick salt gradient near its top as an insulating heat trap, so the whole bottom zone warms by tens of degrees. Inside that hot zone, a dense charged electrolyte lies beneath a lighter one, separated only by a thin salt step about a decimetre thick: enough to stop mixing for weeks, yet short enough to keep the ion-path resistance low, with pond-wide carbon meshes just above and below it. Both electrolytes therefore run hot, which raises conductivity and electrode kinetics on both sides, and capacity is set by pond area and by a layer depth chosen so that the pond's power matches a discharge lasting several weeks.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of electrochemical storage with no tanks, membranes or stacks, where the pond's own salt layering both separates the electrolytes and supplies the free heat that keeps round-trip losses down.

=== VALUED C07 ===
ANCHOR: Cells on plant DC link
NAME: Tap-Switched DC-Link Store
MECHANISM: A bank of static thick-electrode cells sits on a wind or solar plant's DC link, so the plant's own inverter, transformer and grid line, which sit idle in a calm, dark spell, are its only power electronics. Contactors switch how many cells are in series, as a transformer tap changer does, so the bank follows the voltage the plant's power tracking asks for instead of clamping it, and the taps change rarely because state of charge drifts over days. Sized for weeks at a few percent of plant rating, the cells run at very low current density with small losses and charge only from output that would otherwise be curtailed.
VALUE: Plant owners add week-long storage that costs little more than cell material and contactors, because the conversion hardware, the grid connection and the charging energy are already paid for or would otherwise be wasted.

=== VALUED C08 ===
ANCHOR: Warm-stretch, cool-lock entropic cords
NAME: Scrap-Tyre Entropic Spring Store
MECHANISM: A winch stretches warm bands cut from scrap truck tyres rich in natural rubber, which sites are paid to accept, and cooling them lets strain-induced crystallites lock the stretch, so racks of bands can sit for weeks with no holding force and no leakage. One motor-generator winch per shed serves thousands of racks in turn, so a single shared converter is spread over a very large mass of nearly free medium. Gently warming a rack with low-grade waste heat melts the crystallites and the bands contract, drawing part of their output work from that heat to offset conversion losses, while the grid frequency signal sets the warming rate.
VALUE: Industrial sites with waste heat get leak-free, non-hazardous storage lasting weeks whose medium comes with a disposal fee instead of a price, so its low energy density no longer makes it expensive.
