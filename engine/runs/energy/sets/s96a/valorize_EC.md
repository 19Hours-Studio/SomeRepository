=== VALUED C01 ===
ANCHOR: Road embankments as slag-aggregate batteries
NAME: Series-strung battery road embankments
MECHANISM: Road bases and embankments are built from redox-active iron or manganese slag aggregate in a damp, ion-conducting binder, so the construction budget pays for the storage mass and its housing. Each slab of embankment is cast as one cell with buried collectors and dry, insulating joints, and kilometres of slabs are wired in series until the string reaches the kilovolt DC input of one grid-tie inverter at each interchange or substation the road passes. Discharge over days at very low current density across hectares of aggregate keeps conversion loss low without an engineered stack. Continuous load-bearing measurements set how deep each segment may be cycled.
VALUE: Grid operators get multi-day storage along every new road corridor, where the storage mass, cell housing and land are already paid for by the road budget and the only factory-made converter is one inverter per many kilometres.

=== VALUED C02 ===
ANCHOR: Storing energy as unmet clay hydration
NAME: Clay-column hydration energy store
MECHANISM: Surplus power drives a reversible hydraulic pump-motor that presses water out of the interlayers of thin swelling-clay beds, so energy sits as unmet hydration energy in dry clay rather than as stress, with no pressure vessel. The clay never moves: it lies in fixed earthen columns, each under its own passive steel plate, and all columns share one pump-motor through a valve manifold, so only one column at a time is pressed or wetted. Each column runs only inside the high-pressure first-water-layer window where compression and swelling curves nearly coincide, so most energy returns. Adding days of storage means adding clay columns and plates, while the single hydraulic machine is sized only for power.
VALUE: Grid operators get week-long storage whose capacity costs about as much as earthworks and steel plate, with standard hydraulic machinery as the only converter.

=== VALUED C03 ===
ANCHOR: Membraneless pit with forecast-timed charging
NAME: Stratified forecast-charged electrolyte pit
MECHANISM: A lined pit holds one membraneless electrolyte with both reactants, and plain dipped electrodes each react with only one of them, so the electrode array is the whole converter. The reactant pair is chosen so the charged oxidized form is dense and sinks while the charged reduced form is light and floats, with the charging electrodes placed at the bottom and at the top. The pit stratifies on its own and the two charged forms meet only across a thin mixed layer in the middle, which stretches holding time from days to weeks. Charging is still timed by the weather forecast so that the remaining self-discharge costs only a few percent, and the electrodes are sized to average lull demand.
VALUE: Grid operators can ride through lulls longer than any forecast horizon with a hole in the ground, liquid and dipped electrodes, and nothing else.

=== VALUED C04 ===
ANCHOR: Biochar trench as own converter
NAME: Redox-loaded biochar trench battery
MECHANISM: A trench of biochar soaked in neutral brine is both store and converter, and impedance tomography maps pockets that will not charge, which are dried into insulating walls that split the bed into series cells reaching a standard inverter's DC voltage. The char's micropores are loaded with redox-active compounds from its own pyrolysis liquor (quinone-type tars), so charge is held chemically in the pores and not only as a leaky double layer, raising energy per tonne several-fold and stopping capacitive self-discharge. Discharge over days keeps bulk current density tiny, so resistive loss stays small even in a crude bed.
VALUE: Farms and grid operators get weeks of storage dug with earthmoving equipment from char and its own by-products, with an off-the-shelf inverter as the only factory-made part.

=== VALUED C05 ===
ANCHOR: Strain-release electrochemistry of molecular isomers
NAME: Dilute-cation strained-isomer flow cell
MECHANISM: The strained isomer oxidizes at a lower potential than its relaxed partner and then snaps open to the relaxed radical cation, which is reduced at a higher potential, so relaxation gives a cell voltage close to the strain energy per electron. The liquid passes once through a thick, cheap carbon-felt anode at very low current density, so radical cations stay too dilute to oxidize neighbouring strained molecules in a heat-wasting chain, and the stream then carries only relaxed cations on to a downstream carbon cathode. Charging uses a separate radical-anion channel where the strained form is the downhill product, and the neutral liquid sits inert in plain steel tanks.
VALUE: Grid operators get storage where extra days cost only tank volume of one liquid, and the strain energy comes back as electricity instead of heat through a membraneless carbon-felt channel.

=== VALUED C06 ===
ANCHOR: Diffusion-gap sand layers between bipolar sheets
NAME: Three-zone sand-brine bipolar pit
MECHANISM: A lined pit holds stacked brine-soaked sand layers between cheap bipolar carbon sheets, and each layer has three zones: carbon-coated conductive sand against each sheet, where the soluble charged species form throughout the zone, and a plain-sand barrier zone between them. The species are therefore made where they are stored instead of having to diffuse in, while crossover must still cross the barrier, whose diffusion time grows with its thickness squared but whose resistance grows only linearly. A decimetre barrier holds charge for months, and the conductive sand grains give enough surface for days-long discharge at a few amps per square metre, with series stacking raising the voltage.
VALUE: Grid operators get membrane-free storage for weeks in which the whole layer holds charge and both energy and power sides are earthworks, sand, brine and carbon sheet.

=== VALUED C07 ===
ANCHOR: Ore heaps as electrodes, flooded only when used
NAME: Self-forming flood-and-drain ore battery
MECHANISM: An iron oxide ore heap and a manganese oxide ore heap, threaded with steel mesh and separated by sand, form a sealed cell that a shared alkaline reservoir floods only to charge or discharge and drains afterwards, which stops iron's hydrogen-evolving self-discharge. The iron heap is mixed with a few percent of sulfide ore, which suppresses hydrogen evolution during charging and coats grains with conductive films, and the first slow formation charge reduces the hematite to a connected magnetite-iron network, so the whole heap conducts and not just the ore touching the mesh. Hectare-wide beds then run at very low current density, keeping voltage losses small.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of storage whose power converter is an idle, dry ore heap that is fully used and efficient on charge, so both energy and power cost about as much as mined ore.

=== VALUED C08 ===
ANCHOR: Storing charged electrolyte as sugar glass
NAME: Melt-quench sugar-glass redox store
MECHANISM: Charged redox molecules are dissolved in a water-free trehalose-based eutectic that is liquid just above room temperature at the stack and is quenched into a glass for storage, so no water ever has to be evaporated. In the glass even highly reactive, high-voltage, fast-kinetics molecules such as organic radicals barely move, so a small plain carbon stack can run at low overpotential with chemistry chosen for speed, not shelf life. Granules are remelted only as discharge needs them, with a counterflow exchanger passing heat from the outgoing melt to the incoming glass and the stack's own loss heat covering the rest.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of charge stored like grain in silos, feeding a small, fast and efficient stack, without spending stored energy on drying or redissolving.
