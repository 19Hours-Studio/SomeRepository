TOP3:
- A08: It is the only answer that goes after the converter and interconnect capex. That cost dominates at a few cycles a year, and sharing hardware that sits idle during lulls is the right economic move, even though the store still needs its own charge/discharge path.
- A13: Using an ore body where it lies as the electrode removes mining, containment and stacks. It is worth a field test of efficiency and self-discharge.
- A06: A salt-gradient zone used as a membraneless separator turns the pond itself into the cell, which removes both tank cost and stack cost. Power density is the open question.

NOTE: Almost every answer makes the storage medium or its container nearly free (biochar, clay, lignin, ore, waste brine, caverns, pipes, road beds). They ignore round-trip efficiency and the power density of their buried or static electrodes, so only A08 really addresses the converter crux. Several answers attach a borrowed monitoring analogy (ICU impedance tomography, log-rank survival tests, minimum night flow, deuterium NMR, parathyroid hormone (PTH) bone control). That analogy is decoration and adds nothing to the storage mechanism.
