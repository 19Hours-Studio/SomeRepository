D01
MECHANISM: Surplus power splits water at a reversible cell whose negative electrode is cheap activated charcoal or coal-derived char. The protons bind directly to the carbon surface instead of forming hydrogen gas. The loaded carbon is moved out and piled in sealed bins, and fresh carbon is fed in. During a lull the cell oxidizes the stored hydrogen back out against air. Since the bulk store is simply carbon holding about 1 wt% hydrogen, capacity costs about as much as a pile of charcoal.
VALUE: Hydrogen-level energy density with no compression, no caverns and no tanks, at storage costs near those of bulk coal.

D02
MECHANISM: An electrochemical cell hydrogenates acetone directly to isopropanol with protons taken from water, so no hydrogen gas is ever made. The isopropanol is stored in ordinary oil tanks. During a lull the same type of cell oxidizes it back to acetone with air at about 1.1 V. Both liquids are cheap industrial solvents at room temperature, hold roughly 0.7 kWh per liter in theory, and keep indefinitely, so the 2-electron loop stays closed.
VALUE: Long-duration storage can reuse the world's existing fuel-tank and pipeline infrastructure at a few cents per kWh of tank capacity.

D03
MECHANISM: Surplus power reduces oxygen from air at selective carbon cathodes to make dilute hydrogen peroxide, which is stored in plastic tanks with a stabilizer. Peroxide can act as both fuel and oxidant. So during a lull it flows through a cell with no membrane, where one electrode oxidizes it and the other reduces it, giving water and oxygen. Only one cheap liquid made from water and air is stored, and the cell needs no separator.
VALUE: Week-long reserves use one harmless, cheap liquid and a simple cell with no membrane, accepting lower round-trip efficiency in exchange for very low capital cost.

D04
MECHANISM: Surplus wind and solar power drives a proton accelerator. Its beam hits a spallation target inside a thorium blanket that sits just below criticality. Each spallation neutron is multiplied by the blanket, so the electricity breeds uranium-233 that holds far more energy than was used to make it. The bred fuel sits in small vaults and is burned in reactors that run only during long calm, dark spells. Breeding stops as soon as surplus power ends.
VALUE: Storage becomes almost free per kWh and can hold energy for weeks or even seasons, because the stored medium is a million times denser than any chemical store and returns more energy than went in.

D05
MECHANISM: Ships carrying cheap bulk batteries or chemical storage charge at whichever coastal grid has surplus. They then sail to the coastal grid where forecasts show the next lull, plug in at port substations and discharge. A routing optimizer sends the fleet between regions whose lulls rarely happen at the same time. One unit of storage capacity therefore covers lulls in several regions each year instead of sitting idle waiting for its own region's rare lull.
VALUE: Each kWh of storage cycles several times more often per year, so the capital cost per delivered kWh drops by about the same factor.

D06
MECHANISM: Satellites in high orbit collect sunlight that is almost never interrupted and beam it to the ground as microwaves, which pass through cloud. Ground rectennas are built inside existing solar farms so they share the farm's grid connections. Beam power is sent wherever forecasts show a calm, overcast stretch, and is aimed at whichever rectenna site is short on power.
VALUE: The grid gets solar power on demand during dark, calm periods, which replaces weeks of stored energy with power delivered in real time.

D07
MECHANISM: Surplus power drives bipolar-membrane stacks that split plain salt water into a hydrochloric-acid stream and a caustic-soda stream. These are stored in two cheap lined ponds. During a lull the same stacks run in reverse, so the acid and base neutralize across the bipolar junction and give back about 0.8 V. The only stored material is salt and water. Capacity grows by digging ponds, and the costly membrane area only has to match the power rating.
VALUE: Weeks of storage at well under a dollar per kWh of capacity, from materials that never run out and never wear out.

D08
MECHANISM: Very long tethered wings fly at 8–12 km. Winds there are strong and usually keep blowing when a blocking high-pressure system cuts the surface air off from the winds above. The wings fly crosswind loops and either drive generators on board or pull reels on the ground. A forecast model moves the kites between altitude bands to stay in the fastest wind. Only the tether and the light wing need to be built, not a tower.
VALUE: It removes most multi-day wind lulls at the source, so the grid needs far less long-duration storage.
