Feasibility notes on the deciding quantity:
- R22: interface leak checks out (D·C/L ≈ 0.02 A/m² over 5 m; √(Dt) ≈ 0.35 mm at 2 min). The cost of the organic phase (~$30+/kWh) is the open risk.
- R29: ohmic loss ≈ 3 A/m² × 0.4 m / 20 S/m ≈ 0.06 V and leak ≈ 0.12 A/m², both consistent with the claims.
- R15: capacity checks out (~0.7 kWh/m² at full use of the clay's iron). Utilisation and how well MnO2 ore recharges at neutral pH decide it.
- R27: in a 0.5 Pa·s melt, D ≈ 1e-12 m²/s, so the limiting current is about 5x below 30 A/m². Radicals are also unlikely to survive in a sugar melt at 170 °C.
- R10: steel-slag iron is mostly locked in silicates and ferrites, and it puts kV strings and 6 M KOH under a road.
- R04: tyre rubber with carbon-black filler has high hysteresis, and strain fixity at room temperature is doubtful.
- R26: if charging takes one electron per molecule, the charge step caps round-trip efficiency.

TOP3:
- R22: a cavern-scale biphasic, membrane-free store whose diffusion and ohmic numbers hold up. The deciding test is the cost of the organic phase.
- R29: the simplest membrane-free pond (one liquid, two meshes), and its depth-squared loss logic checks out. It is cheap to test at bench scale.
- R15: an earth-pile store built from abundant Fe-clay and MnO2 ore, where a mediator bridges the last 2 mm to carbon. A real test of whether dirt-cost electrodes can work.

NOTE: About a third of the set lands in one new attractor: static, membrane-free "earthworks electrochemistry" (ponds, pits, heaps or slag between carbon sheets: R01, R05, R08, R10, R13–R16, R29). A second cluster cuts converter capex by reusing idle engines or rectifiers (R09, R11, R19, R23, R28).
