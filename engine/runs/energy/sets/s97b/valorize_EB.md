=== VALORIZED B01 ===
ANCHOR: draining electrode beds dry between uses
NAME: Drained Ore-Bed Battery
MECHANISM: Two cheap ore heaps, one iron oxide and one manganese oxide, each threaded with plain steel mesh and separated by a layer of sand, form the cell. The ore heaps themselves are the electrodes. A shared alkaline reservoir floods a bed pair only when it is about to charge or discharge, and drains it afterwards. Draining stops iron's hydrogen-evolving self-discharge, so there is no need for a costly, lossy air electrode or membrane. Because the electrode area is just more ore, the beds can be made hectares wide and run at very low current density, which keeps voltage losses small and round-trip efficiency high.
VALUE: The power converter turns into a cheap heap of ore that sits dry and idle at no cost, giving weeks of storage that is cheap and efficient on both the energy side and the power side.

=== VALORIZED B02 ===
ANCHOR: retired gas mains as electrolyte vessels
NAME: The Main Is the Battery
MECHANISM: In a retired steel or cast-iron gas main, the pipe wall becomes the iron-plating negative electrode and its own current collector, and it stays cathodically protected while charged. A porous separator sleeve is pulled in using standard relining methods, and a carbon-fibre cable down the bore serves as the positive electrode in the iron-salt electrolyte. Each kilometre gives hundreds of square metres of electrode, so the cells run static, with no pumping and at very low current density. The existing night-time segment checks also measure each segment's open-circuit voltage, and the network automatically valves out segments that leak or drift.
VALUE: Buried pipe that is already paid for becomes both the tank and the cell stack, so neighbourhood multi-day storage needs no substation stacks at all.

=== VALORIZED B03 ===
ANCHOR: charged molecules coacervate and sink by gravity
NAME: Self-Sorting Coacervate Pond
MECHANISM: When charged, the redox molecule binds a dissolved polyelectrolyte and sinks as a dense phase onto a carbon-felt liner on the pond floor, which acts as the positive electrode. The counter reaction plates a cheap metal from the clear water onto a floating carbon mat at the surface. The two charged states are held apart by gravity, so the phase boundary does the separator's job, and the reactive species cannot reach the plated metal. The electrodes span the entire pond, so a few watts per square metre is enough to discharge a 10-day store. That low current density keeps losses small, and electrode cost per kWh is tiny.
VALUE: An earthen pond becomes the tank, the separator and the cell all at once, holding cheap leaky chemistry for weeks with a converter that costs about as much per square metre as a liner.

=== VALORIZED B04 ===
ANCHOR: strain energy in a single liquid isomer
NAME: Strained-Isomer Membraneless Cell
MECHANISM: The strained isomer oxidizes at a lower potential than its relaxed partner, and once it is oxidized it snaps open to the relaxed radical cation. That radical cation is then reduced at a second electrode at a higher potential, so relaxing the molecule yields a cell voltage of up to about the strain energy per electron. Charging runs through the radical-anion route instead, where the strained form is the downhill product, so charge and discharge each follow their own one-way path. Because each electrode selects its reactant by potential, both electrodes sit in the same liquid in a short, membraneless channel fed from steel tanks. Radical ions exist only in that channel, so the tank cannot self-discharge through a chain reaction.
VALUE: The whole system is one cheap liquid in plain tanks plus a membraneless two-electrode channel, so adding days of storage costs only tank volume and the converter has no membrane or second electrolyte.

=== VALORIZED B05 ===
ANCHOR: solar-pond salt-gradient zone as separator
NAME: Halocline Battery Pond
MECHANISM: In a lined pond, a still salt-gradient zone separates a dense charged electrolyte layer below from a lighter one above. The zone conducts ions but blocks mixing, and pond-wide horizontal carbon meshes lie just above and below it. As in a solar pond, the same gradient traps sunlight as heat in the bottom layer, warming it by tens of degrees. That raises conductivity and electrode kinetics, so the ohmic and activation losses across the separator are offset by free heat. Because the electrode area equals the pond area, current density stays low, and capacity grows by digging deeper rather than by adding hardware.
VALUE: Weeks of electrochemical storage with no tanks, membranes or stacks, where the separator also harvests the heat that keeps round-trip losses down.

=== VALORIZED B06 ===
ANCHOR: paper-machine electrode laminates as consumables
NAME: Pulp-Made Oversized Disposable Stacks
MECHANISM: Paper machines deposit carbon onto cellulose webs at kilometres per minute and lay down a cellulose separator in the same pass, so cell area costs about as much as paper. The stacks are built five to ten times larger than their rated power and run at a fraction of the usual current density. This turns cheap area into low overpotential and high round-trip efficiency, with no expensive catalysts or membranes. Used laminates are re-pulped every few years to recover the carbon.
VALUE: The converter becomes both cheap and efficient, because area replaces catalyst quality, so stacks that sit idle most of the year stop dominating cost or losses.

=== VALORIZED B07 ===
ANCHOR: vitrifying charged electrolyte in sugar glass
NAME: Sugar-Glass Frozen Charge
MECHANISM: Charged electrolyte is dried with trehalose into a glass, using waste heat from the charging stack. In the glass, even highly reactive molecules barely move, so the store can use high-voltage, fast-kinetics redox molecules such as organic radicals that would normally decompose within days. Their fast electron transfer and high voltage per electron let a small plain carbon stack with no catalyst deliver high power at low overpotential. The glass is stored in bulk like grain and redissolved only as discharge needs it.
VALUE: Vitrification lets the chemistry be chosen for converter speed and efficiency rather than shelf life, which shrinks the stack and its losses while weeks of charge sit in a grain silo.

=== VALORIZED B08 ===
ANCHOR: heavy-water tagging maps where charge is stored
NAME: Isotope-Mapped Lignin Bed
MECHANISM: A water-saturated bed of waste lignin is charged directly through sparse buried carbon electrodes, with no pumps or mediators. During occasional charges, heavy water is added so that reduced quinones take up deuterium, and NMR on core samples shows exactly which zones store charge. Electrodes are then added only to dead zones and removed from saturated ones. Over time, electrode material per kWh falls to the minimum the bed needs, and every active zone stays within a short ion path, which keeps resistive losses low.
VALUE: A nearly free industrial byproduct holds charge for weeks, with converter hardware placed according to the measured behaviour of the bed rather than worst-case spacing.
