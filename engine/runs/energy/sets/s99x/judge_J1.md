F58 - Diffusion time grows with layer thickness squared, but resistance grows only linearly. That one scaling fact turns a membrane-free, stack-free layered pit of sand, brine and carbon sheet into a testable cell for both the storage and the power side.
F65 - It turns strain energy straight into cell voltage. It uses one liquid in plain tanks and a membrane-free channel, and it names the chain-reaction loss mode and handles it.
F04 - In-place magnetite lenses that are never mined become the electrodes, with the inverter as the only factory-made converter. This is truly new if the kinetics and ionic resistance through rock hold up.
NOTE: The set is mostly near-duplicate clusters (sand layers, ponds, clay, gas mains, ash ponds, roads, paper stacks, strained isomers, trickle tiers), and the dominant move is "make the converter huge and crude so it runs at very low current density." That move does attack the crux. But most of these answers assume losses of "a few percent" while ignoring ion transport through thick, crude media and real self-discharge. The trickle-tier group (F33/F44/F51) is flawed: a fast tier that only covers hours cannot fill days of gap between a flat trickle and the actual deficit.
