D01
MECHANISM: A bank of static thick-electrode cells sits on a wind or solar plant's DC link, so the plant's own inverter, transformer and grid line, which sit idle in a calm, dark spell, are its only power electronics. Contactors switch how many cells are in series, as a transformer tap changer does, so the bank follows the voltage the plant's power tracking asks for instead of clamping it, and the taps change rarely because state of charge drifts over days. Sized for weeks at a few percent of plant rating, the cells run at very low current density with small losses and charge only from output that would otherwise be curtailed.
VALUE: Plant owners add week-long storage that costs little more than cell material and contactors, because the conversion hardware, the grid connection and the charging energy are already paid for or would otherwise be wasted.

D02
MECHANISM: The long-duration tier gets a converter rated at about its yearly mean flow, roughly one five-hundredth of its stored energy per hour, and that converter runs nearly all year instead of waiting for rare lulls. Each forecast cycle, ensemble weather forecasts reset one flat rate: slow charging through weeks of surplus, and discharge that starts days before a forecast lull at a high-percentile deficit spread over the lead time plus the lull, so later forecast revisions change the rate only slightly. The fast daily batteries absorb the hour-by-hour mismatch, and at such a tiny, steady current density even catalyst-free electrodes and slow, cheap chemistries reach high round-trip efficiency.
VALUE: Grid planners get a long-duration tier whose converter is sized to the mean deficit rather than the peak and stays busy most hours of the year, which removes both the idle-converter cost and most of the round-trip loss that make multi-week storage expensive.

D03
MECHANISM: Road bases and embankments are built from redox-active iron or manganese slag aggregate in a damp, ion-conducting binder, so the construction budget pays for the storage mass and its housing. Each slab of embankment is cast as one cell with buried collectors and dry, insulating joints, and kilometres of slabs are wired in series until the string reaches the kilovolt DC input of one grid-tie inverter at each interchange or substation the road passes. Discharge over days at very low current density across hectares of aggregate keeps conversion loss low without an engineered stack. Continuous load-bearing measurements set how deep each segment may be cycled.
VALUE: Grid operators get multi-day storage along every new road corridor, where the storage mass, cell housing and land are already paid for by the road budget and the only factory-made converter is one inverter per many kilometres.

D04
MECHANISM: An iron oxide ore heap and a manganese oxide ore heap, threaded with steel mesh and separated by sand, form a sealed cell that a shared alkaline reservoir floods only to charge or discharge and drains afterwards, which stops iron's hydrogen-evolving self-discharge. The iron heap is mixed with a few percent of sulfide ore, which suppresses hydrogen evolution during charging and coats grains with conductive films, and the first slow formation charge reduces the hematite to a connected magnetite-iron network, so the whole heap conducts and not just the ore touching the mesh. Hectare-wide beds then run at very low current density, keeping voltage losses small.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of storage whose power converter is an idle, dry ore heap that is fully used and efficient on charge, so both energy and power cost about as much as mined ore.

D05
MECHANISM: A trench of biochar soaked in neutral brine is both store and converter, and impedance tomography maps pockets that will not charge, which are dried into insulating walls that split the bed into series cells reaching a standard inverter's DC voltage. The char's micropores are loaded with redox-active compounds from its own pyrolysis liquor (quinone-type tars), so charge is held chemically in the pores and not only as a leaky double layer, raising energy per tonne several-fold and stopping capacitive self-discharge. Discharge over days keeps bulk current density tiny, so resistive loss stays small even in a crude bed.
VALUE: Farms and grid operators get weeks of storage dug with earthmoving equipment from char and its own by-products, with an off-the-shelf inverter as the only factory-made part.

D06
MECHANISM: When charged, a redox molecule binds a polyelectrolyte and sinks as a dense phase onto a carbon-felt pond floor, which is the positive electrode. A cheap metal plates onto a floating carbon mat. Gravity and the phase boundary replace the separator. The mat's buoyancy holds it a constant few centimetres above the rising dense layer, which keeps the ionic path, and so the ohmic loss, small. Photovoltaic modules are mounted on the floating mat and feed DC straight into the pond, so charging needs no inverter. Electrode area automatically equals panel area, so current density stays low and losses small over a 10-day discharge.
VALUE: Solar farms gain week-long storage inside the land they already occupy, with a cell that costs about as much as a pond liner and a float.

D07
MECHANISM: A closed coal-ash pond runs as one very large iron-air cell. Steel rod grids reduce the ash's own iron-oxide fraction to iron, with the wet, lime-alkaline pore water as the electrolyte and the unburnt carbon as the conductive network. Regulations already require a closure cap, and here its drainage gravel carries a steel-mesh layer that serves as the air-breathing positive electrode. Each zone's charge in is metered against its charge out, zones below a set efficiency are disconnected, and power flows through the retired plant's switchyard and grid connection, which stay in service.
VALUE: Retired coal plants turn ash-pond closures they must carry out anyway, and their idle grid connections, into multi-gigawatt-hour stores that last for weeks, where the only new hardware is rods, a cap mesh and an inverter, and the low efficiency is paid for with surplus power that would otherwise be curtailed.

D08
MECHANISM: A dissolved organic molecule is reduced, and a partner molecule is oxidized, into strained isomers that crystallize after a delay, so they nucleate on seed crystals in a downstream settling basin rather than on the electrodes. Charging and discharging both happen in one membrane-free open channel lined with carbon. The electrolyte stays at a fixed saturated composition, so the voltage stays flat. The carbon floor carries a potential-gated catalyst that speeds up the snap back only when the floor is held at discharge potential. As a result, the same cheap area charges without reverting its own product and discharges by redissolving slurry fed back up the channel. The two powders sit on covered heaps for months. The converter is a lined ditch run at very low current density, so it is both cheap and low-loss.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of storage where the bulk medium and the single bidirectional converter both cost about as much as earthworks, with no crossover and no electrode fouling.
