D01
MECHANISM: A paper machine lays down carbon electrodes and a cellulose separator in one pass at kilometres per minute. The laminate is then wound with mesh spacers into spiral cartridges like reverse-osmosis elements, so thousands of square metres fit into one mass-produced pressure tube with no frames, bipolar plates or gaskets. The cartridges are oversized five to ten times and paired with a fast outer-sphere redox couple that has no oxygen-evolution step. At that low current density the voltage loss is mostly ohmic and falls in proportion, giving high round-trip efficiency without catalysts or ion-exchange membranes. Spent cartridges are re-pulped to recover the carbon.
VALUE: Any cheap-tank flow chemistry gains a converter that is both near paper cost per square metre and highly efficient, removing the stack cost and loss that dominate long-duration storage economics.

D02
MECHANISM: A lined pit holds stacked brine-soaked sand layers between cheap bipolar carbon sheets, and each layer has three zones: carbon-coated conductive sand against each sheet, where the soluble charged species form throughout the zone, and a plain-sand barrier zone between them. The species are therefore made where they are stored instead of having to diffuse in, while crossover must still cross the barrier, whose diffusion time grows with its thickness squared but whose resistance grows only linearly. A decimetre barrier holds charge for months, and the conductive sand grains give enough surface for days-long discharge at a few amps per square metre, with series stacking raising the voltage.
VALUE: Grid operators get membrane-free storage for weeks in which the whole layer holds charge and both energy and power sides are earthworks, sand, brine and carbon sheet.

D03
MECHANISM: A lined pit holds one membraneless electrolyte with both reactants, and plain dipped electrodes each react with only one of them, so the electrode array is the whole converter. The reactant pair is chosen so the charged oxidized form is dense and sinks while the charged reduced form is light and floats, with the charging electrodes placed at the bottom and at the top. The pit stratifies on its own and the two charged forms meet only across a thin mixed layer in the middle, which stretches holding time from days to weeks. Charging is still timed by the weather forecast so that the remaining self-discharge costs only a few percent, and the electrodes are sized to average lull demand.
VALUE: Grid operators can ride through lulls longer than any forecast horizon with a hole in the ground, liquid and dipped electrodes, and nothing else.

D04
MECHANISM: Two buried magnetite lenses with barren rock between them become the two electrodes. A borehole ring injects a carbonate-buffered alkaline brine so that both charged states stay in place as insoluble solids: magnetite oxidizes to maghemite/hematite on one side and reduces to ferrous hydroxide/green rust on the other. Nothing dissolves, migrates or becomes acid drainage. The same perimeter boreholes pump a slight inward hydraulic gradient, which keeps oxygen-bearing outside water from discharging the store. Self-potential mapping shows where the charged front lags, and current is moved between boreholes to keep the front even. Because each lens spreads the reaction over millions of square metres of mineral surface, current density stays tiny and voltage losses stay small. The only fabricated converter is the inverter.
VALUE: Owners of magnetite deposits and flooded iron-mine voids gain a reversible, non-polluting seasonal store whose cost per kWh is drilling plus an inverter, aimed at utilities that need to cover multi-week lulls.

D05
MECHANISM: Raw mine or industrial waste brine flows through a cascade of cheap cells, each held at a stepped electrode potential. The charge each stage passes, minus the conversion measured there, tells it which couple it is working on. Stages whose residual shows a parasitic couple precipitate it out. Stages whose residual shows a reversible couple, usually iron or manganese, split it into its own purified stream, which becomes a flow-battery electrolyte with no separate purification plant. When it is not storing power, the cascade keeps treating brine for remediation fees, so stack area earns money year-round. New stack area is added only where measured charge return justifies it.
VALUE: Mine operators and nearby grids gain storage electrolyte that is effectively free, made inside a water-treatment plant whose remediation revenue carries the converter capex.

D06
MECHANISM: A salt cavern holds a dense brine carrying one cheap redox couple beneath an immiscible organic liquid carrying the other. At the surface the two flow through stacked shallow trays, where they settle into millimetre layers between plain carbon plates, so there is no membrane anywhere. The cavern is layered by state of charge as well as by phase: charged brine is drawn from the floor and charged organic from the roof, while spent liquid from the trays returns right at the interface, so metres of spent liquid always shield the interface and the charged bulks lose only what diffuses through that buffer over weeks. Liquid stays in the trays for only a few minutes, less than freshly charged molecules need to diffuse across a millimetre layer, and any crossed molecules that collect at the interface are skimmed off for cleaning.
VALUE: Grid operators get gigawatt-hour storage lasting several weeks in which neither the store nor the converter needs a membrane or tank, and the cavern's own spent-liquid buffer holds off the self-discharge that normally ruins membraneless designs.

D07
MECHANISM: A lined solar pond keeps a standard metre-thick salt gradient near its top as an insulating heat trap, so the whole bottom zone warms by tens of degrees. Inside that hot zone, a dense charged electrolyte lies beneath a lighter one, separated only by a thin salt step about a decimetre thick: enough to stop mixing for weeks, yet short enough to keep the ion-path resistance low, with pond-wide carbon meshes just above and below it. Both electrolytes therefore run hot, which raises conductivity and electrode kinetics on both sides, and capacity is set by pond area and by a layer depth chosen so that the pond's power matches a discharge lasting several weeks.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of electrochemical storage with no tanks, membranes or stacks, where the pond's own salt layering both separates the electrolytes and supplies the free heat that keeps round-trip losses down.
