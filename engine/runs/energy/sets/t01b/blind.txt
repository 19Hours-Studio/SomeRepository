D01
MECHANISM: Charged redox molecules are dissolved in a water-free trehalose-based eutectic that is liquid just above room temperature at the stack and is quenched into a glass for storage, so no water ever has to be evaporated. In the glass even highly reactive, high-voltage, fast-kinetics molecules such as organic radicals barely move, so a small plain carbon stack can run at low overpotential with chemistry chosen for speed, not shelf life. Granules are remelted only as discharge needs them, with a counterflow exchanger passing heat from the outgoing melt to the incoming glass and the stack's own loss heat covering the rest.
VALUE: Grid operators get weeks of charge stored like grain in silos, feeding a small, fast and efficient stack, without spending stored energy on drying or redissolving.

D02
MECHANISM: A water-saturated bed of waste lignin is charged in place through sparse buried carbon electrodes, against a cheap counter-electrode layer laid over the bed. Kept mildly alkaline, the bed dissolves its own small lignin fragments, which carry charge between the electrodes and the solid lignin, so the bed mediates itself and its insulating bulk becomes active with no added chemical. Occasional heavy-water charges tag the cycling quinones with deuterium, NMR on cores measures how far this native shuttle really carries charge, and electrode spacing is set to that measured reach, with electrodes added to dead zones and removed from saturated ones.
VALUE: Pulp and biorefinery sites turn a nearly free byproduct into storage that lasts for weeks, in which most of the bed's mass stores charge and electrodes sit only as close together as the bed's own measured shuttle range requires.

D03
MECHANISM: A single membraneless carbon cell array reduces air oxygen to peroxide in soda-ash solution. The peroxide is crystallized into sodium percarbonate and stored in silos. In normal months the cell runs as a merchant electrochemical peroxide plant, ramping up on cheap surplus power and selling percarbonate to pulp-bleaching, water-treatment and detergent buyers. A strategic silo stockpile is held back and, during a dark calm spell, discharged through the same cell back to oxygen and water. Because the converter earns chemical revenue at a high capacity factor, its capex is not stranded across a year with few cycles. Low discharge efficiency matters less because the stockpile was made from near-zero-price power and costs only powder and silo space.
VALUE: Regions with surplus renewables gain a chemical business whose inventory doubles as a weeks-long power reserve, so storage converter capex is paid by product sales rather than by rare discharges.

D04
MECHANISM: Surplus power drives a reversible hydraulic pump-motor that presses water out of the interlayers of thin swelling-clay beds, so energy sits as unmet hydration energy in dry clay rather than as stress, with no pressure vessel. The clay never moves: it lies in fixed earthen columns, each under its own passive steel plate, and all columns share one pump-motor through a valve manifold, so only one column at a time is pressed or wetted. Each column runs only inside the high-pressure first-water-layer window where compression and swelling curves nearly coincide, so most energy returns. Adding days of storage means adding clay columns and plates, while the single hydraulic machine is sized only for power.
VALUE: Grid operators get week-long storage whose capacity costs about as much as earthworks and steel plate, with standard hydraulic machinery as the only converter.

D05
MECHANISM: A retired steel or cast-iron gas main is relined with a porous separator sleeve. Its wall becomes the iron-plating negative electrode and its own current collector, and a carbon-fibre cable down the bore is the positive. A cheap complexing agent holds the iron-salt electrolyte near neutral pH, so the plated iron neither gives off hydrogen nor eats away the wall during weeks at rest. Each segment connects at the street transformer it passes through an inverter that also does daily peak-shaving and voltage support for that feeder, so the converter earns its keep every day, while night-time open-circuit checks valve out segments that leak or drift.
VALUE: Distribution utilities get weeks of storage at every street transformer with no new land, tank or stack, using an inverter that its everyday work on the feeder already pays for.

D06
MECHANISM: The strained isomer oxidizes at a lower potential than its relaxed partner and then snaps open to the relaxed radical cation, which is reduced at a higher potential, so relaxation gives a cell voltage close to the strain energy per electron. The liquid passes once through a thick, cheap carbon-felt anode at very low current density, so radical cations stay too dilute to oxidize neighbouring strained molecules in a heat-wasting chain, and the stream then carries only relaxed cations on to a downstream carbon cathode. Charging uses a separate radical-anion channel where the strained form is the downhill product, and the neutral liquid sits inert in plain steel tanks.
VALUE: Grid operators get storage where extra days cost only tank volume of one liquid, and the strain energy comes back as electricity instead of heat through a membraneless carbon-felt channel.

D07
MECHANISM: Two excavated beds form the cell: iron-rich smectite clay, whose layer iron switches between Fe(III) and Fe(II), as the negative bed, and cheap manganese-oxide ore as the positive bed. A compacted bentonite barrier layer, a standard landfill liner, separates them. A dissolved mediator in the pore water carries electrons from embedded carbon rods to every clay platelet, so beds metres thick work as electrodes at days-long discharge rates despite clay's insulation. Electrodes, separator and store are all bulk earth at tens of dollars per tonne. The only fabricated parts are carbon rods and an inverter.
VALUE: Utilities and landowners gain a weeks-long store built with earthmoving equipment from local clay, in which the electrode itself is the converter, so there is no stack capex to idle through the year.

D08
MECHANISM: A winch stretches warm bands cut from scrap truck tyres rich in natural rubber, which sites are paid to accept, and cooling them lets strain-induced crystallites lock the stretch, so racks of bands can sit for weeks with no holding force and no leakage. One motor-generator winch per shed serves thousands of racks in turn, so a single shared converter is spread over a very large mass of nearly free medium. Gently warming a rack with low-grade waste heat melts the crystallites and the bands contract, drawing part of their output work from that heat to offset conversion losses, while the grid frequency signal sets the warming rate.
VALUE: Industrial sites with waste heat get leak-free, non-hazardous storage lasting weeks whose medium comes with a disposal fee instead of a price, so its low energy density no longer makes it expensive.
