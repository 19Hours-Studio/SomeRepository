TOP3:
- Q14: Puts a membraneless biphasic flow battery in a salt cavern, which gives GWh scale with no tanks or membranes. Its leak and diffusion numbers check out (√(Dt) ≈ 0.35 mm; about 0.04 A/m² leak through a 5 m spent buffer), so it can be tested quickly in trays.
- Q20: An iron/manganese ore heap that is flooded with electrolyte only while it charges or discharges, plus known Bi2S3 hydrogen suppression. The energy medium is mined ore, and the cheapest experiment needs only one heap.
- Q27: Sand-barrier pit cells between bipolar carbon sheets. The numbers hold together (about 0.06 A/m² leak against 1.7 A/m² discharge current, about 8% ohmic loss, about 0.34 kWh/m²), and it is built from earthworks. Its weak point is that it names no specific cheap redox couple.

NOTE: Almost every idea comes in a pair: a loose sketch and a quantified rewrite of the same mechanism. Nearly all of them land in one new basin: bulk earth, pond or cavern electrochemistry where a large electrode run at very low current density is itself the converter, so none of the classic thermal, mechanical or hydrogen families appear. The quantified versions are usually more honest and make smaller claims; the sketches overclaim (for example, Q38 and Q46 compared with Q23 and Q12).
