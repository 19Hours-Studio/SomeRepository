=== VALORIZED B01 ===
ANCHOR: dead char pockets become series separators
NAME: Self-partitioning biochar trench battery
MECHANISM: A trench of biochar soaked in neutral brine is both the energy store and its own power converter, because charge sits on the char's internal surface right where the current flows, so no separate stack or membrane has to be built. Impedance tomography maps pockets that will not charge, and those pockets are dried and left as insulating walls. This splits the bed into many series cells, so its voltage climbs to the DC input level of a standard grid inverter, and the inverter is the only power hardware left. Discharging over days keeps current density in the bulk tiny, so resistive loss stays small even in a thick, crude bed, and the low voltage per cell in neutral brine limits self-discharge.
VALUE: Multi-day storage whose cell, separators and voltage stacking are all dug with earthmoving equipment, leaving only an off-the-shelf inverter as factory-made converter cost.

=== VALORIZED B02 ===
ANCHOR: retired coal-ash ponds as in-place electrodes
NAME: Ash-pond in-situ battery
MECHANISM: Simple steel or graphite rod grids are driven into a closed coal-ash pond, and the oxides and unburnt carbon in the wet ash serve as the electrode mass. The pond is so large that multi-day discharge runs at a very low current density per rod, so cheap rods replace an engineered electrochemical stack and stay inefficient only where the chemistry itself is. Each zone counts the charge it takes in against the charge it returns and is disconnected when its return falls too low, so the battery keeps only its efficient zones. The plant's existing switchyard and grid connection carry the power, so the only new converter is an inverter sized in kW.
VALUE: Thousands of sites with containment, land and grid connections already paid for become multi-day stores whose converter is just rods plus an inverter.

=== VALORIZED B03 ===
ANCHOR: charge stored as percarbonate detergent powder
NAME: Reversible percarbonate powder store
MECHANISM: One membraneless carbon cell array runs in both directions: on surplus power it reduces air oxygen to peroxide in soda-ash solution, and during a lull it oxidizes and reduces that same peroxide back to oxygen and water. The converter is therefore bought once and used for both charging and discharging. The peroxide is crystallized into dry sodium percarbonate and stored in ordinary silos, so energy capacity grows by piling more powder while power capacity stays fixed. Round-trip efficiency remains low, but any stockpile left unused for power is a saleable bulk detergent chemical, so the stored inventory never becomes stranded capital.
VALUE: Weeks of storage in a cheap, tradeable powder, served by a single bidirectional converter rather than two separate stacks.

=== VALORIZED B04 ===
ANCHOR: forecast-timed charging of a deliberately leaky pit
NAME: Just-in-time leaky pit store
MECHANISM: A lined pit of mixed electrolyte, with no membrane, holds both reactants, and simple electrodes dipped into it each react with only one reactant, so the dipped electrode array is the whole converter, with no stack, membrane or pumps. Charging starts only when a weather forecast shows a lull a few days ahead. Because charge is held just for the forecast gap plus the lull, the self-discharge that crossover causes in such a crude design costs only a few percent. The same electrodes are used for charging and discharging and are sized to average lull demand, not peak surplus.
VALUE: Whole-lull storage in a hole in the ground, where forecast timing turns cheap, leaky containment and electrodes into an acceptable round-trip loss.

=== VALORIZED B05 ===
ANCHOR: polymer cords with frozen-in stretch
NAME: Shape-memory reel energy sheds
MECHANISM: A winch stretches warm polymer cords that are then cooled, which locks in their tension so the reels can sit in sheds for weeks with no holding force and no leakage. One motor-generator winch per shed serves thousands of reels one after another, so the converter cost is spread over a very large stored mass. Because the cord's pull comes from chain entropy, gently warming a reel with low-grade waste heat releases it, and part of the output work is drawn from that heat, which offsets the conversion losses. The grid frequency signal sets the warming rate, so output follows demand.
VALUE: Leak-free, non-hazardous weeks-long storage in stacked reels, with one shared electric machine per shed and part of the discharge energy supplied by waste heat.

=== VALORIZED B06 ===
ANCHOR: survival-analysis screening of natural clay lots
NAME: Survival-screened clay paste cells
MECHANISM: Thick paste electrodes of natural smectite clay store charge through the iron(III)/iron(II) reaction in their crystal layers, and discharging slowly over days lets these cheap, thick electrodes act as the converter without a high-performance stack. Each clay lot is tracked as a cohort, and log-rank tests rank lots on how long they keep their round-trip efficiency, not only their capacity. Lots that fail early are retired as soil amendment, and their mineral fingerprints tighten the procurement spec, so the fleet converges on clays that convert efficiently for decades.
VALUE: Electrode and converter material at tens of dollars per tonne, with a sourcing loop that continually selects for low conversion loss.

=== VALORIZED B07 ===
ANCHOR: load-bearing infrastructure as the battery mass
NAME: Structural depletion-limited road batteries
MECHANISM: Road bases and embankments are built from redox-active iron or manganese slag aggregate in a damp, ion-conducting binder, with current collectors buried in them, so the construction budget pays for the storage mass. Spread over kilometres, multi-day discharge runs at very low current density, which keeps conversion loss low without an engineered stack. The structure feeds DC directly to the roadside loads and chargers it already powers, so it reuses their existing power electronics. Continuous load-bearing measurements set how far charge may be drawn down before strength suffers.
VALUE: Massive storage mass and much of its power electronics are already paid for by roads and their roadside equipment, so cycling only a few times a year still pays off.
