FAMILY | FLEXLOAD | Oversized energy-intensive industrial plants run hard during surplus and idle during lulls, with energy "stored" as product inventory | M02, M03
FAMILY | SALINITY | Desalination splits water into fresh and brine ponds; pressure-retarded osmosis or reverse electrodialysis recovers the mixing energy | M04, M05
FAMILY | CO2LOOP | Electrochemically reduce CO2 into a storable carbon fuel (methane or formate), then oxidize it back to power and recapture the CO2 in a closed loop | M06, M08
FAMILY | IRONFUEL | Electrolytically or H2-reduced iron powder stored in piles, burned in air for steam power, with the rust re-reduced (metal-fuel cycle) | M07
FAMILY | CALOOP | Thermochemical calcium looping: power calcines limestone, then CaO and CO2 recombine to release high-temperature heat for a turbine | M09
FAMILY | THERMAL | Cheap sensible or latent heat storage (hot rock, sand, firebrick, molten salt, Carnot battery) with heat-to-power conversion | predicted
FAMILY | CAES | Compressed or liquefied air or CO2 stored in caverns, aquifers, domes or tanks and expanded through turbines | predicted
FAMILY | GEOMECH | Gravity or pumped-hydro variants (underground or seafloor pumped hydro, rock-fracture or geomechanical pressure storage, weights in shafts) | predicted
FAMILY | H2GEO | Electrolytic hydrogen or ammonia stored in salt caverns or depleted fields and reconverted in turbines or fuel cells | predicted
FAMILY | SUPERGRID | Continental or intercontinental HVDC grids that smooth weather across uncorrelated regions, plus overbuild and curtailment, so less storage is needed | predicted
FAMILY | METALAIR | Ultra-cheap metal-air or rust-reversal batteries (iron-air, sulfur, zinc) built from abundant materials for multi-day duration | predicted
CRUX | Every answer makes the energy reservoir (ore, ponds, piles, caverns, inventory) nearly free, but none shows how to make the charge and discharge converters (stacks, membranes, reversible cells, combustors, turbines, oversized plants) cheap per kW, durable, and efficient enough round-trip when they only run a few hundred hours a year, and that converter cost is what decides whether the system is ten times cheaper.
