TOP3:
- P26: Strained-isomer redox uses separate one-way charge and discharge routes and suppresses the radical-cation chain. That gives a membraneless, tank-scaled store whose chemistry can be tested on the bench with known norbornadiene/quadricyclane analogues.
- P53: Storing energy as unmet clay hydration energy, moved by mature hydraulic machinery, is new physics with very cheap capacity. One compression/swelling hysteresis test would show whether it is viable.
- P68: The layered sand cell uses a diffusion barrier, which scales favourably (holding time grows with thickness squared, resistance only linearly), and conductive-sand storage zones. It is a membrane-free static cell that can be built from sand, brine and carbon sheet and checked quickly in a lab.

NOTE: About 80 percent of the set is three-to-four-way near-duplicates (strained isomers, ore and clay beds, ash ponds, roads, caverns, coacervate ponds, trehalose glass, polymer cords, forecast trickle tiers) that fall outside the listed families. Few answers land in the canonical basins. Almost all of them make the same move: "huge area, so tiny current density, so the converter is cheap." That repeatedly skips over self-discharge, the real cost of collectors and inverters per unit area, and energy density per tonne moved.
