TOP3:
Y11 - Clay and ore layered as a bipolar earth pile, with a mediator carrying charge over the last 2 mm. The energy, ohmic-loss and shuttle-leak numbers mostly hold, and the only factory-made part is the graphite sheet, which makes it cheap to test.
Y13 - Clay hydration work stored through a vapour turbine is genuinely new physics for storage. The ~7 Wh/kg figure checks against RT ln(1/RH), but the vapour volume of ~1 m³/s per kW and the heat-exchange walls (about 10 m² per kWh) must be costed first.
Y25 - Earthworks storage with no membrane that has a consistent diffusion-leak budget (about 0.5%/day) and ohmic budget. Energy and power parts are both sand, brine and carbon sheet. Watch for density-driven convection in the sand.

NOTE: The set falls into three tight clusters: membrane-free or porous-separator flow cells (Y03/15/16/18/23/27/29/31/34), thick Fe/MnO2 alkaline slabs (Y06/07/24/33) and ore or earth-bed batteries (Y01/09/11/21/32), with several near-duplicates. Most answers fall outside the listed families but are recombinations of known electrochemistry, and their headline costs often hide the real limit: full MnO2 utilisation, plated-iron thickness limits (Y16), Darcy leak through sand (Y15), or the converter area needed per kW.
