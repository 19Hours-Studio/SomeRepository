**Pair 1.** In both parents, the charged state cannot relax unless the operator opens a gate (electrodes, a catalyst). That lets the bulk mass stay passive and cost nothing per extra day. The offspring makes the charged form an insoluble solid, so it needs no tank, no ore body and no groundwater conduction. Closest work: molecular solar-thermal storage (norbornadiene/quadricyclane) with electrochemically triggered back-conversion. That stores heat from sunlight. The offspring takes electrons in, and its charged product drops out of solution so it can be heaped. Mutated.

**Pair 2.** In both parents, self-discharge is stopped by making the charged species unable to move, through stillness or a glass, rather than by membranes or containers. The offspring gets this immobility from the chemistry itself as it charges, with no gradient zone to maintain and no drying step. Closest work: the PNNL freeze-thaw battery, biphasic membraneless batteries and redox-polymer coacervates studied as materials. None of these uses charge-triggered coacervation as the storage step. Distinct.

**Pair 3.** Both parents use mass whose cost another purpose already paid, and they run it according to how that mass actually responds when measured. The offspring uses a site where the mass, the containment and the grid connection are all paid for already. Closest work: electrokinetic remediation of coal ash, and turning retired coal plants into thermal storage. Neither uses the ash as an electrode. Distinct.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Heaped Strained-Molecule Powder
MECHANISM: Surplus current at an electrode converts a cheap dissolved organic molecule into a strained isomer of itself. That isomer is insoluble, so it settles out and is shovelled onto covered heaps. A high energy barrier keeps it charged indefinitely. For discharge, the powder is fed into a slurry that runs past a catalytic electrode, which lowers the barrier. Each molecule snaps back, releases electrons and redissolves for reuse. The operator adds heaps for extra days and tracks the charged fraction by weighing the solids.
VALUE: Weeks of reserve cost only the price of a covered dirt pad, with no tanks, mining or consumed fuel.

IDEA 2
NAME: Charge-Sinking Coacervate Pond
MECHANISM: An unlined, membraneless pond holds a cheap redox molecule and a dissolved, inexpensive polyelectrolyte. When charged, the molecule binds to the polymer into dense droplets that sink and merge into a viscous bottom phase. The discharged form stays in the clear water above, so the two states sort themselves by gravity and stop mixing. Mesh electrodes sit at the interface and draw charge from the dense phase. Polymer dose sets the trade-off between how fast charge stays in and how much power can be drawn.
VALUE: The cheapest leaky redox chemistries can hold charge for weeks in an earthen basin with no tanks, pumps or separators.

IDEA 3
NAME: Retired Ash-Pond Batteries
MECHANISM: Grids of electrodes are pushed into the ash ponds of retiring coal plants. The iron and manganese oxides and unburnt carbon in the ash, wetted by the pond's own water, form a large in-place electrode mass. The cap and liner required for closure contain it, and the plant's existing substation connects it to the grid. Each electrode zone records the charge it takes in against the charge it gives back. Zones that return little charge are disconnected, so the battery settles onto the parts of the ash that work.
VALUE: Thousands of sites already have containment, land and grid connections paid for, so multi-day storage costs about as much as installing electrodes.
