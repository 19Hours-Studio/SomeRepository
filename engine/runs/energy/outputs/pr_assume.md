I listed about 20 assumptions privately and reversed each one. I kept the 12 reversals that would cut cost the most and are not re-skins of the listed families.

=== PRINCIPLES ===
PRINCIPLE | Depth-tiered capacity | The last kWh of a multi-week store may be used only once a decade, so each depth tier should be costed per use, and the deepest tiers can accept 10-30% efficiency if their capex per kWh is close to zero. | OVERTURNS: a long-duration store is one uniform asset, sized and costed as a whole | GAIN: the deepest 50-70% of capacity at under 1 $/kWh, cutting total storage cost about 3-5x | P: 0.25
PRINCIPLE | Efficiency barely matters | For a store cycled fewer than about 20 times a year and charged with power that would otherwise be curtailed, round-trip efficiency hardly changes the cost, and installed capex per kWh of capacity sets nearly all of it. | OVERTURNS: round-trip efficiency is a key figure of merit | GAIN: opens up media with 20-40% efficiency but about 10x lower capacity cost | P: 0.5
PRINCIPLE | Lull supplies the sink | Winter calm, dark spells bring cold surface air and strong temperature inversions, so a heat-to-power store needs to hold only one temperature reservoir because the weather of the lull supplies the other for free. | OVERTURNS: a thermal store must create and hold both its hot and cold sides | GAIN: halves reservoir hardware, and mild low-grade stores deliver work exactly when needed, about 2x cheaper exergy | P: 0.1
PRINCIPLE | Store heat-pump source | Raising a heat pump's source temperature from about -5 °C to +20 °C during a lull roughly doubles its COP, so a cheap low-grade warm store cuts lull electricity demand with leverage instead of replacing it one for one. | OVERTURNS: the lull deficit must be met with stored electricity or high-grade heat | GAIN: 30-50% less winter-lull electricity demand, using storage about 10x cheaper per kWh | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Environment closes cycle | The hardest step of a storage cycle need not be reversed with electricity if a free ambient process (drying, freezing, sunlight or biology) restores the spent medium between events. | OVERTURNS: a store must be recharged along the same powered path it discharged through | GAIN: removes most charging hardware and losses, since the weeks to months between lulls give slow ambient processes enough time | P: 0.15
PRINCIPLE | Forecast enables slow start | Calm, dark spells can be forecast days ahead, so discharge converters can take hours or days to start, which allows large, slow, high-inertia converters that cost far less per kW than fast ones. | OVERTURNS: storage must be dispatchable within seconds or minutes | GAIN: discharge power cost about 2-4x lower | P: 0.2
PRINCIPLE | Asymmetric charge power | Surplus comes in short gluts while deficits are long and shallow, so charge power should be several times discharge power and bought at near-zero $/kW, not matched one for one. | OVERTURNS: charge and discharge ratings should be equal, ideally in one reversible device | GAIN: captures 2-3x more surplus energy per dollar of power hardware | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Curtailment pays the capex | Much of a storage medium's cost is the energy embodied in making it, so producing the medium with curtailed electricity during surplus turns wasted energy into capital. | OVERTURNS: storage capex is fixed upfront and has nothing to do with surplus energy | GAIN: 2-5x lower medium cost wherever embodied energy dominates | P: 0.15
PRINCIPLE | Volume beats surface | Capacity grows with a store's volume, but containment cost and leakage grow with its surface, so containment cost and self-discharge per kWh fall roughly as 1/size, and a few enormous stores beat many modules. | OVERTURNS: stores should be modular and replicated | GAIN: 10x larger linear size gives about 10x lower containment cost and weekly losses per kWh | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Calendar life sets cost | For a rarely cycled store, levelized cost is set by calendar life and cost of capital, so an inert medium that lasts a century and is financed like infrastructure beats a cheaper short-lived one. | OVERTURNS: cycle life and upfront $/kWh are the metrics that matter | GAIN: annualized capacity cost about 3x lower (100 years at 3% versus 20 years at 8%) | P: 0.35
PRINCIPLE | Deliver service, not electrons | Most demand during a lull is for heat, motion or chemical processes, so storing energy in the form the end use needs avoids converting back to electricity and the losses and hardware that come with it. | OVERTURNS: grid storage must give back electricity | GAIN: avoids 40-60% reconversion loss and the discharge converter for a large share of demand | P: 0.4
PRINCIPLE | Losses become wanted heat | A store's self-discharge and conversion losses are wasted only if they occur where heat is unwanted, so placing stores where their waste heat meets winter heat demand pushes effective efficiency toward 90%. | OVERTURNS: storage losses are pure waste | GAIN: makes cheap, inefficient media competitive, with about 2x higher effective efficiency | P: 0.35
