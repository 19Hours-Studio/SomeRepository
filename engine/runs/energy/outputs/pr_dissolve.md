=== PRINCIPLES ===
PRINCIPLE | Calendar life, not cycles | A multi-day store cycles only 10-30 times a year, so over 30 years it needs only 300-900 deep cycles plus shelf stability, not thousands of cycles | OVERTURNS: grid storage must survive thousands of cycles like daily batteries | GAIN: brings back cheap chemistries rejected for poor cycle life; active-material cost could fall 3-10x | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Hundred-hour rates erase kinetics | When discharge takes 100 hours or more, current density can be about 100x lower, so conductivity, catalysis and overpotential stop mattering, and slow, poorly conducting, impure media become usable | OVERTURNS: active materials must be fast, conductive and catalysed | GAIN: no precious catalysts or engineered nanostructures; cost per kWh down about 5x | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Distance replaces the separator | The time it takes charged species to mix grows with distance squared divided by diffusivity, so a few metres of still medium keeps them apart longer than any lull lasts | OVERTURNS: charged species must be kept apart by a membrane | GAIN: removes the membrane, often the most expensive and fragile part; hardware 2-5x cheaper | P: 0.15
PRINCIPLE | Containerless charged state | For storage that cycles rarely, the enclosure sets the cost rather than the medium, so what decides cost is whether the charged state stays stable in open air, rain and ambient temperature as a bare heap | OVERTURNS: the active material is the main cost and must be protected | GAIN: storage cost approaches what bulk commodities cost to handle, about 1-3 $/kWh | P: 0.2
PRINCIPLE | Square-cube cost scaling | If a medium's cost comes mostly from its boundary (walls, liner, collectors) while its energy grows with volume, cost per kWh falls as one over the linear size | OVERTURNS: storage cost per kWh stays roughly constant with scale | GAIN: a few very large units become about 10x cheaper per kWh than modular ones | P: 0.2
PRINCIPLE | Warned lulls permit locked energy | Calm, dark spells are forecast days ahead, so the charged state can be kinetically dead and take hours to days to activate, which gives almost no self-discharge without any containment | OVERTURNS: stored energy must be available to dispatch instantly | GAIN: metastable charged states that keep for a decade; the reserve costs almost nothing to hold | P: 0.15
PRINCIPLE | Asymmetric one-way paths | Discharging does not have to reverse the charging reaction; two separate one-way reactions that together close a cycle can each be cheap where no single reversible reaction is | OVERTURNS: a storage medium needs one reversible reaction | GAIN: a much wider choice of media and hardware, with each half optimized on its own; perhaps 3x cheaper | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Resting is charging | A large natural energy stock that renews slowly can be shifted in time by drawing harder from it during lulls and resting it during surpluses, so holding the resource back is the charging step | OVERTURNS: storage must absorb surplus electricity | GAIN: no charging hardware and no medium cost; existing natural stocks become buffers lasting several weeks | P: 0.25
PRINCIPLE | Borrowed working inventory | Material stocks that must exist anyway (in process pipelines, warehouses, strategic reserves) can hold energy in their state while their mass is owned for another purpose, so the medium costs no capital | OVERTURNS: the storage medium must be bought for storage | GAIN: medium cost disappears and only the state-change hardware is paid for; about 5-10x cheaper | P: 0.1
PRINCIPLE | Tail lulls need consumables | The worst lulls happen only a few times per decade, so the energy for those extreme cases can be a cheap single-use reserve rebuilt slowly from surplus instead of rechargeable capacity | OVERTURNS: every stored kWh must recharge between one lull and the next | GAIN: the most expensive last 20-30% of capacity becomes a reserve at the cost of a fuel stockpile | P: 0.2
PRINCIPLE | Efficiency barely matters | When the charging energy is curtailed surplus worth almost nothing and cycling is rare, capital sets the cost per kWh delivered, so 20-35% round-trip efficiency is acceptable if capacity is 10x cheaper | OVERTURNS: round-trip efficiency is a key figure of merit | GAIN: low-efficiency but very cheap media become usable; capacity cost 5-10x lower | P: 0.45
PRINCIPLE | Charge over months | Surplus periods are long, so charging power can be a small fraction of discharge power, and charging hardware cost falls by the same factor | OVERTURNS: charging and discharging power must be sized alike | GAIN: charging stage 10-50x smaller; slow trickle charging can replace reversible full-power devices | P: 0.2
