Storage that lasts days to weeks gets ten times cheaper when the part that holds the energy is almost free, needs no container, and loses nothing while it waits. Expensive parts like electrodes, turbines and kilns should be sized only for power. Each of the three designs below takes a different route to that.

1. **Mined ore as the battery's storage mass.** Flow batteries already keep energy separate from power, but tanks and processed electrolytes stay costly. If the reacting solid is raw ore piled outdoors, the heap is the tank. Each extra day of storage then costs about as much as moving ore by the tonne.

2. **Storing heat as a chemical, so it can't leak away.** Hot-rock and molten-salt stores lose heat over weeks. Splitting limestone keeps that heat locked in chemical bonds at room temperature, in materials costing about $10 a tonne, until it is released on purpose.

3. **Making the grid's big industrial users the storage.** The cheapest stored kilowatt-hour is one that never has to be turned back into electricity. Industries that can stockpile their products can carry most of a long calm, dark spell on inventory and leave only a small gap for real storage.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Ore-Heap Redox Battery
MECHANISM: A small electrochemical stack sends a cheap dissolved redox shuttle (an iron or manganese salt) through a lined heap of raw manganese-oxide or iron ore, as in mining heap-leach pads. When charging, the shuttle that comes out of the stack oxidizes or reduces the ore particles on their surfaces; when discharging, the ore gives that charge back to the shuttle, which then releases it in the stack. The energy sits in thousands of tonnes of unprocessed mineral, and capacity grows by adding more ore while the expensive stack stays the same size. Pumping control adjusts the flow to how far the reaction has moved into the heap.
VALUE: Weeks of storage at roughly the cost of moving ore by the tonne, with a stack sized only for the power needed.

IDEA 2
NAME: Limestone Calcination Energy Bank
MECHANISM: Surplus wind and solar power runs a resistance-heated kiln that splits limestone into quicklime and CO2. The quicklime is stored dry in silos and the CO2 as a liquid in tanks, both at room temperature. During a calm, dark spell, quicklime and CO2 recombine in a fluidized-bed reactor and release heat at about 900°C. A closed-cycle CO2 turbine turns that heat into electricity. Because the energy is held in chemical bonds instead of as sensible heat, nothing leaks away whether it is stored for days or months, and the reaction rate can be raised or lowered by controlling how fast CO2 is fed in.
VALUE: Long-duration storage with no heat loss, built from materials costing about $10 a tonne, which suits multi-week calm, dark spells.

IDEA 3
NAME: Inventory-As-Storage Industrial Grid
MECHANISM: Energy-hungry processes are built with more capacity than they normally need and run hard while power is plentiful: electrolytic hydrogen and ammonia, aluminium, cement clinker, desalinated water and green iron. Their products are stockpiled as weeks of inventory. When renewable output drops, these plants shut down and supply their customers from the stockpiles, freeing large blocks of demand. Contracts with grid operators pay plants for each MWh they can shift and for how long they can keep that up, and a forecasting system decides when each plant runs.
VALUE: Most of the gap during long calm, dark spells is covered by cheap inventory of finished goods, not by turning stored energy back into electricity.
