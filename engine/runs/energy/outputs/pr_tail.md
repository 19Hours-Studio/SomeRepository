I drafted the usual spread of answers privately first. It included ore and redox flow, flexible industrial load, salinity gradients, CO2 loops, iron fuel, calcium looping, heat storage, compressed air, pumped hydro, hydrogen in caverns, supergrids and metal-air, plus the listed V13-V27 variants. I dropped every principle with a probability above 0.05. The 12 below come from the remaining low-probability tail, ranked for the size of gain they would give if true. Many may fail when the numbers are checked.

=== PRINCIPLES ===
PRINCIPLE | Borrowed inventory as capacity | The capacity cost of a chemical store equals the carrying cost of its active mass, not its purchase price, if that mass is an existing commodity stockpile that is charged, discharged and returned still within its commercial specification. | OVERTURNS: storage material must be bought and set aside for the store | GAIN: capacity cost falls to interest plus handling, roughly 10-30x below buying the material | P: 0.01
PRINCIPLE | Ambient top-up storage | A store charged as an addition to a large natural energy reservoir, such as geothermal heat or deep cold water, returns the work put in plus part of the reservoir's own, so energy delivered per kWh charged can exceed one. | OVERTURNS: round-trip efficiency is always below 100% | GAIN: about half the surplus input and capacity per delivered kWh, or better | P: 0.03
PRINCIPLE | Lull and cold-sink coincidence | Long calm, dark winter spells come with the coldest surroundings and strong temperature inversions, so heat-to-power efficiency from a low-temperature store peaks exactly when it is needed. | OVERTURNS: low-grade heat is useless for making electricity | GAIN: makes 60-120 C stores at under $1 per thermal kWh viable for lulls, several times cheaper | P: 0.02
PRINCIPLE | Just-in-time storage capacity | Because lulls are forecast days ahead, energy capacity only has to exist during the event, so a medium that can be made in days and dissolved afterward ties up capital for just a few weeks a year. | OVERTURNS: storage capacity is permanent capital that mostly sits idle | GAIN: yearly capacity cost falls by the utilization ratio, possibly 5-10x | P: 0.01
PRINCIPLE | Relocatable shared storage | When lulls in distant regions rarely overlap, one store that is physically moved to each region in turn divides its capacity cost by the number of regions it serves. | OVERTURNS: storage is fixed to the grid it serves | GAIN: about 3-5x less capacity per region for rare multi-day events | P: 0.02
PRINCIPLE | Weather-chasing generation | Lulls are local while weather systems move, so a generator that travels hundreds of kilometers a day to wherever there is wind or sun turns the storage problem into a transport problem. | OVERTURNS: generators sit at fixed sites, so storage must bridge local lulls | GAIN: could cut the multi-day storage needed by more than half | P: 0.02
PRINCIPLE | Orbital light redirection | A dark spell means too few photons reach the panels, not too little solar energy overall, and sunlight reflected from orbit onto existing solar farms can supply that energy with no storage. | OVERTURNS: local sky conditions set solar output during a dark spell | GAIN: removes the dark part of a dark calm spell for solar fleets | P: 0.01
PRINCIPLE | Decorrelated upper-air wind | Surface calms under blocking high-pressure systems are only weakly linked to wind several hundred meters to kilometers up, so generators using those layers could cover most surface lulls. | OVERTURNS: a calm spell is calm at every height | GAIN: cuts the energy that must be stored for multi-day calms by maybe 2-5x | P: 0.03
PRINCIPLE | Efficiency-indifferent capacity | For a store cycled only a few times a year on nearly free surplus power, total cost depends almost entirely on capital per kWh of capacity, so a process that returns only 10-25% but has ten times cheaper capacity beats any battery. | OVERTURNS: round-trip efficiency is a key storage metric | GAIN: opens up processes dismissed for low efficiency, with about 10x cheaper capacity | P: 0.03
PRINCIPLE | Nuclear isomer charging | Electrical energy can be pumped into long-lived nuclear isomers and released on demand, holding about a million times more energy per kilogram than chemical bonds. | OVERTURNS: stored energy must be chemical, thermal or mechanical | GAIN: the active material costs almost nothing, so conversion hardware alone sets capacity cost | P: 0.003
PRINCIPLE | Infinite natural counter-reservoir | An electrochemical store only needs one tanked half if the other reactant and its product are swapped with a practically unlimited natural body such as seawater, which halves stored mass and containment. | OVERTURNS: both halves of a battery must be bought and contained | GAIN: about 2x lower capacity cost, more if the tanked half is cheap | P: 0.03
PRINCIPLE | Field-gated self-discharge | Most of the cost of holding energy for weeks goes into preventing self-discharge with membranes and containment, and if an applied field or signal switches the discharge reaction rate instead, that hardware is no longer needed. | OVERTURNS: charged reactants must be physically separated to stay charged | GAIN: removes the separator and tank costs that dominate long-duration capacity, perhaps 3-10x | P: 0.01
