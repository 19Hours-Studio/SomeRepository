Storage that lasts days to weeks gets cheap when the energy sits in a material that costs almost nothing per kWh: rock piles, ponds or empty geology. The costly converters can then be small, or paid for by another industry. Here are three designs built that way.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Shared Iron Stockpile Grid
MECHANISM: Surplus wind and solar power drives hydrogen or electrolytic reduction of iron ore into iron powder. The powder is kept in open, uninsulated piles at almost no cost per kWh. During calm, dark spells, special combustors burn the powder in air to make steam for existing thermal power plants. The resulting rust goes back to the reducers. The same stockpile also supplies steelmakers, so the steel industry carries part of the converter and inventory cost. A market for the stockpile decides each week whether a tonne of iron becomes steel or electricity.
VALUE: It turns a commodity already held in huge stockpiles into weeks of grid energy that costs pennies per kWh to store.

IDEA 2
NAME: Salinity Gradient Pond Storage
MECHANISM: Surplus electricity runs reverse osmosis that splits saline water into two large lined ponds or old quarries, one fresh and one near saturation. To discharge, the two streams are mixed again through pressure-retarded osmosis or reverse electrodialysis stacks, which turn the mixing energy back into power. Each cubic metre stores roughly as much energy as water pumped up about 300 m. That works on flat land, because the stored quantity is concentration, not height. The membrane stacks are sized for power, so adding more days of storage means only digging more pond.
VALUE: It gives pumped-hydro-like storage at very low cost per kWh in flat or coastal places with no mountains.

IDEA 3
NAME: Closed-Loop Geologic Carbon Battery
MECHANISM: Reversible solid-oxide cells co-electrolyze CO2 and water into methane using surplus power. The methane is injected into a depleted gas field. When power is needed, the same cells oxidize the methane back to electricity, and the pure CO2 they release is pumped into a second reservoir nearby. Carbon shuttles between the two reservoirs, so nothing is emitted. Reservoirs that already exist and hold terawatt-hours make the cost of extra duration close to zero. Running the cells as a heat-integrated pair lifts round-trip efficiency well above that of hydrogen turbines.
VALUE: It reuses the existing gas-field and storage infrastructure as a carbon-neutral store of terawatt-hours for whole seasons.
