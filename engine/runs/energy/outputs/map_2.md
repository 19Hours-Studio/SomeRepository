Here are three ideas for long-duration storage where the stored material is nearly free and can be kept in bulk, rather than built into electrode chemistry. In each design you pay mainly for the machinery that sets the power rating. The energy capacity comes from cheap tanks, ponds or warehouses.

**Salinity gradients:** Holding apart fresh water and concentrated brine stores free energy. Salt, water and lined ponds cost almost nothing per kWh. Only the membranes have to be sized for power.

**Industrial inventory:** Most grid energy ends up in goods anyway. If heavy industry overbuilds its capacity and keeps intermediate products in stock, those warehouses act as buffers. They are already paid for and hold their value for weeks.

**Liquid electro-fuel loop:** Turning CO2 into formate, a non-flammable liquid, lets you store weeks of energy in ordinary plastic tanks. It avoids the compression and leakage problems of hydrogen.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Osmotic Brine Pond Battery
MECHANISM: Surplus electricity runs desalination units that split seawater or brine into fresh water and a very concentrated salt solution, kept in separate lined ponds or in old salt caverns. During calm, dark periods, reverse electrodialysis or pressure-retarded osmosis turns the energy of mixing back into electricity. Capacity grows simply by digging more pond volume. The power rating depends only on how much membrane area is installed, which the controller can expand or idle as needed.
VALUE: Week-scale storage for a few dollars per kWh of capacity, using only salt, water and earthworks.

IDEA 2
NAME: Inventory-Buffered Flexible Industry
MECHANISM: Energy-intensive plants (electrolytic iron, aluminum, ammonia, cement clinker, chlor-alkali) are built with two to three times their average production capacity and large stockpiles of intermediate products. Software that reads multi-week weather forecasts runs them flat out during windy, sunny surpluses, runs them down to idle during lulls, and draws on stock to meet steady downstream demand. The energy is stored as finished goods, and the system learns over time how big each stockpile needs to be.
VALUE: Takes terawatt-hours of demand off the grid during long lulls, using storage that is valuable goods rather than dedicated hardware.

IDEA 3
NAME: Formate Liquid Electrofuel Loop
MECHANISM: Cheap electrolyzers turn captured CO2 and water into formate or formic acid, which can sit for months in unpressurized plastic tanks at room temperature. When renewable output is low, direct-formate fuel cells or catalytic dehydrogenation turbines convert it back to power. The CO2 released is captured again on site, which closes the carbon loop. Electrolysis and generation are sized separately, so capacity can be set to the worst historical lull.
VALUE: Seasonal and multi-week storage at gasoline-tank cost per kWh, without the compression, embrittlement and leakage problems of hydrogen.
