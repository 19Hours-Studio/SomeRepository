The hard part in abstract terms: each kWh has to be held for weeks, but it is used only about 10 to 30 times a year. Holding capacity therefore has to cost almost nothing (about $1 to $5/kWh). Leakage, containment, conversion hardware and reliability margins all have to be paid for without the throughput that normally pays for them.

Each principle below comes from a different field: geology (metastable minerals), astrophysics (how long photons take to diffuse out of a star), sport (slow fuel for endurance), biology (fat built from surplus), economics (marginal cost of surplus input), ecology (rare-event reserves), information theory (redundant coding), music (spectral bands), materials science (fatigue budgets), cooking (reducing a sauce), agriculture (silage heaps) and sociology (graded entitlements).

=== PRINCIPLES ===
PRINCIPLE | Barrier height replaces the container | Self-discharge time is roughly 1e-13 s x exp(Ea/kT), so a charged state guarded by an intrinsic activation barrier of about 1.2-1.3 eV or more lasts months to decades at ambient temperature with no vessel, insulation or membrane, and storage cost falls to the bare medium | OVERTURNS: holding energy needs engineered containment against leakage | GAIN: removes containment and separation hardware, often 30-60% of capacity cost; about 2-3x cheaper | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Retention scales with size squared | Any diffusing quantity (ions, heat, dissolved species) leaks from a stagnant store with time constant L^2/D, so dissolved ions (D about 1e-9 m^2/s) separated by 1 m take about 30 years to mix, which means separation at metre scale needs no membrane | OVERTURNS: keeping charged and discharged species apart needs membranes whose area scales with capacity | GAIN: membrane-free bulk stores priced as raw medium; about 3-5x cheaper capacity | P: 0.25
PRINCIPLE | Slow discharge admits sluggish chemistry | A store emptied over 100-300 hours needs current densities 100-300 times lower than a battery, so uncatalysed reactions with 100 times lower exchange currents and millimetre-scale solid diffusion still keep overpotential acceptable | OVERTURNS: a candidate chemistry needs battery-grade kinetics | GAIN: opens the large pool of cheap, slow redox and phase reactions; electrode cost per kWh falls about 10x | P: 0.35
PRINCIPLE | Pay capacity in surplus energy | When most of a storage medium's cost is embodied energy (reduction, smelting, synthesis), making and expanding the medium only with curtailed electricity at near-zero price cuts its capital cost to the non-energy remainder | OVERTURNS: storage capital is a fixed money cost paid before operation | GAIN: 3-5x cheaper capacity for media whose cost is 60-80% energy, and capacity grows every windy year | P: 0.15
PRINCIPLE | Efficiency is cheap to sacrifice | At about 10 deep cycles a year and $20/MWh surplus, cutting round-trip efficiency from 80% to 40% costs about $0.25 per kWh of capacity per year (about $3/kWh present value), so any medium that saves more capex than that wins despite poor efficiency | OVERTURNS: round-trip efficiency is a primary figure of merit | GAIN: admits media with 5-10x lower capacity cost at 30-45% efficiency | P: 0.55
PRINCIPLE | Rarest reserve need not cycle | Energy deficits from lulls are heavy-tailed, so the tier that covers only once-a-decade events can be filled once and refilled slowly over years, and only its holding cost counts, not cost per cycle or recharge speed | OVERTURNS: every kWh of storage must be quickly rechargeable and cycle often | GAIN: the last 30-50% of required energy capacity at about one tenth the cost | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Redundancy replaces unit quality | When many independent storage units each fail or degrade with probability p, the binomial law makes aggregate availability predictable, so buying 10-20% extra cheap units provides the same firm capacity as fewer high-quality ones | OVERTURNS: grid storage units must be individually durable and bankable | GAIN: looser tolerances, lifetimes and quality control; about 2x lower installed cost | P: 0.2
PRINCIPLE | Size energy to the bass | The energy needed to buffer a net-load swing scales with amplitude divided by frequency while power scales only with amplitude, so almost all required kWh sit in the multi-day band, which needs about 1/100 kW per kWh and can use a medium chosen only for $/kWh | OVERTURNS: one storage technology should cover hours through weeks | GAIN: power hardware nearly vanishes from long-duration cost; about 3-5x | P: 0.35
PRINCIPLE | Cycle life barely matters | Weekly-scale storage sees about 10-30 full cycles a year, so 30 years needs only about 300-900 cycles, which many materials rejected for poor reversibility already meet | OVERTURNS: grid storage needs thousands of cycles of stability | GAIN: widens the eligible materials pool to ones 3-10x cheaper per kWh | P: 0.4
PRINCIPLE | Store solute, not solution | In dissolved-reactant storage most of the mass and tank volume is solvent, so keeping the charged species as a dense solid or concentrate and adding solvent only inside the converter cuts stored volume per kWh about 3-10x | OVERTURNS: the electrolyte inventory must be stored as working solution | GAIN: 3-10x less tankage and land per kWh | P: 0.35
PRINCIPLE | Containment cost falls with height | Liners, covers and land scale with footprint while capacity scales with volume, so containment cost per kWh falls as 1/height, and a 30 m heap at 50 kWh/m^3 under a $2/m^2 film costs about $0.001/kWh to contain | OVERTURNS: containment is a major cost of bulk storage | GAIN: containment drops from dollars to fractions of a cent per kWh | P: 0.3
PRINCIPLE | Graded firmness cuts storage need | Required storage energy equals the worst lull duration times the load guaranteed through it, so guaranteeing only a contracted firm fraction (say 40%) through extreme lulls cuts the energy capacity needed roughly in proportion | OVERTURNS: all load must get the same reliability | GAIN: about 2-2.5x less storage energy for the same essential service | P: 0.4
