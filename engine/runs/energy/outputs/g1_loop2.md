Diagnostic loops

1. Water chemistry: the ion charge-balance error. If total cations minus total anions (in meq/L) is off by more than about 5%, an ion has not been measured. Port: in a flow store whose electrolyte is unrefined waste brine (acid mine drainage, spent pickling liquor, leachate holding Fe, Mn, Cu and V together), track the electron-balance error. That is the coulombs passed minus the conversions of the couples that are measured. Loop: a lasting error at a given potential reveals a hidden couple. If the couple is useful, a side stream is split off and gets its own stack tuned to it. If it is parasitic, it is removed by a pH-step precipitation. Honesty: the closest work is iron flow batteries run on pickling liquor. Those purify the feed first. Here the mixture sorts itself and stays unrefined.

2. Metabolic flux analysis: isotope labelling shows which pathways actually carry flux. Port: in a static, wet bed of pulp-mill lignin or humic solids with buried electrodes, add a pulse of heavy water (D2O) while charging. Quinones that get reduced take up deuterium, so NMR on core samples maps which parts of the bed really cycle. Loop: new electrodes are driven into dead zones, and electrodes in saturated zones are removed or rewired in series. Honesty: the closest work is CMBlu's lignin SolidFlow and sediment fuel cells. Both rely on pumping or microbes. This design has no mediator and no pumping, and its electrode layout changes based on the label maps.

3. Epidemiology: the log-rank test compares survival curves between groups to find a hidden risk factor. Port: build cheap paste electrodes from iron-bearing smectite clay, whose structural iron can be partly and reversibly reduced. Each lot from a different deposit is a cohort, and capacity fade is its hazard. Loop: lots whose survival curves diverge are pulled out of service and reused as soil amendment. Their mineral covariates (layer charge, iron content) change the procurement spec, and long-surviving lots set the sourcing standard. Honesty: iron-clay electrodes have only been studied in academic electrochemistry. No grid store uses bulk natural clay.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Self-sorting waste-brine flow storage
MECHANISM: Unrefined industrial and mining waste brines with many redox couples sit in lined ponds and cycle through electrochemical stacks. The controller keeps computing an electron-balance error: the charge passed minus the conversions of the couples it measures. A residual at a particular potential points to an unknown couple. A useful couple is split into its own stream with its own stack; a parasitic one is removed by precipitation. Over time the plant takes on the structure that its local waste chemistry supports.
VALUE: Storage medium costs near zero because it is never purified, which is the main cost in today's flow electrolytes.

IDEA 2
NAME: Isotope-mapped lignin bed battery
MECHANISM: A static, water-saturated bed of waste lignin or humic solids is charged directly by buried electrodes, reducing quinone groups with no mediator and no pumping. During some charges, heavy water is added; quinones that get reduced take up deuterium, and NMR on core samples maps which parts of the bed actually store charge. Electrodes are added to dead zones and removed or rewired from saturated ones, so the electrode network keeps adapting to how the bed really behaves.
VALUE: Turns a nearly free paper-industry byproduct into storage that runs for weeks, with very little hardware per kWh.

IDEA 3
NAME: Survival-screened iron-clay electrodes
MECHANISM: Cheap natural smectite clays store charge as reversible iron(III) to iron(II) changes in their crystal layers, pressed into large paste electrodes. Each clay lot is tracked as a cohort, and log-rank tests on capacity-fade curves pick out lots that are failing early. Failing lots are retired and reused as soil amendment, and their mineral fingerprints change the procurement spec. Lots that last longest set the standard for future sourcing.
VALUE: Uses a mineral that costs tens of dollars per tonne as electrode material, and continually weeds out clay sources that fade fast.
