All three tasks have little or no AI work behind them. Each one relies on data or simulations that fit a single RTX 3060 and 32 GB of RAM, and each model is shaped around the physics of its task.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Inverse Glaze Chemistry Solver
MECHANISM: Potters still develop ceramic glazes by firing test tiles one at a time. AI has failed here because the data are small and noisy (about 20k community recipes with uneven photos), and surface color depends nonlinearly on oxide chemistry, kiln atmosphere and cooling, which gives generic vision models nothing to anchor to. The model turns a recipe into oxide ratios (Unity Molecular Formula) through a frozen stoichiometry layer that maps materials to oxides. A 30M-parameter conditional decoder takes those ratios plus cone, atmosphere and cooling curve and predicts calibrated Lab color, gloss and the probabilities of crawling and crazing. It trains on photos white-balanced against a printed color card, and a crazing head is supervised by thermal-expansion mismatch computed against the clay body. At inference, gradient descent in recipe space restricted to the user's own materials finds a recipe for a target photo. Training takes about 6 GPU-hours and a search takes seconds.
VALUE: A potter photographs a surface they want and gets a recipe made from materials they own that is chemically valid and safe from crazing, which neither an image generator nor a recipe lookup can give.

IDEA 2
NAME: Violin Plate Carving Copilot
MECHANISM: Luthiers shape spruce violin plates by tapping them and listening for the free-plate modes (1, 2 and 5). AI has stayed out because every billet of wood is different and no datasets exist. A material-identification network reads phone recordings of taps at 5 points plus a few caliper readings. It infers the wood's directional stiffness values and density. A graph-network surrogate on a 2k-node plate mesh predicts mode frequencies and shapes from a thickness map plus those material values. It trains on about 2M finite-element eigen-solves run on the same PC's CPU and 32 GB of RAM (about a week), then is fine-tuned on a few real plates. Carving advice comes from optimizing the surrogate under a remove-wood-only constraint, which outputs a map of how much wood to take off and where to hit the target modes.
VALUE: It predicts the acoustic effect of cuts that cannot be undone before the gouge touches the wood, which no audio app or generic simulator does for a specific plank.

IDEA 3
NAME: Smartphone Tree-Ring Dating
MECHANISM: Dating old timber from its tree rings (dendrochronology) needs cores, microscopes and an expert to match ring widths against regional records. Machine learning has only detected rings on clean lab scans and has never dated a rough phone photo of a barn beam or a stump. An 8M-parameter U-Net traces several radial paths and extracts ring-width series with an uncertainty for each ring. An encoder turns the detrended series into embeddings. Dating then treats offset-only matching as a 1D convolution against about 4,000 regional chronologies from the ITRDB public tree-ring database, held in RAM. The result is a probability for each calendar year of the outermost ring, plus species and region. Training uses the database's millions of measured series, rendered into synthetic wood images with saw marks, cracks and missing or false rings, so both stages learn end to end.
VALUE: Homeowners and conservators get a calendar date with a stated confidence from one photo, where today it takes a lab visit and weeks of expert matching.
