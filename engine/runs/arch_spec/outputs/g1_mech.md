Three tasks with no paired data, each with its own cheap way of getting a training signal. Each uses a small learned function that fits easily on one RTX 3060. None of them reuses the forbidden families or the home field's named methods.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Seasonal Riverbed Reveal
MECHANISM: The task is showing paddlers, before they commit, where submerged rocks, undercuts and trapped logs sit under fast whitewater. AI has been absent here because nobody has ground truth for what lies beneath moving water. Input is a 10-second phone clip of a rapid. Tracking how foam patches move from frame to frame gives a 128x128 field of surface speed and direction plus a surface-texture field. A learned function of about 5M weights maps these to a grid of depth below the surface and a per-cell probability that water flows under rock. The seasons supply training pairs for free: the same stretch is filmed at high water and photographed at low water, and the two are aligned by GPS and fixed landmarks, so the riverbed exposed at low flow is the target for the high-flow surface. The weights are driven by the gap between predicted and exposed bed heights, with the gauge-level difference as an extra input. Model and batches fit in 12 GB, a few thousand river-stretch pairs train in days, and a clip is answered in under a second.
VALUE: Paddlers get a read of what the surface hides from a phone, which no same-budget sensor or rule of thumb provides.

IDEA 2
NAME: Footprint Decay Clock
MECHANISM: The task is estimating how many hours ago a footprint was pressed into mud, sand or snow, so search-and-rescue teams can tell which trail is fresh. AI has failed here because no aged tracks with known times exist, and how a print decays depends on the weather since it was made. Volunteers press a standard boot into the ground next to a staked phone that photographs the print every 20 minutes from two flash angles, while a cheap weather station logs temperature, rain and wind. Each photo pair becomes a relief stored as rim sharpness, wall slope and fill-depth profiles. A small learned function takes the current profiles plus the weather summed since each candidate start time and scores every elapsed-hour bin. Training pushes the true elapsed time to the top score, and an ordering penalty forces later photos of the same print to be judged older. Each print is a few thousand numbers and the weather sums live in RAM, so millions of timed photos train on one GPU.
VALUE: From a single photo it gives a calibrated age range such as "3-6 h ago" with its uncertainty, which trackers' intuition cannot give consistently.

IDEA 3
NAME: Neighbour-Predicted Tap Map
MECHANISM: The task is finding hidden rot in wooden boat hulls and timber frames, where surveyors tap with a hammer and listen. AI is absent because every hull rings differently, so fixed sound-to-rot rules never carry over from one hull to the next. The user taps about 300 points while the phone films the hammer head to get each position and records each 50 ms ring. Each ring is reduced to a 64-band spectrum plus a decay rate per band. A learned function of under 1M weights predicts each tap's spectrum from its 8 nearest neighbours' spectra, their distances and the grain direction. Sound wood is predictable from its neighbours, so the per-point prediction error forms the rot map. Training first hides points on thousands of hulls and predicts them back. A small second stage then maps error maps to awl-probe softness depths that surveyors record when the boats are hauled out, and both stages train overnight.
VALUE: It gives an objective rot map for each hull from a phone and a hammer, where a generic sound classifier on the same budget fails because no two hulls share a baseline.
