PAIR 1
 Parent A: MECHANISM: The task is estimating how many hours ago a footprint was pressed into mud, sand or snow, so search-and-rescue teams can tell which trail is fresh. AI has failed here because no aged tracks with known times exist, and how a print decays depends on the weather since it was made. Volunteers press a standard boot into the ground next to a staked phone that photographs the print every 20 minutes from two flash angles, while a cheap weather station logs temperature, rain and wind. Each photo pair becomes a relief stored as rim sharpness, wall slope and fill-depth profiles. A small learned function takes the current profiles plus the weather summed since each candidate start time and scores every elapsed-hour bin. Training pushes the true elapsed time to the top score, and an ordering penalty forces later photos of the same print to be judged older. Each print is a few thousand numbers and the weather sums live in RAM, so millions of timed photos train on one GPU.
   VALUE: From a single photo it gives a calibrated age range such as "3-6 h ago" with its uncertainty, which trackers' intuition cannot give consistently.
   nearest published work: Rajs et al., latent fingerprint time-since-deposition by DESI-MS plus machine learning (Scientific Reports; 744 prints, r about 0.54) https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/determining-time-since-deposition-of-latent-fingerprints-on-foren/ ; "Deep learning analysis and age prediction from shoeprints" (Univ. Missouri, title-only hit, content unverified) https://bondlsc.missouri.edu/?p=11401 (also fingerprint time-lapse imaging of degradation, 76-86% two-class accuracy; tracker field heuristics on edge sharpness)
 Parent B: MECHANISM: The task is mapping lost 19th-century clay field drains under farmland from drone RGB and thermal images taken after rain. AI has been absent because no ground truth exists and the drains are invisible, so only slow GPR surveys have been used. A 20M-parameter U-Net predicts surface wetness from a current drain graph, using a fast non-differentiable Darcy flow solve on a 512² grid, and observed minus predicted gives the difference map. Ridges above 3σ insert drain segments by tracing along the ridge, troughs below −3σ delete segments, and each edit is kept only if R-free, the error on 5% of held-out pixels, improves. The U-Net is pretrained on simulated fields with random drain layouts, then fine-tuned self-consistently on real flights. Everything fits easily in 12 GB.
   VALUE: It turns one post-rain drone flight into a validated drain network with honest error bars, which a pixel segmenter cannot do without labels.
   nearest published work: Allred et al., Overall results and key findings on use of UAV visible, multispectral and thermal imagery for mapping agricultural drainage pipes, Agricultural Water Management (2020) https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70208285 ; Breitkopf et al., Advanced Deep Learning Architectures for Accurate Detection of Subsurface Tile Drainage Pipes from Remote Sensing Images (2022) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02071 (also USGS satellite U-Net tile-drain delineation https://www.usgs.gov/publications/machine-learning-model-delineate-sub-surface-agricultural-drainage-satellite-imagery)

 Parent A: MECHANISM: The task is showing paddlers, before they commit, where submerged rocks, undercuts and trapped logs sit under fast whitewater. AI has been absent here because nobody has ground truth for what lies beneath moving water. Input is a 10-second phone clip of a rapid. Tracking how foam patches move from frame to frame gives a 128x128 field of surface speed and direction plus a surface-texture field. A learned function of about 5M weights maps these to a grid of depth below the surface and a per-cell probability that water flows under rock. The seasons supply training pairs for free: the same stretch is filmed at high water and photographed at low water, and the two are aligned by GPS and fixed landmarks, so the riverbed exposed at low flow is the target for the high-flow surface. The weights are driven by the gap between predicted and exposed bed heights, with the gauge-level difference as an extra input. Model and batches fit in 12 GB, a few thousand river-stretch pairs train in days, and a clip is answered in under a second.
   VALUE: Paddlers get a read of what the surface hides from a phone, which no same-budget sensor or rule of thumb provides.
   nearest published work: Deep learning technique for fast inference of large-scale riverine bathymetry, Ghorbanidehno et al., Adv. Water Resources (2021) https://wrrc.hawaii.edu/?p=7004 ; Variational encoder geostatistical analysis (VEGAS) with an application to large scale riverine bathymetry (2021) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.11719 (also USGS UAS-video PIV + depth retrieval for North Santiam River, Legleiter/Harrison, 2022 https://www.usgs.gov/data/video-acquired-uncrewed-aerial-system-uas-and-hydroacoustic-measurements-flow-velocity)
 Parent B: MECHANISM: The tracked bursts from Idea 1 form genealogies: carrier line, first fork, second-generation forks. These are scored under a multitype Galton–Watson branching process with timed delays. The process's offspring mean, generation depth and delay distribution are estimated by exact tree likelihood, as phylodynamics estimates R from pathogen trees. Carbon drives CO-explosion branching. Elements such as Mn, Cr, W and Mo shift the delay distribution, the colour type and terminal shapes (W gives dull red; Mo gives detached "arrowhead" tips). Pressure changes how many trees appear, not the statistics within each tree, so the estimates do not depend on it. Inference is CPU-bound over a few thousand trees and runs in seconds in 32 GB RAM.
   VALUE: Its composition estimates come from per-tree statistics that stay the same across grinders, wheels and operators, which no same-budget image model can match.
   nearest published work: Spark imaging plus deep learning to predict carbon content in steel, Maribor/Stor Steel (2025) https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/478140 ; Spark test image regression, ResNet-18 + Grad-CAM, NTUST thesis (2023) https://etheses.lib.ntust.edu.tw/thesis/detail/529f625eee44a1c1599d3da166cdfafc/ (also spark length/spread/deviation-angle study https://jii.araku.ac.ir/article_727684_ada638d2da050cb74ecea592be55a6da.pdf ; ResNet18 spark-to-machinability, MDPI Metals 2024 https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/8/955)

PAIR 3
 Parent A: MECHANISM: The task is finding hidden rot in wooden boat hulls and timber frames, where surveyors tap with a hammer and listen. AI is absent because every hull rings differently, so fixed sound-to-rot rules never carry over from one hull to the next. The user taps about 300 points while the phone films the hammer head to get each position and records each 50 ms ring. Each ring is reduced to a 64-band spectrum plus a decay rate per band. A learned function of under 1M weights predicts each tap's spectrum from its 8 nearest neighbours' spectra, their distances and the grain direction. Sound wood is predictable from its neighbours, so the per-point prediction error forms the rot map. Training first hides points on thousands of hulls and predicts them back. A small second stage then maps error maps to awl-probe softness depths that surveyors record when the boats are hauled out, and both stages train overnight.
   VALUE: It gives an objective rot map for each hull from a phone and a hammer, where a generic sound classifier on the same budget fails because no two hulls share a baseline.
   nearest published work: Shi et al., Automated non-invasive wooden pole health assessment (2026) https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/96887/1/Shi-etal-2026-Automated-non-invasive-wooden-pole-health-assessment.pdf (hammer-test waveforms, classifiers, drill-test labels) ; Poole, Hartley et al., Force and sound data fusion for enhanced tap testing scanning of composites, IEEE Access (2023) https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/85622/7/Poole_Hartley_IEEE_Access_2023_Force_and_sound_data_fusion_for_enhanced_tap_testing.pdf (also unsupervised autoencoder acoustic anomaly detection, Planing It by Ear, 2025 https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04819v1)
 Parent B: MECHANISM: Natural changes in air pressure (barometric tides and weather fronts) serve as the forcing signal, and a cheap ultrasonic anemometer at each entrance records the airflow response. Together they give an impedance spectrum Z(f) per entrance, the same way the forced oscillation technique tests lungs. A small transformer maps the spectrum, plus the volume and resistance already known from the survey, to a posterior over a multi-compartment model (an Otis-style network of resistances and compliances): how many hidden compartments there are, how big they are, and how strongly they are connected. It is trained on millions of synthetic networks made by deleting passages from real surveys, then fine-tuned on caves whose volume was later measured. The model is under 20M parameters and trains in hours on the 3060.
   VALUE: It gives a measured, uncertainty-bounded estimate of volume nobody has entered yet from a $40 sensor, where the same budget otherwise buys only geologic guesswork.
