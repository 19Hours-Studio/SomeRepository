M01
MECHANISM: Task: historical shorthand archives, which AI has avoided because meaning is carried by stroke weight, slant and line position, and there is almost no labeled data. A procedural renderer encodes each shorthand system's rulebook and turns modern text into synthetic pages with jittered, pen-pressure strokes. These synthetic pages train a 25M-parameter stroke-vectorizing CNN encoder. That encoder feeds a differentiable finite-state transducer, built from the same rulebook, which outputs a phonetic lattice. A 100M-parameter language model of the target era rescores the lattice. The model then self-trains on unlabeled scans by keeping only high-confidence lattice paths. All models are small and the FST is sparse, so training fits in 12 GB of VRAM and synthetic data costs nothing to generate.
VALUE: It can read a writing system that has no training corpus, because the shorthand rules themselves supply the labels.

M02
MECHANISM: The task is refitting excavated stone flakes back onto their cores to recover how prehistoric tools were knapped. Archaeologists still do this by hand over years because datasets are tiny, the fragments are plain grey rock, and generic 3D matchers ignore fracture physics. Each flake and core is scanned with a phone into a 20k-point cloud. A 15M-parameter point transformer embeds every fracture surface (scars, bulb, ripple direction), and a neural implicit "fracture field" predicts which flake would break off for any core state and strike. Most training is synthetic: a low-resolution GPU fracture simulator generates millions of (core, strike, flake) triples overnight, and a contrastive loss on the few hundred published refit sets aligns the model to real scans. At inference, a beam search over removal order puts the flakes back onto the core and scores each step for physical plausibility, all within 6 GB of VRAM.
VALUE: It reconstructs the full knapping order from a pile of debris, which shape-matching alone cannot do because neighbouring flakes rarely share clean edges.

M03
MECHANISM: Potters still develop ceramic glazes by firing test tiles one at a time. AI has failed here because the data are small and noisy (about 20k community recipes with uneven photos), and surface color depends nonlinearly on oxide chemistry, kiln atmosphere and cooling, which gives generic vision models nothing to anchor to. The model turns a recipe into oxide ratios (Unity Molecular Formula) through a frozen stoichiometry layer that maps materials to oxides. A 30M-parameter conditional decoder takes those ratios plus cone, atmosphere and cooling curve and predicts calibrated Lab color, gloss and the probabilities of crawling and crazing. It trains on photos white-balanced against a printed color card, and a crazing head is supervised by thermal-expansion mismatch computed against the clay body. At inference, gradient descent in recipe space restricted to the user's own materials finds a recipe for a target photo. Training takes about 6 GPU-hours and a search takes seconds.
VALUE: A potter photographs a surface they want and gets a recipe made from materials they own that is chemically valid and safe from crazing, which neither an image generator nor a recipe lookup can give.

M04
MECHANISM: Dating old timber from its tree rings (dendrochronology) needs cores, microscopes and an expert to match ring widths against regional records. Machine learning has only detected rings on clean lab scans and has never dated a rough phone photo of a barn beam or a stump. An 8M-parameter U-Net traces several radial paths and extracts ring-width series with an uncertainty for each ring. An encoder turns the detrended series into embeddings. Dating then treats offset-only matching as a 1D convolution against about 4,000 regional chronologies from the ITRDB public tree-ring database, held in RAM. The result is a probability for each calendar year of the outermost ring, plus species and region. Training uses the database's millions of measured series, rendered into synthetic wood images with saw marks, cracks and missing or false rings, so both stages learn end to end.
VALUE: Homeowners and conservators get a calendar date with a stated confidence from one photo, where today it takes a lab visit and weeks of expert matching.

M05
MECHANISM: The task is retuning or replacing keys of a Balinese or Javanese gamelan. Each ensemble has its own tuning (embat) and its own beating between paired instruments (ombak), so no equal-tempered tool applies and AI has never been tried. The inputs are tap recordings of every key plus a phone 3D scan of the damaged or new bronze key. A set transformer over all the keys' measured overtone spectra learns a latent embat vector for the ensemble, and a mesh graph network stands in for finite-element vibration analysis, mapping key shape to its first eight overtones. The output is a grinding map: an optimization through that stand-in model proposes where and how much bronze to remove so the key matches the ensemble's target overtones and its beat rate against its partner. The stand-in model trains on about 200k GPU finite-element simulations of randomized key shapes (hours on a 3060), and the embat encoder trains on a few hundred recorded ensembles by learning to reconstruct masked keys.
VALUE: It lets a non-specialist restore a gamelan's own tuning signature, which a generic tuner cannot even define.

M06
MECHANISM: The task is transcribing and teaching whistled languages (Silbo Gomero, Turkish kuş dili, Hmong whistled speech). They have only minutes to hours of recordings and no written corpus, and speech recognition treats them as music. The model forces everything through an explicit articulatory bottleneck: a distilled 30M-parameter encoder trained on ordinary spoken Spanish or Turkish predicts F2 formant paths and consonant types. A differentiable layer, initialized from linguists' rules, then turns those into a single whistle pitch-and-loudness contour. Training uses cycle consistency (speech to synthetic whistle to recovered phonemes), plus a small adversarial loss against the few real whistle recordings. The reverse decoder outputs phoneme lattices that a small spoken-language model held in system RAM rescores, and the whole system stays under 100M parameters and trains in days with mixed precision.
VALUE: It learns whistled speech from spoken-language data alone, where any end-to-end model fails for lack of transcribed whistle recordings.

M07
MECHANISM: Task: Labanotation dance scores, which motion AI has ignored because no paired score-and-motion data exists. A rule-based notator converts public motion-capture data (for example AMASS) into Labanotation symbol columns, which creates millions of synthetic pairs. A symbol-graph transformer (about 60M parameters) reads each column's symbols, durations and body-part staff. It outputs target poses and timing for a physics-simulated skeleton, run as a GPU rigid-body simulation at 24 joints. Training combines pose-reconstruction loss, a loss for re-notating the generated motion and matching the score, and balance and torque penalties. Then the model is fine-tuned on a few dozen real scores that have archival film.
VALUE: Archival scores become physically plausible performances that a dancer can watch and learn from.

M08
MECHANISM: Task: forecasting how one specific plank will move. AI has been absent because wood science works at species level and grading AI needs industrial scanners. Phone photos of both end-grain faces and two long faces feed a 30M-parameter U-Net. It infers a 3D field of growth-ring orientation, density and sapwood/heartwood. That field drives a differentiable finite-element model with about 20k elements, using orthotropic shrinkage, which predicts cup, bow and twist under any humidity schedule. The network pretrains on procedurally grown synthetic logs sawn virtually. It then self-supervises from users photographing the same board at two humidity levels: the measured deformation becomes the loss, backpropagated through the finite-element model. The finite-element model runs in milliseconds on the GPU, and the photo-to-field network is small.
VALUE: Woodworkers can choose and orient individual boards before cutting, which no lumber-grading system or species table can do.

M09
MECHANISM: Luthiers shape spruce violin plates by tapping them and listening for the free-plate modes (1, 2 and 5). AI has stayed out because every billet of wood is different and no datasets exist. A material-identification network reads phone recordings of taps at 5 points plus a few caliper readings. It infers the wood's directional stiffness values and density. A graph-network surrogate on a 2k-node plate mesh predicts mode frequencies and shapes from a thickness map plus those material values. It trains on about 2M finite-element eigen-solves run on the same PC's CPU and 32 GB of RAM (about a week), then is fine-tuned on a few real plates. Carving advice comes from optimizing the surrogate under a remove-wood-only constraint, which outputs a map of how much wood to take off and where to hit the target modes.
VALUE: It predicts the acoustic effect of cuts that cannot be undone before the gouge touches the wood, which no audio app or generic simulator does for a specific plank.
