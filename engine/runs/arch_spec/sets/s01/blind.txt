A01
MECHANISM: Task: finding where two particular bridge partners interpret their bids differently. Existing bridge bots model a standard bidding system, not the private habits of a real pair. Inputs are the auction as tokens, the bidder's hand and a learned partnership embedding; per-pair LoRA adapters sit on a 40M-parameter base. Outputs are a posterior over the partner's suit lengths and high-card points, plus the action the partner will take next. Training uses millions of online hand records, where all four hands are revealed after play, so every auction comes with the true hands. When what the partner inferred, judged from their next bid, differs sharply from what the bidder actually held, that agreement is flagged as misunderstood.
VALUE: It points to the exact agreements a specific partnership misreads, which no general bridge bot or coach could find from the same data.

A02
MECHANISM: A tiny convolutional hit detector (~2M parameters) runs on each frame of 240 fps video and outputs streak-segment "hits" with position, direction and two-colour brightness ratio. A bank of Kalman filters follows each spark with a ballistic state (position, velocity, drag coefficient, temperature) and a predict/correct update, as charged-particle tracking does. Vertex finding detects burst points where one track ends and several begin. The detector is trained self-supervised: it is rewarded for hits that Kalman tracks predict with high likelihood, and frames are alternated with track refits (EM). No labels are needed. Everything fits easily in 12 GB and runs at video rate on a 3060.
VALUE: It turns an unlabelled phone clip into a physical event record: drag-derived particle size, colour temperature and burst vertices. Pixel classifiers trained on the same budget cannot produce these quantities, and they break when the camera or lighting changes.

A03
MECHANISM: The task is finding hidden rot in wooden boat hulls and timber frames, where surveyors tap with a hammer and listen. AI is absent because every hull rings differently, so fixed sound-to-rot rules never carry over from one hull to the next. The user taps about 300 points while the phone films the hammer head to get each position and records each 50 ms ring. Each ring is reduced to a 64-band spectrum plus a decay rate per band. A learned function of under 1M weights predicts each tap's spectrum from its 8 nearest neighbours' spectra, their distances and the grain direction. Sound wood is predictable from its neighbours, so the per-point prediction error forms the rot map. Training first hides points on thousands of hulls and predicts them back. A small second stage then maps error maps to awl-probe softness depths that surveyors record when the boats are hauled out, and both stages train overnight.
VALUE: It gives an objective rot map for each hull from a phone and a hammer, where a generic sound classifier on the same budget fails because no two hulls share a baseline.

A04
MECHANISM: Natural changes in air pressure (barometric tides and weather fronts) serve as the forcing signal, and a cheap ultrasonic anemometer at each entrance records the airflow response. Together they give an impedance spectrum Z(f) per entrance, the same way the forced oscillation technique tests lungs. A small transformer maps the spectrum, plus the volume and resistance already known from the survey, to a posterior over a multi-compartment model (an Otis-style network of resistances and compliances): how many hidden compartments there are, how big they are, and how strongly they are connected. It is trained on millions of synthetic networks made by deleting passages from real surveys, then fine-tuned on caves whose volume was later measured. The model is under 20M parameters and trains in hours on the 3060.
VALUE: It gives a measured, uncertainty-bounded estimate of volume nobody has entered yet from a $40 sensor, where the same budget otherwise buys only geologic guesswork.

A05
MECHANISM: Surveyed passage segments are treated like contigs (assembled genome fragments). "Mate-pair" links between separate passage ends come from dye traces, cave radio fixes, cross-correlated temperature and airflow loggers (the lag gives the travel distance), and shared sediments. A genome-scaffolder engine keeps a graph of passage ends with a distribution of gap lengths on each edge. It solves ordering and orientation with an exact integer program, held in RAM. A graph network, trained on the dated history of club surveys (hide connections found later, then predict them), scores how plausible each edge is from rock-layer orientation and fracture alignment taken from the elevation model. The output is the most likely set of connections with gap lengths, which turns into dig targets.
VALUE: It combines different kinds of weak evidence into connection hypotheses that can be checked, which no geometric interpolation can do.

A06
MECHANISM: The task is attributing hand-hewn timber in historic buildings to individual axes and carpenters from phone photos of tool marks. AI has been absent because there are no labels, marks are worn, and it is a craft-history niche. A small CNN (about 15M parameters) turns each mark into binary "nick loci", the presence of blade-edge defects at positions along the edge, and these are clustered into blade prototypes stored in RAM. Within each prototype, pairwise linkage disequilibrium between loci is measured. One blade should give independent noise, so significant LD means two blades were mixed together. A split tested by mutual information then creates two prototypes. If the LD pattern is nested (one nick set is a subset of the other), the split is re-typed as a resharpening successor edge in a time-ordered blade lineage. The encoder trains contrastively on augmented crops, with re-clustering after each split.
VALUE: It recovers how many tools were used, their sequence, and the build phases of an undocumented structure from cheap photos.

A07
MECHANISM: The tracked bursts from Idea 1 form genealogies: carrier line, first fork, second-generation forks. These are scored under a multitype Galton–Watson branching process with timed delays. The process's offspring mean, generation depth and delay distribution are estimated by exact tree likelihood, as phylodynamics estimates R from pathogen trees. Carbon drives CO-explosion branching. Elements such as Mn, Cr, W and Mo shift the delay distribution, the colour type and terminal shapes (W gives dull red; Mo gives detached "arrowhead" tips). Pressure changes how many trees appear, not the statistics within each tree, so the estimates do not depend on it. Inference is CPU-bound over a few thousand trees and runs in seconds in 32 GB RAM.
VALUE: Its composition estimates come from per-tree statistics that stay the same across grinders, wheels and operators, which no same-budget image model can match.

A08
MECHANISM: The task is estimating how many hours ago a footprint was pressed into mud, sand or snow, so search-and-rescue teams can tell which trail is fresh. AI has failed here because no aged tracks with known times exist, and how a print decays depends on the weather since it was made. Volunteers press a standard boot into the ground next to a staked phone that photographs the print every 20 minutes from two flash angles, while a cheap weather station logs temperature, rain and wind. Each photo pair becomes a relief stored as rim sharpness, wall slope and fill-depth profiles. A small learned function takes the current profiles plus the weather summed since each candidate start time and scores every elapsed-hour bin. Training pushes the true elapsed time to the top score, and an ordering penalty forces later photos of the same print to be judged older. Each print is a few thousand numbers and the weather sums live in RAM, so millions of timed photos train on one GPU.
VALUE: From a single photo it gives a calibrated age range such as "3-6 h ago" with its uncertainty, which trackers' intuition cannot give consistently.

A09
MECHANISM: The task is showing paddlers, before they commit, where submerged rocks, undercuts and trapped logs sit under fast whitewater. AI has been absent here because nobody has ground truth for what lies beneath moving water. Input is a 10-second phone clip of a rapid. Tracking how foam patches move from frame to frame gives a 128x128 field of surface speed and direction plus a surface-texture field. A learned function of about 5M weights maps these to a grid of depth below the surface and a per-cell probability that water flows under rock. The seasons supply training pairs for free: the same stretch is filmed at high water and photographed at low water, and the two are aligned by GPS and fixed landmarks, so the riverbed exposed at low flow is the target for the high-flow surface. The weights are driven by the gap between predicted and exposed bed heights, with the gauge-level difference as an extra input. Model and batches fit in 12 GB, a few thousand river-stretch pairs train in days, and a clip is answered in under a second.
VALUE: Paddlers get a read of what the surface hides from a phone, which no same-budget sensor or rule of thumb provides.

A10
MECHANISM: Task: choosing hand-plane direction and depth on figured wood (curly or interlocked grain) without tear-out. AI is absent because the work is tactile, every board is different, and tear-out only appears after the cut. Phone macro video of the board is cut into tiles, and a fixed random projection turns each tile into 2048 sparse binary cells. A contact microphone on the plane sorts each stroke, with a fixed spectral threshold, into a smooth hiss or a crackle. A 2048×16 cell-to-direction link table is updated locally: links active during a smooth stroke are strengthened and those active during a crackle are inhibited, and no output is ever scored. Recall is one sparse product per tile, so a full map of safe directions for the board renders live.
VALUE: It gives a safe-planing map for a board it has never seen, learned from the sound of the user's own strokes.

A11
MECHANISM: Every dated survey leg is treated as an event in a self-exciting point process, as in aftershock forecasting. A background intensity field comes from a small CNN over the elevation model and geology layers, and each discovery raises the chance of more discoveries nearby, fading over time and distance (Omori-like kernels). Parameters are fitted by maximum likelihood on decades of version-controlled club survey history. The output is the expected new passage per trip for each open lead, which feeds a simple scheduler for expedition planning. The whole model fits in under 1 GB and fits in minutes.
VALUE: It turns the exploration record itself into a calibrated forecast of where effort will pay off, which caver intuition and geology-only maps cannot provide.

A12
MECHANISM: The task is building an archaeological Harris matrix, the ordering of soil layers, from trench photos, level readings and excavators' notes. AI has been absent because each site is unique, labels are tiny and errors are buried in hand-drawn sheets. A 30M-parameter segmentation-plus-relation network turns photos and notes into a graph: nodes are contexts typed deposit, cut or fill, and edges are above/below relations with confidences. A weighted least-squares "state estimator" fits a latent depth-time coordinate to each node from every redundant relation and elevation. The largest normalized residual (power-grid test) names the single worst relation, which is then deleted, reversed, or used to re-type its node. Training is self-supervised from internal consistency on about 200 published matrices plus synthetic layer-cake simulations, and the solver is sparse, so all of this runs in seconds on the 3060.
VALUE: It finds and fixes the one wrong relation or mis-typed layer behind a contradiction, not just reporting that a cycle exists.

A13
MECHANISM: Task: live accompaniment of unmetered, ornamented solo singing such as sean-nós or Persian āvāz, where beat- and score-based systems fail because there is no meter. A GPU bank of 8192 phase oscillators with periods from 0.2 to 20 s is driven by the singer's breath and syllable onsets, picked up by a phone microphone. Each oscillator shifts its frequency by the sign of its phase lag to the input, splits when it locks onto two alternating periods, and dies after a long time unlocked. Drone swells and harp strikes fire when large locked groups pass phase zero together, and a ring buffer of past phrase phases lets recurring phrases lock in again at once. Nothing is trained offline, and it runs in milliseconds within a few hundred MB.
VALUE: It accompanies free-rhythm singing by following the singer's breathing, which tempo trackers and sequence models cannot do on any budget.

A14
MECHANISM: Following population-pharmacokinetics practice, the branching and track parameters are modelled as composition fixed effects (shared across every user) plus random effects for each session (wheel grit, RPM, camera white balance), with fixed and random effects fitted jointly by Laplace approximation. In each session the user grinds one or two reference bars of known grade, which pin down that session's random effects. Each unknown bar then gets a posterior over a grade list, with credible intervals. Fixed effects are refined as uploaded sessions accumulate, and each new session needs only seconds of CPU on the PC. The posterior feeds a small lookup table of heat treatments (hardening temperature, quench medium, temper) that takes the uncertainty into account.
VALUE: It gives calibrated "this is probably 1080 versus O1, here is how sure" answers, and recommends the right quench for unknown scrap, from two reference bars per session instead of thousands of labelled samples.

A15
MECHANISM: Task: dry-stone walling, choosing the next stone from an irregular pile. AI is absent here because a wall's stability only shows over seasons, and no dataset or reliable simulator exists. Phone depth video of each placed stone is fused on the GPU into contact patches, and a fixed, untrained curvature hash turns each patch into a 64-bit code. A ledger in RAM stores integer counts for pairs of codes, and 32 GB holds tens of millions of them. Each weekly rescan adds one count to every contact pair whose stones have not moved and deletes pairs that shifted, so there is no target and no error, only survival. To place a stone, the system ranks the scanned pile by the summed survival counts of each stone's possible contacts against the gap, a hash sweep that takes seconds.
VALUE: It learns stability from real walls surviving frost and time, which no same-budget simulator or labelled set can supply.

A16
MECHANISM: The task is mapping lost 19th-century clay field drains under farmland from drone RGB and thermal images taken after rain. AI has been absent because no ground truth exists and the drains are invisible, so only slow GPR surveys have been used. A 20M-parameter U-Net predicts surface wetness from a current drain graph, using a fast non-differentiable Darcy flow solve on a 512² grid, and observed minus predicted gives the difference map. Ridges above 3σ insert drain segments by tracing along the ridge, troughs below −3σ delete segments, and each edit is kept only if R-free, the error on 5% of held-out pixels, improves. The U-Net is pretrained on simulated fields with random drain layouts, then fine-tuned self-consistently on real flights. Everything fits easily in 12 GB.
VALUE: It turns one post-rain drone flight into a validated drain network with honest error bars, which a pixel segmenter cannot do without labels.

A17
MECHANISM: Task: deciding when each dish on each order is started ("fired") in a small restaurant kitchen. Kitchen display systems use fixed timers, and no learning system has ever modelled how cooking stations compete for time. Inputs are an event stream of open orders (items, modifiers, station), each station's queue, the cooks on shift, and the time. A 20M-parameter transformer that treats the open orders as a set outputs, for each item, a probability of firing it now. It is trained by offline RL (implicit Q-learning) on the timestamp logs already stored in the point-of-sale and display systems. The reward favours a table's courses reaching the pass together, short order times and few remakes. A year of logs is about 1M events, and it trains overnight on the 3060.
VALUE: It times orders across the stations that compete for the same cooks and equipment, using only that restaurant's existing logs, which fixed timers and generic arrival-time predictors cannot do.

A18
MECHANISM: Task: programming lighting cues for live theatre. AI has never been used here, and DJ "sound-to-light" fails because it reacts to loudness and ignores the drama. Inputs are the lighting console's channel states (compressed by a small autoencoder that groups channels into about 256 fixture tokens), the script position aligned to rehearsal audio, and the cue history. A 60M-parameter transformer outputs the change to the next cue state and its fade time. It is pretrained on thousands of show files that designers share. It is then fine-tuned on the designer's own edit and undo stream from technical rehearsals, where each correction is a preference pair. Everything fits in 12 GB at FP16, and inference runs live during rehearsal.
VALUE: It learns one designer's personal style in a single technical week and drafts the next 300 cues, which neither a generic model nor reactive sound-to-light can do.
