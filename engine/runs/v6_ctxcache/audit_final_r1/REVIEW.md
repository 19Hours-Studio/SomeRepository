=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Popularity order with second-sighting cache writes
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 1.06–1.83x counts only document input tokens, but a real bill also has the system prompt (cached in both cases), the question and output tokens priced at 5x input, and these shrink the saving to the whole request a lot. | Example request: 2k-token system prompt read at 0.1x, 2.5k document tokens, a 50-token question, and 300 output tokens at 5x. Baseline cost is 4,250 input-token units. The idea's document ratios of 1.06x and 1.83x become **1.04x and 1.36x** for the whole request.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The quality test has no sample size, and a reorder effect of 1 F1 point cannot be detected without a very large paired set. | With a per-question paired F1 standard deviation of about 0.35, 1 point needs n ≈ (1.96+0.84)²·0.35²/0.01² ≈ **9,600 paired questions** per model at 80% power.
DEFECT | MINOR | The rule for choosing the TTL is backwards at high traffic: in my replay the 1-hour TTL beats the 5-minute TTL even at 1,000 requests per 5 minutes, because reads that hit refresh the entry for free and writes are rare. | In my simulation at 1,000 requests per 5 minutes, document ratio with second-sighting was 1.26 (5-min) vs 1.40 (1-h) at Zipf 1.05, and 2.44 vs 2.96 at Zipf 1.5.
DEFECT | MINOR | All 4 allowed breakpoints go to document depths 2–5, so no breakpoint is left for the system prompt and tool definitions; on a depth-2 miss that block may be written or billed at full price, which the bill does not show. | Anthropic allows at most 4 cache_control breakpoints per request.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 1,024-token minimum prefix is out of date for current small and flagship models. | Third-party documentation (orq.ai, LiteLLM, AWS Bedrock) lists a 4,096-token minimum for Opus 4.5 and Haiku 4.5; I did not confirm this on Anthropic's live page.
SURVIVES | My own replay roughly reproduces the stated document-only ratios. At 10–1,000 requests per 5 minutes: 1.01–1.26 (5-min TTL) and 1.09–1.40 (1-h) at Zipf 1.05; 1.44–2.44 and 1.83–2.96 at Zipf 1.5. With second-sighting the idea never fell below 1.0x, against 0.84x for always-write at Zipf 1.05 and 10 requests per 5 minutes. An online counter gave the same reuse as oracle ranks to 3 decimals in every regime. The multipliers (1.25x, 2x, 0.1x) are confirmed on Anthropic's documentation.
RATIO | 1.2 | Whole request with output tokens included, mid-range traffic, Zipf 1.2, against sending documents uncached with the system prompt cached.
KNOWN | partly | RAGCache (knowledge tree of document KV plus cache-aware reordering, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12457v1), CacheWeaver (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19667) and Berkeley's relational-query reordering (https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b5dc49f44db2fadc5c4d717c57f4a424-Abstract-Conference.html). Second-hit admission (as in CDNs and TinyLFU) applied to breakpoint placement looks new, but it is a small step.
NEW FACT | Anthropic pricing for Sonnet 4: $3 base input, $3.75 for 5-minute writes, $6 for 1-hour writes, $0.30 for reads per million tokens | https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-caching

IDEA 2: Canonical ordering plus position-independent chunks
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 5–10x is measured against prefix caching with random order, but the idea itself puts canonical ordering in front. The fair baseline is prefix caching that also uses canonical order, and against that the saving is much smaller. | Steady-state replay (100k chunks, top-5): canonical-order reuse 0.467/0.618/0.840; random 0.231/0.327/0.546; chunk seen before 0.931/0.954/0.984. Document prefill is 4.6–11.6x lower than random-order prefix caching, but only **3.2–4.1x** lower than canonical-order prefix caching. Counting a 300-token decode, the whole-request gain is **1.17–1.47x**.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The storage figure is per 100k tokens, but the chunk store must hold the whole corpus, and the 85–90% hit rate assumes nearly every chunk has already been stored. | 100k chunks × 500 tokens = 50M tokens: **14.9 TiB** in BF16 at 70B (6.0 TiB at 8B), or about 2.5–3 TiB at 3 bits. The idea's own 30.5 GiB per 100k tokens checks out.
DEFECT | MAJOR | "100% for the canonical ones" is wrong: a canonical order gives an exact hit only if that same prefix was already sent within the TTL or before eviction. | Canonical-order reuse was 0.47–0.84 with unlimited memory, and 0.16–0.70 with a 5-minute TTL at 10–1,000 requests per 5 minutes.
DEFECT | MINOR | "One KV copy per chunk" really means one copy per (system prompt, chunk) pair, because each chunk is encoded with the shared system prompt in front of it. | Storage multiplies by the number of distinct system prompts or tenants, and HijackKV-style isolation makes this worse.
DEFECT | MINOR | The "about 6x" from roughly 3-bit compression ignores per-group scale factors. | 16/3 = 5.3x before overhead.
SURVIVES | Berkeley paper: up to 3.4x job completion time and 32% cost saving under OpenAI and Anthropic pricing (confirmed). HijackKV exists (arXiv 2607.19957). 30.5 GiB per 100k tokens at 70B BF16 reproduces.
RATIO | 1.3 | Whole request including decode, against canonical-order prefix caching, before counting the cost of 6–15 TiB of KV storage and its I/O.
KNOWN | yes | CacheBlend, EPIC and APE, with LMCache's CacheBlend integration; RAGCache for document trees; Berkeley reordering for canonical order.
NEW FACT | CacheWeaver's greedy ordering reaches 97.5% of the oracle-ordering gain and cuts median TTFT by 20–33% against retrieval-order prefix caching on vLLM | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19667

IDEA 3: Popularity-first exact-prefix document ordering
DEFECT | MAJOR | The reuse figures 0.400/0.546/0.768 and the 1.17–1.78x are given without a request rate, yet reuse depends heavily on traffic, and with always-write the method loses money at low traffic. | My replay with a 5-minute TTL at 10 requests per 5 minutes: reuse 0.05/0.17/0.41, document ratio **0.84/0.95/1.28x**. With unlimited memory: 0.467/0.618/0.840. The stated values fall between these, so they depend on an unstated rate.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The ratio is for documents only; with output tokens and the question included it shrinks. | With the same example request as Idea 1, document ratios of 1.17x and 1.78x become about **1.1x and 1.33x** for the whole request.
DEFECT | MINOR | "Stateless counter" is wrong: it holds about 100k counters, and they must be shared by every client instance to be global. | The online counter matched oracle ranks exactly in my replay, so this costs operations work, not reuse.
DEFECT | MINOR | The test does not size the sample. | Same as Idea 1: about 9,600 paired questions to detect 1 F1 point.
SURVIVES | Break-even reuse of 0.217 is exact: 1.25 − 1.15r = 1 gives r = 0.217. "Hash order loses" reproduces (about 0.93x at r = 0.152). Counter order ≈ trie-greedy order holds.
RATIO | 1.15 | Whole request, averaged over traffic regimes. It is below 1 at 10 requests per 5 minutes with flat popularity, because this version lacks Idea 1's admission fix.
KNOWN | partly | RAGCache's cache-aware reordering (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12457v1) and CacheWeaver (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19667). Idea 1 supersedes this idea.

IDEA 4: Content-addressed position-independent KV with gated repair
DEFECT | FATAL | "Fall back to exact recompute when the gate fails" cannot work per request: the shadow check runs on only ε = 1% of traffic, so 99% of requests are never checked, and their failures can be neither caught nor recomputed. The gate can only tune r after the fact. | It also has a statistical floor: bounding the failure rate below 1% at 95% confidence needs about 300 clean shadow samples (rule of three), which is **30,000 requests per chunk type** at ε = 1%.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "3–10x" is prefill-only, and the 10x end is EPIC-only repair, which the idea itself says fails on multi-hop. The recommended full stack with shadow cost gives far less, and decode is unchanged. | 0.83 / (0.232 + 0.01) = **3.4x** on prefill. With a 300-token output (about 1,500 prefill-token equivalents) the whole request is (4,250 + 1,500) / (1,239 + 1,500) ≈ **2.1x**, and only about 1.4x against canonical-order prefix caching.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Loading reused KV from NVMe caps throughput below what the GPU could do with the saved compute. | 4,096 tokens × 128 KiB = 0.537 GB per request; 7 GB/s ÷ 0.537 GB = **13 requests/s** per drive, against about 28 requests/s of prefill capacity at 23.2% of 152 ms.
DEFECT | MINOR | Storing keys before rotation forces the kernel to re-apply RoPE to every cached key at each decode step, and standard paged FlashAttention kernels store rotated keys. Re-rotating rotated keys once at splice time is already exact. | The extra cost is per decode step, not one-time.
DEFECT | MINOR | Reuse figures do not quite match the stated recompute budget: r = 30% plus EPIC plus splice windows recomputes (256 + 1,229 + 128) / 5,120 = 31.5% of tokens, so reuse is 68.5%, not the stated low end of 74%. | 256 EPIC tokens + 1,229 (30% of 4,096) + 128 splice tokens.
SURVIVES | 128 KiB per token at 8B and 320 KiB at 70B (328 GB per 1M tokens) reproduce. So do 10.7 ms over PCIe5 at 50 GB/s, 77 ms from NVMe at 7 GB/s, prefill of about 152–166 ms at about 50% MFU on an H100, the system-prompt-only share of 19–20%, and prefill compute of 9.9/5.3/3.6/3.0x.
RATIO | 1.5 | Whole request including decode and the 1% shadow, using the full repair stack, averaged between random-order and canonical-order prefix-caching baselines. Fallback and storage are excluded.
KNOWN | partly | CacheBlend, EPIC, Cache-Craft, KVShare and LMCache already do position-independent reuse with repair. Content-defined offset lookup and the quality SLO are the new parts, and the SLO as written is defective (see FATAL).
