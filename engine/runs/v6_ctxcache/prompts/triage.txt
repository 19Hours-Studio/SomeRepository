Hosted LLM APIs (Anthropic, OpenAI and others) offer prompt caching. The provider stores the computed intermediate state (the attention key/value, or KV, cache) for a request's token prefix. A later request is served from that state only if its prefix is token-for-token identical, and the cached portion is billed at a discount. An edit in the middle of a prompt, an insertion, a changed tool output, or a different document order breaks the match from the first differing token onward. Everything after that point is recomputed and billed at full price. The asker wants a way to make cached context much cheaper and to reach a cache-hit rate close to 100%. This must include requests that differ from earlier ones in the middle, by insertions, tool outputs or reordered documents, and it must work without retraining or fine-tuning the model. The question does not say whether the asker runs their own serving stack or uses a third-party API, or whether the solution may sit on the client side, the server side, or both. What is the best way to do this?
A good answer: A good answer proposes one or more concrete, new or non-obvious approaches. Each must state three things:
- which request-change cases it covers: middle edit, insertion, tool output, document reordering;
- its expected hit rate and cost change, with the reasoning or a quantitative estimate;
- its effect on output quality compared with full recomputation, and how that effect could be tested.
Each approach must also say where it can run (client only, own serving stack, or provider-side) and what it costs in memory, storage and latency. It must be checked against existing prior art and say what it adds. It must state honestly which cases cannot reach about 100% under the stated constraints.
Constraints: 
- No retraining or fine-tuning of the model.
- Target hit rate close to 100% across non-identical prefixes.
- Much lower cost than current prefix caching.
- Nothing else was stated.

Candidate answers (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: Quote-anchor output expansion
MECHANISM: The model writes `⟦c3: "first six words … last six words"⟧` instead of re-typing text it is quoting from a retrieved chunk. The client fuzzy-matches the anchors against the chunk and splices in the exact text, re-asking on a miss. This runs client-side on any hosted API with no stored state. It differs from OpenAI Predicted Outputs, the closest prior art, which bills rejected tokens and so cuts latency, not cost; quote anchors instead remove output tokens before they are generated. The deciding quantity is the quoted share of answer tokens multiplied by the anchor-resolution accuracy, which should be tested against full answers on multi-hop QA with an LLM judge plus exact-match on the quoted spans.
VALUE: If at least 35% of answer tokens are quotes, B's output cost falls at least 17%, which is the output share needed, alongside a 10x input cut, to bring RAG Q&A to the 3x target.

IDEA 2
NAME: Signed public-prefix KV pool
MECHANISM: Agent harnesses publish signed, version-pinned system prompts and tool schemas, assumed to be about 15–20k tokens. Their KV is precomputed once and pinned on every replica. Requests whose prefix hash matches a signed entry get a hit without a per-organisation write. Closest earlier work: per-organisation prompt caching, which isolates organisations because shared caches leak through timing side channels. Sharing only public signed content removes that leak. The deciding quantity is the harness-prefix share of all writes, including writes repeated after expiry. The estimate is a 5–9% bill cut, with no change in quality. It runs provider-side only.
VALUE: Session starts and cross-replica first turns stop paying the write premium for content that is identical across all users.

IDEA 3
NAME: Canonical ordering plus position-independent chunks
MECHANISM: Runs on your own stack or provider-side and covers reordered documents and RAG sets. First, sort retrieved documents and tool schemas by stable ID and popularity, so most reorders become exact prefix hits. Berkeley's relational-query reordering already shows this works, with up to 3.4× faster jobs and 32% lower cost under OpenAI and Anthropic pricing. For the rest, use a store of KV per chunk, computed once with the shared system prompt in front of it. APE showed this shared prefix removes the per-chunk attention-sink error with no training, keeping 93–98% of sequential-encoding quality. At assembly time, re-rotate positions exactly, recompute the first few tokens of each chunk (EPIC: up to 8× TTFT and 7× throughput, with negligible accuracy loss) and selectively recompute 10–15% of tokens (CacheBlend: 1–3% quality loss, 2.2–3.3× TTFT). Token hit rate is then about 85–90% for permuted sets and 100% for the canonical ones. Storage is one KV copy per chunk instead of one per prefix: 30.5 GiB per 100k tokens in BF16, about 6× less with roughly 3-bit compression. To test, compare answers under canonical, random and adversarial document orders against full recompute, which measures both the order effect and the blending error. Chunks must be isolated per tenant: HijackKV shows shared position-independent KV can be poisoned across users.
VALUE: Adding canonical ordering in front of CacheBlend/EPIC turns most reorders into lossless exact hits, so blending's 1–3% loss applies only to the remainder. Expect about 5–10× less prefill than prefix caching on RAG traffic where the document set varies.

IDEA 4
NAME: Append-only journal with cost-optimal compaction
MECHANISM: This runs on the client only, works with any plain API, and is the only approach here that is exact. Never change earlier tokens. A middle edit or insertion becomes a short correction appended at the end ("§3.2 now reads …"). Tool outputs are never cut back in place. Documents go in a fixed order, sorted by content hash, and the relevance ranking is given in a short note at the end. Rewriting from the earliest edit point (compaction) happens only when a ski-rental rule says so: compact once the 0.1x re-read spend on accumulated corrections reaches (1.25−0.1)·(N−p_min). This rule is guaranteed to cost at most 2x the best possible schedule. My exact arithmetic for a 100k context with a 400-token edit at 30k: rewriting in place costs 90,500 base-token units, appending costs 10,500, so appending is 8.6x cheaper this turn and stays cheaper for 2,000 later turns. For idle gaps longer than the 5-minute TTL, a 1-token "keep-alive" request costs 0.1N against 1.25N for rewriting after a miss. That pays for up to 12.5 pings (about 62 minutes) if the user is sure to return, or 5.8 pings if the chance is 50%. A 1-hour write costs the same as 7.5 pings. On the cost side, there is no extra memory and no extra latency. The appended text adds a few hundred tokens per edit, re-read at 0.1x.
VALUE: Every request type becomes a pure prefix extension, so the hit rate equals 1 − (new tokens / total). That is typically 94–98% per agent turn. Against a client that edits in place, a single missed 10% of old context per turn adds 69% to the bill (35% at 5%). Quality risk: the model now sees the stale text plus a correction rather than a clean edit. Test it on your own evals (SWE-bench-style pass rate) and measure how often stale facts leak into answers, comparing journal against rewrite. This needs a real-model run.

IDEA 5
NAME: Chunk-keyed speculative draft trees
MECHANISM: On your own vLLM or SGLang stack, store past generations in suffix trees indexed by the hashes of the retrieved chunks they were conditioned on. Draft from the trees whose chunk sets overlap the current request, and let the target model verify the drafts exactly. SuffixDecoding and REST, the closest prior art, use global or per-request stores, so their acceptance falls when unrelated traffic dilutes the store. Keying by document narrows the candidates to text actually written about these sources, and storage is only text, a few KB per answer. The deciding quantity is mean accepted tokens per verification step at production batch size.
VALUE: Repeat-document RAG could get roughly 2–3x fewer decode GPU-seconds at moderate batch with output unchanged, which is the decode cut that has to accompany a 10x prefill cut to reach 3x.

IDEA 6
NAME: Edit-local contiguous KV repair with fallback
MECHANISM: Runs on your own serving stack or provider-side; plain API users can't do it. It covers middle edits, insertions, deletions and changed tool outputs. Tokens before the first changed token are an ordinary exact prefix hit. Only the edited span is prefilled fresh. The old suffix after it is reused with an exact RoPE position shift (the rotation is exact; V is untouched). Then one contiguous window, from the edit to the end of its block, is recomputed, not scattered "important" tokens. At matched budget, contiguous windows beat signal-based token picking by a wide margin and run 13–21× faster than a re-prefill (2609.17983). Recomputing 2–5% of the suffix gets close to full-recompute exact match on four multi-hop QA datasets (2610.17338). Scattered selection fails on real fact edits: CacheBlend at 15% recompute scores 0.13 against 1.00 for full re-prefill (2606.17107). My addition is a stored dependency index written at prefill time: for 4 layers, each token's top-8 source blocks with their attention mass, about 96 B/token, or 0.03% of Llama-70B's 320 KiB/token. It sizes the repair window, and the request falls back to a full suffix prefill when long dependency chains point at the edited span. Arithmetic for a uniformly placed edit: prefix caching alone recomputes 50% of the context; this recomputes 1–2.5%, or 7.5% at CacheBlend's 15% rate. That is 20–50× less prefill than prefix caching, plus the cost of loading the reused KV. Test it on paired fact-edit and deletion tasks: the answer must match full recompute, and how often the fallback fires is the honest hit-rate figure.
VALUE: About 97–99% of post-edit tokens are reused instead of about 50%, so a middle edit costs about 20–50× less prefill than prefix caching. The quality claims rest on 2026 preprints and still need a run on a real model.

IDEA 7
NAME: Erratum-append discipline with rebase-on-cold
MECHANISM: Client-only, so it works on any API (Anthropic: reads 0.1×, 5-minute writes 1.25×; OpenAI GPT-5: $0.125 cached vs $1.25 input). The context is append-only and serialized deterministically (sorted JSON keys, no timestamps up front, as in the Manus write-up). A middle edit or changed tool output becomes an appended erratum ("field X is now Y"), never an in-place change. Exact arithmetic for a 100k-token context: an in-place edit at a uniform position costs 6.78× a fully cached turn; an erratum costs 1.03×, a 6.6× saving per edit. Over 200 simulated turns with edits on 20% of them, total cost was 1.85× lower than in-place edits and within 4% of a context that never changes. The rule I add is to fold errata back into the context only when the cache is being rewritten anyway (TTL expiry, compaction), which costs nothing extra. A ski-rental rule never triggered in the simulation for 200-token errata. Quality caveat: the model uses the erratum reliably mainly with chain-of-thought (2606.17107). Without it, test erratum vs in-place on your own tasks and compare to a fresh prompt. That paper reports a 98.5% hit rate in an online vLLM benchmark.
VALUE: Hit rate near 100% and about 1.85× lower input cost for edit-heavy agents on any API. The floor is the provider's read price (0.1×); nothing client-side can go below it.

IDEA 8
NAME: Content-addressed position-independent KV with gated repair
MECHANISM: This runs on your own serving stack or on the provider side. Split token streams at content-defined boundaries: a rolling hash over token IDs plus message and tool delimiters. A re-sent span (a reordered document, a re-read file, a moved tool output, the text after an insertion) is then found at any offset with an O(1) hash lookup. Store keys before the positional rotation is applied, so reusing them at a new position is free inside the attention kernel. Repair in layers: recompute the first ~32 tokens of each chunk to remove the false attention-sink effect (EPIC), the top-r tokens by key deviation (CacheBlend), and a 16-token window after each splice point. Add a quality gate: on ε≈1% of traffic, run a full recompute in shadow, compare the next-token distributions (KL) on the first 32 output tokens, and adjust r per chunk type. Fall back to exact recompute when the gate fails. My model for 8×512-token chunks with a 1k system prompt in a new order on an 8B model, against full recompute: EPIC-only repair needs 8.4% of full compute, r=15% needs 15.6%, the full stack needs 23.2%, and r=30% for multi-hop needs 27.9%. Prefix caching here needs ~83% of full compute, because only the system prompt hits. Memory: 128 KiB of KV per token at 8B (320 KiB at 70B, so 328 GB per 1M tokens). Loading 4,096 reused tokens takes 10.7 ms over PCIe5 or 77 ms from NVMe, against ~152 ms for full prefill. The shadow check adds ε of full prefill.
VALUE: On reordered or re-positioned content, compute drops 3–10x against prefix caching, and token reuse rises from 19% to 74–92%. Prior art covers most of the parts: CacheBlend, EPIC, Cache-Craft, KVShare, SemShareKV, and LMCache's CacheBlend for agents. What this adds is offset-independent lookup plus a quality SLO with exact fallback. Quality is model-dependent. KVShareArena finds that repairs recover only half to two-thirds of the gap on multi-source questions, and that unrepaired caches can be worse than no cache. So "≈100% hit at full quality" is not reachable for multi-hop work. The deciding test is KL/F1 against full recompute on 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue and agent replays (needs a real model).

IDEA 9
NAME: Erasure-coded pooled KV memory
MECHANISM: Each content-addressed KV chunk, keyed by its prefix hash, is striped in FP8 across the CPU DRAM of a replica group using a k-of-n erasure code (for example 8+2). This replaces full replication, so the pooled memory holds about 2.4× more sessions than 3× replication at the same capacity. Any replica can rebuild a chunk from any k stripes. Closest earlier work: Mooncake Store replicates chunks, and its capacity limit causes the evictions that lead to rewrites. The deciding quantity is the share of rewrites caused by eviction or placement rather than by expiry. Losing a stripe costs one decode, not a recompute. Output quality is unchanged because the stored values are exact FP8.
VALUE: It cuts the roughly 23%-of-bill rewrite term wherever capacity misses drive it, on your own stack or provider-side.

IDEA 10
NAME: Cut the re-read bill, not misses
MECHANISM: This runs on your own stack or provider-side. Once hits are near 100%, re-reading cached context dominates. In my model of a 50-turn agent (20k base context, +2k tokens per turn, 300 output tokens per turn), cached reads at 0.1x are 60.5% of the bill, writes 26.3% and output 13.2%. Re-read cost grows with the square of the number of turns. So hold cached KV pages in CPU/SSD and fetch only the top-K pages per query (Quest-style min/max key bounds per page, no training) for decode and for attention from new tokens. With a 2–4k token budget, that reads 6.4–12.8% of the dense KV at 32k context and 1.6–3.2% at 128k. Page metadata adds about 6% of the KV size. Quest reports up to 7.03x faster self-attention and 2.23x lower latency, with negligible accuracy loss.
VALUE: This targets the largest remaining cost line at a near-100% hit rate. It could plausibly lower the per-token cost of cached reads by about 2–5x end to end. Providers could then price cached reads by pages actually touched. Quality risk is on retrieval-heavy long-range tasks. Test it with RULER/LongBench and agent replays against dense attention (needs a real model).

IDEA 11
NAME: Entity-overlap recompute selection
MECHANISM: Cross-chunk attention is lost mostly on tokens that refer to content elsewhere, such as shared entities, coreferents and numbers. Selection uses a cheap index of entity/n-gram overlap between spliced chunks, computed without any model pass, and recomputes only those tokens plus the chunk-start sink tokens, at 2–4%. CacheBlend's layer-1 deviation selection needs a partial forward pass and keeps about 15% because its proxy is noisy. Lexical bridges target multi-hop links directly. The deciding quantity is the recall of the top-deviation tokens at a 3% budget against a full-recompute oracle.
VALUE: It cuts recompute roughly 4x while aiming at the multi-hop failures that hurt training-free reuse most.

IDEA 12
NAME: Return-hazard priced keep-alive
MECHANISM: The client logs the gaps between turns per user and per tool, including long test runs. From this it fits a survival curve, S(t) = the probability the user has not yet returned after t minutes. Just before a 5-minute expiry, it sends a 1-output-token read of the whole prefix only if the expected cost of rewriting (P(return soon) × 1.25 × prefix) exceeds 0.1 × prefix. It uses the 1-hour TTL only on the stable header when parallel sub-agents will read it. The closest prior art is fixed keep-alive scripts and the blanket 1-hour TTL. Those either ping dead sessions forever or pay +0.75× on every write, whereas this rule prices each gap. Deciding quantity: the share f of write tokens that are rewrites after expiry. If f = 0.4, the saving is about 15% of the bill (about 1.18×). It runs client-only and adds roughly zero latency; time-to-first-token after an idle gap also improves.
VALUE: It removes rewrites after expiry and during tool waits for about 0.1× per window, without changing output.

IDEA 13
NAME: TTL-aware keepalive and write-tier choice
MECHANISM: Client-only, and covers misses caused by expiry, not by changed content. Reads refresh Anthropic's 5-minute TTL for free. On a 100k-token context, a ping every 4 minutes costs 0.1N each; the 1-hour tier costs an extra 0.75N once; letting the cache expire costs 1.15N to rewrite. Keepalive is cheapest for idle gaps under about 30 minutes (70k vs 75k units at 30 minutes), the 1-hour tier for gaps of about 30–60 minutes, and expiry beyond that. Quality is unaffected because reuse stays exact. Latency is unchanged; the extra traffic is tiny pings. Gemini's explicit caching instead charges storage ($4.50 per million tokens per hour on Pro), so the same rule has to compare that hourly fee against the rewrite cost. Check your provider's terms before running keepalive pings.
VALUE: For bursty agent or chat sessions this removes the TTL miss term, up to 1.15N per idle gap, without touching the prompt.

IDEA 14
NAME: Diff-encoded repeated tool outputs
MECHANISM: The client hashes every tool output it has already placed in context. When a file or command result is requested again, it appends only "unchanged since turn 12" or a unified diff, never a second full copy. This shrinks the genuinely new tokens that pay the 1.25x write charge. The closest prior art is the file-unchanged notices in some agents. Those cover only identical re-reads; this also handles changed outputs as deltas, so a changed tool output costs the size of its diff, not its full size. The deciding quantity is the share of written tokens that duplicate content already in context, measurable from agent traces. Quality should be tested by SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench pass rate and by error rates when applying diffs.
VALUE: Directly shrinks Archetype A's largest term, cache write at 47% of the agent bill, when re-reads dominate the written tokens.

IDEA 15
NAME: Per-hash public prefix pool
MECHANISM: The provider keeps one KV entry for byte-identical content that has been registered as public: widely used agent system prompts, tool schemas and public documentation. The entry is keyed by a hash of the content plus the model version. The first write is amortised across tenants, so every tenant pays only the 0.1× read rate plus a small per-hash fee. Entries are admitted only once at least k tenants use them, and latency is padded, which blocks the cross-tenant timing leak found in prompt-cache audits. The closest prior art is per-organisation isolation by Anthropic and OpenAI. That isolation exists for privacy, and the privacy reason does not apply to content that is public by declaration. Deciding quantity: the share of written tokens that are public across tenants. An assumed 20k-token header in a 250k-token-write session gives about 3% of the bill. The share is larger for short sub-agent calls. It runs provider-side only, and the extra storage is one copy per hash.
VALUE: It removes cold-start and post-expiry writes of the shared header for every tenant.

IDEA 16
NAME: Write-anchor audit and skip
MECHANISM: The client scans usage logs for write spikes larger than half the context. These are full rewrites caused by a turn adding more than about 20 blocks (for example, parallel tool results) past the provider's lookback limit. It fixes them by always keeping a second breakpoint at the end of the previous request and by merging tool-result blocks. It also places tokens that will never be read again after the last breakpoint, so they are billed at 1.0× instead of 1.25×. Examples are a sub-agent's final call and outputs that are about to be cleared. The closest prior art is Anthropic's breakpoint guidance, which states the lookback rule but does not detect or price the misses. Deciding quantity: the miss rate per turn. At 5k new tokens per turn, one 100k-token miss every 50 turns adds 40% to the write term.
VALUE: It removes hidden full-prefix rewrites, which can be a large share of the 47% write term.

IDEA 17
NAME: Corpus-centroid conditioned chunk encoding
MECHANISM: Encode every chunk after a fixed, realistic prefix rather than an empty context. The prefix is the system prompt plus a short, medoid-selected corpus passage. Store the chunk's KV with that prefix trimmed and positions remapped by RoPE rotation. This removes the spurious attention-sink and out-of-distribution "document start" state that EPIC suppresses only by recompute. It also shrinks residual deviation, so any recompute budget goes further. The deciding quantity is the KV L2 deviation versus true context, relative to empty-prefix encoding, measured per layer.
VALUE: It gives a free, offline reduction of splice error that lowers the recompute share needed for parity across all chunk orders.

IDEA 18
NAME: Attention at the KV store
MECHANISM: On each agent turn, only the new suffix is prefilled. The suffix's queries go to the node that already holds the session's prefix KV, and that node returns partial attention outputs with log-sum-exp values. These merge exactly with local attention over the suffix, so the KV never moves and the output matches full recomputation bit-for-bit up to floating-point order. Closest earlier work: Lamina (2024) offloads decode attention only; this applies the same split to suffix prefill. The deciding quantity is traffic, n·Hq/(L·Hkv) = 8n/L. It wins whenever the n new tokens are fewer than L/8, for example 2k new tokens over a 100k context gives about 6× less traffic than a BF16 fetch. Costs: 80 per-layer round trips per turn, and compute on the store node. It runs on your own stack or provider-side.
VALUE: Hits on 100k+ contexts become cheap from any replica without moving the KV. This lowers the provider's cost of cache reads (42% of the bill), but it does nothing for middle edits.

IDEA 19
NAME: Cost-sized corpus shard prefixes
MECHANISM: Cluster the corpus offline into shards of S tokens, each in a fixed, canonical order. Each shard is a cached prefix on the hosted API. Each request is routed to one shard and appends only "relevant: §17, §203" plus the question, so varying or reordered chunk sets never change the prefix. The closest prior art is cache-augmented generation (CAG) and CacheWeaver. CAG preloads the whole corpus and pays 0.1x on every corpus token each request. CacheWeaver only shares partial prefixes. Choosing S explicitly against the 0.1x read price avoids both limits. The deciding quantity is S relative to the retrieved size R (4k): reading the shard costs 0.3·S against 3·R, so S = 16k gives $0.0048 against $0.012 for the document term. That is $0.0102 per request, or 1.7x, plus a shard write of $0.06 per cache lifetime, amortized over requests. Quality, the share of answers recovered when a needed chunk falls outside the shard, and pointer-versus-inline reading should be tested on MuSiQue and HotpotQA against inline RAG.
VALUE: Order-independent, near-100% hits on the document part of RAG through any exact-prefix API, giving about 1.5–2x per request within the 3.8x ceiling.

IDEA 20
NAME: Composable pairwise low-rank KV deltas
MECHANISM: Offline, for chunk pairs (A,B) that co-occur often in retrieval logs (the distribution is Zipf-skewed), run B after A once. SVD the difference between the contextual and isolated KV of B per layer, and store rank-8 factors. At splice time, add the summed pairwise deltas of all preceding chunks, as a first-order additive approximation. Fall back to sparse recompute for uncovered pairs. The closest prior art is Kamera's fitted rank-16 patch and Cache-Craft's per-context variants, which are respectively learned/global and non-composable. These deltas are forward-pass-derived, per pair, and compose across any order. The deciding quantity is the fraction of true KV deviation variance that the summed pairwise deltas capture on three or more chunks.
VALUE: It replaces CacheBlend's 15% recompute with a GEMM costing under 1%, for any order of covered chunks.

IDEA 21
NAME: Errata ledger, expiry-aligned rebase
MECHANISM: Middle edits, insertions and changed tool outputs are never applied in place. They are appended as compact errata ("tool result #12 superseded: …"), so the cached prefix survives. The closest prior art is Manus append-only discipline and Anthropic context editing; each edit or clear there forces a rewrite of everything after the change. Here the client instead tracks dead tokens D and rebases (compacts) the context only when D·T > 12.5·C, where T is the number of remaining turns and C the context length. It also rebases during idle gaps longer than the cache lifetime, when a rewrite is due anyway, so compaction costs nothing extra. Example: an edit at the midpoint of a 100k context costs about $0.19 in place, against about $0.002 as a 500-token erratum. The deciding quantity is the share of current cache writes that are re-writes of invalidated suffixes rather than first-time tokens. It can be measured from logs as cache_creation_input_tokens minus the new tokens in each turn. Quality should be tested as SWE-bench pass rate and stale-fact override accuracy with errata versus in-place edits.
VALUE: Cuts the agent cache-write term in proportion to how much of it is invalidation-driven, and covers edits, insertions and tool-output changes.

A hit still requires the same prefix, and each load costs bandwidth. One vendor benchmark: Qwen3-32B at ~131K tokens drops from 11 s to 1.5 s TTFT. Quantised offload raised the hit rate from 75.2% to 86.8% at a fixed memory budget, not to ~100%. | [Ray](https://docs.ray.io/en/latest/serve/llm/user-guides/kv-cache-offloading.html), [AMD](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/4bit-KV-LMcache/README.html)

ATTEMPT 5: KV compression and eviction (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM) | academia, 2023–24 | niche | WHY: it shrinks the decode-time cache, not prefill, because the full KV must be built first. At 200k tokens, full prefill took 82.4 s and Ada-SnapKV took 88.4 s (+7.2%). Methods that do cut prefill lose accuracy at useful budgets. | [FastKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01068v3)

ATTEMPT 6: Prompt Cache (modular schema, own position IDs per module) | Yale and Google, MLSys 2024 | niche | WHY: needs a prompt markup language to define modules up front. Modules are encoded without seeing each other, so cross-module attention is lost. It reports 8× (GPU) and 60× (CPU) TTFT gains. I did not see its accuracy tables. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04934)

ATTEMPT 7: CacheBlend (reuse chunk KV at any position, recompute ~15% of tokens) | Chicago and LMCache, EuroSys '25 | niche (in LMCache) | WHY: it needs own-stack access to KV tensors, so it cannot run on a hosted API. Gains are 2.2–3.3× TTFT and 2.8–5× throughput, not ~10×. Recomputation is still ~15%. The "100% hit rate" in the LMCache blog is marketing, not a measured result. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16444v3), [LMCache](https://blog.lmcache.ai/2025-03-31-eurosys)

ATTEMPT 8: EPIC / LegoLink (position-independent caching that suppresses the attention-sink artefact at each chunk start) | ICML 2025 | niche | WHY: it still recomputes a few tokens per chunk and needs own-stack access. It claims up to 8× TTFT with negligible accuracy loss. The evidence is the authors' experiments only, with no independent replication that I found. | [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/hu25j.html)

ATTEMPT 9: Cache-Craft (chunk caches with selective recompute and cache management) | 2025 | niche | WHY: it recomputes ~30% of tokens and keeps only ~90% of base ROUGE F1. It saved 51% of GPU compute vs prefix caching, not ~100%. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15734)

ATTEMPT 10: Block-Attention (independent blocks, re-encoded positions) | ICLR 2025 | niche | WHY: it needs fine-tuning, which the constraints exclude. At 32K tokens it cut TTFT by 98.7% and FLOPs by 99.8%. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15355v5)

ATTEMPT 11: TurboRAG (precomputed chunk KV, independent attention, reordered RoPE) | EMNLP 2025 | niche | WHY: the arXiv abstract says it fine-tunes the model, but the EMNLP abstract does not say so. It reports TTFT down 8.6× on average and up to 9.4×. It is limited to RAG chunk stores on own infrastructure. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07590)

ATTEMPT 12: Training-free reuse tested on reasoning tasks (KVShareArena 2026, Kamera 2026) | preprints | rejected as a general fix | WHY: quality depends on the model. Position alignment alone scores 0.41 on multi-hop with Llama-3.1-8B but 0.03 with Qwen3-8B. CacheBlend helps on Qwen3 but not Llama. Blind reuse took Kamera's multimodal reasoning from 0.59 to 0.41. A rank-16 patch recovered it to 0.64, which suggests a missing cross-chunk signal. These are non-peer-reviewed preprints. | [KVShareArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10266), [Kamera](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23581)

ATTEMPT 13: Trained encoder for position-independent caching (COMB) | 2026 preprint | rejected under these constraints | WHY: it argues existing training-free position-independent caching "often causes substantial accuracy loss" and so trains a component, which the no-training rule forbids. | [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2602.01519)

ATTEMPT 14: Semantic response caching (GPTCache, MeanCache, InstCache) | open source and academia, 2023–25 | niche, Thoughtworks "assess" | WHY: it caches the answer, not the KV, so it is wrong whenever context matters. In MeanCache's test, GPTCache gave 54 false hits on 250 queries that should have missed. InstCache measured 29.9% and 33.6% mismatch (LLM-judged) at thresholds 0.9 and 0.95. | [MeanCache](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.02694), [InstCache](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13820)

ATTEMPT 15: Prompt compression (LLMLingua) | Microsoft, 2023 | niche | WHY: it makes the prompt shorter but changes its tokens, so the exact-prefix match breaks unless the compressed text is itself stable. It reached 20× with a 1.5-point drop on its own benchmarks (GSM8K, BBH, ShareGPT, Arxiv-March23). | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05736)

ATTEMPT 16: Context editing, clearing old tool results (Anthropic) | 2025 | standard | WHY: each clear invalidates the cached prefix from that point and forces a new write. The docs' own mitigation is a `clear_at_least` threshold, which means it only pays off when a lot is cleared at once. | [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing)

LESSONS:
- Hosted APIs expose only exact-prefix matching and no KV handles, so the position-independent methods (attempts 6–11) cannot run client-side. They need your own serving stack, or the provider itself would have to adopt them.
- Every training-free position-independent method either recomputes a share of tokens (15–30%) or loses accuracy. The loss is worst on multi-hop reasoning and varies by model (attempt 12). Anything claiming ~100% hits needs a quality test per model.
- Methods that skip recomputation fully need fine-tuning or a trained component (attempts 10, 13). That is excluded here.
- Whatever hit rate is counted, the speedups reported are 2–9×, and the effective cost depends on the recompute share and the load bandwidth.
- Client-only options (attempts 2, 16) just reduce the damage from changes. Edits, insertions and reordering in the middle stay uncached, so about 100% is unreachable on a hosted API.
- Gaps: I did not verify official OpenAI or Anthropic pricing pages, per-token KV storage cost, or RAGCache's hit rates.
