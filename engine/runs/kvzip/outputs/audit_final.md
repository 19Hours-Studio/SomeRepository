=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Folded online KLT water-filling
DEFECT | FATAL | The main value claim, being 1.2× cheaper per served token on MLA models, cannot happen: absorbed-MLA decode is already compute-bound at FP8, so shrinking the cache further adds batch capacity but no decode throughput. | 128 heads × 2 × (576+512)/576 FLOP per element gives 242 FLOP/B at BF16, 484 at FP8 and 1,412 at 2.74 b, against an H800 ridge of about 295 (989/3.35). FlashMLA's FP8 sparse decode kernel is stated to run "compute-bound" at 410 TFLOPS. The throughput ceiling vs FP8 is therefore ≈1.0×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The basis overhead of 0.0625 b is correct only for d=128 at 32k. For the stores the idea now targets, and for shorter prompts, it is 3.5–32× larger and eats the ≥0.5 b gain the idea depends on. | One FP16 512² c_KV basis costs 0.222 b/value at 32k and 1.78 b at 4k. A d=128 basis costs 0.50 b at 4k and 2.0 b at the 1,024-token switch-over. MLA basis memory is 32 MB per request (61 layers), which is 8% of the 394 MB compressed cache at 32k.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The decisive gain of 0.17–1.6 b on non-RoPE stores comes from simulation, not from real c_KV, value or NoPE tensors. The value claim is explicitly conditional ("if ≥0.5 b"), and the low end of 0.17 b is below the 0.22 b MLA basis cost. | The gain is from the idea's own text. Net at 32k on MLA is −0.05 to +1.38 b. No measured c_KV spectrum is given.
DEFECT | MINOR | The +0.39% decode-FLOP figure is for d=128. On MLA the per-head Uᵀq and the value fold run on the 512-wide latent for all 128 heads, which costs about 4× more, and this lands on a decode that is already compute-bound. | 2·2·128·512² / (128·32768·2·1088) = 1.47% at 32k. That is 1.47% added directly to step time.
DEFECT | MINOR | The query covariance and the basis both come from prefill. After that the basis is frozen for 32k generated tokens of chain-of-thought, whose queries and keys can drift, and each refresh adds another per-segment basis plus its own q′ pass. No measurement of the drift is given. | Rate × window: one basis serves up to 32,768 decode tokens, and nothing checks whether it is still valid.
DEFECT | MINOR | Water-filling gives each component a different bit width, which does not fit the fixed-width tiles that FlashAttention and paged-KV kernels use. Packing needs extra metadata or padding that is not counted. | NOVA-KV (arXiv 2608.04074) had to add equal-volume partitioning to solve this same problem.
SURVIVES | The 0.0625 b basis cost at d=128/32k, the 5.8× ratio (16/2.74), the 0.39% FLOP figure for d=128 GQA, and the exactness of computing scores in the post-RoPE KLT basis (Uᵀ orthogonal) all reproduce. Llama-4 NoPE layers hold about 57% of KV memory at 32k (RoPE layers are chunked to 8k), so that target is real.
RATIO | 1.0 | Against FP8 FlashMLA on MLA models, decode is compute-bound, so the gain is 0. On NoPE layers the net gain over TurboQuant is unmeasured once the basis is paid for. Rank 2 of 4.
KNOWN | partly | kvtc, a calibrated PCA transform with bit allocation and entropy coding (arXiv 2511.01815). NOVA-KV, query-weighted optimal transforms with VQ (arXiv 2608.04074). RateQuant, reverse water-filling allocation (arXiv 2605.06675). I found no published work that estimates the covariance online per request.
NEW FACT | FlashMLA's FP8-KV sparse decode reaches 410 TFLOPS in a compute-bound configuration on H800 | github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA (mirrors)
NEW FACT | Llama 4 config: a NoPE layer every 4th layer, chunk size 8192 for the RoPE layers | HF transformers configuration_llama4.py

IDEA 2: Window-mass per-head precision gating
DEFECT | MAJOR | The bound "error ≤ rel(b)·m_q" is linear, but 3-bit key error enters through exp(). Logit noise therefore pushes attention mass out of the FP16 window and onto quantized tokens, and the gate stops holding on sharp heads. | My simulation (outlier-channel keys, per-channel K3 g128, per-token V2, prefill m_q=0.133) at logit std 3.5 gives mean m_q of 0.172 after quantization (max 0.29) and output error of 0.103 (max 0.47), against a linear bound of 0.096. At logit std 5.2 the mean is 0.204 and the max 0.98. The bound holds only at logit std ≈1.7.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Precision is decided once, from prefill queries, and cannot be undone. The watchdog upgrades only new 128-token blocks, so prompt tokens already stored at K3V2 cannot be recovered when a decode-time chain-of-thought starts retrieving from them. | With a 132-token FP16 window, 99.6% of a 32k context sits at the precision fixed at prefill. The watchdog measures Σp², which is a different metric from the m_q gate, and reacts at least one block (128 tokens) late.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is too weak. TurboQuant, which needs no calibration, claims neutral quality at 3.5 b, inside this idea's 3.22–3.80 range. KVTuner is 3.25 b on Llama-3.1-8B, not "about 3.5", and it needs 4.0 b on Qwen2.5-7B, where K3 keys are risky. | TurboQuant paper: LongBench 50.06 at 3.5 b vs 50.06 at FP16. KVTuner, ICML 2025 (arXiv 2502.04420).
DEFECT | MINOR | Mean-centering (0.125 b) duplicates the zero-point that per-channel asymmetric key quantization already has. A K4V4 head costs 4.44 b, more than uniform 4-bit at 4.31 b, so a retrieval-heavy prompt comes out 3% larger than the baseline. | (4+4)/2 + 0.25 + 0.125 + 0.064 = 4.44 vs 4.31.
DEFECT | MINOR | "Beats K3V3 at the same size" is only true at the low end of the range. At 3.80 b the design is 15% larger than K3V3 at 3.31 b. The text also never says how m_q is combined across the query heads in a GQA group (for example, taking the maximum). | 3.80/3.31 = 1.15.
SURVIVES | The per-head bit costs reproduce (K3V2 2.94, K4V3 3.94, K4V4 4.44, K3V3 3.31), as do 57–67 vs 50 requests (50·4.31/3.80 = 56.7, 50·4.31/3.22 = 66.9) and 4.2–5.0×.
RATIO | 1.0 | 3.5/(3.22–3.80) = 0.92–1.09 against TurboQuant at 3.5 b, before counting the quality risk from the failed key bound. Rank 3 of 4.
KNOWN | partly | DuoAttention (retrieval vs streaming heads), KVTuner (calibrated per-layer K/V precision pairs) and ReadKV (arXiv 2610.11245, per-query precision).
NEW FACT | An independent reproduction of TurboQuant's channel split found about a 7-point LongBench drop | AnchorKV, arXiv 2608.02901

IDEA 3: Mass-gated dithered value codes
DEFECT | FATAL | The gate compares the absolute error at small E_h with single-token 4-bit error (0.10). But the useful output of a flat head, o − μ, shrinks at the same rate, about σ·E_h, so the error relative to what the head actually contributes does not depend on E_h. Dithered 1-bit ends up noise-dominated. | My simulation (N=470, E_h=0.046, Hadamard-like Gaussian values): the useful output is 0.044 RMS. Dithered 1-bit error is 0.028/0.056/0.082 (clip 1/2/3σ), which is 0.65–1.87× that output. 4-bit on the same head is 0.0053, or 0.12×. At the same head, 1-bit is 5–15× worse than 4-bit, not "under" it.
DEFECT | MAJOR | E_h is the 99th percentile over prefill queries, but value precision, including 1-bit, is fixed for good. Decode queries that sharpen onto old tokens read the 1-bit codes, and nothing upgrades them. | All tokens older than the 132-token window keep their prefill-time precision. No decode re-gate is given for blocks already written.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Every value needs a regenerated hash dither. At 1-bit that is 8 values per byte, so the dequant cost exceeds the H100 CUDA-core ridge and the 1-bit heads become compute-bound. Their bandwidth saving is therefore partly lost. | 3–6 int/FP ops per value gives 24–48 op/B against a ridge of about 20 op/B (67 TFLOPS FP32 / 3.35 TB/s), and INT32 throughput is half the FP32 rate.
DEFECT | MINOR | The dither's advantage applies only to repeated content. On distinct tokens, deterministic 2-bit already averages down to the same error as dithered 1-bit. | In my simulation at N=470: 2-bit deterministic 0.028 vs dithered 1-bit (clip 1σ) 0.028.
DEFECT | MINOR | The request count is understated at the low end. | 50·4.31/3.46 = 62.3, not 60. At 3.95 b the figure is 54.6, which survives.
SURVIVES | The 1/√60 averaging (0.50/√60 = 0.065) reproduces, as do the simulated 1-bit absolute error range (0.028–0.082 vs the claimed 0.025–0.063), 4.1–4.6×, and the implied value bit range of 2.54–3.52 b.
RATIO | 0.95 | (3.46–3.95) vs TurboQuant at 3.5 b gives 0.89–1.01, plus the 1-bit decode slowdown. Against uniform 4-bit it is 1.09–1.25, but the quality gate behind it is wrong. Rank 4 of 4.
KNOWN | partly | Subtractive-dithered quantization is textbook (Schuchman/Gray). Mass-based head precision follows DuoAttention and KVTuner. I found no published work using hash-dithered KV values.

IDEA 4: RoPE-band-ranked Hadamard-E8 KV
DEFECT | MAJOR | One FP8 scale per token per head is shared by 16 bands whose energies differ by a large factor, by construction, since that is why they are ranked. A fixed-radius 2-bit codebook at a mismatched scale is far from its 0.091–0.118 MSE, and per-band scales are not specified or counted. | Proxy with 2-bit Lloyd-Max at a fixed scale: relative MSE is 0.118 when matched, 0.23 at 1/3 the variance, 0.76 at 1/10 and 3.19 at 1/30.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The only edge claimed over Block-GTQ, ≤2 points vs −2.5 on AIME24, cannot be measured at practical sample sizes. The cost edge also does not follow from the stated accuracy. | 30 problems: the SE of the paired difference is 3.5 points at 8 samples per problem and 1.25 at 64. Resolving 0.5 points at 2 SE needs about 1,600 samples per problem. A 0.5-point gain is worth 52.2/51.7 = 1.0097 (about 1%), not the stated 5.17/5.06 = 1.022.
DEFECT | MINOR | A 6.2× decode-throughput gain cannot exceed the 6.1× memory ratio when weight reads and dequantization are also on the critical path. | 6.2 > 6.11 (16/2.617).
DEFECT | MINOR | The comparison with Block-GTQ is not like-for-like. Block-GTQ's K3V2 AIME result is reported without an FP16 recent-key buffer, while this design adds one (4 sink + 128 recent tokens). | +0.054 b at 32k and +0.43 b at 4k (Block-GTQ, arXiv 2606.24033).
DEFECT | MINOR | Shared-memory table bandwidth, not ALU, limits decode. Each 2-byte code reads a 16-byte FP16 entry from the 256-entry table, and random indices cause bank conflicts. | 3.35 TB/s × 8 = 26.8 TB/s, against about 33 TB/s of H100 shared-memory bandwidth (128 B/clk × 132 SMs × 1.98 GHz). That is about 80% before FlashAttention's own tile traffic.
DEFECT | MINOR | "RoPE never changes a pair's norm" makes each token's ranking invariant to position. It does not guarantee the ranking holds when decode content changes (chain-of-thought vs prompt). | No measurement of decode-phase band energy is given.
SURVIVES | 2.62 b and 6.1× at 32k, 3.0 b and 5.3× at 4k, Lloyd-Max 2-bit MSE 0.118, INT2 min-max g128 MSE ≈0.25, 12.5 op/B being under the ~20 op/B ridge, and exact score computation with a permuted, band-rotated query all reproduce. Block-GTQ's −2.5 on AIME24 and uniform TQ-MSE's 0.0 are confirmed in the paper (54.2 → 51.7).
RATIO | 1.01 | Same memory and throughput as Block-GTQ K3V2. At most about 1% from an accuracy edge that cannot be measured. Rank 1 of 4, as the least overstated.
KNOWN | yes | Block-GTQ already does energy-ranked bit allocation per RoPE frequency block (arXiv 2606.24033). E8 lattice k2v2 KV quantization is published (NeurIPS 2026, Nedelkoski et al.), and so is NexusQuant (Hadamard + E8, about 5.8×). arXiv 2610.15384 itself was not found, so that ID is unverified.
NEW FACT | E8 k2v2 is about 20% smaller than scalar k3v2 and beats it in 80% of settings across 5 model families | neurips.cc/virtual/2026/171327
NEW FACT | NVIDIA reports NVFP4 KV cache within 1% of the FP8/BF16 baselines on RULER-64K, LiveCodeBench and MMLU-Pro. That is 4.5 b and runs natively on Blackwell tensor cores, a production baseline none of the ideas compare against | developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-inference-for-long-context-and-large-batch-sizes-with-nvfp4-kv-cache/
