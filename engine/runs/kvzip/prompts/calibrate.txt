Which architecture for compressing the LLM KV cache (the stored attention keys and values) is best if it has to be much more aggressive than FP8, which is 8 bits per value or about 2× smaller than FP16/BF16? It has to work as a drop-in replacement for the cache storage format in an existing pretrained transformer model, with no retraining, fine-tuning or architecture change, and with almost no loss of quality. The cache may be compressed along any axis: numeric precision, the number of stored tokens, channels or heads, redundancy across layers, or entropy coding. The asker calls this a "super-zip" for the KV cache. The answer should be a concrete architecture, covering what is stored, how it is encoded and decoded, and how attention reads it. It should also give the expected compression ratio and quality loss on standard long-context and reasoning benchmarks, and say what runtime cost the design adds.
A good answer: 
- It gives a specific, implementable design. Naming a family of methods is not enough.
- It states the effective bits per value or the compression ratio vs FP16, counting all metadata (scales, zero-points, indices, codebooks).
- Quality loss is stated against the FP16 cache on named benchmarks and models, for example LongBench, RULER, needle-in-a-haystack, GSM8K or MATH-style reasoning, and perplexity. "Almost no loss" is given a number, for example ≤1% relative drop.
- It is checked against the failure modes below: outlier channels in keys, RoPE, attention sinks, GQA/MLA models, long contexts, multi-turn or multi-query reuse, and reasoning-model chains of thought.
- It estimates decode and prefill latency and throughput, including dequantization or decompression cost and compatibility with FlashAttention-style fused kernels.
- It shows how it beats or complements the current state of the art (see CONTEXT), and says what is new.
- It names a cheap experiment that could falsify it.
Constraints: 
- No training, fine-tuning or distillation of the base model. The asker said "without retraining". Whether a one-time offline calibration pass on a small data sample counts is UNCLEAR.
- It must apply to any ready-made model by changing the cache storage format only.
- Compression must be far beyond FP8. This is stated as "much stronger" with no numeric target.
- Quality must drop almost not at all.

The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: RoPE-band-ranked Hadamard-E8 KV
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: a seeded Hadamard rotation plus an E8 lattice code decoded from a 256-entry shared-memory table. The original design rotated all 128 dimensions globally at one rate, and that is now published work (arXiv 2610.15384, E8 k2v2). It is also the configuration that collapses on reasoning: TurboQuant-MSE K3V2, which uses one rotation and one rate, scores 0.0 on AIME24 (Block-GTQ paper). In the repaired design, the 64 RoPE pairs are ranked by their prefill energy. RoPE never changes a pair's norm, so the ranking holds during decode. They are grouped into 16 bands of 8 dimensions, with a Hadamard rotation inside each band. Bands are coded at 4, 3 or 2 bits with E8 (2b+2b residual, 2b+1b, 2b), giving keys a 3.0-bit average. Values use E8 at 2 bits, which is where E8 helps most. Simulated MSE is 0.091 for E8 against 0.118 for Lloyd-Max and 0.25 for KIVI-style INT2 g128. Each token and head stores one FP8 scale. 4 sink tokens and the last 128 tokens stay in FP16. The query gets the same permutation and band rotation, so keys are stored post-RoPE exactly. Total is 2.62 b/value at 32k (6.1× vs FP16) and 3.0 b at 4k (5.3×). Decode dequantization is 12.5 op/B, under the H100 CUDA-core ridge of about 20 op/B. Prefill encode cost is +0.007%.
VALUE: Same 6× memory and about 6.2× the decode throughput of BF16 at 32k as Block-GTQ K3V2. The prediction is that it loses less on AIME (≤2 points vs −2.5). That would be about $5.06 vs $5.17 per 1,000 correct AIME answers, roughly 2% cheaper, which is a small edge.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: One FP8 scale per token per head is shared by 16 bands whose energies differ by a large factor, by construction, since that is why they are ranked. A fixed-radius 2-bit codebook at a mismatched scale is far from its 0.091–0.118 MSE, and per-band scales are not specified or counted. (Proxy with 2-bit Lloyd-Max at a fixed scale: relative MSE is 0.118 when matched, 0.23 at 1/3 the variance, 0.76 at 1/10 and 3.19 at 1/30.)
- MAJOR: The only edge claimed over Block-GTQ, ≤2 points vs −2.5 on AIME24, cannot be measured at practical sample sizes. The cost edge also does not follow from the stated accuracy. (30 problems: the SE of the paired difference is 3.5 points at 8 samples per problem and 1.25 at 64. Resolving 0.5 points at 2 SE needs about 1,600 samples per problem. A 0.5-point gain is worth 52.2/51.7 = 1.0097 (about 1%), not the stated 5.17/5.06 = 1.022.)
- SURVIVES: 2.62 b and 6.1× at 32k, 3.0 b and 5.3× at 4k, Lloyd-Max 2-bit MSE 0.118, INT2 min-max g128 MSE ≈0.25, 12.5 op/B being under the ~20 op/B ridge, and exact score computation with a permuted, band-rotated query all reproduce. Block-GTQ's −2.5 on AIME24 and uniform TQ-MSE's 0.0 are confirmed in the paper (54.2 → 51.7).
- RATIO: 1.01 | Same memory and throughput as Block-GTQ K3V2. At most about 1% from an accuracy edge that cannot be measured. Rank 1 of 4, as the least overstated.
- KNOWN: yes | Block-GTQ already does energy-ranked bit allocation per RoPE frequency block (arXiv 2606.24033). E8 lattice k2v2 KV quantization is published (NeurIPS 2026, Nedelkoski et al.), and so is NexusQuant (Hadamard + E8, about 5.8×). arXiv 2610.15384 itself was not found, so that ID is unverified.

IDEA 2
NAME: Window-mass per-head precision gating
MECHANISM: The anchor stays: each KV head's precision is set per prompt from the prompt's own attention, measured during prefill, and no token is dropped. Four repairs remove the causes of death. First, 1-bit and 2-bit keys are banned; the menu is now K4V4, K4V3 or K3V2. Second, there is an error target instead of a fixed 2.1-bit budget. Third, ranking heads by entropy (which needed Spearman stability across prompts) is replaced by an absolute gate on m_q, the share of attention that falls outside the FP16 sink-plus-recent window. Fourth, every head gets per-block channel-mean centering (0.125 bit). Deterministic error can stay correlated across tokens, but it is still bounded by rel(b)·m_q, so the gate holds without dither. The thresholds are K3V2 if m_q ≤ 0.133, K4V3 if m_q ≤ 0.31, and K4V4 otherwise. A decode-time Σp² watchdog upgrades a head's new 128-token blocks if it turns sharp during chain-of-thought.
VALUE: 3.22–3.80 bits (4.2–5.0×), with error at most 1.2–1.67× that of uniform 4-bit. That is 57–67 vs 50 concurrent requests (+14–34%). It matches the calibrated KVTuner (about 3.5 bits) while needing no calibration, and it beats uniform K3V3 (3.31 bits, about 2.15× error) on error at the same size.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The bound "error ≤ rel(b)·m_q" is linear, but 3-bit key error enters through exp(). Logit noise therefore pushes attention mass out of the FP16 window and onto quantized tokens, and the gate stops holding on sharp heads. (My simulation (outlier-channel keys, per-channel K3 g128, per-token V2, prefill m_q=0.133) at logit std 3.5 gives mean m_q of 0.172 after quantization (max 0.29) and output error of 0.103 (max 0.47), against a linear bound of 0.096. At logit std 5.2 the mean is 0.204 and the max 0.98. The bound holds only at logit std ≈1.7.)
- MAJOR: Precision is decided once, from prefill queries, and cannot be undone. The watchdog upgrades only new 128-token blocks, so prompt tokens already stored at K3V2 cannot be recovered when a decode-time chain-of-thought starts retrieving from them. (With a 132-token FP16 window, 99.6% of a 32k context sits at the precision fixed at prefill. The watchdog measures Σp², which is a different metric from the m_q gate, and reacts at least one block (128 tokens) late.)
- MAJOR: The baseline is too weak. TurboQuant, which needs no calibration, claims neutral quality at 3.5 b, inside this idea's 3.22–3.80 range. KVTuner is 3.25 b on Llama-3.1-8B, not "about 3.5", and it needs 4.0 b on Qwen2.5-7B, where K3 keys are risky. (TurboQuant paper: LongBench 50.06 at 3.5 b vs 50.06 at FP16. KVTuner, ICML 2025 (arXiv 2502.04420).)
- SURVIVES: The per-head bit costs reproduce (K3V2 2.94, K4V3 3.94, K4V4 4.44, K3V3 3.31), as do 57–67 vs 50 requests (50·4.31/3.80 = 56.7, 50·4.31/3.22 = 66.9) and 4.2–5.0×.
- RATIO: 1.0 | 3.5/(3.22–3.80) = 0.92–1.09 against TurboQuant at 3.5 b, before counting the quality risk from the failed key bound. Rank 3 of 4.
- KNOWN: partly | DuoAttention (retrieval vs streaming heads), KVTuner (calibrated per-layer K/V precision pairs) and ReadKV (arXiv 2610.11245, per-query precision).

IDEA 3
NAME: Folded online KLT water-filling
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: each request accumulates its own covariance during prefill, takes a KLT basis from it, and allocates bits per component by reverse water-filling, with no calibration data. The fatal step was rebuilding every key (819 FLOP/B against a 295 FLOP/B ridge). That is removed by computing the KLT on post-RoPE keys, so attention scores from the stored coefficients using q′ = Uᵀq, and by folding the value basis into the output (o = U_v·Σp·c). This adds +0.39% decode FLOPs at 32k. The distortion is weighted by the request's own prefill query covariance. The basis is refreshed every 32k generated tokens. Prompts under 1,024 tokens fall back to Hadamard. The corrected basis overhead is 0.0625 b/value per FP16 basis at 32k, not "<0.01". Total is about 2.74 b at 32k (5.8×). The model's decisive number is that the post-RoPE KLT gains only 0.0–0.3 b over plain RoPE-band energy allocation on RoPE keys. On stores with no RoPE it gains 0.17–1.6 b over Hadamard: values, MLA latents c_KV (folded into the absorbed W_UK/W_UV) and NoPE layers. So the design is retargeted at those stores.
VALUE: For MLA models (DeepSeek-V3/R1, Kimi-K2) and NoPE/iRoPE layers, it is a calibration-free way to read a compressed cache directly in HBM. If the real-tensor gain is ≥0.5 b, it would be about 1.2× cheaper per served token than Hadamard/TurboQuant at the same quality. On ordinary RoPE keys it is about equal to Block-GTQ (≈1.0×).
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The main value claim, being 1.2× cheaper per served token on MLA models, cannot happen: absorbed-MLA decode is already compute-bound at FP8, so shrinking the cache further adds batch capacity but no decode throughput. (128 heads × 2 × (576+512)/576 FLOP per element gives 242 FLOP/B at BF16, 484 at FP8 and 1,412 at 2.74 b, against an H800 ridge of about 295 (989/3.35). FlashMLA's FP8 sparse decode kernel is stated to run "compute-bound" at 410 TFLOPS. The throughput ceiling vs FP8 is therefore ≈1.0×.)
- MAJOR: The basis overhead of 0.0625 b is correct only for d=128 at 32k. For the stores the idea now targets, and for shorter prompts, it is 3.5–32× larger and eats the ≥0.5 b gain the idea depends on. (One FP16 512² c_KV basis costs 0.222 b/value at 32k and 1.78 b at 4k. A d=128 basis costs 0.50 b at 4k and 2.0 b at the 1,024-token switch-over. MLA basis memory is 32 MB per request (61 layers), which is 8% of the 394 MB compressed cache at 32k.)
- MAJOR: The decisive gain of 0.17–1.6 b on non-RoPE stores comes from simulation, not from real c_KV, value or NoPE tensors. The value claim is explicitly conditional ("if ≥0.5 b"), and the low end of 0.17 b is below the 0.22 b MLA basis cost. (The gain is from the idea's own text. Net at 32k on MLA is −0.05 to +1.38 b. No measured c_KV spectrum is given.)
- SURVIVES: The 0.0625 b basis cost at d=128/32k, the 5.8× ratio (16/2.74), the 0.39% FLOP figure for d=128 GQA, and the exactness of computing scores in the post-RoPE KLT basis (Uᵀ orthogonal) all reproduce. Llama-4 NoPE layers hold about 57% of KV memory at 32k (RoPE layers are chunked to 8k), so that target is real.
- RATIO: 1.0 | Against FP8 FlashMLA on MLA models, decode is compute-bound, so the gain is 0. On NoPE layers the net gain over TurboQuant is unmeasured once the basis is paid for. Rank 2 of 4.
- KNOWN: partly | kvtc, a calibrated PCA transform with bit allocation and entropy coding (arXiv 2511.01815). NOVA-KV, query-weighted optimal transforms with VQ (arXiv 2608.04074). RateQuant, reverse water-filling allocation (arXiv 2605.06675). I found no published work that estimates the covariance online per request.

IDEA 4
NAME: Mass-gated dithered value codes
MECHANISM: The anchor stays: keys and values are treated differently. Keys are always deterministic 4-bit (per-channel, 128-token groups, 4.25 bits with scales), because key error is exponentiated in the softmax. Values carry a subtractive dither regenerated from a position hash, so it costs no stored bits. The repair is where the dither matters. It does not reduce error per value (0.50 relative RMS at 2 bits either way), and simple per-block mean-centering beats it on channel-offset data (0.49 vs 1.33). It is, however, the only one of the three schemes that makes errors on repeated content (repeated phrases, code, CoT loops) shrink as more tokens are averaged: at 60 copies, 0.065 vs 0.44–0.52 for the other schemes. That makes a provable gate possible. Each KV head's value precision is chosen from E_h = √Σp² over its quantized tokens: 4-bit above 0.31, 3-bit for 0.133–0.31, 2-bit for 0.046–0.133, and 1-bit dithered at or below 0.046 (about 470 or more tokens effectively attended). At that 1-bit setting the simulated error is 0.025–0.063, under the 0.10 that single-token 4-bit already gives, while deterministic 1-bit stays at 0.38–1.10. Values are Hadamard-rotated and mean-centered, 4 sink tokens plus the last 128 tokens stay FP16, and E_h is accumulated inside the attention kernel (the maximum over the GQA group, 99th percentile over queries).
VALUE: 3.46–3.95 bits (4.1–4.6×), with total attention-output error at most 1.2–1.43× that of uniform 4-bit. That is 55–60 vs 50 concurrent 32k requests on an H100 (+10–20% requests per GPU), with no calibration.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The gate compares the absolute error at small E_h with single-token 4-bit error (0.10). But the useful output of a flat head, o − μ, shrinks at the same rate, about σ·E_h, so the error relative to what the head actually contributes does not depend on E_h. Dithered 1-bit ends up noise-dominated. (My simulation (N=470, E_h=0.046, Hadamard-like Gaussian values): the useful output is 0.044 RMS. Dithered 1-bit error is 0.028/0.056/0.082 (clip 1/2/3σ), which is 0.65–1.87× that output. 4-bit on the same head is 0.0053, or 0.12×. At the same head, 1-bit is 5–15× worse than 4-bit, not "under" it.)
- MAJOR: E_h is the 99th percentile over prefill queries, but value precision, including 1-bit, is fixed for good. Decode queries that sharpen onto old tokens read the 1-bit codes, and nothing upgrades them. (All tokens older than the 132-token window keep their prefill-time precision. No decode re-gate is given for blocks already written.)
- MAJOR: Every value needs a regenerated hash dither. At 1-bit that is 8 values per byte, so the dequant cost exceeds the H100 CUDA-core ridge and the 1-bit heads become compute-bound. Their bandwidth saving is therefore partly lost. (3–6 int/FP ops per value gives 24–48 op/B against a ridge of about 20 op/B (67 TFLOPS FP32 / 3.35 TB/s), and INT32 throughput is half the FP32 rate.)
- SURVIVES: The 1/√60 averaging (0.50/√60 = 0.065) reproduces, as do the simulated 1-bit absolute error range (0.028–0.082 vs the claimed 0.025–0.063), 4.1–4.6×, and the implied value bit range of 2.54–3.52 b.
- RATIO: 0.95 | (3.46–3.95) vs TurboQuant at 3.5 b gives 0.89–1.01, plus the 1-bit decode slowdown. Against uniform 4-bit it is 1.09–1.25, but the quality gate behind it is wrong. Rank 4 of 4.
- KNOWN: partly | Subtractive-dithered quantization is textbook (Schuchman/Gray). Mass-based head precision follows DuoAttention and KVTuner. I found no published work using hash-dithered KV values.
