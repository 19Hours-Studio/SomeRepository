Most figures below are self-reported by the method authors. Several come from secondary write-ups, and I mark those.

ATTEMPT 1: FP8 (E4M3) KV cache, native in the attention kernels | vLLM/SGLang, NVIDIA, 2023–26 | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It gives only 2× and is not always safe. vLLM runs the whole attention in FP8, and one secondary write-up reports 128k needle-in-a-haystack falling from 91% (BF16) to 13% on Hopper until a two-level-accumulation fix restored it to 89%. | [vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-04-22-fp8-kvcache), [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/ji_ai/fp8-kv-cache-quantization-the-memory-math-and-the-accuracy-cliff-1e9m)

ATTEMPT 2: NVFP4 KV cache (4-bit with block scales) | NVIDIA, TensorRT-LLM/vLLM/SGLang, 2026 | standard (new) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It saves only "up to 50%" versus FP8, so about 4× versus FP16 at best. NVIDIA reports <1% loss (a vendor claim). Values must be dequantized to FP8 before the attention matmuls, so the compute path is not native. | [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-inference-for-long-context-and-large-batch-sizes-with-nvfp4-kv-cache/)

ATTEMPT 3: KIVI, tuning-free 2-bit: per-channel keys, per-token values, FP16 residual window | Rice/TAMU/Stevens, ICML 2024 | niche (the reference baseline) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Total peak memory is only 2.6× lower, because of group metadata and the FP16 window. On long-CoT reasoning, a later paper reports KIVI-K2V2 about 13 points below FP16 on AIME24/25 at 32K tokens. A separate paper found KIVI-2bit close to full-KV on Qwen3-8B, so the evidence conflicts. | [KIVI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liu24bz.html), [Kitty](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18643), [faithfulness study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01631)

ATTEMPT 4: KVQuant: per-channel pre-RoPE keys, sensitivity-weighted non-uniform codebooks, dense-and-sparse outliers | UC Berkeley, NeurIPS 2024 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It reaches <0.1 perplexity loss at 3 bits, but the 3-bit setting is still 5× smaller than FP16 at best. Reaching 10M context needs nuq2 on 8 GPUs. It requires offline calibration and custom kernels (the paper reports only ~1.7× speedup on matrix-vector products), and the outlier handling costs extra bits. | [KVQuant](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.18079)

ATTEMPT 5: TurboQuant: random rotation, Lloyd-Max bins, 1-bit QJL residual correction | Google Research, ICLR 2026 | newly standard (vLLM backend merged Apr 2026) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Paper claims quality neutrality at 3.5 bits and marginal loss at 2.5 bits. One review reports GSM8K falling from 0.900 to 0.720 at 3 bits on Qwen3-4B. A vLLM benchmark (secondhand) found FP8 gave the better overall balance. An independent test measured only +3.1% decode throughput on an RTX 5090, and community docs say QJL adds attention noise. | [summary and review](https://yage.ai/share/turboquant-kv-cache-3-bit-en-20260325.html), [reproduction](https://dev.to/carryologist/turboquant-four-months-later-chasing-googles-6x-vram-claim-into-the-wild-4gh1), [vLLM tag](https://vllm.ai/blog/tags/turboquant)

ATTEMPT 6: Hybrid 2-bit schemes with dynamic precision boost or vector quantization (Kitty, VecInfer, MILLION) | 2025 preprints | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Kitty narrows the 2-bit gap on AIME-style reasoning only to 3–4 points. VecInfer reports plain KIVI and MILLION at 2 bits failing to produce coherent long-CoT output. | [Kitty](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18643), [VecInfer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06175)

ATTEMPT 7: Query-aware eviction: H2O (cumulative attention), SnapKV (end-of-prompt window), PyramidKV | 2023–24 | standard in open toolkits, not production-safe | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Scores depend on the question. When the cache compressed from the first query is reused, accuracy drops sharply. On RULER, accuracy falls roughly linearly with retention in the query-agnostic setting. H2O also leaves the prompt cache uncompressed. | [KVzip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23416), [Compactor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08143), [SnapKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14469v2)

ATTEMPT 8: StreamingLLM: attention sinks plus a recent window | MIT, 2023 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It gives a fixed-size cache but discards old content, so it cannot do retrieval. The multi-turn work cited here finds that keeping positional structure intact matters as much as the token-keep ratio. | [stateful KV study](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04686v1)

ATTEMPT 9: KVzip: query-agnostic eviction scored by context reconstruction | SNU, NeurIPS 2025 oral | niche (the best eviction result) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It gives 3–4× with negligible loss, up to 170K context, on three model families. That is below what quantization alone reaches. Scoring needs a reconstruction pass over the context. Aggressive budgets still collapse: RestoreKV reports 86.4 RULER at 16× only after adding learned restoration on top of KVzip+. | [KVzip](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/oral/118742), [RestoreKV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01247)

ATTEMPT 10: Head-split caches: DuoAttention and RazorAttention (full cache only for retrieval heads) | MIT / Huawei, ICLR 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: DuoAttention saves up to 2.55× on MHA models but only 1.67× on GQA models, the common modern case. RazorAttention claims >70% reduction. Both need a head-identification step. Savings are small where heads are already shared. | [DuoAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10819), [RazorAttention](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15891v1)

ATTEMPT 11: Reasoning-specific eviction (R-KV: attention importance plus redundancy) | Microsoft et al., NeurIPS 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It is built for math chains of thought and shown on math reasoning only. Earlier eviction methods reach only about 60% of full performance there. The published and arXiv headline figures disagree (105% of full with 38% of the cache vs 16%). | [R-KV](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24133v4), [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/120110)

ATTEMPT 12: Low-rank key/value projection (Palu: group-head SVD, Fisher rank search, then 3-bit quantization) | NTU/NVIDIA, ICLR 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It loses <1% on LongBench only at 30% rank reduction. At 50% the authors admit accuracy is hard to keep. Low-rank projection creates latent outliers that hurt low-bit quantization. Combined, it reaches about 11.4× with 3-bit latents. | [Palu](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21118v2)

ATTEMPT 13: Post-training cross-layer compression: MiniCache (merge adjacent layers), xKV and CommonKV (cross-layer SVD) | 2024–25 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: MiniCache assumes adjacent layers are similar, which xKV says often fails. xKV reports up to 6.8× more compression than inter-layer baselines, but the compression ratio is not stated per model. CommonKV holds quality only at a 0.6 compression ratio. | [xKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18893v1), [MiniCache](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.14366), [CommonKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16134v1)

ATTEMPT 14: Cross-Layer Attention (CLA): share one KV cache across adjacent layers | MIT-IBM, 2024 | rejected for this brief | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It is an architecture change that needs training, so it cannot be applied to a ready-made model. | [xKV/CLA description](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18893)

ATTEMPT 15: Lossless entropy coding of BF16 KV (exponent-only: SplitZip, ZipNN-style, Intel FP8/FP4 extension) | 2025–26 | niche (used for KV transfer) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: BF16 exponent entropy is only 2.89–3.59 bits. SplitZip's fixed-length code tops out near 4/3 (~25% smaller). A blog estimate is ~30% (not peer-reviewed). Slack shrinks as bit-width falls. | [SplitZip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01708), [Doubleword](https://blog.doubleword.ai/speculative-kv-coding)

ATTEMPT 16: Predictor-based "speculative KV coding" on top of lossy FP8 | Doubleword, blog, 2026 | dropped/idea stage | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It claims up to ~4× on top of lossy FP8 using a predictor model, but this is a blog with no peer review. It needs a second model run for decoding. | [Doubleword](https://blog.doubleword.ai/speculative-kv-coding)

LESSONS:
- The ceiling for plain quantization without recovery is about 3–4 bits (4–5×). Below 3 bits, hard reasoning degrades (AIME −13 points for KIVI-2bit; GSM8K 0.90→0.72 for TurboQuant at 3 bits, secondary report). Averages like perplexity and LongBench hide this, so a new design must be tested on long-CoT.
- Eviction beyond 3–4× fails when the future query is unknown, and fails further for multi-turn reuse and reasoning chains. Any new design must be query-agnostic and must not lean on eviction for the main saving.
- Accounting must include metadata, FP16 residual windows, outlier sidecars and codebooks. Many "2-bit" claims land near 2.6× total, not 8×.
- Savings from heads and layers shrink on GQA/MLA models, which are already compressed. A drop-in design must show gains there.
- Speed claims are not credible until there is a fused FlashAttention-style kernel on real hardware: independent TurboQuant tests show +3% decode, and FP8's long-context failure came from accumulation precision, not storage.
- It is still unclear whether one-time calibration counts as "no retraining." KVQuant, Palu and DuoAttention all depend on it. A design that needs no calibration would avoid the question.

I used 14 of 15 searches. I did not search Quest or MLA-specific quantization, and attention-sink and RoPE results are covered only indirectly through the cited papers.
