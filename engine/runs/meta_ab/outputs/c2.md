## Continuum: target and metrics

- **Workload:** data-center inference of frontier MoE models with long context (≥1M tokens). This is where the market's spending is heading. Training is supported, but as a secondary goal.
- **"Best" means:** joules per token and $ per million tokens, measured at a fixed interactivity floor of ≥200 tokens/s per user. Peak FLOPS is not the metric.

## Architecture

- **Compute:** 3D hybrid-bonded logic-on-DRAM stacks. MAC tiles sit under each DRAM bank group, so weights stay in place and activations travel to them.
- **Memory:**
  - Bank-local DRAM holds weights.
  - Logic-die SRAM holds the hot KV cache.
  - Pooled CXL/optical DRAM holds cold KV.
- **Interconnect:** co-packaged optics with a statically scheduled, deterministic fabric similar to Groq's, extended to a full rack.
- **Numerics:** codebook (vector-quantized) weights, roughly 2–3 effective bits. Activations use FP8, and accumulation is FP32.
- **Programming model:** an ahead-of-time compiler takes the model graph and produces a schedule, placement and power profile. PyTorch export is the front end.
- **Packaging and power:**
  - Chiplets stay under the reticle (~858 mm²) to avoid wafer-scale yield risk.
  - Power is about 1 kW per package, with liquid cooling.

## Against the incumbents

- **Rubin-class GPUs** (~288 GB HBM4, ~20+ TB/s) pay the energy cost of moving weights off-chip. That is roughly 5–10 pJ/bit for HBM, against well under 1 pJ/bit for bank-local access.
- **Ironwood** (192 GB HBM, 9,216-chip OCS pods) has the same limit.
- **WSE-3** (44 GB SRAM) and **Groq** (~230 MB SRAM per chip) avoid that cost. But SRAM density has nearly stopped scaling from N3 to N2, so trillion-parameter models need racks of their chips. DRAM-resident compute gets about 10× the capacity per mm².

## Limits and what must be true

- **Thermal:** DRAM retention degrades above ~85–95 °C. Logic under the DRAM must stay under roughly 0.5 W/mm².
- **Process:** DRAM-process logic is about 2–3 generations slower. It only wins if inference is bandwidth-bound, which holds for decode at batch sizes under ~64.
- **Yield and supply:** hybrid bonding yield and the supply of HBM-class DRAM, which is controlled by three vendors.
- **Model accuracy:** 2–3-bit codebook models must stay within ~1% of FP8 quality.
- **Workloads:** MoE has to remain the dominant model type.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Tokens Travel, Experts Stay
MECHANISM: Each MoE expert is permanently pinned inside a DRAM bank that has its own multiply-accumulate logic beneath it, so weights are never transferred. The router sends each token's small activation vector across an on-stack mesh to the banks that hold its selected experts, and partial results are returned and reduced along the way. Expert placement is periodically rebalanced from observed routing statistics so that popular experts are replicated across banks.
VALUE: Decode energy falls to bank-local access levels while capacity stays at DRAM scale, so trillion-parameter models fit in a few packages instead of racks.

IDEA 2
NAME: Lookup-Accumulate Tensor Cores
MECHANISM: Weights are stored as indices into small learned vector codebooks rather than as numbers. The cores precompute products of activations with codebook entries, then compute by table lookup and addition instead of multiplication. Codebooks are trained jointly with the model and can be re-fit per layer during fine-tuning.
VALUE: Weights take roughly 2–3 bits each, and the energy of each operation drops below FP4 without the accuracy loss of uniform quantization.

IDEA 3
NAME: Compiler-Scheduled Power Envelope
MECHANISM: Execution is fully deterministic, so the compiler knows the switching activity of every tile in every cycle. It emits a matching per-tile voltage and current profile alongside the instruction stream. Voltage regulators are pre-staged ahead of load steps, so the chip needs no safety margin for sudden current surges.
VALUE: Removing the usual 10–20% voltage guardband lets tiles run near-threshold safely, giving double-digit energy-per-token savings at no extra area cost.
