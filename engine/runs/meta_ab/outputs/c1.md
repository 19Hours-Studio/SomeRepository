## Continuum: target and metrics

- **Workload:** data-center inference for long-context, agentic LLM decode, including MoE. This is where cost now dominates. Training is secondary.
- **Metrics:** tokens/s per watt and tokens/s per dollar, both measured at a fixed per-user latency target (≤20 ms/token) with contexts of 128K tokens or more.

## Why the leaders fall short

- **Rubin-class GPUs and Ironwood** (reported ~192–288 GB HBM at ~7–20 TB/s) are limited by memory bandwidth during decode. Most of their energy goes to moving weights and KV cache rather than to arithmetic: about 5–10 pJ/byte for HBM versus under 1 pJ for an FP8 MAC.
- **WSE-3** (~44 GB SRAM) and **Groq** (~230 MB SRAM per chip) remove the bandwidth wall. But SRAM bit-cells have stopped shrinking below N3, so large models need many wafers or racks, which drives up cost per GB.

## Architecture

- **Compute:** a logic die (N2) with lookup-accumulate tiles instead of multiplier arrays.
- **Memory:** hybrid-bonded 3D DRAM stacks, each with its own attention engine.
- **Interconnect:** co-packaged optics that the compiler configures as circuits.
- **Numerics:** learned 4–6-bit codebooks with FP16 accumulation.
- **Programming model:** a static dataflow compiler. It takes PyTorch graphs and emits fixed schedules with no runtime arbitration.
- **Packaging and power:** CoWoS-L-class packaging, about 1.2 kW per package, liquid-cooled, with 48 V supply converted on-package.

## What must be true

1. Hybrid bonding pitch must reach ≤3 µm with acceptable yield on DRAM-on-logic stacks.
2. Codebook quantization must stay within 0.5% of FP8 quality on frontier models.
3. Co-packaged optics must reach ≤2 pJ/bit with field-replaceable lasers.
4. Static schedules must tolerate MoE routing variance, for example by overprovisioning expert slots by about 15%.

## Known limits

- **Reticle limit:** ~858 mm² per die.
- **Thermal density:** roughly 1 W/mm² is the practical ceiling under DRAM stacks.
- **Supply:** DRAM wafer capacity and CoWoS capacity are the economic chokepoints.

## Target

About 3× tokens per joule versus Rubin-class at the same latency target. This is unproven and needs validation.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Attention Inside Memory Stacks
MECHANISM: Each 3D DRAM stack's base die holds a small engine that scores queries against locally stored, compressed KV cache, runs top-k selection and softmax, and returns only the weighted value vectors. Inactive KV moves to deeper layers of the stack, and frequently attended entries are promoted to faster layers based on observed attention statistics.
VALUE: Long-context decode no longer streams the full KV cache over the memory bus, so it is bounded by in-stack bandwidth instead of HBM pins.

IDEA 2
NAME: Lookup-Accumulate Codebook Compute
MECHANISM: Weights are stored as 4–6-bit indices into small codebooks learned for each layer, and activations are pre-multiplied into on-tile lookup tables. Matrix products then become table reads plus adds, with no multiplier arrays. Codebooks are re-fitted offline per model and can be hot-swapped per layer.
VALUE: Arithmetic energy and die area drop by several times at near-FP8 quality, which frees silicon for more memory bandwidth.

IDEA 3
NAME: Compiler-Scheduled Optical Circuits
MECHANISM: Co-packaged photonic links form a circuit-switched mesh whose paths are set by the compiler at each phase of the model, such as prefill, expert dispatch and all-reduce. There are no packet routers or runtime arbitration. Spare circuits absorb variance in MoE routing, and the schedule is recompiled when the traffic profile drifts.
VALUE: A rack of chips behaves like one deterministic, low-latency machine, without the switch power and tail latency of packet networks.
