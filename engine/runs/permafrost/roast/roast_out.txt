== 2a. Self-charging separator: energy and current ==
work done by the field per kg: 0.90 J/kg = 0.25 Wh/t (grinding ~10 kWh/t = 36,000 J/kg)
charging current 272 uA total; power at 45 kV 12 W
-> critique right that kV != power, but separation needs FIELD, not power: the energy to deflect the grains
   is ~1 J/kg, ~10^4x below grinding. Energy is not the bottleneck; charge CONTRAST between minerals is.

== 2b. Space charge of the falling curtain (a real throughput limit) ==
 1.0 m wide: 27.22 kg/s/m, 18.15 kg/m3, unipolar space-charge field   51.2 kV/cm
21.8 m wide:  1.25 kg/s/m,  0.83 kg/m3, unipolar space-charge field    2.4 kV/cm
-> at 1 m width the stream's own charge exceeds air breakdown (30 kV/cm); the researcher's 21.8 m wide stages
   bring it to ~1-2 kV/cm, the same order as the applied 3 kV/cm, so throughput per metre is capped.

== 2c. 'Humidity 90%' - which humidity ==
air at -40 C, 90% RH over ice: vapour pressure 11.6 Pa, 0.107 g/m3
  grain surface at  -40 C: relative humidity at the surface  90.0%
  grain surface at  -20 C: relative humidity at the surface  11.2%
  grain surface at    0 C: relative humidity at the surface   1.9%
-> it is RH at the grain surface (sets the adsorbed water film), not ore moisture. Ore moisture is a separate
   problem: permafrost ore carries ice; pegmatite ~0.5% -> 5 kg/t to sublimate/evaporate ~14 MJ/t (4 kWh/t).
   Unknown: surface conductivity at -40 C may be far below that at +20 C at equal RH, so the original
   'cold keeps charge' claim is untested either way - a cold-room Faraday-cup measurement decides it.
