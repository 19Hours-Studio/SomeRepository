=== VALORIZED B01 ===
ANCHOR: heavy layer riding down a sun-driven thaw front
NAME: Sun-thawed, wave-winnowed placer
MECHANISM: The overburden is stripped from an ice-rich placer, and a small spillway holds a shallow pond of the ground's own meltwater on top. The dark water absorbs round-the-clock summer sun and thaws a few decimetres of gravel beneath it each season. Summer wind drives waves across the shallow pond, and they rework each freshly thawed layer the way surf works a beach: silt and light sand are lifted into suspension and leave over the spillway, while gold, heavy sands and coarse stones drop as a lag onto the still-frozen floor. Each year the spillway is lowered to keep the pond shallow, so the lag rides down with the thaw front to bedrock, and only that thin bottom layer is dug and washed.
VALUE: Sun, wind and the deposit's own ice do the digging and most of the concentrating, so a placer is mined by moving a small fraction of its gravel, with no pumps, washing-plant fuel or reagents.

=== VALORIZED B02 ===
ANCHOR: buoyant ore sacks carried under river ice by winter current
NAME: Under-ice raw-ore drift
MECHANISM: Run-of-mine ore, neither crushed nor concentrated at the mine, is packed in tough 1 t sacks with a closed-cell foam collar that makes each sack slightly lighter than water. The sacks are pushed through a slot into a large river that keeps flowing under its ice all winter. They ride against the underside of the ice, and the current carries them roughly 10–40 km a day to a net under a slot at a downstream town that has power, where an ordinary mill separates the metal. Nobody chases sacks that snag: spring breakup frees them, and a boom at the same downstream site catches them in open water.
VALUE: Because the river moves whole ore all winter with no fuel, a remote deposit needs no plant, power or reagents of its own, and separation happens where those already exist.

=== VALORIZED B03 ===
ANCHOR: metal riding an oil trunk line as tracked slugs
NAME: Gold slugs in crude pipeline
MECHANISM: At a pump station near a gold or platinum-metal deposit, a side stream of the line's own crude is dosed with a gold-selective extractant made only of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen (for example dibutyl carbitol, which does no harm in refining) and stirred with the deposit's chloride leach liquor. The precious metal moves into the oil and the stripped liquor goes back to the heap, so the reagents run in a closed loop and only metal leaves the site. The loaded crude rejoins the line as a density-tracked slug of about 2% of throughput, using the station's existing power and the pipeline's pumping. At the coastal terminal the slug is cut into its own tank and washed with hot oxalic acid, which drops the gold out as metal powder, and the crude is then exported as ordinary crude.
VALUE: Precious metals from a roadless deposit reach a seaport with no trucks and no carrier to bring back, and the only inbound freight is a small top-up of leach reagent and extractant.

=== VALORIZED B04 ===
ANCHOR: ore cassettes replacing ballast water on LNG carriers
NAME: Two-way ballast cassettes
MECHANISM: Ice-class LNG carriers leave Arctic terminals below their weight limit and sail back empty, so both legs carry thousands of tonnes of seawater ballast. On the outbound leg, sealed cassettes of briquetted concentrate take the place of part of that ballast in double-bottom spaces, and they are swapped for seawater at the port where the LNG is unloaded. The empty return leg needs even more ballast, so the cassettes come back full of diesel, lime and process reagents for the mines. In summer, river barges on the Ob and Yenisei shuttle the cassettes between the deposits and the terminal.
VALUE: A remote mine's concentrate goes out, and its fuel and reagents come in, on ships that sail the route anyway, so the supply line that makes on-site processing expensive costs almost nothing.

=== VALORIZED B05 ===
ANCHOR: sun-thawed channel etched into ice-rich yedoma
NAME: Solar-etched yedoma barge canal
MECHANISM: Along a surveyed line from a lowland deposit to a navigable river, the moss is stripped or darkened in a strip while insulating mats shield the edges, so meltwater pools in the strip and absorbs the 24-hour summer sun. Yedoma is often more than half ice, so each thawed layer collapses into mud that flows down the strip's slope and out to the river. This deepens the trench every summer until it reaches ice-poor ground at barge depth, and ground-temperature probes show where to darken again the next season. Once barges can reach the deposit, ore can leave unprocessed for a plant that already has power and reagents, so nothing has to be built on site.
VALUE: Solar heat digs the access route, so a lowland deposit gets a barge channel for the cost of surface treatment instead of a road at millions of dollars per kilometre.

=== VALORIZED B06 ===
ANCHOR: river under winter ice as free wash water
NAME: Submerged-stockpile winter sluicing
MECHANISM: In summer, placer gravel is dug and screened by gravity down to sand size. Before freeze-up the sand is heaped on a long riffle mat laid on the bed of a deep river reach that keeps flowing under its ice. Sitting under 0 °C water, the heap never freezes, and all winter the current wears it away grain by grain: light grains drift off, while gold and heavy minerals drop into the mat's riffles. In late winter the mat is pulled up through a slot in the ice and the concentrate is cleaned by hand.
VALUE: Placer gold is washed through the whole winter with no pumps, heated plant or tailings pond, using the one thing in a Siberian winter that stays unfrozen.

=== VALORIZED B07 ===
ANCHOR: ore pods riding the Transpolar Drift in sea ice
NAME: Fram-hulled pods on drift ice
MECHANISM: Sealed, buoyant, satellite-tagged pods of concentrate have rounded, sledge-like hulls like Nansen's Fram, so when pressure ridges squeeze them they ride up onto the ice instead of being crushed. In winter the pods are set on young ice at the outer edge of the Laptev Sea fast ice near the Lena delta, where offshore winds push new ice north into the Transpolar Drift, which carries it to Fram Strait in 2–3 years. If a floe melts on the way, its pod simply floats on with the surface current and is frozen in again the next winter, and ships collect the pods in the open water of Fram Strait by their tag positions.
VALUE: Bulk concentrate reaches European waters with no ship, fuel or icebreaker on the Arctic leg, and the only costs are the pods and the time.

=== VALORIZED B08 ===
ANCHOR: airlifted inflatable weirs turning a stream into a barge staircase
NAME: Airlifted inflatable-weir barge staircase
MECHANISM: Folded rubber weirs are flown in and inflated in series on a small tributary between the deposit and a navigable river, and each pond fills with snowmelt and rain. Shallow-draft barges of concentrate wait in the top pond. Using simple level gauges, each gate release is timed and held just long enough to carry the barges over the next shallow stretch into the pond below, and empty barges are winched back up across the flat pools. The top pond's head also feeds the mine's washing water by gravity, and after the season the weirs are deflated and flown out, leaving no road or rail.
VALUE: An ordinary summer stream becomes a temporary waterway, so bulk tonnage moves at water-freight cost without building a corridor.
