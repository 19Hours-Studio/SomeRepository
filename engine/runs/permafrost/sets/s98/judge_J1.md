TOP3:
F34 - Nickel travels as 0.1 mol% Ni(CO)4 in a gas line that already exists. The vapour pressure, the roughly 100 kt/yr rate and the CO needed to stop it breaking down all check out, so one simple move does both separation and transport.
F58 - The current under river ice works as a free conveyor for seven months. The string design deals with the real risk (pods stalling and freezing in) and also carries diesel and reagents down to the mine, and one river reach is enough for a cheap trial.
F29 - Siberian Platform lithium brines, which operators now pay to reinject, become concentrate through freezing past the CaCl2 eutectic under sky cooling. The chemistry is specific and the product has a real market.

NOTE: Three clusters make up about a third of the set: about 10 pods drifting under river ice, 8 pykrete/ice hulls and 6 Transpolar Drift pods. Most transport answers avoid the crux by shipping raw ore to a plant that already has power. Only a few (F02, F23, F26, F28/F34, F29, F49, F56) separate the metal in the cold with a mechanism of their own. Several frost-based claims fail a quick order-of-magnitude check, among them sublimation rates (F42, F56), keeping solar-pond heat through winter (F37) and freeze-thaw comminution (F08, F41).
