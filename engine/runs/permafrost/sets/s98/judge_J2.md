TOP3:
F26 - Freezing the leach liquor does two jobs at once: it powers the plant and crystallizes copper sulfate away from iron. The rough energy balance nearly closes (about 2-3 MWh of electricity from freezing per tonne of copper, against about 2 MWh/t needed for electrowinning). This attacks the crux directly.
F56 - Pore ice sublimes into dry wind, then the wind blows off the light grains. This is a dry winnowing step with no water, reagent or fuel. The rate (about 0.1-0.2 kg/m2/day of ice) roughly matches its own sizing of about 1 km2 per season.
F23 - A self-charging triboelectric cascade removes the power supply from dry separation. Each stage is cheap, and it can be tested on a bench in a cold room within weeks.
NOTE: About two thirds of the set are transport schemes (pykrete hulls, under-ice river pods, Transpolar-drift pods) that hand separation to a distant plant, with heavy near-duplication (for example F04/F05/F40/F50/F54, the seven ICEHULL variants, F58/F69, F28/F34). These ideas mostly fail on what it costs to collect the pods at the far end and on thin buoyancy and melt margins, not on their headline physics. Only about a dozen answers separate the metal on site with their own source of energy (freeze crystallization, sublimation winnowing, tribo- or corona-electrostatics, carbonyl).
