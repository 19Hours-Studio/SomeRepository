=== VALORIZED B01 ===
ANCHOR: freezing under-ice water powers a cold-air engine
NAME: Leach-liquor freeze power plant
MECHANISM: Copper-bearing liquor from a summer heap leach is stored in a pond, where it stays liquid at 0–4 °C under the winter ice. It is pumped out and sprayed over coils, where it freezes into slush. The heat given off as it freezes boils propane, which drives a turbine and then condenses in finned radiators open to −35 to −50 °C air, so about 30 kg/s of liquor gives roughly 0.5–1 MW. Ice pushes out dissolved salts as it forms, so the brine that drains from the slush heaps is several times stronger than the feed and goes straight to an electrowinning cell run on the same power. Each kilogram of liquor frozen yields about 15–30 kJ of electricity, which is roughly what it takes to win the 1–2 g of copper it carries, and the cell turns the copper sulfate back into sulfuric acid, which returns to the heap with the ice meltwater in spring.
VALUE: A remote copper deposit makes its own winter power and its own metal from its own leach liquor, with no diesel and almost no acid shipped in.

=== VALORIZED B02 ===
ANCHOR: black film and snow fences ratchet ground heat
NAME: Snow-blanketed self-leaching copper outcrop
MECHANISM: On a gently sloping pyritic copper-sulfide outcrop, the thawed layer is ripped loose and left in place. Each spring it is covered with black geotextile that lets air and water through and soaks up the round-the-clock summer sun, and each autumn it is fenced so that drifting snow buries it under a thick insulating blanket. Once the ore is warm, bacteria oxidise its own pyrite, which makes sulfuric acid and heat; under the snow this keeps leaching going all winter, and the thaw front sinks deeper every year. In spring the snow the fences caught melts and flushes the dissolved copper down to the still-frozen floor, which acts as a natural liner and carries it downslope to a sump at the foot packed with scrap steel (old fuel drums litter northern sites), where the copper plates out as cement copper.
VALUE: A shallow copper deposit leaches itself in place, using sun, snow and its own pyrite for heat, water and acid, and ships out only cement copper, with no plant, power line or reagent supply.

=== VALORIZED B03 ===
ANCHOR: ballistic freight with artillery-style fire correction
NAME: Two-way ballistic supply line
MECHANISM: Two fixed guns, one at the deposit and one at a railhead or river port more than 100 km away, fire steel-cased slugs of about 0.5 t at roughly 1.5 km/s into each other's sand-and-water catch basins. Radar measures where each slug lands, and the miss distance corrects the next shot, as in artillery adjusting fire. Casings fly out full of concentrate and come back full of diesel and reagents. Each inbound casing carries roughly two to three times the fuel the deposit's gun burns on an outbound shot, so the same loop powers the gun, the mill and the camp and needs no road, convoy or seasonal return route.
VALUE: A stranded deposit is linked both ways all year, concentrate out and fuel and reagents in, by two fixed installations and no corridor at all.

=== VALORIZED B04 ===
ANCHOR: near-neutral spheres rolling under river ice
NAME: Under-ice sphere freight
MECHANISM: Rigid spheres about 1 m across, with closed-cell foam shells, sealed holds and no gas, valves or electronics, are trimmed to about 0.5% positive buoyancy. They ride lightly against the smooth underside of a big Siberian river's winter ice and roll downstream with the current, and when they meet a hanging dam or a ridge the flow pushes them down and around it. Because these rivers flow north from the railway, spheres pushed through a slot at an upstream railhead carry diesel, brought to neutral buoyancy by dense reagents such as acid or lime in a second hold, down to a riverside mine. There they are emptied, refilled with concentrate, re-trimmed and pushed back under the ice to roll on to a boom at a downstream port, and the empties go back upriver by barge in summer.
VALUE: Through the seven months when nothing else moves on the river, one under-ice current both supplies a riverside mine with fuel and reagents and carries its concentrate to port, with no vehicles, fuel or tending.

=== VALORIZED B05 ===
ANCHOR: winter-only track laid on frozen tundra
NAME: Frozen-season tundra railway
MECHANISM: Prefabricated track panels are laid straight onto frozen tundra compacted with snow, with no permanent embankment. Rivers are crossed on ice thickened by repeated flooding, tamping machines re-level the line each autumn to undo the summer heave, and trains run only in the 5–6 months when the ground is rigid. Because the line carries rail-scale tonnage at rail cost, it hauls raw ore, not concentrate, to a mill on the permanent rail network where grid power and reagents already exist, and the empty trains come back with fuel. The deposit therefore needs only a pit and a loading ramp, and nothing has to be separated in the cold.
VALUE: Rail-scale bulk haulage reaches remote deposits for a fraction of the cost of an all-season Arctic railway, and lets them send raw ore to grid-powered mills instead of building a plant on permafrost.

=== VALORIZED B06 ===
ANCHOR: dry winter wind sublimes ore ice
NAME: Winter sublimation deflation pad
MECHANISM: In deep winter, frozen placer gravel is ripped and spread in a thin layer on a pad at a windy tundra, coastal or ridge-top site. The dry −30 °C wind sublimes the pore ice from the top of the layer, freeing up to about a millimetre of sand a day, and because no liquid water ever forms, the freed grains lie loose and unbonded. The same wind then carries off the light quartz and feldspar grains, while gold, cassiterite, wolframite and other heavy grains (all but the finest) need a far stronger wind to move and stay behind with the gravel as a residue. A harrow turns the layer to expose fresh frozen sand, the residue is swept up and dry-screened to remove the gravel, and a snow fence downwind catches the blown waste sand.
VALUE: Placer gold and heavy-mineral sands are concentrated in midwinter, when sluicing is impossible, with no water, fuel or reagent, using only the wind that dries them.

=== VALORIZED B07 ===
ANCHOR: triboelectric sorting held by −40 °C dry air
NAME: Self-charging cold electrostatic separator
MECHANISM: Dry crushed ore slides down a chute lined with a chosen material, so each mineral picks up its own sign of charge, and in −40 °C air, which holds almost no water vapour, that charge does not leak away. The grains fall between two plates into two metal-lined bins, and each bin is wired to the plate on the far side. The charge carried into the bins therefore keeps driving the plates to a higher voltage, as in a Kelvin water dropper, until a fixed spark gap caps the field at tens of kilovolts. The separator builds its own field from the ore's charge and gravity, with no power supply, water or reagent, and its only setting is the splitter between the bins, moved according to the grade measured in each bin.
VALUE: Valuable minerals are separated from waste on site all winter with no water and no power for the separation itself, cutting the tonnage to be hauled by 5–20 times.

=== VALORIZED B08 ===
ANCHOR: Fram-hulled pods riding the Transpolar Drift
NAME: Transpolar Drift cargo pods
MECHANISM: Each autumn, sealed, buoyant pods with the hull shape of Nansen's Fram, so that ice pressure squeezes them upward instead of crushing them, are filled with coal or concentrate and set on newly formed Laptev Sea ice. The Transpolar Drift carries them to the ice edge in Fram Strait in about two years, where an ordinary ship collects them by beacon; if their floe melts early, they float on with the same currents. Launch sites are chosen from satellite ice-motion maps traced backward from Fram Strait, keeping only starting points whose paths reliably lead out rather than into the Beaufort Gyre, and the choice is updated each season from the pods' beacon logs.
VALUE: Bulk cargo moves from the Laptev coast to the North Atlantic with no engines, icebreakers or port, trading about two years in transit for almost no freight cost.
