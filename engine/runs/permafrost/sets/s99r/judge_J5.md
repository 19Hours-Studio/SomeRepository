TOP3:
- R28: The most original move in the set: nickel is turned into carbonyl gas and shipped in the existing gas line. The key throughput check holds (0.04 mol% of 38 bcm/yr is about 40 kt Ni/yr), so the open questions are toxicity and the cost of replacing the carbon monoxide (CO) used up, not the physics.
- R44: The only design that attacks the crux directly. It separates the mineral dry, using a static-charge field built by the falling grains themselves with no power supply, and it is honestly costed. One bench test of the per-stage split (85/45) settles whether it works.
- R43: Ore pods drifting under winter river ice are new. It is costed realistically at 2-4x cheaper than a winter road on a named 330 km Kolyma test reach, and that field test would be cheap.

NOTE: About a third of the set re-describes two stock ideas, throwaway pykrete flood hulls (8 answers) and freeze-concentration of brine (7), plus frost cracking and kite sleds. The answers that redesign an earlier idea ("anchor kept") are the most concrete, but their own numbers usually cut the gain to 0.5-2.3x rather than several-fold. Only the static-charge separator, wind-sorting and freeze-powered-engine lines tackle cold separation and site power with a mechanism of their own.
