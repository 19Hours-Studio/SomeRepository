A01
MECHANISM: An insulated, lined pond holds a salinity gradient made from local Siberian Platform brines. The gradient traps summer insolation in the dense bottom layer at 60–80 °C, and in winter the ice lid adds insulation. In winter an organic Rankine cycle draws heat from the bottom layer and rejects it to −40 °C air. That temperature difference of about 110 K roughly doubles the efficiency of solar-pond plants in temperate climates. Gradient sensors trigger brine injection whenever the non-convecting layer starts to mix.
VALUE: Supplies winter heat and electricity to remote operations from local salt and sunlight, cutting the fuel that has to come in by winter road.

A02
MECHANISM: Track panels are laid straight onto snow-compacted frozen tundra, with no permanent embankment, and rivers are crossed on artificially thickened ice. The line runs only during the 5–6 months when the ground is rigid. Each autumn, tamping machines re-level the track to undo the summer heave.
VALUE: Rail-scale bulk capacity reaches remote deposits for a fraction of the cost of an all-season Arctic railway.

A03
MECHANISM: Pads, embankments, tailings and foundations are covered with polymer radiative-cooling film. It reflects sunlight and radiates heat through the 8-13 µm atmospheric window, so the surface stays below air temperature even in the 24-hour summer sun. Buried thermistors show where the ground is warming, and more film layers or wider coverage go there.
VALUE: Ice-rich ground stays frozen with no power, piles or thermosyphons, so foundations cost far less and stop failing.

A04
MECHANISM: In winter, sprayed water builds an ice wall across a small creek, and a peat cover keeps it from melting. Snowmelt fills the basin behind it, and the water is released through a gate in midsummer as a timed crest. Flat-bottomed barges ride the crest down to a river deep enough for ships. Downstream depth gauges control the gate opening so the crest stays deeper than the barges' draft for the whole distance.
VALUE: Creeks that are too shallow for boats become seasonal freight routes from remote deposits, with no road or dredging.

A05
MECHANISM: Sealed sacks of about 1 t of ground valuable material each carry a gas bladder, set so the sack is slightly lighter than water and rides pressed against the smooth underside of a frozen river. The current under the ice, which flows all winter, pushes them 25–50 km a day with no fuel or vessel. If a pressure sensor detects a stall, a valve lets out gas so the sack drops back into the flow, then refills it from a cartridge. A net under a slot cut in the ice collects the sacks far downstream.
VALUE: Freight moves along frozen rivers through the whole winter at almost no energy cost, when today nothing moves on them.

A06
MECHANISM: The surface of ice-rich yedoma is stripped or darkened along a planned line toward a navigable river, while insulating mats protect the banks as a mask. Each summer, meltwater ponds in the strip, absorbs more sun and deepens the thaw. Mud drains away until thawing stops at ice-poor ground, leaving a channel deep enough for barges. Ground-temperature probes guide each year's re-darkening and widening, like etch-rate control.
VALUE: Solar heat does the digging, so lowland deposits get a waterway for the cost of surface treatment instead of a road at millions of dollars per kilometre.

A07
MECHANISM: Crushed dry rock is tumbled against a plate so that different minerals pick up different electric charges. The near-zero moisture of −40 °C air stops that charge from leaking away. The grains fall between two charged plates and are pushed into separate bins according to their charge. Readings from each bin continuously adjust the plate voltage and the dividers between bins.
VALUE: Valuable material is separated from waste on site through the winter with no water, cutting the tonnage to be hauled by 5–20 times.

A08
MECHANISM: A gas or electric gun on the deposit fires steel-cased slugs of 100-500 kg of concentrate at about 1.5 km/s into a sand-and-water catch basin at a railhead or river port more than 100 km away. Radar measures where each round lands, and that miss distance corrects elevation and charge for the next round, exactly like artillery adjusting fire. Casings are recovered and sent back on the seasonal supply route.
VALUE: A stranded deposit is connected to market by two fixed installations and no corridor at all.

A09
MECHANISM: Sealed, buoyant ore containers with satellite tags are set into Laptev Sea fast ice near the Lena delta. The Transpolar Drift carries the ice and containers to Fram Strait in 2–3 years without fuel or escort. When the floes melt, the containers float in open water and ships pick them up by their tag positions.
VALUE: Bulk ore can reach European waters with no icebreakers, paying only the cost of time.

A10
MECHANISM: Inflatable rubber weirs are flown in folded and set up in series on a small tributary between the deposit and a navigable river; each pond fills with snowmelt and rain. Shallow-draft barges loaded with concentrate wait in the top pond. A hydraulic routing model times the gate releases so that each pulse lifts the barges over the next shallow stretch into the pond below, and the model re-plans from water-level gauges after every pulse. Empty barges are winched back across the flat pools, and the weirs are deflated after the season, leaving no road or rail.
VALUE: Turns an ordinary summer stream into a temporary waterway, so bulk tonnage moves at water-freight cost without building a corridor.

A11
MECHANISM: Ice-class LNG carriers leave Arctic terminals with full tanks but under their weight limit, so they also carry thousands of tonnes of seawater ballast. Sealed cassettes of briquetted concentrate, brought by barge from deposits on the Yenisei and Ob, take the place of that ballast in double-bottom spaces and are swapped for seawater at the port where the LNG is unloaded. For each voyage, cargo reports and the ship's draft from AIS tracking give the ratio of paid tonnes to available deadweight. That ratio sets how many cassettes the next loading plan assigns, turning ballast space into cargo space.
VALUE: Arctic concentrate rides ships that already sail to Asian and European markets, at almost no extra cost for fuel or icebreakers.

A12
MECHANISM: In winter, slightly buoyant torpedo-shaped pods of concentrate are pushed through a slot cut in the ice of a big Siberian river's main channel. They ride the current along the smooth underside of the ice, out of reach of wind and waves, to a downstream recovery station with a boom and slot at a port. Weekly current profiles taken through the ice show where the flow stays fast. Where it slows, or where slush ice builds a dam below the ice, a relay slot is cut, the pods are lifted past the blockage and released again, and the spacing between releases is adjusted to the measured speed.
VALUE: Ore reaches Arctic ports during the frozen months and is already on the quay, instead of competing for the short summer window on the river.

A13
MECHANISM: In deep winter, frozen placer gravel is dug and crushed while it stays below −20 °C. At that temperature the ice behaves as a brittle, light mineral. Air tables and dry-washers separate the gold and heavy minerals from gravel and ice by density, with no water at all, and the tailings are stacked frozen.
VALUE: Placer mining can run all year without thawing, water or tailings ponds.

A14
MECHANISM: Reusable sealed pods, shaped like Nansen's Fram so that ice pressure squeezes them upward instead of crushing them, are filled with coal or concentrate whose value does not suffer from slow delivery. In autumn they are set on newly formed Laptev Sea ice, drift with the ice to the ice edge at Fram Strait, and are picked up there by an ordinary ship. Comparing daily satellite radar maps of the ice gives drift paths traced backward from the Fram Strait exit. Starting sites whose paths turn off into the Beaufort Gyre are dropped, new sites are added where paths converge on the exit, and pod placement is re-optimized each season from beacon logs.
VALUE: Bulk cargo moves from the Laptev coast to the North Atlantic with no engines, no icebreakers and no infrastructure, trading about two years in transit for almost no freight cost.

A15
MECHANISM: Each spring, black UV-stable geotextile is laid over the deposit to absorb round-the-clock summer sun. Each autumn, rows of cheap snow fences are set up to trap a thick snow blanket that blocks most winter refreezing. Buried temperature strings track the thaw front, and film coverage and fence spacing are re-tuned every season to maximise net heat gain, so the unfrozen zone deepens year after year. Light rippers and loaders can then dig the thawed gravel without blasting or heavy frozen-ground machinery.
VALUE: Replaces diesel-intensive excavation of frozen ground with free solar heat for placers and shallow overburden.
