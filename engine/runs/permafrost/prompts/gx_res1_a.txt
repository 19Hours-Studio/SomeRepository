Russia holds vast natural resources (oil, gas, metals, diamonds, coal, rare earths) in Siberia, Yakutia and the Arctic, mostly under permafrost and far from roads, railways and ports. Propose a fundamentally new way to reach, extract, process or bring these resources to market that is extremely cheap (several times cheaper than current practice), has never been tried or seriously costed by anyone, can be deployed within a few years, and works with known physics. Not incremental improvements of existing mining, drilling, pipelines or winter-road logistics.

DEAD 1
NAME: Jacked Frozen-Block Stripping
MECHANISM: Overlapping hot-water-drilled holes cut frozen overburden into 20×20 m blocks, and summer creek water injected at bedrock thaws only the gold-bearing gravel beneath one block. Hydraulic jacks bracing against the next frozen block push the ~8,000-t block into the previously mined cell, exposing the thawed paystreak for excavator and sluice. Blocks advance downstream like sliding-puzzle tiles; kerf spacing adapts to measured ice content.
VALUE: Overburden is pushed rather than ripped, using about 0.1 l diesel/m³ instead of ~1 l/m³, cutting placer earthmoving roughly tenfold.
DEATH CERTIFICATE: killer: push force to slide an 8,000-t block over its thawed paystreak vs shear reaction of the next frozen block's base | margin 1.46 | chain ratio 1.39 | WOUNDED: The block can be pushed with about 1.5x reaction headroom and the kerfs cost only 0.13 l/m³, but drilling and jacking make overburden about 2.9x cheaper rather than 10x, so delivered cost falls about 28 %, short of the 1.5x bar.

DEAD 2
NAME: Self-Sorting Wave-and-Ice Pond
MECHANISM: Over a stripped, ice-rich placer, a spillway holds a shallow pond of the ground's own meltwater. The pond absorbs the round-the-clock summer sun and thaws the gravel beneath it, wind waves lift silt and sand over the spillway, and their pressure pulses jig gold and heavy sand down through the stones onto the frozen floor. Each autumn the pond is set just shallower than the winter ice will grow, so the ice freezes onto the tops of the stones. In spring the spillway is closed, snowmelt lifts the bottom-fast ice, and the wind pushes the floes and their stones onto a dump shore, the way lake ice builds boulder ramparts. The spillway is lowered each year so the thin heavy layer rides down with the thaw front to bedrock, where only that layer is dug and washed.
VALUE: Even in the cobbly gravels typical of Siberian placers, sun, wind and winter ice remove the silt, sand and stones in place, so only a few percent of the deposit is ever dug and washed, with no pumps, washing-plant fuel or reagents.
DEATH CERTIFICATE: killer: near-bed wave orbital velocity needed to stir 0.5-mm sand in a 1-m-deep pond | margin 0.24 | chain ratio 1.9 | DEAD: Waves on a pond this small are too short to reach the floor: even a gale stirs it at ~2 cm/s, a quarter of what 0.5-mm sand needs and ~5% of what 20-mm stones need, so the sorting that would halve placer cost never starts.

DEAD 3
NAME: Spodumene Through Nepheline Alumina Kilns
MECHANISM: Spodumene concentrate is blended into kiln feed at Russia's existing nepheline-limestone sinter alumina plants (Achinsk, Pikalyovo); at 1,250°C the lime opens spodumene as in the old lime-roast lithium process, and its alumina joins plant output. Dissolved lithium is caught by steps already running: it co-precipitates as lithium-aluminium hydroxide during CO2 carbonation or crystallizes as carbonate in the soda-potash evaporators. Only a small strip-and-purify circuit is new.
VALUE: Lithium carbonate capacity for roughly a tenth of a greenfield chemical plant's capital, the largest cost term.
DEATH CERTIFICATE: killer: lithium the alumina hydrate can carry within smelter spec | margin 0.024 | chain ratio 1.46 | DEAD: Lithium co-precipitates with the hydrate as intended, but 45 kt/yr would then need a full-stream strip removing 97.6% of it from all of Achinsk's alumina hydrate, not a small new circuit (and RUSAL said in Aug 2026 it may close Pikalyovo).

DEAD 4
NAME: Shipyard-Built Floating Concentrator Fleet
MECHANISM: Crushing, grinding and flotation are built as 10 Mt/yr barge modules in an ordinary shipyard at normal productivity, towed by river or sea in summer into a dredged basin beside the deposit, and moored in water kept open by process heat and bubblers. Floating eliminates piles, heated halls and site-built civil works on permafrost. When the orebody is exhausted, the modules are towed to the next deposit.
VALUE: Escaping the 2-3x Far North build premium and reusing hulls across two or three deposits cuts capital per tonne roughly 5-8x.
DEATH CERTIFICATE: killer: water depth on the tow route vs draft of a 10 Mt/yr module (1,240 t/h; ~18 kt of mills, mill charge, flotation slurry and heated superstructure -> ~28 kt displacement -> 4.7-6.3 m draft; Akademik Lomonosov, 21.5 kt on 144x30 m at 5.6 m draft, as real-world check) | margin 0.42 | chain ratio 1.15 | WOUNDED: The module draws more than twice the depth Siberian rivers guarantee, and with pit, tailings, road, port and power still onshore, even a 6.5x cheaper concentrator cuts delivered cost only about 13%.
