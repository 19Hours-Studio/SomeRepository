=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Spring Sun-Wind Deflation Placer
DEFECT | FATAL | The whole Mar 15 to May 10 window falls inside the tundra snow season, which the design ignores. The 0.48 km² field must first be stripped of its snowpack, then re-cleared after every snowfall or drift, and 1 cm of new snow raises the albedo from 0.12 to about 0.8, cutting absorbed sunlight about 4x. | The Indigirka tundra snowpack is about 35 cm at 0.3 g/cm³ and clears in late May to early June (UNESCO tentative-list entry); in the Lena Delta melt is mid-to-late May (MODIS). That means 105 mm w.e., about 50,000 t or 166,600 m³ of snow over 0.48 km². Tiksi averages about 4 snow days in April and 2.5 in May.
DEFECT | FATAL | No wind speed removes 25-500 µm quartz while leaving gold. This kills the old-tailings variant, whose gold sits in exactly the most mobile size range. | Shao & Lu (2000) threshold friction velocities: quartz at 25 µm = 0.34 m/s and at 500 µm = 0.35 m/s; gold minimum = 0.37 m/s at 40 µm. That is a 5% gap at best, and it turns negative (-3% to -8%) across the published range of the cohesion parameter (1.65-5×10⁻⁴). At a 7 m/s wind (z0 0.5-3 mm) u* is 0.28-0.35, so neither 25 µm nor 500 µm quartz leaves. Gusts at 1.4x (u* 0.40-0.48) move gold finer than about 100 µm. Gold in Yakutian technogenic placers is mainly 0.044-0.1 mm (Rudmet 2020).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "no liquid water" anchor fails in the second half of the window, and mid-March is too cold to supply the needed rate. | The design needs a mean of 0.51 mm w.e./day over 57 days. Tiksi: April mean daily high about -11 °C; May mean -4.1 °C, typical May day -7 to -3 °C. My energy balance puts a dark surface in sun 15-20 K above air: about 0 to +5 °C at noon by mid-April and +15 to +20 °C in early May, so pore ice melts, the fines wet and turn cohesive, and the surface crusts. In mid-March the noon surface is only about -18 °C.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The decisive sublimation rate of 0.4-0.7 mm w.e./day is assumed for bare ice. In practice about 70% of each day's 3.7 mm of freed material stays on the surface as a non-deflating dry lag until the daily rake. | Vapour-diffusion resistance of a 1, 3 or 10 mm dry layer is 204, 612 or 2,041 s/m against about 83 s/m aerodynamic, cutting the rate 3.4x, 8.3x or 25x. A pebble lag (frontal-area index 0.05-0.2) also raises the deflation threshold 1.8-3.0x (Raupach drag partition). The measured analogue, Antarctic ice-cemented ground under a dry lag, recedes 0.4-0.6 mm per year (McKay et al.).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Costed bottom-up, the method is not 3.6x cheaper, and the baseline is wrong for tailings: there it should be re-washing tailings with no stripping, not $6.0/m³ virgin summer washing. Both $1.7 and $6.0 are unsourced. | My tally is $2.4-6.6/m³: daily harrow and rake of 0.48 km² takes 12-26 tractor-hours a day (707-1,507 h a season), $0.85-3.0; lag loading $0.27-0.66; washing a 25-35% residue $0.75-2.1; snowpack strip $0.5-0.8. That gives 0.9-2.5x. In pebble dumps up to 50% of the material is unwashed clayey sand that will not deflate (Snetkov & Talgamer 2014).
DEFECT | MAJOR | A pore-ice fraction of 0.15 holds only for drained, clean gravel. Saturated fine tailings and silty pay refreeze near 0.30, which halves the release rate and doubles the area and machine hours. | At 0.30 pore ice, 0.4-0.7 mm w.e./day frees only 1.45-2.5 mm/day. The area grows to about 0.95 km² and the total cost to $3.2-9.6/m³.
DEFECT | MINOR | The process is an unpermitted fugitive dust source on open tundra. | At a 20-30% deflated share it emits 32,000-48,000 t per season, or 560-840 t/day, from 0.48 km².
DEFECT | MINOR | No test is proposed that could show "no gold moves"; it would need a tagged-gold tracer. | With a placer grade CV of 60-100% between samples, detecting a 5% loss at 2σ takes about 580-1,600 paired samples. For 1 mm gold, Poisson counting alone needs 16-32 m³ per arm.
SURVIVES | The stated arithmetic reproduces: 0.014-0.041 mm w.e./day gives 0.05-0.15 mm/day at pore ice 0.30; 0.4-0.7 gives 2.9-5.1 mm/day at 0.15; 0.21 m over 57 days is 3.7 mm/day; 100,000 m³ needs 0.48 km²; the 13-23 W/m² latent flux is energetically available in April.

IDEA 2: Self-feeding ore-ice buttress dam
DEFECT | FATAL | The 0.8 m/day self-dosing (and the 20-100x heat-transfer margin) assumes a clean ice face. The creek film cannot clear the thawed gravel, which stays on the face and insulates it. | 47 L/s over a face 40-100 m wide (800 m², from 640 m³/day ÷ 0.8 m/day) is a 0.7-1.0 mm film with 4-8 Pa of shear; it moves only clasts under 6-11 mm. A thawed debris coat of just 0.9-1.6 cm caps the flux at the needed 935 W/m², and a 10 cm coat cuts retreat to 0.10 m/day, 8x short. Lena island heads retreat only 2-40 m a year over 6-39 active days even under the full river, and heat-transfer-only models overpredict about 10x.
DEFECT | FATAL | A hoisted winter stockpile is a loose dump of frozen lumps, not an ice-saturated dam: as described the forebay cannot fill. Frozen water-retaining dams in Yakutia degrade by seepage and talik growth. | At K = 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹ m/s, 8 m head and 75 m length, seepage through 800 m² is 0.09-8.5 m³/s against a 0.047 m³/s creek. Ice-saturating 60,000 m³ needs 16,000-22,000 m³ of winter water while the creeks are frozen. Seepage has compromised Western Yakutia frozen dams (Sytykan, Vilyuy) for about 20 years, and Irelyakh has lost its frozen foundation (GeoQuebec 2015; 2020 hydrotechnical journal).
DEFECT | MAJOR | An 8 °C water supply is assumed but unavailable from small permafrost creeks over a 94-day season, so the season output drops to about half. | The design needs 748 kW, i.e. 3.8 K extracted at 47 L/s. Order I-II streams in the Upper Kolyma run 4.7 °C colder in summer than order VI-VII (Samokhvalov & Ukhov 2018), and the Kolyma main stem is only 10-15 °C at midsummer. At a 4 °C creek with the design's own 40-70% heat extraction, feed is 270-470 m³/day, or 25,000-44,000 m³ a season instead of 60,000.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Because feed equals flow times temperature, feed is uncontrolled and pushes the sluice outside its working water ratio, packing the riffles and losing fine gold. The cited norm is also self-contradictory. | At a fixed 47 L/s, creek water at 2 °C gives 252 m³/day and at 10 °C gives 1,599 m³/day (6x), so water:solids swings 16 to 2.5. Working sluices run about 3.2-14 m³ water per m³ (Yukon placer atlas, 1,000-2,900 US gal/yd³). The text calls "4.3-7.6 m³" "inside" a norm it quotes as 2.2 m³/m³.
DEFECT | MAJOR | This is a water-retaining structure whose downstream support is consumed on purpose (75 m down to 0 in 94 days), with the chute and crew at its toe. | The "reduced" 128 m³/s peak is about 2,700x baseflow (Froehlich: about 12,000 m³ stored at 8 m head). The October 2019 Seyba placer dam failure near Shchetinkino killed at least 15 people.
DEFECT | MAJOR | "No dozers or pumps" ignores that boulders and cobbles cannot travel in a 47 L/s chute and that 640 m³/day of tailings still has to be stacked. The $6.7 and $1.3 figures are unsourced, and washing is a minor stage in the more labour- and cost-intensive underground placer mining. | 640 m³/day of oversize plus tailings means the same dozer hours. So a 5x saving on one stage is far smaller per unit of recovered gold.
SURVIVES | These reproduce: 101 MJ/m³ (30% ice at -5 °C; 67-86 MJ at 20-25% ice); 4.3-7.6 m³ means 3.2-5.6 K extracted; 75 m ÷ 0.8 m/day = 94 days, about 60,000 m³ at 6.3 m³/m³ from 47 L/s; peat thaw 0.48-0.67 m (Stefan, 800-1,400 °C·day).

IDEA 3: Freeze-and-float boulder pond
DEFECT | FATAL | 0.08-0.17 m³/m² is the zero-freeboard limit with no margin. Spring loads remove most of it, while continued freezing into the bed adds as much or more frozen weight than the floe can lift, so it stays grounded. | Stone/ice volume ratio at neutral buoyancy = 83/1650 = 0.050. With 40-120 kg/m² of snow or slush and 0.3 m of top melt, natural-ice capacity falls to 0-0.06 m³/m². After the ice touches in March, freezing goes 11-33 cm into the bed in 30-45 days (Stefan), locking in 0.08-0.23 m³/m² of solids.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Freezing into the bed also bonds the gold-bearing matrix to the boulders, so floes carry pay to the dump bay. | Matrix is typically 60-85% of the frozen layer's solids, i.e. 1.5-5.7 m³ of pay per m³ of boulder, unless the boulders protrude more than the 11-33 cm freeze depth.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Pond depth is fixed in autumn, but "touch in March" is a band of only about 0.15 m of ice growth, much narrower than year-to-year scatter. A few-winter pilot cannot prove reliability. | With an interannual σ of 0.10-0.25 m (FDD alone gives 0.07-0.10 m; snow adds more), P(touch in March) is 0.24-0.55 per winter; the rest ground early or never touch. A 3-winter pilot gets 3/3 only 3-17% of the time, and estimating P to ±0.15 takes about 40 winters.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is not what operators pay. Dozers handle boulders up to 1.0-1.2 m with the gravel, and only larger oversize is drilled and blasted. | ztu.edu.ua gives 100-120 cm as the dozer limit. So $6/m³ applies only to oversize, and the dozing that handles most boulders is close to the unsourced $1/m³. Bouldery ground that dredges reject is already mined open-pit, so the 6x falls to about 1x for most boulders.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The clearing rate delays mining by years, and the delay costs more than the saving. | At 0.04-0.17 m³/m² a year, 2-3 m of pay with 15-25% boulders (0.3-0.75 m³/m²) needs 1.8-19 winters of ponding. A 3-5 year delay alone costs 25-50% of NPV at 10-15%, more than the claimed 1.25-1.5x washing saving.
DEFECT | MINOR | Floes are heavy and must be winched across rotting spring ice, in a pond that is itself a water-retaining dam. | 10-20 m floes weigh 178-714 t and must be winched during a 1-2 week rise. The pond carries the same dam-break exposure as Seyba.
SURVIVES | These reproduce: wave bottom orbital velocity 0.032 m/s at 1 m (Hs 0.1 m, T 1.2 s); lift of 0.080-0.166 m³/m² at zero freeboard; summer thaw under the pond 1.44-2.27 m (600-1,500 °C·day). Dropping in-place sorting is correct.

IDEA 4: Trimmed snow-buoyed under-ice ore pods
DEFECT | FATAL | The +3 to +9 kg trim cannot survive the pod's own stated shell melt: within days the pod sinks and stays on the bed (or, with frazil accretion, pins to the ice). | 6 cm in 45 days over 39.3 m² is 2.36 m³ of ice at 83 kg buoyancy per m³, a loss of 4.35 kg/day. The pod is neutral after 0.7-2.1 days and 196-296 kg heavy at 45-68 days. Bed friction of 960-1,340 N then beats the 170 N drag. In the other direction, 1 cm of frazil accretion adds +33 kg.
DEFECT | FATAL | The named routes contradict the assumed smooth thermal ice. Warm-release dams leave long polynyas whose frazil settles as hanging dams in exactly the 0.2-0.6 m/s reaches the pods need. | The Yenisei below the Krasnoyarsk dam never freezes for 200-300 km (Wikipedia); the Vilyuy reservoir raised winter river temperature 5-6 °C (Wikipedia mirror). Frazil deposits below about 0.61 m/s (Kivisild, Fr 0.08), 0.69 m/s (MacLachlan) or 0.8-1.5 m/s (Newbury), and documented accumulations are 0.3-3 m thick.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Shell life is hypersensitive to water warmth too small to measure, and even at the stated rate the slowest pods melt through an 8 cm shell. | Ice-water h is about 360-500 W/m²K at 0.2-0.3 m/s. The stated 6 cm/45 days corresponds to just +0.01 K; at +0.03 K the shell is gone in 19-33 days, at +0.05 K in 11-20 days (Ashton 1973: hundredths of a degree matter). At the stated rate, a 68-day pod melts 9.1 cm.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Soaking to 0 °C upsets the trim and needs a huge open slot, none of it in the $7.3/t. | Contents at -20 to -30 °C freeze 0.8-1.2 t of ice onto the shell (+72 to +108 kg buoyancy). Conduction takes about 14 days, so 840-1,680 pods (9,000-18,500 m²) of open water must be kept ice-free at -40 °C, about 900-1,850 m³/day of new ice to clear.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Pods arrive in Feb-Apr at a downstream slot with no winter onward transport, so the cargo waits for summer navigation just as a stockpile shipped by summer barge would. The winter-road ratio is also not like-for-like. | The fair comparison is the barge leg alone, $12-37/t by their own ratio. At their winter-road rate of $0.118/t·km, 700 km is about $83/t, not $59-176.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Depth under late-winter ice is not shown to exist at every riffle. | Ice 1.2-2.0 m thick has 1.1-1.8 m draft, so a 1.2 m pod needs 2.3-3.0 m of water and a 2.2 m-tall fin needs 3.3-4.0 m at every riffle over 700 km. The 170 N drag implies Cd 1.21 only if the 5.0×2.2 m face is frontal (Cd 2.21 on 5.0×1.2 m). No winter riffle depths are given for the Kolyma or Vilyuy.
DEFECT | MINOR | The pod bookkeeping is wrong for coal, the claimed best cargo: sealed crushed coal is already over-buoyant without snow and does not fit 10 t per pod. | Bulk coal at about 0.82 t/m³ sealed is +220 kg/t. The 0.28/0.74/0.86 m³ figures reproduce only with flooded voids and snow at about 75-100 kg/m³. A 13.2 m³ pod holds at most 8.0-8.7 t of coal at 60-65% packing and is then 1.1-1.8 t over-buoyant, needing ballast.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 5% loss is an assumption that leaves cargo in the river, and it is expensive to measure. | 5% is 7,200 t of coal or sulphide ore a season, a waste discharge into water bodies barred under the Russian Water Code, with sulphide oxidation and fish wintering pits at risk. Measuring 5% to ±2% needs about 456 pods per route-year.
SURVIVES | These reproduce: the 1-12 kg stall limit for a 1 t pod (CdA 1.0-1.3, μ 0.5); 700 km in 41-68 days = 0.12-0.20 m/s; 1,200 t/day × 120 days = 144 kt; the 8.1-24.1x ratios.
