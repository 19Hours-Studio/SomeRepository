# Task: the frontier scout - fresh, demonstrated capabilities from FAR fields

An invention team is working on this question: Russia holds vast natural resources (oil, gas, metals, diamonds, coal, rare earths) in Siberia, Yakutia and the Arctic, mostly under permafrost and far from roads, railways and ports. Propose a fundamentally new way to reach, extract, process or bring these resources to market that is extremely cheap (several times cheaper than current practice), has never been tried or seriously costed by anyone, can be deployed within a few years, and works with known physics. Not incremental improvements of existing mining, drilling, pipelines or winter-road logistics.

Your job is NOT to propose answers. Your job is to bring them raw material that lies outside what they already know:
capabilities that were first DEMONSTRATED (lab with numbers, pilot, or first commercial use) in 2020-2026, mostly in
fields far from mining, oil and gas, and Arctic logistics. Examples of far fields: materials science, synthetic
biology, electrochemistry, robotics, aerospace, batteries and power electronics, semiconductors, agriculture, medicine,
telecoms and satellites, construction, ocean engineering, AI, chemistry, textiles, food processing, defence-civil.

Use WebSearch/WebFetch. Work only in `/home/user/SomeRepository/engine/runs/permafrost/genius/frontier/`: read no
other files in the repository, write files only there, do not commit.

Rules:
- 24 capabilities from at least 10 different fields; at most 4 may come from mining, metallurgy or oil and gas.
- Each must have a DEMONSTRATED number (rate, cost, efficiency, size, lifetime) with year and source - no roadmaps or
  press-release promises without a measured result.
- Prefer capabilities that are surprising: a property, rate or cost that was believed impossible or 10x worse before.
- Exclude anything already in routine use in mining or in the Arctic (autonomous haul trucks, ore sorting, thermosyphons,
  drones for survey, Starlink, small modular reactors in general, etc.).
- Do NOT suggest applications to the question. Describe each capability on its own terms.
