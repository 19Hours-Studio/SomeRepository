=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Self-Detaching Thaw-Face Stripping
DEFECT | MAJOR | The approach is not new, and the baseline leaves out hydraulic stripping of frozen muck, which has been practised for a century and removes muck about 10x faster per unit of face. | Fairbanks Exploration Co. ran 36-48 giants for 150-180 days a year, thawing and removing overburden at about 9 in/day (0.23 m/day = 34-41 m per season), against 3 m per season here (ASME landmark history). Russian placer practice already uses hydraulic stripping (гидровскрыша) and sprinkler thawing (дождевальная оттайка).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 3x cost ratio leaves out the cost of carrying stripping 4-9 seasons ahead of mining. | $0.30/m³ carried forward 4-9 yr at 10-15% becomes $0.44-1.06/m³ at mining, against $1.10-1.15 for one-season natural thaw, so the advantage is 1.1-2.5x. At 20% and 9 yr it is 0.78x, worse than baseline (python).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Detachment at 10-12° is not robust: at the high-cohesion/low-R corner the critical sheet is about one full season of natural thaw, and the design's own 0.9 m upper sheet gives 1.6-2.3 m/season, not 3 m. | Infinite-slope check with γ 16.5 kN/m³, γ'/γ 0.45, φ' 28°: z_crit at 10°, c' 2 kPa, R 0.9 is 1.47 m, against 1.6 m natural thaw. Stefan with periodic removal (k 1.3 W/mK, 10 °C, 100 d): 0.45 m sheets give 3.3-4.7 m, 0.9 m sheets give 1.6-2.3 m. At 1.6-2.3 m per season the yield is ~60-100 m³ per m and the face for 0.5 Mm³ is 5-8 km. Lab thaw of sandy silt on a 12° slope (Harris et al. 1995, PPP 6:293) gave creep, no rapid failure.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The runout depends on a slurry yield stress below 500 Pa that is not sourced; with measured thawed strengths the thin sheets stall on the 4° path and re-bury the toe, which is how natural thaw slumps stabilise. | Measured undrained strength after thaw is 0.8-9 kPa (Harris et al. 1995). A Bingham layer at ρ 1600 kg/m³ needs h ≥ τy/(ρ g sin4°) = 0.73-8.2 m to flow. Even at 500 Pa it needs ≥0.46 m, against 0.2-0.9 m sheets.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The mined cell must hold fluid mud for years: the slurry reaches 0.64 of its frozen volume only after consolidation, and at first it occupies about 0.95 of it. | The cv implied by R 0.9-1.6 is 0.5-2.8 m²/yr, so t90 = 0.85H²/cv = 7.6-41 yr for a 5 m single-drainage fill. Ice-to-water shrinkage is only 9% of the ice volume. The cell therefore needs ~50% more capacity than designed, plus a slurry dyke beside the active cut.
DEFECT | MINOR | The $0.3/m³ leaves out the first cut that grades the 10-12° face, which has to be moved by the old method. | For H 7.3-8.8 m (implied by 115-138 m³/m at 3 m normal retreat), the wedge is 0.5H²/tan11° = 137-199 m³ per m. Over 4 km that is 0.55-0.8 Mm³, or 1.1-1.6 seasons of output at $1.0-3.5/m³, adding $0.08-0.5/m³ over a 10-season life.
DEFECT | MINOR | The Yedoma case behind the 40% claim uses the same face productivity, but the higher latent heat lowers thaw per season. | At 65% ice instead of 35%, L rises from 107 to 199 MJ/m³. The same sheet then thaws 46% less: 2.0-2.5 m per season at 8-10 °C. The face grows to ~6-8 km per 0.5 Mm³, and ice wedges melt to water, not slurry.
DEFECT | MINOR | No proposed test can confirm the cycle rate within a season. | A season gives 3-10 detachment cycles (10-50 d each). The exact 95% Poisson interval for n=5 is 0.32-2.3x the observed rate, so several seasons and faces are needed.
DEFECT | MINOR | An engineered thaw slump cannot be switched off at closure, which leaves an open-ended reclamation liability. | The Batagay headwall is still retreating 5-15 m/yr decades after it began. This design retreats 3/sin11° ≈ 15.7 m horizontal per season along 4 km of face.
SURVIVES | The thaw-consolidation sliding threshold reproduces at 1.8-6.4° for φ' 25-33° and γ'/γ 0.4-0.5. 0.5 Mm³ ÷ 115-138 m³/m = 3.6-4.3 km. The 15%/40% gold-cost cuts imply plausible stripping shares of 21%/44%. About 3 m/season is physically possible if sheets stay ≤0.45 m and the mean surface temperature is ≥10 °C.

IDEA 2: Reservoir-Talik Tributary Dredging
DEFECT | FATAL | Open-water tailings discharge cannot meet the fishery limit: the plume figure assumes complete mixing into the whole river, and the cutter alone loses about the entire allowance before any plant tailings. | Back-calculating 1.0-2.6 mg/L at 5% escape (1.2 Mm³, 1.9 t/m³, 15-30% fines, 120 d) requires full mixing into 635-3,300 m³/s. At the cut, 0.5% escape is 0.48 kg/s of fines, which needs ~1,900 m³/s of mixing to stay within +0.25 mg/L (highest/first category, Minselkhoz Order 552/2016). Compliance is judged at a control section near the source, and the reservoir supports a commercial whitefish fishery. Cutterhead resuspension alone is 0.02-3.93% (Nakai 1978), with a hydraulic-dredge mean of 0.77% (USACE/Clarke). Plant tailings return all of the fines.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The depth window clashes with the reservoir cycle: the level rises up to 19.5 m (UMO 432 to NPU 451.5) during the same open-water season, which puts most of the band beyond cutter reach. | The base of an 8 m section lies 9.5-33 m below water at UMO and 29-52.5 m at NPU. Submerged-pump units of 600-720 m³/h are listed to ~30 m (ADST-600E), Damen cutters to -25 m, and -50 m only with plain-suction DOP pumps that have no cutter. If the spring minimum sits above UMO (no source shows it reaches UMO each year), every depth increases further.
DEFECT | MAJOR | There is no talik margin: an 8 m section plus 0.5-1 m of bedrock cleanup needs 8.5-9 m of thaw. The design's own model gives 8.5 m only at a mean bottom water of 2 °C, and that temperature is assumed, not measured. | A Neumann check (ice 25%, ground -4 °C, 40 yr) reproduces 5.6/8.5/10.8/12.7 m at 1/2/3/4 °C. Each 1 °C changes the talik by 2.3-2.9 m, and at 1 °C 2.7-3.2 m of the pay/bedrock stays frozen.
DEFECT | MAJOR | By the idea's own numbers it fails the brief: it is not several times cheaper. | Its cost is 0.7-1.0x the 1,050-2,000 rub/g of open-cut mining, so at best 1.4x cheaper.
DEFECT | MINOR | If the reservoir is at UMO at the end of winter, the shallow fringe of the band (floor 1.5-2.5 m below UMO) is reached by ice and refreezes from above each year. | End-of-winter ice on the upper Kolyma is 1.05-2.5 m (water-rf.ru).
DEFECT | MINOR | The "sterilised resource" is not evidenced. | The band is 130 of 292 km² at UMO (State Water Register), but no source found shows drowned placers there, and the share of tributary mouths with sections ≤8 m and overburden:pay ≤2 is unquantified.
SURVIVES | NPU 451.5, UMO 432.0 and FPU 457.6 m are confirmed (RusHydro), and filling ran 1980-1994. 1.2 Mm³ in 120 d is 69% utilisation of a 600 m³/h dredge. The 0.48-1.25% allowance arithmetic and the talik depths reproduce.

IDEA 3: Re-Floatable Pond-Moored Mine Plants
DEFECT | MAJOR | The idea is not new, and relocating floating plants has turned out to be expensive. | Floating wet concentrators in ponds dug at the site are standard in mineral sands. Kenmare moved the 7,100 t WCP B 23 km to Pilivili on a US$106M budget, which then overran by about 17%. That is ~$15-17k per tonne of plant, against $3k/t for hull steel. Bucket-line gold dredges have floated their processing plants in their own ponds since the 1920s. The 45-55% retained value has no source.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 3 m draft cannot reach the riverside deposits that the only paying regime relies on. | Guaranteed Lena depths are 2.2 m (Ust-Kut to Vitim), 2.6 m (Sinsk to Yakutsk) and 2.9 m (Yakutsk to Zhigansk), with ~1 m on the upper Lena in low water. At 2.2 m draft the payload drops from 2.9 to ~1.6 kt (lightship ~2.0 kt implied), so about twice as many hulls are needed.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The foundation saving is overstated: the mills, which carry the heaviest dynamic loads, still stand on bedrock foundations, and the 20-25% foundation share of capex is assumed. | From the claim, the hull/pond/moat penalty is 20-25% minus 15-19% = 5-6% of capex. If the foundations actually saved are 10-15% of capex, or 60% of 20-25% once the mills are excluded, the capex saving is 4-10% and the delivered-cost gain about 3-7%.
DEFECT | MAJOR | By its own numbers it fails the brief: 10-14% lower delivered cost in one niche and about 0% elsewhere is not several times cheaper. | The idea's own VALUE line.
DEFECT | MINOR | For 5-7-year gold deposits the competent baseline is often heap leach with no concentrator, not a mill on piles. | Seligdar's Podgolechny heap leach in Yakutia treated 648 kt of ore for 325 kg Au in 2024.
DEFECT | MINOR | The White Sea-Baltic lock "fit" leaves almost no slack, and the onward Arctic sea tow is not addressed. | A 14.0 m beam in a 14.3 m chamber leaves 0.15 m per side and 5 m of length, with no room for fenders or an ice belt (korabel.ru gives the chamber as 14.5 m).
DEFECT | MINOR | A power-house outage closes the moat within a day. | Bare ice grows 0.22-0.25 m in 24 h and 0.39-0.43 m in 72 h at -40 to -50 °C.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 0.5-2 m/yr bank retreat is assumed for warm, ice-rich ground next to a heated pond. | At 5 m/yr the 20-50 m setback is gone in 4-10 yr, which is within the 5-7 yr life.
SURVIVES | The moat heat is conservative: a bulk-flux estimate gives 0.2-1.0 MW per hull for 592 m² of moat (wind 2-10 m/s, -30 to -55 °C), against 0.9-1.6 MW claimed. A 130×14×3 m hull displaces 4.6-5.2 kt, consistent with 2.9 kt payload. The 135×14.3 m locks with 4 m depth are confirmed. A floating hull is indifferent to settlement of the pond floor. Cold permafrost giving ~0% gain is credible.

IDEA 4: Bluff-Anchored Barge Coal Augers
DEFECT | MAJOR | The required market (at least 60 kt/yr on one river system) barely exists, and on the Kolyma a riverside incumbent with spare capacity already serves it; the baseline omits that option. | All waterborne communal coal delivered in Yakutia in 2024 was 122 kt (Yakutugol, PortNews). The Zyryanka riverside pit plans ~250 kt/yr, its sales are limited by logistics, and it already ships by river to six districts.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Against the cheapest alternative named, the margin is only 16% on a modelled, unbuilt cost, and at single-village scale the idea loses. | 3,700/3,200 = 1.16x, so a 16% overrun erases the advantage. One village at 6,500 rub/t is 0.57-1.58x the alternatives (3,700-10,300).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The waterline adfreeze anchors sit in a bluff washed by 10-15 °C summer river water; near 0 °C their capacity falls below the thrust. | SP 25.13330 adfreeze strengths for clays are ~200 kPa at -3 °C, 100 kPa at -1 °C and 60 kPa at -0.5 °C. The stated 283-424 kN per anchor implies ~-3 °C. At -1 to -0.5 °C two anchors give 170-424 kN against 300 kN of thrust, a safety factor of 0.57-1.4.
DEFECT | MAJOR | At the minimum economic scale, the season window does not hold. | 60 kt at 33-40 t/h and 80-100% availability needs 62-95 days of augering, against a 60-120 day window. The Kolyma stage range is up to ~14 m (BRE), against the ±3 m frame.
DEFECT | MINOR | There are no barrier pillars, so a web failure can cascade along the bluff above the moored barge. | NIOSH highwall practice uses barrier pillars between panels. With 0.6 m webs at 50 m cover the safety factor is 1.0, and the 60 kt programme needs 435 m/yr of bluff with a seam ≥1.8 m within ±3 m of summer water level.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 33-53 t/h output is a cycle model only, with no frozen-coal auger trial behind it. | The 16% margin over the micro-pit alternative is smaller than the 33-53 t/h (±23%) spread of the model.
SURVIVES | Each hole is 254.5 m³ × 1.30 = 331 t; 20 kt needs 60.4 holes; 26 days matches 40 t/h at 80% availability; 145 m of bluff = 60 × 2.4 m. Mark-Bieniawski web safety factors of 3.4/1.7/1.0 (6.2 MPa coal) reproduce the stated 3.1/1.5/0.9. The 1.16-3.22x ratio range reproduces.
