=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Frozen-Roof Jet Adits
DEFECT | FATAL | The face cannot be thawed as fast as the design advances: moving 3.7-8 m/h through 107 MJ/m³ frozen gravel takes 110-240 kW/m² over the whole 4.4-12 m² face, which means a face-averaged heat-transfer coefficient h of 18,000-79,000 W/m²K with 3-6 °C water, so neither the advance rate nor the 30% heat use can be reached. | That h is 22-190x the h implied by the idea's own "6 °C water thaws the roof 2-4 m/day" (410-830 W/m²K). The stagnation h of a 60-100 mm water jet at 20-40 m/s is 11,000-17,000 W/m²K (Nu = 0.745·Re^0.5·Pr^0.33). Even with the whole face held at that h it retreats only 1.8-2.9 m/h, and a realistic jet footprint of ≤10-20% of the face gives ≤0.2-0.6 m/h. 30% heat use at about 190 L/s needs hA ≈ 284 kW/K, which is ≥19 m² at stagnation h, more than the face area. Single-pass heat use is 2-12%, so output is about 2-18 m³/h, not 35-45.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The pillars are not safe over the claimed "≥20 m cover" range: the quoted 0.4-1.2 MPa is simply 20 m of cover at 0-67% extraction, and the top of that range already exceeds the 1 MPa lower long-term strength. | σ = ρgH/(1−e) with ρ = 2.0 t/m³: 20 m at 67% gives 1.19 MPa (safety factor 0.84 against 1 MPa); 30 m gives 1.18-1.78 MPa; 40 m gives 1.57-2.38 MPa (safety factor 0.42-1.3).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 1,200-1,400 rub/m³ cost does not show the pumping energy (the jet plus a hydro-elevator lifting slurry ≥20 m), and for valley-bottom placers it also leaves out the decline or shaft needed to put a portal at bedrock level. | A hydro-elevator needs a nozzle head of about 6x the lift (the Ruble elevator lifted 16-17 ft on 100 ft of head). That gives 330-800 kW for 0.15-0.2 m³/s at 20-30 m of lift, plus 120-370 kW of jet pumps: 10-34 kWh/m³, or 180-1,370 rub/m³ at a diesel fuel cost of 18-40 rub/kWh (15-100% of the stated total).
DEFECT | MAJOR | An unmanned jet head cannot take the top of the fractured bedrock that drift mining normally removes for its gold, and cannot pass boulders larger than the elevator throat, so both recovery and advance fall. | Every 10% of oversize at 40 m³/h leaves 4 m³/h, a 0.24 m-thick bed of boulders per metre of advance across the 3 m floor, lying on the gold-bearing contact. The implied recovered grade of only 2.12-2.18 g/m³ (1,200/550 and 1,400/660) carries no allowance for this loss.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 25-45 h roof life counts only the drive: retreating the pillars of the same 167-200 m panel at the same rate roughly doubles exposure, and humid 2-4 °C adit air thaws the roof faster than the stated 3-5 cm. | Sensible plus condensation heat flux of 34-71 W/m² (h = 10 W/m²K) thaws 3-11 cm in 25-45 h and 6-21 cm in 50-90 h.
DEFECT | MINOR | The production arithmetic does not reproduce (it is understated). | 3.7-5.1 MW × 30% ÷ 107 MJ/m³ = 37-51 m³/h, not 35-45.
SURVIVES | Creek 0.1 m³/s × 5 K = 2.09 MW, giving 21 m³/h; pond 2 ha × 80-150 W/m² = 1.6-3.0 MW; 107 MJ/m³ matches about 300 kg of ice per m³; 2-4 m/day flood thaw holds and is if anything low (turbulent 6 °C flow gives about 2-5 m/day, and the Douglas et al. 2023 flume tests eroded about 3x faster than theory); the per-gram ratios reproduce (1.6-3.0x against underground mining).

IDEA 2: Year-Round Under-Ice Talik Crawler
DEFECT | FATAL | Under ice, with no flushing, winter work cannot meet the fishery limit on suspended solids, because even a well-deployed silt curtain leaks orders of magnitude more than the limit allows. | The limit is +0.25 / +0.75 mg/L over background (Minselkhoz order 552, replaced in Sept 2025 by Rosrybolovstvo order 296). Staying under 0.75 mg/L for a single day needs 99.98% containment of 540 t/day in 160k m³. Curtains reach 80-90% at best, in quiescent water (JBF Scientific / USACE WES 1978). That leaves +340-680 mg/L per day, accumulating over a 150-200-day ice season.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The claimed 1.2-1.35x advantage falls short of the brief's "several times", and it sits inside the error of a desk estimate, so even the sign of the saving is not established. | A 17-25% saving against AACE 18R-97 Class 5 accuracy (−20 to −50% / +30 to +100%). A +20% overrun on the crawler case gives 1,920 rub/g, the same as draining the lake (1,930).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 540 t/day of fines (about 13% of the dug mass) assumes gravel, but the upper part of a thermokarst lake floor is silt-dominated lake fill. | 0.6 Mm³/yr ÷ 270 d × 1.9 t/m³ = 4,220 t/day dug. At 30-60% fines that is 1,270-2,530 t/day (2.4-4.7x the stated figure), and the jig would be working in slime-laden cell water.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A crawler that cannot leave through the 1.4 m hole cannot be serviced for the roughly 7-month ice season, yet the output figure implies 62% utilisation. | 0.6 Mm³ ÷ (270 d × 24 h × 150 m³/h) = 0.62. One cutter, pump or jig failure in December idles it until breakup in mid-to-late May, up to about 150 days.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The crawler's ability to move on soft lake mud is not shown. | 40 t on 8-20 m² of track gives 17-43 kPa submerged, against 9-17 kPa bearing capacity for lake-bed surface sediment with undrained shear strength su ≈ 1.8-3.3 kPa (vane data, 5.14·su).
DEFECT | MINOR | Digging organic-rich lake sediment under ice adds oxygen demand to bottom water that is already anoxic in winter, and releases methane that collects under the ice and in the enclosed moon-pool shelter. | Hughes-Allen et al. 2021 (Limnology & Oceanography): winter bottom-water anoxia and methane build-up under ice in all Central Yakutian thermokarst lake types.
DEFECT | MINOR | The annual volume does not follow from the stated rate without an unstated 62% utilisation, and the floor area needed grows with it. | 270 d × 24 h × 150 m³/h = 0.97 Mm³/yr (13.9 ha at 7 m), not 0.6 Mm³ (8.6 ha).
DEFECT | MINOR | A Stefan coefficient of 2.7 is the snow-free upper bound, so with snow on the ice the date the ice reaches 1.1 m slips by 2-5 weeks. | Yakutsk normals with freeze-up on 15 Oct: a = 2.7 gives 18 Dec, a = 2.4 gives 30 Dec, a = 2.0 gives 23 Jan.
SURVIVES | Ice load (Gold's formula P = A·h²: 25 t needs only 0.85 m at A = 3.5, and 1.1 m carries 42 t); moon-pool heat (1.7-2.0 kW for 2 m² at −40/−50 °C); 540 t in 160k m³ = 3,375 mg/L per day; 8.6 ha at 7 m for 0.6 Mm³; the 1.21-1.34x ratios reproduce.

IDEA 3: Wintering Coal CHP Barge
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is wrong: the village diesel plant has to stay for summer, the shoulder months and standby, so the barge saves only diesel fuel, not the full 45-75 rub/kWh cost. | Avoided fuel = 0.28-0.46 kg/kWh × 66-120k rub/t ≈ 18-55 rub/kWh (isolated-settlement diesel at 43-66k rub/t and 231-460 g/kWh per EUROSOLAR Russia; Yakutia retail diesel about 88 rub/L). Against that, 19-32 rub/kWh is 0.6-2.9x cheaper, not 1.9-4.3x.
DEFECT | MAJOR | All of the saving comes from combined heat and power, which a shore-based ORC at the existing boiler house would get without the barge's penalties; a floating Arctic CHP feeding a town heat network also already exists. | The barge alone loses 104 + 44 = 148 kW, 9.6% of the 1.54 MW reject heat, and adds ice and flood risk and a heat main from the bank. Akademik Lomonosov at Pevek (70 MWe + 50 Gcal/h, in service since 2019-20).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Wintering at a village bank rather than a protected backwater exposes a barge wired to shore heat mains and cables to spring ice drift and ice-jam floods, and it must be cut loose while the village still needs heat. | Lena ice jam in May 2026: Peledui gauge at 13.05 m against a 12.0 m critical level; Kolyma level range up to 14 m (Great Russian Encyclopedia). Loaded draft plus 1.5-2 m of ice needs about 4-5 m of late-winter water depth at the berth.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The cheapest figure (14 rub/kWh at 1 MWe) does not fit the single-barge concept, and "up to 6x" pairs that 1 MWe cost with a small village's 88 rub/kWh diesel. | 1 MWe burns 1,780/0.3 = 5,930 t per 194-day winter, which is three 2,000 t barges. At matched size the ratio is 88 ÷ 19-32 = 2.8-4.6x, before the avoided-cost correction.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The cost does not show round-the-clock certified staff for a thermal-oil coal boiler plus ORC (a hazardous production facility), and the village boiler house and its stokers stay on as heat standby. | My estimate of about 9 staff (2 per shift) at about 1.5 M rub/yr each is 13.5 M rub/yr, or +7.5-10 rub/kWh over 1.3-1.8 GWh/yr. Capex alone at 10-15% over 15 years already gives 10.5-29 rub/kWh.
DEFECT | MINOR | One barge-load does not cover the frozen-in period at full output. | 1,780 t lasts 194 days at 2.34 MW of coal (LHV 22.1 MJ/kg). From the end of navigation to breakup is about 230-270 days, needing 2,110-2,470 t, more than 2,000 t unless output is cut.
DEFECT | MINOR | 44 kW keeps the hold unfrozen only if about 1,500 m² of hatches and topsides are insulated, and a warmed, closed hold of coal for 6-8 months is the classic case for self-heating, CO and methane. | Hold heat loss at −45 °C is 30 kW at U ≈ 0.4-0.6 W/m²K, against 180-580 kW for bare steel. Coal is IMSBC Group B (self-heating, methane, oxygen depletion; daily gas monitoring advised).
DEFECT | MINOR | The heat credit is fuel-neutral only against boiler houses at ≤65% efficiency, and the 1.9-4.3x range uses a single 60 rub/kWh instead of the stated 45-75. | At 75-80% boiler-house efficiency the CHP burns +0.97-1.40 kWh of coal per kWh_e (+1.6-2.3 rub/kWh). 45-75 ÷ 14-32 gives 1.4-5.4x.
SURVIVES | Energy balance (0.3/(0.8×0.16) = 2.34 MW of coal, 1.575 MW reject heat, 1.54/0.65 = 2.37 MW displaced); electric-only 12.7 rub/kWh (at about 22 MJ/kg); ash 381 m³ (15% ash, 0.7 t/m³) against 2,094 m³ of hold freed; moat 104 kW keeps about 0.5-0.7 m of open water at −40/−50 °C; ORC 16% is conservative (AHDB: about 17% of fuel).

IDEA 4: Barge-Mounted Bluff Auger Coal
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 1,800 rub/t needs 80 kt/yr of sales within 150 km of one bluff, and below about 23-35 kt/yr the rig loses to a pit at the same bank. | From the idea's own two points (20 kt gives 4,100 rub/t, 80 kt gives 1,800): fixed cost ≈ 61 M rub/yr and variable ≈ 1,030 rub/t, so it breaks even with a 2,800-3,700 rub/t pit at 23-35 kt/yr. The whole of Yakutia's 2024 northern supply delivery to utility and production enterprises was >312 kt of all fuels together, so 80 kt would be about 26% of it from one site.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 60 t/h as a season average is not supported by auger cycle times, because adding and pulling 13 auger flights and re-bolting the reaction frame every 2.1-2.4 m dominate each cycle. | 80 kt in 100 days is 33 t/h around the clock, one 190 t hole every 5.7 h with no downtime. Cutting at 60-140 t/h, plus 26 flight moves at 6-9 min and 1-2 h to move and re-bolt, takes 5.0-9.1 h per hole: 21-38 t/h at 100% availability (USGS planning rate 175-250 t/shift ≈ 22-31 t/h). At 75% availability that is 38-68 kt/yr and 1,930-2,650 rub/t, only 1.06-1.9x cheaper than the pit.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 3 m of frame travel cannot follow the river over a 100-day navigation season, which includes the flood recession and rain floods, not just a 1-3 m low-water drop; and holes at the waterline flood whenever the river rises above their mouths. | Kolyma: high water from mid-May to September and a level range up to 14 m (Great Russian Encyclopedia); Lena ice-jam level of 13 m at Peledui in May 2026.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 62% is the geometric face recovery; field auger recovery is much lower once short, deviated or collapsed holes are counted, so each tonne needs more holes and more bluff. | Practical recovery is 30-50% against 78% theoretical (Ukrainian auger practice with BShK and AVSh rigs). That means 1.2-2.1x more holes per tonne and 1.1-1.9 km of bluff a year instead of 0.9.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The crew and barge work at the toe of a 20-30 m permafrost bluff on an outer bend, the kind that fails by thermo-erosional undercutting and block collapse, and the rig removes 62% of the seam at that toe. | No exclusion zone, bluff monitoring or crew protection is costed; summer thaw of the face and flooded hole mouths attack the toe throughout the 100-day campaign.
DEFECT | MINOR | Auger coal is fine-sized, which grate-fired village boilers handle poorly (losses through the grate, unburnt carbon), so a tonne is worth less than sized pit coal. | Cutting with auger-type cutters yields 40-50% fines (Zapiski Gornogo Instituta, 2022).
DEFECT | MINOR | The volume and bluff-length arithmetic depend on factors that are not stated. | 60 t/h × 24 h × 100 d = 144 kt, so 80 kt implies 56% utilisation. A 1.5 m hole at 62% recovery needs 2.42 m spacing, so 421 holes use 1.02 km of bluff, not 0.9.
DEFECT | MINOR | Auger mining itself is not new (Soviet and Ukrainian BShK-2DM and AVSh rigs); only the barge mounting is. | Kuzbass and Donbass auger practice; USGS auger planning rates.
SURVIVES | 190 t per 80 m hole matches a 1.50-1.53 m auger at 1.30-1.35 t/m³; the 1.56-2.06x and 3.9-6.4x ratios reproduce; the 0.9 m webs carry ρgH/(1−0.62) = 1.0-1.55 MPa at 20-30 m of cover, well within short-term frozen-coal strength; 150-300 kN of thrust against about 360 kN of spud capacity is fairly called "marginal".
