IDEA 1: Thermal Drift-and-Drop Placer Caving
DEFECT | FATAL | The window "stays open while worked, caves afterwards" does not exist: the 25 m working span is the failure span at the 30-second strength (1 MPa), and over the real 2.3–77 working days the strength has already fallen most of the way to its long-term value | Clamped beam σ=γL²/2t, γ=20 kN/m³, t=6 m: 1 MPa gives 24.5 m, so the factor of safety at 25 m is 0.96. Vyalov's law σ=β/ln(t/B), fitted to the idea's own 1 MPa (30 s) and 0.2–0.5 MPa (long-term), gives 0.27–0.67 MPa at 2.3–77 d. Safe span is then 13–20 m against the 11–18 m it must exceed to cave: a window of 1.8–2.6 m, about the ±1–2 m span scatter from an assumed ±20% strength scatter. A 1.5 m summer active layer with no tensile strength cuts the 1 MPa span to 18.4 m.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Mining with summer thaw water under a shallow (6–15 m) frozen roof is exactly what Russian practice avoids: small shallow underground placers are worked mainly in winter because summer thaw of the frozen host rock destabilises workings, and warm water in the room heats the roof toward 0 °C | Heat penetration √(4αt), α=1e-6 m²/s: 0.9 m at 2.3 d, 1.6 m at 7 d, 3.2 m at 30 d, 5.2 m at 77 d into a 6–15 m roof (SB RAS, sibran.ru; chamber parameters are set by RD 06-326-99).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The drift advance rate does not fit the water season: at field thaw rates a drift takes about as long as or longer than the ~100–120-day positive-water season, so part-worked rooms stand open through 8 winter months, the long-term regime in which they are designed to fail | 30–60 m ÷ 0.15–1 m/d = 30–400 d per drift; widening 2→25 m takes a further 2.3–77 d.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Wrong baseline: underground placer mining (3,500–7,000 rub/g) is not used at 10 m of cover. Against open cut the gain is 1.18–2.06x, and it falls to 0.82–1.86x if recovery is 10–30% lower: 0.3–1 MPa jets cannot clean frozen bedrock, and pay left on the floor is buried by the dropped roof. That is not "several times" | 2,270/1,930=1.18, 3,980/1,930=2.06; at 70–90% relative recovery 0.82–1.86x. The grade and recovery behind 1,930 rub/g are not stated.
DEFECT | MINOR | The low end of the water figure (3 m³ per m³ of pay) cannot carry the thaw heat | Thaw heat is 67–98 MJ/m³ (20–30% ice plus warming from −3 °C). With water cooling 4–7 K and 60% heat use this needs 3.8–9.8 m³/m³; 3 m³ would need ΔT ≥5.3–7.8 K at 100% heat use.
DEFECT | MINOR | Fuel at 60 rub/m³ is below the pumping energy of a hydro-elevator (η≈0.2) lifting slurry at 8–12% solids through ~20 m of equivalent head, plus the monitor water | 1.5–1.9 L of diesel per m³ = 117–205 rub/m³ at 80–110 rub/L.
DEFECT | MINOR | The trench equals 9% of overburden only with 60 m drifts on both sides of the trench | Trench floor 4–8 m wide, walls 1:0.5, 10 m deep: 7–10% with 60 m drifts on both sides, 14–34% with 30 m drifts.
DEFECT | MINOR | The geomechanics are worked out for 6 m of cover, where by the idea's own statement it has no gain; at the 10 m value case the numbers differ, and 62 m³ per metre cannot be reproduced | At t=10 m: 14–22 m long-term and 32 m short-term spans. 62 ≈ 10 m × 5 m × 1.24 swell, but the toppling geometry is not given.
SURVIVES | The 11–18 m and ~25 m beam spans reproduce (clamped beam, γ=20 kN/m³, t=6 m); the 1.2–3.6x ratio arithmetic reproduces.

IDEA 2: Channel-Talik Seam Dredging
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Kovin cross-check is false: Kovin's ~67 t/h comes from a 750 kW bucket-wheel cutter on a ~10 MW dredge, not a 150 kW cutter. Scaled by cutter power, 150 kW gives ~13 t/h, so a 10 kt year takes 31–52 dredge-days and one dredge serves about 1 bend a season, not 4 | 4,400,863 t ÷ 66,056 h = 66.6 t/h (Serbian 2024 paper); Kovin I has a 750 kW cutter and 10,090 kW total (DredgePoint); 66.6/750×150 = 13.3 t/h. The claimed 46–65 m³/h implies 8.3–11.7 MJ/m³ = 1.2–2.3×UCS, which only ideal sharp-tool cutting reaches, and no test backs it.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 32% total moisture after slurry transport and a dewatering screen is impossible for a true lignite: with 10–15% surface moisture, the coal's own (bed) moisture would have to be ≤20–24% (brown-coal class B3), whereas classes B1/B2 are 30–>40% | Totals of 37–49% give LHV 2,640–3,400 kcal/kg (dry ash-free GCV 6,800, 12% ash), raising rub/Gcal by ×1.08–1.39. Soviet classes: B1 >40%, B2 30–40%, B3 <30%.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The June–July window cannot hold 4 bends: these rivers are ice-free only from early June, and break-up brings ice jams and floods | Kolyma is under ice ~250 d/yr and ice-free only from early June (Britannica, Wikipedia). 4 × (8–25 d + 3 d transit) = 44–112 d; at 25 d per bend only 1–2 bends fit in 45–61 d, so 730–930 rub/Gcal holds only at the low end of the dredge-day range.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Fresh brown coal sits in stockpiles through the warm Aug–Sep months before freeze-up, brown coal is the rank most prone to self-heating, and no storage limit or fire control is costed | Brown coal in open storage starts to self-ignite after ≥2 weeks (TPU); the plan stores it ≥60 d before freeze-up.
DEFECT | MINOR | The headline 3.0–3.9x uses only the shared-dredge subset and compares fuel heat, not useful heat, and the 15k rub/t baseline is one point in a sourced 3.5–25k rub/t range | 2,830/(730–1,170) = 2.4–3.9x. With boiler efficiency 0.50–0.58 for wet lignite against 0.60–0.65 for 5,300 kcal/kg coal it is 2.0–3.5x. Delivered coal costs 3.5–25k rub/t (E3S 2020), and the ratio scales linearly with that price.
DEFECT | MINOR | A screen separates by size, not density, so channel gravel cut along with the seam ends up in the product as ash; "the river already removed the cover" ignores bedload lying on the seam | 0.1–0.5 m of gravel (1.9 t/m³) over a 2 m seam (1.25 t/m³) = 8–38% gravel by mass.
DEFECT | MINOR | Tonnage and day counts are internally inconsistent | 10 kt at 46 t/h takes 9.1 d at 100% utilisation, so "8 days" is impossible; 46–65 m³/h of in-place lignite at 1.15–1.3 t/m³ is 53–84 t/h, not 46–65; the 1.4–2.4x against a micro-pit cannot be reproduced because the micro-pit cost is not given.
DEFECT | MINOR | Hazards and logistics are left out. Dredging at the toe undercuts frozen bluffs 28–156 m high. The Yana, Indigirka and Kolyma connect only by sea, so a river dredge's 4 bends must all lie on one river. Fishery and turbidity permits are needed | Bluff height = 15–25 m³/t × 1.25 t/m³ × 1.5–5 m seam = 28–156 m.
SURVIVES | 2,700–4,300 ÷ 3.67 = 736–1,172 rub/Gcal and 15,000/2,830 = 5.3 Gcal/t reproduce; 3,670 kcal/kg at 32% moisture is consistent with dry ash-free GCV 6,300–7,200 and 8–15% ash; the cutting energy is within 1.2–2.3×UCS.

IDEA 3: Wintering Barge Bunker Swap
DEFECT | FATAL | The costed berth has 1.2 m of water, less than the measured end-of-winter ice, so the backwater freezes to the bed. The hull is then frozen to the bottom, the buoyancy and moat logic no longer applies, and the hull must ride out break-up and flood while frozen in. A hull that stays afloat needs ≥2.6–3.0 m of autumn depth, which by the idea's own figures means dredging (+2,226 rub/t, so it loses money) | Mean end-of-April ice at the river mouths (AARI): Indigirka/Chokurdakh 167 cm, Yana/Yubileynaya 152 cm, Kolyma/Chersky 142 cm, against 120 cm of water. That leaves −0.22 to −0.47 m under the ice even before the winter water-level drop. Kolyma break-up comes "with vast ice jams and widespread flooding" (Britannica).
DEFECT | MAJOR | It is not several times cheaper: avoided cost over own cost is 0.8–2.5x (net −260 to +2,040 rub/t), and 66–68% of the avoided cost is the unsourced 5–15% stockpile loss | At 15k rub/t that loss is 750–2,250 of the 1,100–3,400 rub/t; with a 1–2% loss the ratio is 0.37–1.07x.
DEFECT | MAJOR | One 660 t hull holds only 25–51% of a 2.4 MW boiler's yearly coal, so the shore stockpile, loader and trucks stay in use and their fixed costs are not avoided. Full cover needs 2–4 hulls, each with its own 55 kW moat | 2.4 MW peak, design −45…−48 °C, season mean −17…−19 °C, 290–305 d, efficiency 0.6–0.7, 5,300 kcal/kg → 2,150–2,660 t/yr (1,290–1,600 t/yr at a 1.44 MW real peak). 2–4 moats alone take 4.6–9.2% of boiler output (6.8–13.7% with hold heat), not 3.4%.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Moving coal 150 m from the hull to the boiler at −40…−50 °C is not costed, nor is piping 82 kW of heat back to the hull (itself a small heat main), and any such capital cost outweighs the whole 1,360 rub/t | Each 10 M rub of capital cost (15 yr, 12%, capital-recovery factor 0.147) adds 2,225 rub/t at 660 t/yr.
DEFECT | MINOR | 27 kW of hold heat cannot keep the coal from freezing into one mass against the hull at −40 °C unless the deck and freeboard are insulated, and no way of drawing coal from a sealed hold is given | Heat loss from a 1,290 m² deck plus 423 m² of freeboard at −40 °C is 40–205 kW (U_deck 0.45–2, U_side 1–6 W/m²K).
DEFECT | MINOR | The load figures use the 1,500 t rating, not the 660 t actually carried, and the inerting step contradicts itself: drying does not remove O₂, and by the idea's own 0.3 kW figure plain air ingress is harmless | 1,500 t over a 184–188 m perimeter = 78–80 kN/m; 660 t = 34–35 kN/m (ice stress 0.53–0.66 MPa, about the flexural strength). 1,800 m³ ≈ 1,500 t/0.85 t/m³, while drawing 660 t frees ~780 m³.
SURVIVES | Oxidation by 1,800 m³ of air reproduces at 0.24–0.44 kW (self-heating negligible at −30 °C); (55+27)/2,400 = 3.4%; 660 t at 1.2 m draft is conservative for a 75×17.2 m hull (~800 t).

IDEA 4: Ice-Deck Borehole Talik Mining
DEFECT | MAJOR | A 9 m reach for an underwater jet in gravel is not credible without megawatt-scale pumping: at 9 m the jet's centreline speed is 1.2–3.4 m/s, against 1.6–2.8 m/s needed to move 5–15 cm gravel. Reaching it takes ≥10–15 MPa and 237–419 m³/h, and the jet's fuel alone costs 0.24–3x the whole stated cost per m³. Cut gravel also has to travel 9 m to the intake over a floor sloping only 6–18° (pay 1–3 m), below its ~30–35° angle of repose | Jet decay u_c = 6.2·d/x·u₀; 0.66–1.74 MW of hydraulic power = 475–3,454 rub/m³ of fuel at 20–40 m³/h, against 1,130–1,970 rub/m³ total.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The ice margin rests on Gold's formula, which is for moving loads, yet the modules stay parked for days beside an open cased hole on partly flooded ice | NWT DOT 2015: Gold's guidance is "not appropriate" for stationary loads and drill pads. Westergaard's edge-to-interior factor of 0.66 cuts 57 t to 38 t; counting the flooded layer at 50% cuts it to 26 t, against 20–25 t modules (safety factor 1.0–1.3). Each hole takes 1.6–11 d.
DEFECT | MAJOR | On an 18 m square grid, R=9 m circles leave 21.5% of each cell outside any jet, so recovery per unit area is at most 68–70%, not 86–89%; the rest is left behind unless spacing drops to 12.7 m, which doubles the number of holes | π/4 = 0.785; 0.785 × 0.86–0.89 = 0.68–0.70; (18/12.73)² = 2.0.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline ignores draining the lake and dry-mining the talik, which is already thawed and needs no thaw energy. Where the cover is thin enough to strip, the open-cut cost used in Idea 1 overlaps this idea's cost | 2,270–3,980 rub/g against 3,000–4,800 rub/g gives 0.47–1.33x.
DEFECT | MINOR | "4.4x" does not follow from the stated alternative | ≥8,800 rub/g against 3,000–4,800 gives 1.8–2.9x; 4.4x would need an unstated 13,200 rub/g.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 86–89% footprint recovery and D=3.4–3.9 are model outputs that jig monitoring cannot verify: the gold in place under each footprint is unknown, and the stop rule acts only after daily jig clean-ups | Reconciling to ±10% at 90% confidence with grade CV≈1 needs n=(1.645·1/0.1)² ≈ 270 independent samples. A day's lag lets a hole overdraw 480–960 m³ (24 h × 20–40 m³/h) against 1,100–3,800 m³ drawn per hole.
DEFECT | MINOR | 74–78% of what is drawn is waste returned under the ice of a fishery lake during the winter oxygen minimum; turbidity and permit costs are not counted | Pay share of drawn material = 0.86–0.89 ÷ 3.4–3.9 = 22–26%.
SURVIVES | 90 MJ = 25 kWh; Gold with A=3.5 gives 50/113 t short-term and 25/57 t halved for 1.2/1.8 m ice; 57 m is 2.6–3.0 ice characteristic lengths, enough to keep loads from interacting; the cost-per-gram range reproduces with an implied 1.3–1.6 g recovered per m³ of pay; 1.6–11 d per hole fits a ~100-day ice season.
