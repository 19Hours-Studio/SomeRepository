=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Carbonyl nickel rides gas pipelines
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: nickel goes to market as Ni(CO)4 vapour carried in a trunk gas line's own flow, and is split back to nickel pellets at the gas plant where the line ends. The repair caps the dose at 0.04 mol%, not 0.1 mol%. At 9.8 MPa a 0.1% dose condenses below about -13 °C, while 0.04% stays at 70% of saturation at -20 °C. That still carries about 37 kt Ni a year on a 38 bcm line. The carbonyl is pumped in as a liquid (3.4 kg/s), the way methanol is injected now, together with 0.15 mol% CO, which keeps it thermodynamically stable up to 80 °C in compressor stations. At the gas plant a recuperated decomposer at 180-200 °C splits out 99.97% of it. It burns 0.12% of the line's gas and costs about $440/t Ni. Carbon guard beds are dropped because activated carbon is poor at holding Ni(CO)4. The best feed is a sulfur-free Ni-Fe material such as awaruite or a pre-reduced calcine, because then there is no SO2 to handle. Injection should be as close to the plant as possible, so the toxic section of line stays short.
VALUE: Moving the nickel costs about $7/t Ni in pipeline share against $1,067/t Ni by truck and rail (about 150x cheaper), but the whole chain is only 1.0-1.8x cheaper than selling concentrate for sulfide feed and 2.0-2.35x for awaruite-type feed.

IDEA 2
NAME: Freeze-Engine Vitriol Winning
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: freezing the stored copper leach liquor both powers the plant (the heat released by freezing boils propane, which drives a turbine and condenses in -35 to -45 °C air) and pushes CuSO4·5H2O out of solution. Corrected numbers: the engine gives 12 kJ of electricity per kg of ice at -35 °C air, 15 at -40 °C and 19 at -45 °C, not the claimed 15-30. Dilute liquor (2.5 g/L Cu, 80% recovery) needs 494 kg of ice per kg Cu. That covers 80-104% of the electrowinning power only in -35 to -40 °C air. Ice grown in place on panels in the pond would need 2.55 km² of panels and fails. The repaired design boils propane directly in the liquor (direct-contact crystallizer), separates ice in a wash column, and condenses in air-cooled condensers. For 10 kt Cu a year that is 127 MW of heat, 4.5 MW of electricity and $63M of capital. Iron and acid set how far the liquor can be concentrated, so the process is limited to oxide-ore liquor that is low in iron (Fe ≤ 0.5-1 g/L, acid ≤ 2-3 g/L, iron kept ferric). Under those conditions 80-90% of the copper crystallises each winter.
VALUE: About $1,090/t Cu against $1,420/t Cu for solvent extraction plus electrowinning on diesel power at a remote Arctic heap leach, so roughly 1.3x cheaper, not several times.

IDEA 3
NAME: Self-Charging Frost Separator Cascade
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: falling grains charge themselves on the chute lining, and the bins that collect them, wired across to the opposite plates, drive the separating field with no power supply, like a Kelvin water dropper. Many stages are stacked down a slope. Grains charged to 5% of the physical maximum (about 10 µC/kg at 300 µm) drift 30-60 cm sideways while falling between 1.5 m plates. A 1 m wide stage self-charges to 45 kV in about 1.4 s, and it does so even with 10^10 Ω of leakage. A passive corona regulator replaces the spark gap. The repair concerns humidity. Grains at -40 °C air temperature sit at about 90% relative humidity, so the original "dry cold air" claim was wrong. The cascade is therefore enclosed and ventilated with outdoor -40 °C air warmed by the grinding mill's heat (about 690 m³ per tonne of ore). At 0 °C that air is at 1.9% relative humidity, the same dryness as preheating the feed to about 85 °C in an ordinary plant. With middlings recycled, 9 stages that each send 85% of the valuable mineral and 45% of the waste forward lift spodumene ore from 1.2% to 6.1% Li2O at 82% recovery. The circulating load is 7.2x the feed and the total height is about 27 m.
VALUE: About $27/t ore including haul of the 16% mass concentrate, against $105/t to haul raw ore 700 km by winter road (3.9x cheaper), but only about 1.2-1.6x cheaper than an Arctic flotation plant plus haul.

IDEA 4
NAME: Night-sky lithium freeze cascade
MECHANISM: Anchor tested as stated: trays under film that radiates through the 8-13 µm atmospheric window are pushed below the CaCl2-ice eutectic (about -50 °C), so CaCl2 hexahydrate and ice lock up together and LiCl stays liquid. At -50 °C that band carries only 30 W/m² (21% of blackbody). With no brine load at all the film settles only 7.8 K below -40 °C air. At -52 °C it removes no net heat unless the air is already -45 °C or colder. At Udachny (January mean -39.4 °C) that gives about 273 usable hours, which freeze about 55 kg of brine per m² each winter. That means 11,600 m² of trays per tonne of lithium carbonate equivalent (LCE) per year, about $55,000/t LCE. Above the eutectic, ambient cold concentrates Li only 1.0-1.55x, so the anchor cannot be rescued at scale. The only workable niche drops the calcium part of the anchor: the same trays freeze sorbent eluate that is free of calcium (LiCl only, eutectic -75 °C) with plain -40 °C air. That concentrates it about 30x using roughly 100 m² per t LCE/yr, about $500/t LCE.
VALUE: As stated the idea is about 10x more expensive than the whole sorption route ($3.4-6.2k/t LCE), so it fails; only the eluate-freezing niche, which does not use the eutectic, is cheaper than the evaporation step it would replace.

=== NOTES ===
IDEA 1 (Carbonyl nickel). Anchor kept: yes.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Ni(CO)4 vapour pressure from the NIST correlation via Chemeo: 18.5 kPa at 0 °C, 11.3 at -10 °C, 6.6 at -20 °C.
  - Power of Siberia per Gazprom: 9.8 MPa, 38 bcm/yr. That is 1.58e12 mol/yr, so 0.04% carries 37 kt Ni/yr.
  - Stability, from NIST/CRC thermodynamic data (ΔH -160.8 kJ/mol, ΔS -410 J/molK): CO needed is 0.05% at 60 °C and 0.12% at 80 °C.
  - Decomposer: 395 MW recuperated duty, 43 MW net firing (0.12% of throughput).
- Cost (carbonyl route vs selling concentrate):
  - Selling concentrate costs $5.0-6.0k/t Ni: payability 70-76% (Centaurus Jaguar 2024; CRU via RNC) plus $1,067 freight. Freight assumes 338 km of road (Kun-Manie, Mining Weekly) and 2,500 km of rail at an assumed $0.12 and $0.035 per t·km.
  - Carbonyl route with assumed capital of $8-18k per annual tonne: $3.4-5.0k for sulfide feed, $2.3-2.8k for awaruite-type feed.
- Hazards:
  - 400 ppm Ni(CO)4 is as toxic as about 1% H2S (NIOSH IDLH 2 vs 50 ppm), so the line falls under sour-gas rules.
  - The decomposer must run continuously.
  - CO carried through the line ends up in the gas plant's nitrogen/fuel fraction.
  - Pipe steel can in principle form Fe(CO)5: thermodynamically allowed, kinetically slow below 60 °C at under 1 bar CO, but it needs monitoring.
  - Roasting sulfide feed gives SO2, which must be reduced to elemental sulfur using the line's methane (about $400/t Ni).
  - H2S in the gas makes Ni(CO)4 foul surfaces, so only sweet gas works.
- Closest existing work: Mond/Inco carbonyl refining and vapour-phase transport theory, and metal-carbonyl guard beds (US6165428, Clariant ActiSorb 400). I found no case of carbonyl being transported in a gas line.
- Verdict: NICHE. The physics works. "Several times cheaper" holds for the transport step only. No Russian nickel deposit currently sits on a trunk line that ends at a gas plant.

IDEA 2 (Freeze engine). Anchor kept: yes.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Engine output 12-19 kJ/kg ice, consistent with glaciothermal-engine literature: Smith (Univ. Tasmania thesis) reports 9-11% net efficiency at colder Antarctic sites; Lock 1989 (Arctic 42:253) reports about 5%.
  - 494 kg ice per kg Cu at 2.5 g/L, with CuSO4 solubility 14.3 g/100 g at 0 °C (CRC).
  - Electrowinning assumed at 2.0 kWh/kg.
- Cost: diesel power at 10-40 RUB/kWh (ipolytech); I used $0.33/kWh. The freeze route comes to $1,090/t Cu against $1,417/t for solvent extraction plus diesel electrowinning, which uses an assumed $750/t for the solvent-extraction plant (capital and running costs). Equipment costs are also assumed: crystallizer $1,200 per m³/day, air-cooled condenser $130/kWth.
- Hazards: propane inventory (fire); ferrous iron co-crystallising as melanterite unless it is oxidised; acid building up in the bleed brine; pond liner and permafrost thaw under the pond.
- Closest existing work: glaciothermal and Arctic heat engines (power only); IMWA 2018 freeze treatment of copper leachate (water recovery, not copper); Chilean solar-evaporation chalcanthite from leach liquor (78% Cu); Udokan's conventional leach-SX-EW, started September 2026.
- Verdict: NICHE, about 1.3x cheaper. It works only with dilute, low-iron oxide-ore liquor and air at -40 °C or colder.

IDEA 3 (Frost separator cascade). Anchor kept: yes, with the dryness mechanism corrected.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Maximum grain charge from the Gauss limit (ε0 × 3 MV/m): 148-590 µC/kg.
  - Drift from a drag model: 30-60 cm.
  - Self-charging time: 1.35 s.
  - Relative humidity from ice vapour pressure (Murphy-Koop): 90% for grains at -40 °C, 1.9% for outdoor air warmed to 0 °C. Free-fall separation of feldspar from quartz is known to need the feed preheated above 100 °C (Peretti & Zucca), so dryness is the controlling variable.
  - 9 stages if each stage enriches at least 1.9x; at 1.6x per stage, 25 stages are needed.
- Cost: assumptions are $40k per metre-wide stage, $0.15 per t·km haul, and $25/t ore for an Arctic flotation plant. The cascade gives 3.9x against hauling raw ore and 1.6x against flotation, falling to about 1.2x after a recovery penalty of about $14/t ore.
- Hazards: dust explosions mean no coal or sulfide ores; ozone and NOx from corona; high-voltage safety; fine dust; ice released from permafrost ore.
- Closest existing work: free-fall and STET belt tribo-electric separators (external high voltage); the Mekhanobr-Tekhnika friction-charged drum (particles still charged by a high-voltage source). I found no separator powered by its own collected grains.
- Verdict: WORKS against hauling raw ore for coarse-liberating non-sulfide ores (spodumene, feldspar/quartz, fluorite/calcite, cleaning cassiterite). NICHE against flotation. The per-stage selectivity is assumed and must be measured.

IDEA 4 (Night-sky lithium). Anchor kept: tested and failed; the niche drops the CaCl2-eutectic half.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Planck integration: 30 W/m² in the 8-13 µm band at -50 °C. The polar penalty is confirmed by arXiv 2410.04109.
  - 55 kg brine/m² per winter; 11,600 m² per t LCE/yr.
  - Brine: Siberian brines carry up to 0.6 g/L Li (Gornyi Zhurnal 2012); I used 0.4 g/L.
- Cost: about $55k/t LCE against $3.4-6.2k/t for the whole sorption route (Argonne).
- Hazards: a LiCl·CaCl2·5H2O double salt can trap the lithium (J. Chem. Eng. Data 2019); the crust traps brine; the meltwater must be reinjected; large areas of film to manage.
- Closest existing work: cryogenic brine metamorphism studies in Siberian permafrost and freeze desalination. I found no radiative-cooled eutectic lithium process.
- Verdict: FAILS. The eluate-freezing niche is roughly $500/t LCE.
