=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Under-Ice Brine-Thawed Tin Dredging
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: seawater keeps the shelf tin placer unfrozen, so a dredge can dig it with no thawing, stripping or roads. The summer-only version has already been tried twice. Sevmorolovo worked Vankina Bay in the late 1970s and stopped because waves upset its onboard gravity plant; the BIMA dredge at Nome (1986-90) lost money. Repaired design: the same dredge also works December to June in a covered slot under the stationary landfast ice of the SE Laptev Sea, where there are no waves or storms. That raises effective days from about 58 to about 214 a year. Holding the slot open takes 0.65 MW against 2.25 MW of diesel waste heat. Ice ahead (0.17 m³ per m³ of ground) is sawn and lifted out, because melting it would need 4.3 MW. Work is limited to water deeper than about 4 m and at least 0.3-1 km offshore, where ice-bonded permafrost lies below the 17 m dig depth (Buor-Khaya borehole: 28 m down at 800 m offshore).
VALUE: For a 600 m³/h dredge, cost falls from about $126k/t Sn in the open-water-only mode to $37k/t at 0.5 kg/m³ and $23k/t at 0.8 kg/m³, 3-5× cheaper than the known seasonal mode. It beats PT Timah's offshore cost ($14-22k/t) only if the shelf grade is at least 0.85 kg/m³.

IDEA 2
NAME: Self-Detaching Thaw-Face Stripping
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: excess pore pressure at the thaw front of ice-rich muck carries the thawed layer downslope into the mined cell by gravity. The mechanism checks out: R = α/(2√cv) is 0.9-1.6, which puts the frictional sliding threshold at about 2-5°. The original layout fails, though: with 0.5-2 kPa of cohesion, a whole surface graded at 5-8° only slides once the sheet is 1.9-12 m thick, more than the 1.6 m that thaws in a season. Repaired design: cut long, south-facing faces at 10-12° and sprinkle them with creek water. Sheets 0.2-0.9 m thick then detach several times a season, which keeps the face bare and thawing about 3 m per season. Each metre of face yields 115-138 m³ per season, so 0.5 Mm³ needs about 4 km of face. The slurry (yield stress under 500 Pa) runs down the roughly 4° drop into the adjacent mined cell and settles to 0.64 of its frozen volume. Stripping has to start 4-9 seasons ahead of mining.
VALUE: Stripping drops from about $1.0/m³ (natural thaw plus dozing) to about $0.3/m³, 3× cheaper. That cuts delivered gold cost by about 15% in ordinary muck and by up to about 40% in Yedoma (over 60% ice), where dozers bog down and the alternative is digging frozen ground at about $3.5/m³.

IDEA 3
NAME: Frozen-In Barge Auger Drift Mining
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: horizontal augers from a barge at the bank bore the frozen paystreak and leave the frozen overburden in place as roof. The ground holds for a while: under 10-20 m of cover the pillars between holes carry 0.9-1.8 MPa against 5-8 MPa short-term strength. This holds only if tailings are pumped back and freeze in each hole within weeks. Two design fixes are needed. Round holes centred in the gravel miss the gold on bedrock, so the hole centre is set 0.3-0.5 m above bedrock; that sweeps 65-74% of the bedrock surface but dilutes the feed with 0.4-0.6 m of bedrock, and only 0.61 of the seam is extracted. A 130-day open-water season gives only 70-100k m³ at $68-96/m³, so the barge is frozen into a notch in the bank and run for 280 days with a heated plant (215k m³ at $45-55/m³). The target becomes deep river-cut placers (30-40 m cover) with at least 1.8 m of pay, gold spread through the gravel and clasts under 0.4 m, which are now drift-mined or left in the ground.
VALUE: Even repaired it costs about $36/g at 3 g/m³, against $23/g for frozen open pit and an estimated $29/g for drift mining. It is not cheaper, so it is only a niche for deep placers that open pits cannot reach.

IDEA 4
NAME: Re-Floatable Pond-Moored Mine Plants
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: the concentrator, power house and camp float on hulls built in a shipyard, in a pond dug at the site, so the talik their heat creates does not matter. The pond is 6 m deep (2.2 m of maximum ice, 3 m of draft and a margin). Waste heat keeps a 2 m moat open around each hull against ice pressure, using 0.9-1.6 MW of the roughly 35 MW the power house rejects. Mills stand on bedrock 20-50 m back from the edge to allow for 0.5-2 m/yr of bank retreat. The hulls (130×14 m, 3 m draft, about 2.9 kt payload each) fit the 135×14.3 m White Sea-Baltic canal locks. Against the best current practice, a modular plant on piles, a cold-permafrost site gains nothing (capex +4%). The anchor pays off in one regime: warm, ice-rich ground where foundations are 20-25% of capex, cheap $3k/t barge hulls, and short-lived (5-7 year) riverside or coastal deposits. There the plant is re-floated and towed to the next deposit, keeping 45-55% of its value.
VALUE: In that regime capex is 15-19% below a modular plant on piles and delivered cost 10-14% lower. On ordinary cold permafrost the gain is about 0%.

IDEA 1. Anchor kept. A literature check showed the plain idea is already known: [Sevmorolovo](https://goarctic.ru/news/gornodobyvayushchaya-promyshlennost-arkticheskoy-yakutii/) used the dredge-lighter "Gornyak" with an onboard plant on the Chokurdakh placer (reserves about 19 kt Sn). An [eyewitness account](https://www.drive2.ru/b/593631276712331195/) says it stopped because waves upset the gravity separation. [BIMA at Nome](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1988/07/10/Mining-the-Bering-Sea-for-gold/1019584510400/) was unprofitable. Under-ice operation is the version I did not find on record.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Ice-bonded permafrost at 28 m, 800 m offshore, degrading about 3 cm/yr ([Overduin, AWI](https://epic.awi.de/id/eprint/34557/)).
  - SE Laptev has the most extensive landfast ice in the Arctic ([AWI](https://epic2-clone.awi.de/id/eprint/39273)).
- Best-practice comparison, per tonne of tin delivered:
  - PT Timah: $13.7-22.3k/t ([ITRI](https://www.internationaltin.org/pt-timah-profits-and-production-down-in-2011/)); its ground grades 0.4-0.5 kg/m³ and dredging plus concentration costs under $1.10/m³ ([MRT](https://mrt.stgro-2780.saas.squiz.cloud/mrtdoc/tasxplor/download/04_5020/EL322001_200404_10_app01.pdf)).
  - Tin price about $30k/t ([2024 average](https://metalcharts.org/tin-price-history/2024)).
  - Onshore Tirekhtyakh tin (Yanolovo, 1,100 t in 2025): I found no cost figure.
  - Break-even grade for a year-round 600 m³/h dredge: 0.62 kg/m³ at the tin price, 0.85 kg/m³ to match Timah. The known open-water mode needs 2.1-2.9 kg/m³.
- Consistency: everything checks except the inner zone (under 0.5-1 km offshore or under 2 m of water), where the seabed freezes and is excluded. Ice carries the saw and crane from about December (50 t at 1.2 m thick).
- Hazards: lasting turbidity under the ice and harm to wintering fish; break-out at the fast-ice edge (stay in the inner bay); winter fire and evacuation.
- Deciding unknown: shelf grade beyond the 4 m depth line. Experiment: about 35 vibrocores from the ice on a 500 m grid, plus resistivity survey lines (n = (1.96×0.6/0.2)² ≈ 35 for ±20% on the mean), and one winter slot trial with a small dredge.
- Verdict: NICHE.

IDEA 2. Anchor kept.
- Decisive numbers:
  - Slope stability uses the McRoberts-Morgenstern infinite-slope method, and the claimed threshold holds only with no cohesion; low-angle slides are confirmed in the field ([review](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-916/egusphere-2026-916.pdf), [Fosheim, 2->40°](https://openpolar.no/Record/crwiley:10.1002%2Fppp.578)).
  - The Batagay slump retreats 5-15 m/yr and moves about 1 Mm³/yr ([Kizyakov 2024](https://epic.awi.de/58947/1/Kizyakov%20et%20al_2024_Characterizing%20Batagay%20megaslump%20topography%20dynamics%20and%20matter%20fluxes.pdf)). A vertical headwall gives only 80 m³ per m per year, which is why I used inclined faces.
- Best-practice comparison: natural thaw plus dozing ($1.0/m³, $12.6/g of stripping), hydraulic monitors ([Klondike practice](https://www.nps.gov/yuch/learn/historyculture/placer-mining.htm), about $0.6/m³), and frozen drill-and-blast with excavators ($3.5/m³, $44/g). The gravity face costs $0.3/m³, or $3.8/g.
- Consistency: everything passes at 10-12° and fails at 5-8°. Lead time (4-9 seasons) is the binding schedule window.
- Hazards: uncontrolled Batagay-type retrogression (stop it by leaving untouched ground as a buffer and burying the headwall), sediment release, carbon and methane from thawing Yedoma.
- Deciding unknown: cv and cohesion of the thawed muck and the slurry yield stress. Experiment: 18 test faces (8/10/12° × sprinkled or not × 3 replicates), with piezometers at the thaw front, over one summer.
- Closest work: natural thaw slumps, ground sluicing, layer-by-layer thawing.
- Verdict: WORKS, but modestly: the stripping line gets 3-10× cheaper, delivered cost falls 15-40%.

IDEA 3. Anchor kept.
- Decisive numbers: auger mining in coal reaches 1-2 m holes, up to 200 m long and 60 kt/month ([source](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/design-and-performance-of-highwall-mining/31810576)), with 24-35% recovery in the [Mae Tan](https://doi1.nrct.go.th/ListDoi/listDetail/10.14457%2FCU.the.2015.227) case.
- Novelty: frozen-roof room-and-pillar mining of placers is already standard practice ([RD 06-326-99](https://legalacts.ru/doc/instruktsija-po-razrabotke-mnogoletnemerzlykh-rossypei-podzemnym-sposobom-kamernye-i/)), and cutter machines for frozen gravel exist ([Mining Journal 2016](https://rudmet.net/media/articles/Article_MJ_09_16_pp.41-45_1.pdf)). The barge-auger itself I did not find.
- Best-practice comparison per m³ of pay at 10-20 m cover: open pit with thawing and dozing $9-14, frozen open pit $21-39, auger $45-96. Under 30 m cover, per gram at 3 g/m³: frozen open pit $23, drift mining $29 (estimate), auger $36.
- Consistency: pillars pass in the short term and are marginal long-term, so backfill is mandatory. Bedrock capture is only partial.
- Hazards: jammed auger strings, collapse of the thawed bank at the hole collar, spring ice drift.
- Deciding unknown: cutting rate and pick wear in frozen gravel. Experiment: a hired coal auger drills 5-10 holes of 30-50 m alongside a bulk-sample pit.
- Verdict: NICHE, close to FAILS on cost.

IDEA 4. Anchor kept.
- Precedents: floating concentrators in dredge ponds ([Moma, 3,000-3,500 t/h](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/moma-titanium-mine-wet-concentrator-plant-a-upgrade-and-transition-project-mozambique-update-2025-10-17), [relocated by Hatch](https://www.hatch.com/projects/metals-and-minerals/relocation-of-wet-concentrator-plant)), the Sevmorolovo accommodation ship, and floating power plants (Akademik Lomonosov, Severnoye Siyanie; K). A hard-rock plant frozen into a pond dug inland I did not find.
- Decisive number: foundations are 20-50% of the cost of a mill structure ([AIME](https://onemine.org/documents/mining-foundations-for-mill-construction-on-clay-and-permafrost)).
- Best-practice comparison is against a modular plant on piles, not stick-built: delivered cost ×1.017 on cold ground, ×0.955 on warm ice-rich ground, ×0.86-0.90 with re-floating and re-use.
- Consistency: pond depth, moat heat, mill setback and canal locks all pass.
- Hazards: hull fire, the plant as a single point of failure, thermokarst bank retreat, pond freezing or seepage.
- Deciding unknown: ice-hull loads and the yard cost of a hull-mounted plant. Experiment: freeze a surplus river barge into a 6 m pond for one winter with load cells and thermistors.
- Verdict: NICHE.
