PAIR 1
Shared move: the ground's own ice changing phase does the work that machines normally do. In the canal, thaw digs the channel. In dry-washing, frozen ice behaves as a light mineral that can be separated out. Either way, the trick is to choose when the ice is solid or liquid.
Offspring: let the sun-driven thaw front also do the gravity separation. Ice-rich placer ground with its overburden stripped is covered by a shallow meltwater pond, which absorbs sun. Each summer the top layer thaws into a weak, water-saturated slurry because its excess ice turns to water. Heavy grains such as gold, cassiterite or tungsten minerals sink through it and come to rest on the still-frozen floor. As the thaw front moves down year by year, it carries the heavy fraction down with it, the way a placer forms on bedrock over geological time, but in a few seasons. Only the bottom few decimetres are ever dug.
Closest existing work: natural placer enrichment on "false bedrock", and Klondike cold-water point thawing (that thaws ground for digging, not for separating). The mechanism here is different, so I kept it.

PAIR 2
Shared move: the frozen river is a sealed, windless duct with a free winter current, so the cargo needs no vehicle.
Offspring: remove the gas bladders, valves, sensors and relay slots. The cargo rides in rigid foam-shelled spheres. Their buoyancy is set to only about 0.5% above water, so the drag of the current is larger than their lift. When an obstacle hangs down from the ice, the current pushes the sphere down and around it, and on smooth ice it rolls along the underside instead of sliding.
Closest existing work: under-ice drifting oceanographic floats, pipeline pigs and log driving. None of these is a tuned freight body that dives under obstacles, so I kept it.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Thaw-Front Self-Panning Placers
MECHANISM: The overburden is stripped from an ice-rich placer, and a shallow pond of meltwater is kept on top so it absorbs round-the-clock summer sun. Each summer the top layer thaws, and its excess ground ice turns it into a weak, saturated slurry. Gold and other heavy minerals sink through the slurry and settle on the still-frozen floor. Year by year the advancing thaw front carries this heavy layer down to bedrock. Ground-temperature probes track how deep the front has gone, and the pond depth is adjusted to speed it up or slow it down. Then only the thin enriched bottom layer is dug and washed.
VALUE: Sunlight and gravity do most of the excavation and concentration, so a placer is mined by moving a few percent of the gravel that conventional digging moves.

IDEA 2
NAME: Drag-Diving Under-Ice Ore Spheres
MECHANISM: In winter, concentrate is packed into rigid spheres about 1 m across with closed-cell foam shells, which have no gas, valves or electronics. They are pushed through one slot cut in the ice of a large Siberian river's main channel. Each sphere's buoyancy is trimmed to roughly 0.5% above that of the water, so the drag of the current is larger than its lift. The sphere rolls along the smooth underside of the ice, and when it meets a hanging slush dam or an ice ridge, the current pushes it down and around. A boom under a slot at a downstream port collects the spheres, which are sent back upriver empty in summer.
VALUE: Ore moves hundreds of kilometres to the quay all winter with no fuel, vehicle or tending, at a time when nothing else moves on these rivers.
