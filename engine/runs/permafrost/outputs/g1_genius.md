**1. Transpolar Drift Freight**
- **Hard:** shipping by sea from the Siberian coast needs icebreakers. The unstated assumption is that ice is the obstacle, when it could be the vehicle.
- **Move (a), drop the assumption:** ice in the Transpolar Drift travels from the Laptev Sea to Fram Strait in 2–3 years, using no fuel and needing no infrastructure.
- **One sentence:** buoyant, satellite-tagged ore containers are frozen into Laptev fast ice and collected in open water off Svalbard.
- **Check:** "Of course. Nansen's *Fram* did exactly this." Being slow only costs interest, and holding inventory of bulk ore is cheap.
- **Closest existing work:** the *Fram*, MOSAiC and drifting buoys. Nobody has costed the drift as a freight route.

**2. Winter-Only Railway**
- **Hard:** the cost of an Arctic railway is mostly the embankment, culverts and bridges built to survive the summer thaw. The unstated assumption is that a railway must run all year.
- **Move (c), remove the permanent roadbed:** frozen tundra and river ice are rigid, flat foundations for five to six months a year.
- **One sentence:** track is laid straight on frozen tundra, crosses rivers on thickened ice, is re-levelled every autumn and runs only in winter.
- **Check:** steel wheels on steel rail need about a fifth of the energy that trucks need on ice roads. One track running for a few winter months moves millions of tonnes.
- **Closest existing work:** the Baikal ice railway of 1904 and portable field railways. Winter roads carry trucks, not trains.

**3. Frozen Placer Dry-Washing**
- **Hard:** frozen placer deposits (loose gravel carrying gold or tin) have to be thawed and washed with water, so mining stops for about eight months a year. The unstated assumption is that frozen gravel must be thawed first.
- **Move (b), it is already solved elsewhere:** at −30 °C water is just another solid mineral, so a frozen placer works like a dry desert placer, and desert placers are solved with dry-washers and air tables.
- **What does not map:** the ice binding the gravel has to be crushed, and fine particles stay locked in ice. Ice is brittle and cracks first, which helps.
- **One sentence:** frozen gravel is crushed in winter, gold is separated by air while everything stays below zero, and tailings are stacked frozen.
- **Check:** no energy for thawing, no ponds, and a mining season roughly twice as long.
- **Closest existing work:** desert dry-washing. Arctic placers have always been processed wet.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Transpolar Drift Freight
MECHANISM: Sealed, buoyant ore containers with satellite tags are set into Laptev Sea fast ice near the Lena delta. The Transpolar Drift carries the ice and containers to Fram Strait in 2–3 years without fuel or escort. When the floes melt, the containers float in open water and ships pick them up by their tag positions.
VALUE: Bulk ore can reach European waters with no icebreakers, paying only the cost of time.

IDEA 2
NAME: Winter-Only Railway
MECHANISM: Track panels are laid straight onto snow-compacted frozen tundra, with no permanent embankment, and rivers are crossed on artificially thickened ice. The line runs only during the 5–6 months when the ground is rigid. Each autumn, tamping machines re-level the track to undo the summer heave.
VALUE: Rail-scale bulk capacity reaches remote deposits for a fraction of the cost of an all-season Arctic railway.

IDEA 3
NAME: Frozen Placer Dry-Washing
MECHANISM: In deep winter, frozen placer gravel is dug and crushed while it stays below −20 °C. At that temperature the ice behaves as a brittle, light mineral. Air tables and dry-washers separate the gold and heavy minerals from gravel and ice by density, with no water at all, and the tailings are stacked frozen.
VALUE: Placer mining can run all year without thawing, water or tailings ponds.
