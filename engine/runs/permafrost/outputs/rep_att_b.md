=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Ice-Deck Borehole Talik Mining
MECHANISM: Anchor: drill from winter lake ice into the sub-lake talik, which is already thawed, and mine the pay by borehole hydraulic mining. That means no thaw (saves 90 MJ/m³, about 25 kWh/m³), no dike, no drainage and no stripping. Repair: 1.2 m of ice carries only ~25 t of parked load, so the ice is flood-thickened to ≥1.8 m (Gold's formula at A=3.5 kg/cm² gives 113 t short-term, ~57 t parked). The 20-25 t modules sit more than 57 m apart, and the casing is insulated where it passes through the ice. Loose saturated talik gravel holds no roof and caves into the cavity, so the method is limited to narrow (25-40 m), deep, fine-gravel buried channels (<15 cm, the intake limit). Holes go on an 18 m grid (jet reach R=9 m) and are airlifted at 20-40 m³/h. Drawing stops at D≈3.4-3.9 m³ drawn per m³ of pay, set by on-ice jig grade monitoring; this extracts 86-89% of the pay in each hole's footprint, and tailings go back down mined holes. Cost: 1,130-1,970 rub per m³ drawn, which is 3,000-4,800 rub/g of gold recovered.
VALUE: Recovers gold from narrow, deep sub-lake channels that today go unmined, at 3,000-4,800 rub/g. The cheapest alternative, a summer pontoon suction dredge, costs ≥8,800 rub/g, so this is 1.8-4.4x cheaper; on channels wider than 80 m it is only 1-1.5x cheaper.

IDEA 2
NAME: Frozen-Roof Jet Adits
MECHANISM: Anchor: an unmanned jet and hydro-elevator head on a hose drives 3 m adits along bedrock under intact frozen overburden. Pillars are jetted out on retreat so the roof settles, and only the pay is thawed and lifted. Repair: a realistic creek (6→1 °C, 100 L/s) gives only 2.1 MW, which is 21 m³/h, not 60. Adding a 2 ha black-lined recirculating settling pond (net 80-150 W/m²) brings the heat to 3.7-5.1 MW, which is 35-45 m³/h at 30% heat use (107 MJ/m³ of pay) over a 90-day summer. The adit must never flood, because 6 °C water thaws the roof 2-4 m/day. So adits are driven up-gradient, the hydro-elevator handles 1.3x the inflow, and the portal has an insulated curtain; there is no ventilation because nobody is inside. Advance of 3.7-8 m/h limits the roof's life to 25-45 h, with 3-5 cm of roof thaw, and pillar stress is 0.4-1.2 MPa against 1-2 MPa long-term strength for frozen gravel. Cost: 1,200-1,400 rub/m³ of pay including washing, which is 550-660 rub/g.
VALUE: For frozen placers under ≥20 m of cover it costs 550-660 rub/g, against ~1,040-1,670 rub/g for winter underground mining and 1,270-2,400 rub/g for open pit: 1.6-2.5x cheaper overall and 2-3.5x on the mining step alone. It loses to bulldozer open pits where cover is under 15 m.

IDEA 3
NAME: Channel-Talik Seam Dredging
MECHANISM: Anchor: a dredge cuts the coal seam in place inside the unfrozen channel talik, where the river has already removed the cover, and pumps it into a hopper barge. Repair: only seams at thalweg level under high bluffs, where a bank pit would have to strip 15-25 m³/t. A leased dredge with a 150 kW cutter cuts lignite (UCS 5-7 MPa) at 46-65 m³/h, which is about 46-65 t/h and matches Kovin's measured 67 t/h. A 10 kt village-cluster year therefore takes 8-25 dredge-days, not "a week", and one dredge visits about 4 bends a season. An on-barge dewatering screen brings the coal to ~32% moisture (3,670 kcal/kg). Cutting in June-July lets stockpiles drain before freeze-up. Delivered cost is 2,700-4,300 rub/t, which is 730-1,170 rub/Gcal.
VALUE: 730-930 rub/Gcal with a shared dredge, against 2,830 rub/Gcal for northern-delivery coal at 15k rub/t (3.0-3.9x cheaper) and 1.4-2.4x below a bank micro-pit under high bluffs. There is no gain where the same seam has thin cover on land.

IDEA 4
NAME: Wintering Coal CHP Barge
MECHANISM: Anchor: the bunker barge that brings the winter's coal stays frozen in at the village and burns its own cargo as a combined heat and power plant. Repair: a thermal-oil coal boiler (80%) feeds an ORC (16% net), run heat-led into the village heat network. The 1.54 MW of reject heat replaces boiler-house coal burned at 65%, so the net extra fuel per kWh_e is about 0. Electric-only would cost 12.7 rub/kWh_e at 10k rub/t; the original 20-30x claim compared kWh of heat from coal with kWh of electricity from diesel. A 0.3 MWe unit needs 1,780 t of coal per winter, which fits one 2,000 t barge, and the 381 m³ of ash fits the hold space freed as coal is burned. Reject heat of 104 kW keeps an ice moat open around the hull and 44 kW keeps the hold unfrozen. Capex dominates the cost: 19-32 rub/kWh at 0.3 MWe (132-237 M rub) and 14 rub/kWh at 1 MWe for district centres.
VALUE: Winter electricity at 14-32 rub/kWh against 45-75 rub/kWh full diesel cost (1.9-4.3x cheaper, up to 6x where costs reach 88 rub/kWh), with the heat nearly free.

=== NOTES ===
**IDEA 1 – Ice-deck borehole talik mining**
- **Anchor kept?** Yes.
- **Decisive numbers:**
  - Russian borehole mining (BHM) trials: 10-40 m³/h of solids and 1.2-3.5k m³ per well (TPU).
  - Tool capacity 30-90 m³/h, jet reach up to 10 m (Acta Montanistica 2004).
  - USBM/NIOSH borehole miner: 890 L/min at 6.9 MPa, 9 t/h, pump passes −15 cm gravel, placers carry up to 15% cobbles and boulders.
  - Gold's formula A=3.5-10 kg/cm², lower bound used (TAC/Barrette).
  - Gold price 12,088 rub/g (CBR, Jan 2026).
- **Deciding quantity:** dilution in a cave that cannot hold a roof. I modelled it as a mixing zone, P(V)=P0·exp(−V/(P0(1+c))). The cost of drawing material (1,130-2,600 rub/m³) is dominated by crew, not energy (154 rub/m³).
- **Baseline, per gram recovered (narrow channel W=40 m, 50 m cover):**
  - Drain the lake and open-pit: stripping ratio 117, 13,900 rub/g.
  - Summer pontoon suction dredge: 176 m³ lifted per m³ of pay, 8,800 rub/g (the cheapest alternative).
  - Borehole mining: 3,000-4,800 rub/g.
  - At W=80 m: 1.1-1.3x; at W≥150 m: ≤1x.
- **Consistency checks:**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Ice parked load | Fails at 1.2 m; passes at ≥1.8 m with modules >57 m apart (l=23 m) |
| Ice season (~100 days) vs work | 141 holes/team, 78 m drilled/day, 1.6 ha |
| Draw per hole | 1,280-1,490 m³, inside the TPU 1.2-3.5k range only with R=9 m; R=6 m under-uses holes |
| On-ice plant heat | +2 °C water carries 466 kW vs 30 kW tent loss: OK |
| Bare casing through ice | Melts 0.64 m³/day; insulated sleeve needed |
| Boulders | Fine-gravel channels only |
| Near-shore grounded ice | Holes >50 m off shore |

- **Hazards:**
  - Ice creep failure.
  - Oxygen depletion under ice from turbidity, hence a closed water loop and tailings sent down holes.
  - Lake-bed subsidence of 1-2 m.
  - About 15% gold loss in bedrock crevices.
  - Intake jamming.
- **Deciding unknown:** the cave-mixing ratio c (c=0.5 gives 3,000 rub/g; c=2 gives 6,000 rub/g).
- **Experiment:** 10 pilot holes on one lake in one ice season. Sample drawn solids every 20 m³ to get the grade-decay curve, and drill 3 cores around each hole after drawing (30 cores) to measure pay left behind. That pins c to about ±30%.
- **Closest work:** USBM borehole miner (frozen ground, 15 m); Russian BHM on Ti-Zr sands; Chukotka buried coastal-placer jetting and airlift (Gornyi Zhurnal 2019); drilling from lake ice for hydraulic tests (IMWA 2008). I found no production borehole mining from lake ice into a talik.
- **Verdict:** NICHE.

**IDEA 2 – Frozen-roof jet adits**
- **Anchor kept?** Yes. The heat source changed (creek plus solar pond) and the operating rules were added.
- **Decisive numbers:**
  - Low-order streams run about 4.7 K colder than big rivers in summer (Upper Kolyma thermal-regime study), so 9 K of usable cooling is not realistic.
  - Frozen-ground strength falls more than 3x from −18 to −1 °C. Underground placer mining is done only in winter because of thaw collapse (RD 06-326-99 literature).
  - Bulldozer stripping 80-90 rub/m³, dragline 27-29; washing ≈3x stripping (zolotodb.ru). I inflated these 1.5x to 2026 prices.
- **Roof thaw rates:** flooded 216-431 cm/day, splash zone 69 cm/day, air-filled 2.7 cm/day, open portal +16-29 W/m².
- **Baseline per gram (3 g/m³):**
  - Open pit: 459 rub/g at 10 m cover; 1,270-2,400 rub/g at 20-40 m.
  - Winter underground mining: 1,040-1,670 rub/g. This uses 2,500-4,000 rub/m³, which is my estimate; I found no source.
  - Jet adit: 550-660 rub/g.
- **Consistency checks:**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Heat available vs rate | Passes only with the pond |
| Roof life vs thaw | 25-45 h gives 3-5 cm: OK |
| Flooding | Must never happen; up-gradient drive and 482 m³/h pumping |
| Pillars | 0.4-1.2 MPa vs 1-2 MPa: OK only for lives under 2 days |
| Portal air | Curtain required |
| Warm-water season | Mid-June to mid-September, 90 days, only with the pond |

- **Hazards:**
  - Flooding leads to roof collapse onto the head (equipment loss only, since nobody is underground).
  - Surface subsidence of 1.3-1.6 m, leading to thermokarst ponding.
  - Hose entrapment.
  - Gold lost in pillars and bedrock (recovery 72% vs 87% open pit).
- **Deciding unknown:** the share of the water's heat that actually goes into the frozen face (η), plus roof ravelling.
- **Experiment:** 3 test adits of 30 m each at 2 water temperatures (6 runs), with roof thermistor strings every 5 m, measuring m³/h per MW.
- **Closest work:** Kuzbass underground hydraulic coal mines; Fairbanks cold-water thawing; USBM borehole miner; Rochev 2002 thesis on frozen-soil disintegration in water.
- **Verdict:** WORKS for cover ≥20 m (1.6-2.5x cheaper, not "several times" against underground mining); FAILS for shallow placers.

**IDEA 3 – Channel-talik seam dredging**
- **Anchor kept?** Yes.
- **Novelty:** dredging lignite underwater is already known. Kovin, Serbia produced 4.40 Mt in 66,056 h (67 t/h), and its cutting resistance underwater proved higher than lab tests. A Danube floating cutter dredge was proposed in 1989. The version not yet done: a permafrost channel talik as the only unfrozen ground the river has already stripped, a visiting leased dredge, and barges straight to the villages.
- **Decisive numbers:**
  - Lignite UCS 4.9-7.1 MPa (Ordos); weak coal 0.7-4.9 MPa; mechanical dredge cutting limit ~50 MPa (IADC/PIANC).
  - Lena-basin seams are lenticular, 1-10 m and up to 30 m thick (BSE), with northern deposits including Taymylyr and Chai-Tumus.
  - Delivered coal in Yakutia costs 3.5-25k rub/t (Tkach et al. 2020).
  - Arctic districts took about 88 kt from Dzhebariki-Khaya plus ~115 kt from another source in 2016.
- **Baseline per Gcal delivered:**
  - Northern delivery at 15k rub/t: 2,830 rub/Gcal.
  - Bank micro-pit (the category proposed by Fedorov 2026): 670-1,000 rub/Gcal at low cover; 1,230-1,840 at stripping 15-25 m³/t.
  - Dredge: 730-1,170 rub/Gcal.
- **Consistency checks:**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Cutting rate vs pump limit (122 m³/h) | OK |
| Dredge time vs open-water season (~110 days) | OK |
| Thalweg winter depth vs ~2 m ice | Must be greater, so the talik is really unfrozen; check per site |
| Ladder depth | ≤10-15 m |
| Low-water barge draft on tributaries | Part loads only |
| Moisture | 40% as dredged, 32% after screening |
| Bluff toe | 30-50 m standoff, 1:3 underwater slopes |

- **Hazards:**
  - Coal-fines plume in fish rivers; spawning windows may close the June-July slot.
  - Bank undermining.
  - Wet coal freezing into monoliths in village stockpiles.
  - Boiler derating on wet lignite.
  - Subsoil licensing inside a water body.
- **Deciding unknown:** whether usable seam exposures exist at the thalweg near Arctic villages.
- **Experiment:** sub-bottom profiler plus 3-5 cores at 10-15 candidate outside bends on known seams, then a 1-week cutting trial at the 2 best sites measuring t/h and fines loss.
- **Verdict:** NICHE (works where a high bluff blocks bank mining).

**IDEA 4 – Wintering coal CHP barge**
- **Anchor kept?** Yes. The fuel-ratio claim was corrected.
- **Decisive numbers:**
  - ORC electrical efficiency 17% (UK AHDB), 18% ORC and 15% plant at Lienz; about 5 units of heat per unit of power.
  - ORC capex 1,500-5,000 €/kW with O&M 2%/yr (Dürr Cyplan); UK £4-5k/kW.
  - Fuel is ~60% of Sakhaenergo's generation cost (50-80% elsewhere); some Yakutia districts reach 88 rub/kWh.
- **Assumptions I made:** diesel delivered at 90-150 rub/kg (Far East retail averages 51.7 rub/L); 0.30 kg/kWh; coal 5,300 kcal/kg; village boiler house 65%; availability 0.85.
- **Baseline per kWh delivered:**
  - Existing diesel: 45-75 rub/kWh full cost.
  - Solar or wind hybrids: little help in the polar winter.
  - ORC on an existing land boiler house: similar, plus the Arctic construction premium and double coal handling.
  - Barge: 14-32 rub/kWh.
- **Consistency checks:**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Coal per winter vs barge size | 1,780 t vs 2,000 t: OK |
| Ash vs freed hold | 381 m³ vs 2,091 m³: OK |
| Ice moat and hold heat vs reject heat | 148 kW vs 1.54 MW: OK |
| Heat sink | Needs a village of ≥~1,000 people to absorb 1.5 MW in shoulder months; 1 MWe needs about 3,500+ |
| Stay window | Mid-October to early June: OK |

- **Hazards:**
  - Ice pressure at freeze-up; break-up and ice-jam floods. A protected backwater harbour (zaton) is mandatory, and the plant disconnects before break-up.
  - Fire and CO aboard.
  - Flexible heat link across shore ice.
  - Regulation of the thermal-oil boiler.
- **Deciding unknown:** installed barge capex per kW and winter availability.
- **Experiment:** one containerized 0.2-0.3 MW ORC plus thermal-oil boiler on a riverside coal boiler house for one winter (n=1, ~4,700 logged hours).
- **Closest work:** Akademik Lomonosov floating nuclear plant (60 MW plus 50 Gcal/h); earlier floating power plants; biomass ORC CHP (Lienz). I found no coal-wintering bunker CHP.
- **Verdict:** WORKS, heat-led only.
