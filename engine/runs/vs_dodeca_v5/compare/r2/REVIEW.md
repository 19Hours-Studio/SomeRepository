IDEA 2: Seasonal sighting calendar for sowing
DEFECT | FATAL | The test it proposes measures the wrong thing. arctan(diameter difference / face distance) gives only about 0.1–2.4° (my calculation for differences of 0.2–3.3 mm over 80 mm), and a hand-held object with no level or horizon reference cannot fix the sun's elevation (about 16–63° at noon at 50°N). The geometry cannot set a date. | FACT 2, FACT 5; own calculation
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Versions for different latitudes" needs sizes to follow latitude, but sizes run continuously from 40 to 100 mm with no module, and finds all lie within roughly 44–55°N. | FACT 5, FACT 10, FACT 11
DEFECT | MAJOR | An "agricultural spread" does not fit contexts that are mostly urban or rural settlements, with forts, baths, a theatre, graves and rivers. | FACT 14, FACT 15
DEFECT | MAJOR | It leaves the knobs, the ringed holes and the unringed or oval pair unexplained. | FACT 1, FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | It does not explain the lack of wear on what would be a working instrument. | FACT 20
FIT | 4 of 14 | Only the varied holes, the opposite pairing and the broad distribution are addressed.
SURVIVES | Nothing substantive; it already concedes that the lack of standard sizes counts against it.
KNOWN | yes | It is listed among standard theories (Wikipedia; english-heritage.org.uk Corbridge exhibition; thedebrief.org). I could not verify the original proposer.

IDEA 6: Unwritten twelve-face lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | A lot read by hole size requires every face to be told apart, but some opposite pairs are near-identical. | FACT 4
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its own refutation test predicts micro-flattening on the knobs, while the corpus already reportedly rarely shows wear (Norton Disney is unworn), so as stated the idea is close to refuted. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | It leaves unexplained why a lot would be hollow, open on every face and ringed; a solid marked die does the job better. | FACT 1, FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | The one known dodecahedral lot from the region, the Geneva die, is marked with written zodiac names, which undercuts the "unwritten" premise. I verified this only partly: 1982, cathedral site, zodiac names (grandcurtius.be). | FACT 31/42 (NEW)
DEFECT | MINOR | Casting thin shells of about 1 mm, up to 580 g, onto ground would risk denting them, and nothing is said about the military or river contexts. | FACT 6, FACT 7, FACT 15
FIT | 7 of 16 | It covers the varied holes, the absence of a scheme or inscription, and the lack of text.
SURVIVES | The resting geometry holds: equal corner knobs make it come to rest on five knobs around one face, with one face up (I checked: the knob hull is itself a dodecahedron). The test is feasible and can refute it.
KNOWN | yes | Dice and divination appear in Wikipedia; the Geneva die is linked to divination games by grandcurtius.be/en/museums-collections/archaeology/dodecahedron.
