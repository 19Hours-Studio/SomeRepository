=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Rangefinder, gauge or knitting tool
DEFECT | MAJOR | Near-identical opposite holes give a zero difference, so for those pairs the similar-triangle range formula has no solution and the instrument fails on its own terms. | FACT 4 (Guggenberger & Leach 2025); the formula L = GH·B/(Dα−Dα′) in arxiv 1206.0946
DEFECT | MAJOR | A gauge or measure needs a shared scale, but hole sizes and object sizes follow no common scheme, so every piece would need its own calibration table, and no such table exists. | FACT 2, FACT 5 (Guggenberger 2013)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The knitting version uses only the knobs and leaves the twelve graded holes, the triple rings and the special unringed pair without a job. | FACT 1, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | The decisive test cannot refute the rangefinder version: sighting through holes never touches the rims, so finding no abrasion says nothing about it. The test only works against the gauge and knitting versions. | FACT 20; mechanism as stated
DEFECT | MINOR | The pieces rarely show wear, which goes against heavy practical use. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | The "votive contexts" given as counter-evidence are mostly ordinary: over half the finds come from civil settlements and only about 8.5% from plausibly sacred sites. | FACT 14
FIT | 3 of 12 | It accounts for holes that differ within an object, and little else.
SURVIVES | It is the only family that gives the differing opposite-hole sizes a function at all.
KNOWN | yes | Sparavigna 2012 (arxiv 1204.6497, 1206.0946) for the rangefinder; gauge and knitting ideas are in the theory lists on Wikipedia and thedebrief.org.

IDEA 2: Gallo-Celtic cult object, cosmological or divinatory
DEFECT | MAJOR | It claims every face can be told apart by its hole, but the commonest type has a matched unringed opposite pair and ringed holes in a narrow 10.5–22 mm range, and some opposite pairs are near-identical. Faces are therefore not reliably distinguishable. | FACT 3, FACT 4
DEFECT | MAJOR | The deposits it calls "classic votive patterns" are a minority: over half the finds are civil, under a fifth military, about 8.5% sacred and about 4% hoards. | FACT 14 (nunc.ch survey; Cambridge 2025)
DEFECT | MAJOR | It is not specific: divination, thread, sighting and staff are offered together, and none of them gives a mechanism for the thin hollow shell, the triple rings or the special unringed pair. | FACT 1, FACT 3, FACT 7
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test cannot refute it: "no wear, but possible wax, textile or organic traces" fits both a positive and a negative result. The only refutable part, the deposition-rate comparison, depends on contexts that are mostly unknown. | FACT 16, FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | Oral Druidic transmission into the 2nd–4th centuries is assumed, but sources report Druidism suppressed under Tiberius and Claudius. | Pliny, Natural History 30.13; Suetonius, Claudius 25
FIT | 7 of 14 | It covers distribution, silence of the texts, no inscriptions, little wear and no standard size, but not the type 2a layout or the find-context proportions.
SURVIVES | Its fit to the Gallic, Germanic and British distribution with no Mediterranean finds holds.
KNOWN | yes | Guggenberger & Leach 2025 (Antiquaries Journal 105:31-54); G. Creemers, Tongeren museum, on sorcery and fortune-telling (vrt.be 2023).

IDEA 3: Bronzesmith's hollow-casting display piece
DEFECT | MAJOR | If opposite holes are matched because the core was removed along six axes, every pair should match, but some opposite pairs are very different. | FACT 4 (Guggenberger & Leach 2025)
DEFECT | MAJOR | By its own admission it leaves unexplained the commonest and most distinctive feature: one larger, unringed, sometimes oval opposite pair. | FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | Core support and removal need openings but not twelve graded sizes; the size spread between axes is left unexplained. | FACT 2
DEFECT | MINOR | A shared template across about 130 pieces in 9 countries implies one tradition, yet no workshop, mould or core debris is known. | FACT 1, FACT 11; GAPS
DEFECT | MINOR | The ancient repair at Kortessem fits use, or at least valuing, more naturally than an unused showpiece. | FACT 23 (vrt.be)
FIT | 6 of 12 | It accounts for a hole in every face, the thin walls, little wear, no standard size and the silence of the texts.
SURVIVES | The rim test is feasible and can refute it.
KNOWN | partly | A "bronze caster's masterpiece" appears in the theory lists (Heidelberg journal article, journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/fuabt/article/view/54652); the six-axis core variant seems new.

IDEA 4: Masterpiece or prestige display object
DEFECT | MAJOR | It is not specific: "show of skill" fits any fine Roman bronze and gives no mechanism for graded holes, triple rings, the unringed pair or the knobs. | FACT 1, FACT 2, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | It assumes a Roman guild-masterpiece institution for which no evidence exists. | FACT 18; the absence is conceded in Idea 3
DEFECT | MINOR | It does not say why prestige pieces end up mostly in ordinary settlement contexts rather than mainly in rich graves. | FACT 14
DEFECT | MINOR | The test's prediction ("consistently high quality") is vague; any crude piece could simply be called an apprentice's work. | test as stated
DEFECT | MINOR | The lead argument ("26% lead") rests on one disputed pXRF reading (18% or 27%). | FACT 8 (soft)
FIT | 4 of 12 | It accounts for little wear, no standard size, no text and no inscription.
SURVIVES | Nothing distinctive beyond what Idea 3 already explains better.
KNOWN | yes | Listed as a "Meisterstück eines Bronzegießers" theory (Heidelberg journal article above); I could not confirm the attribution to Rüdiger Schwarz.

IDEA 5: Seasonal sighting calendar for sowing
DEFECT | MAJOR | The mechanism does not work geometrically: arctan(Δd/H) gives a sightline's angular tolerance, not its elevation, so a sun-altitude date cannot come from hole-size differences. A piece resting on a face puts all five oblique axes at the same 26.57° elevation, whatever the hole sizes. | own calc. (angle between face normals 63.43°)
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Different sizes for different latitudes" contradicts itself: scaling a whole piece leaves every angle unchanged, so size cannot encode latitude. Sizes also vary continuously. | FACT 5; own geometry
DEFECT | MAJOR | Near-identical opposite pairs give an angle of about 0°, so those pairs would set no date. | FACT 4
DEFECT | MAJOR | It does not explain why Mediterranean farmers, who also sowed winter grain, never used it. | FACT 11
DEFECT | MINOR | The triple rings, the unringed pair and the knobs are left without a role. | FACT 3
FIT | 2 of 10 | It accounts only for holes that differ within an object and the north-western rural spread.
SURVIVES | The proposed latitude-correlation test is feasible and refutable once per-object hole tables are published.
KNOWN | yes | Sjra Wagemans (c. 2010, cited on Wikipedia and thedebrief.org), for the autumn sowing date of wheat.

IDEA 6: Staff-mounted provincial symbolic bronze head
DEFECT | MAJOR | It proposes no decisive test, so as written it cannot be refuted (an obvious one, which it omits, is mounting marks on the unringed-pair rims that the ringed holes lack). | task criterion 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Holes match axis by axis" does not hold across the corpus, since some opposite pairs are very different. The strong match rests on one unverified Tongeren table. | FACT 4; FACT 36 (NEW, its wingarden.de source could not be retrieved)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Core support explains a hole in every face, but not why the holes differ from one axis to the next. | FACT 2
DEFECT | MINOR | Staff mounting rests on a single association, Gellep, with a bone object that was not recovered. The reported ~3 cm rod width also sits awkwardly with ~21×26 mm unringed holes. | FACT 17 (soft); FACT 33 (NEW); FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | The zodiac meaning comes from the Geneva piece, a different class of object: a silvered-lead die (~297 g) with inscribed faces, not a pierced hollow bronze. | averweij.web.cern.ch/zodiac.htm; FACT 31 (NEW)
FIT | 10 of 14 | It accounts for a hole in every face, the unringed pair, thin unworn shells, the distribution including Danube and Slovak finds, no text or inscription, the Kortessem repair and the mixed contexts.
SURVIVES | It is the only idea that gives the unringed, sometimes oval opposite pair a mechanical role while keeping wear low.
KNOWN | partly | Guggenberger & Leach 2025 already suggest the Gelduba piece was mounted on a handle through its production holes (cambridge.org core reader); the "sceptre" reading goes back to the excavator's interpretation of the Gellep grave.

IDEA 7: Unwritten twelve-face lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its own test is already close to failing: repeated casting would wear the knob tips, yet the pieces rarely show wear and Norton Disney is unworn. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | Faces cannot be told apart by size: the unringed pair is matched, and some opposite pairs are near-identical. | FACT 3, FACT 4
DEFECT | MAJOR | A ~1 mm hollow shell is an odd choice for a thrown object; a solid die would do the job better. | FACT 7 (soft, derived); FACT 6
DEFECT | MINOR | The one known twelve-face divinatory object from the region labels its faces in writing, which goes against "unwritten". | Geneva zodiac die (averweij.web.cern.ch; FACT 31, NEW)
DEFECT | MINOR | The triple rings and the hollow interior are left unexplained. | FACT 1, FACT 3
FIT | 4 of 11 | It accounts for holes that differ within an object, no shared scheme, no inscription and no text.
SURVIVES | Its knob test is cheap and can refute it.
KNOWN | partly | Dice and fortune-telling are in the standard lists (Wikipedia; Creemers in vrt.be 2023).
