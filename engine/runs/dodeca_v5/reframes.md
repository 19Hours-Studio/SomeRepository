**ASSUMPTIONS:**
- All ~130 objects shared one function. (Contradicted in part: there are several types, a few solid pieces, sizes running from 35 g to over 1 kg, and mixed contexts.)
- They are all hollow. (Contradicted: 129 of 134 are hollow.)
- They were "used" by handling, as a tool or instrument. (Contradicted for mechanical use: wear is rare and the Norton Disney piece is unworn.)
- Hole diameters encode a measure or scheme shared between objects. (Contradicted: there is no shared hole-size scheme, no standard module and no marks.)
- The function is either practical or symbolic, not both or neither.
- The current evidence is enough to pick a "best" explanation. (Contradicted: there is no use-wear, residue or fibre survey, no per-object hole tables, no wall-thickness or CT data, few stratified finds, and alloy data for only one piece.)
- The 2nd–4th c. date and the Gallic-Empire link are established. (Contradicted in strength: dating rests on context only, and the link is untested because no workshop or mould is known.)
- Distribution is confined to the north-western provinces. (Contradicted at the edges: pieces from Serbia and Slovakia, including finds outside the Empire.)
- "How it was used" can be inferred from form alone. (Contradicted: the knitting, candle and rangefinder proposals all rest on form, and each failed on wear, residue or standardisation evidence.)

**REFRAME 1:** Which of the surviving proposals (symbolic or staff-head, thread divination, wax forming, sighting device) predict different, measurable traces on objects already in museums, such as polish or deformation on knobs and hole rims, interior residues (wax, lipid, fibre) and handling wear? **REFRAME 2:** Within each object, do the twelve hole diameters, the opposite-pair ratios and the triple-ringed versus unringed pattern of type 2a follow a structure? Does that structure scale with body size or vary at random, as tested on Guggenberger's list 1–134 and Nouwen 1993? | CHANGES: sub-question, attacking the one feature every theory must explain, using existing raw data | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: medium-high | WHY: sighting, forming and symbolic readings make opposite predictions about hole structure, and the data exist but have never been tabulated per object.

**REFRAME 3:** What was the dominant type 2a for, with other types treated as possibly different objects? | CHANGES: unit, from the whole corpus to one type | IMPORTANCE: medium-high | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: averaging over heterogeneous objects may be why no single theory fits everything.

**REFRAME 4:** Where, when and by how many workshops were they made, judged from alloy groups (Hitchens' pXRF), wall thickness against size, and CT of cores and casting technique? | CHANGES: means, putting production before function | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: this would test the Gallic-Empire link and the Mediterranean absence, and new data are already being collected.

**REFRAME 5:** In the stratified finds and graves (Gellep 3040, Bachem, Saint-Parize, Jublains, Norton Disney), what objects travel with dodecahedra, and do those deposits show deliberate placement or breakage rather than loss? | CHANGES: scope, to a small set of contexts with full inventories | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: low-medium | WHY: this could separate valued ritual or symbolic use from everyday use, but fewer than a handful of well-recorded contexts exist.

**REFRAME 6:** Why are they absent from Italy, Spain, Africa and the East but present beyond the Rhine–Danube frontier? Which proposed function predicts this pattern and which does not? | CHANGES: target, using distribution as the test | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: low | WHY: the pattern is robust, but most candidates can be made to fit it after the fact.
