=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Platonic cosmic receptacle
DEFECT | MAJOR | It explains the 12 faces through the Platonic solid but calls the holes and knobs "decorative or ritual variation", which leaves the most distinctive patterned features without a mechanism: why ten ringed holes plus one opposite pair of larger, unringed holes. | FACT 2, FACT 3 (Guggenberger 2013)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Druid-Pythagorean link needs a working Druidic intellectual class in the 2nd-4th c., but Druidism was banned under Tiberius and Claudius in the 1st c. AD, and the dated contexts run c. 200 to the 4th c. | FACT 13; Suetonius, Claudius 25; Pliny, NH 30.13
DEFECT | MAJOR | The sceptre-mount claim rests on one bone rod that was too decayed to recover, and the bone had passed through the "production holes". A staff mount would also predict socket wear on one hole pair, which is not reported. | FACT 17, FACT 20; livescience (above); Cambridge 2025 core reader
DEFECT | MAJOR | The evidence is texts plus distribution, not the objects themselves, and the "test" (better-contexted finds, evidence of mounting) names no result that would refute it. Mounting evidence would fit insignia readings just as well. | FACT 29, FACT 16
DEFECT | MINOR | "Celtic provincial origin" does not explain why there are none from Celtic Cisalpine Gaul or Hispania, or why some reached Germanic Slovakia. | FACT 11, FACT 12
FIT | 9 of 17 | It fits the geography, the silence of texts and art, the absence of inscriptions, the lack of wear and the varied contexts, but only by setting the hole system aside.
SURVIVES | The 12-face form does match the Platonic cosmic solid, and the north-western distribution is described correctly.
KNOWN | yes | Guggenberger & Leach 2025, Antiquaries Journal 105:31-54; Guggenberger's sceptre reading of Gellep (Wikipedia; Tongeren museum essay)

IDEA 2: Apotropaic household display object
DEFECT | MAJOR | It is geometrically wrong: with a knob at each of the 20 corners, the support planes are the 12 face planes, so the object always rests on five knobs around a face and never on three. A convex-hull check gave 5 knob tips on every support plane. That also makes the proposed "three-knob triad wear" test impossible. | FACT 1; my python convex-hull check
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Free variation set by the modeller" contradicts the repeated, structured type 2a: one opposite pair of larger, unringed, sometimes oval holes recurs across the corpus, which is not random. | FACT 3 (Guggenberger 2013, "by far" commonest)
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Shrine" and "eyes averting harm" are not supported by findspots: only about 8.5% are plausibly sacred, over half are civil, and no shrine or lararium assemblage is reported. | FACT 14
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its first refutation test is already decided: the corpus is known to lack a shared hole scheme, so the "ratio scheme" outcome cannot occur, and the test cannot refute anything. | FACT 2, FACT 4
DEFECT | MINOR | There are no lamp soot or residue reports to support the "light through holes" role, and the single wax report is read as a casting residue. | FACT 21
FIT | 9 of 17 | It fits the lack of wear, the mixed deposition, Kortessem, the uninscribed faces and the silence of texts, but not the patterned type 2a or the geometry.
SURVIVES | Reading the triple rings as "eye" motifs is a reasonable iconographic suggestion; ring-and-dot motifs are common on Roman small finds.
KNOWN | partly | General religious, candle-holder and decorative readings are widely listed (Wikipedia; mentalfloss); the specific apotropaic "eye" reading has no named proponent that I found.

IDEA 3: Cord-wound divinatory frame
DEFECT | MAJOR | It depends on cord use, but no fibre or yarn residue has ever been reported. | FACT 22
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Stations distinguishable by hole size" fails where opposite holes are near-identical, and in type 2a the two big holes are one matched pair. | FACT 4, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Spindle of Necessity link and the private-diviner use are assumptions with no object or textual support, and "unofficial practice" does not explain why there is none in Italy or the Mediterranean, where private divination was common. | FACT 11, FACT 18
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test is weakened by its own escape clause: having already predicted "slight wear" from occasional use, a nil result for polish and fibres can be explained away. It refutes the idea only if pre-registered as "no groove polish = refuted". It is feasible (SEM/FTIR on Bonn and Tongeren pieces). | FACT 20; GAPS
DEFECT | MINOR | Its claim that military readings fail on FACT 14 is overstated: under a fifth military is not zero. | FACT 14
FIT | 8 of 17 | It fits the knobs as anchors, the unmarked holes, the lack of wear and the civil contexts, but not the matched pairs or the distribution.
SURVIVES | The test (SEM for polish at knob roots and hole rims plus FTIR for fibres) is the right kind of test and can be run on existing material.
KNOWN | partly | Knitting and cord use (hobbyist glove-knitting, thedebrief) and divination (mentalfloss) were both proposed separately; I found no source for this combined form.

IDEA 4: Farmer's sowing-date instrument
DEFECT | FATAL | A fixed-aperture sun sight calibrated per latitude can't work across a corpus with no shared hole scheme and no markings, found from Britain to Serbia (more than 7° of latitude). Each piece would need its own unrecorded calibration, and nothing on the objects records one. | FACT 2, FACT 5, FACT 12, FACT 19
DEFECT | MAJOR | A device that reads sun elevation gives the same reading on dates symmetric about the solstice, so it can't tell the spring from the autumn equinox, and the object has no levelling or noon reference. The knobs give stable rest on a face but not a horizontal or meridian alignment. | FACT 1; solar geometry
DEFECT | MAJOR | Rich graves of both sexes, hoards, rivers and forts are not farming contexts, and over half the finds are urban or rural civil settlement rather than specifically agrarian. | FACT 14, FACT 15
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "test" is confirmatory, done on a replica by the proposer (Wagemans), and was never independently reproduced on measured originals. It sets no condition for refutation. | thedebrief; arxiv 1204.6497
DEFECT | MINOR | It doesn't say why bronze casting and knobs were needed when a stick gnomon does the same job, or why it is absent from Mediterranean farming. | FACT 8, FACT 11
FIT | 4 of 17 | It fits only the differing holes, the uninscribed faces, the lack of wear and the northern distribution, and the last only weakly.
SURVIVES | Differently sized opposite holes can form an angular aperture; that piece of geometry holds.
KNOWN | yes | Sjra Wagemans 2010, cited in Sparavigna arXiv 1204.6497 and thedebrief

IDEA 5: Unwritten twelve-face lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its central claim that the faces had to be uninscribed is undercut by a related 4th-c. object: the silvered lead dodecahedron from Saint-Pierre, Geneva, carries the Latin zodiac names on its 12 faces. When a 12-face prediction object was wanted, people inscribed it. | ge.ch "Objets archéologiques - Episode#1" (NEW FACT)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Identification by hole size fails where opposite holes are near-identical, and in type 2a the ten ringed holes span only about 10-22 mm, about 1.2 mm per step. That is hard to read reliably by eye. | FACT 3, FACT 4 (Newhall)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test contradicts itself: the idea says occasional use on soft ground leaves no wear, yet makes "no knob flattening" the refuting result. That result is already the reported state of the corpus. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | The two "special" large holes are an opposite pair, so one is always up when the other is down; the idea doesn't say which face counts. Graves, hoards and military finds are not explained beyond "private use". | FACT 3, FACT 14
FIT | 9 of 17 | It fits the differing holes within each object, the lack of a scheme between objects, the absence of inscriptions, the silence of texts and resting on a face, though resting on a face holds for any knobbed dodecahedron.
SURVIVES | The knobbed dodecahedron does come to rest on one face (5-knob support, my check), so "lands on a face" is mechanically correct.
KNOWN | yes | Dice and divination are long listed; criticised on the inscription and knob grounds (mentalfloss; Wikipedia); "gaming use discarded" (exarc 2026)

IDEA 6: Coincidence rangefinder
DEFECT | MAJOR | A military surveying tool clashes with findspots: under a fifth military against over half civil, rich women's graves, and none from the Mediterranean or most frontier provinces where the same army surveyed. | FACT 14, FACT 11, FACT 17
DEFECT | MAJOR | No shared module and no hole scheme mean every piece would need its own unwritten range table, yet nothing is inscribed. | FACT 2, FACT 5, FACT 19
DEFECT | MAJOR | The mechanism misstates the optics: in a sight with the eye at the near hole, only the far hole's diameter and the body height set the angle, so "pairs of differing diameters" add nothing, and near-identical pairs work just as well. | FACT 4; arxiv 1206.0946
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test (hole-pair ratios against "plausible" target sizes) has free parameters (target size, range), so almost any ratio fits; it cannot refute the idea as stated. | FACT 27
DEFECT | MINOR | It doesn't account for the knobs (gripping is generic), the triple rings, or how an unworn survey tool fits the deposition record. | FACT 3, FACT 20
FIT | 4 of 17 | It fits the opposite holes, the fort finds and a vague hand-held size, and little else.
SURVIVES | Sighting through opposite holes is physically possible.
KNOWN | yes | Sparavigna 2012, arXiv 1204.6497 and 1206.0946

IDEA 7: Gallo-British elite insignia
DEFECT | MAJOR | It has no mechanism for the distinctive features (holes of differing size in every face, ring pattern, knobs); "recognisable identity" would fit any badge. | FACT 2, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its own analogy works against it: crossbow brooches, the cited model for rank markers, do appear in late Roman art (e.g. Stilicho diptych, Ravenna mosaics), so being "too unofficial for art" is a special pleading for dodecahedra. | FACT 18; known iconography
DEFECT | MAJOR | It says the template is fixed and includes ringed holes, but rings mark only type 2a and are inconsistent even within a piece, such as the one unringed hole at South Shields. | FACT 3, FACT 25 (soft)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test is hard to run because most finds have no known context and few are stratified, so a size-versus-status table would be small and biased. A null result could be blamed on noise. | FACT 16
FIT | 9 of 17 | It fits the confined distribution, the Slovak imports, the varied contexts, the lack of wear, the silence of texts and the size and weight range.
SURVIVES | The confined provincial distribution plus a few beyond the frontier is a pattern typical of regional prestige goods.
KNOWN | partly | Status and sceptre-head readings (Guggenberger; Tongeren museum; Wikipedia); I found no source for the crossbow-brooch rank-badge form.

IDEA 8: Thin-wall casting masterpiece
DEFECT | MAJOR | Core supports don't require holes that differ systematically from 6 to 40 mm within one object, a recurring large oval opposite pair, or engraved triple rings. Those are deliberate finishing choices that a casting rationale doesn't explain. | FACT 2, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | A guild "masterpiece" system is a medieval institution with no Roman evidence. Bronzesmiths worked throughout the empire, so the idea leaves the absence from Italy and the Mediterranean unexplained. | FACT 11; Wikipedia (Schwarz's own caveat)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Twenty corner vents or feeders is not credible foundry practice for a ~1 mm shell, and the knobs are finished balls. No vent stubs are reported. | FACT 1; FACT 7 (my recalculation 0.60/1.58/1.07/0.64 mm before subtracting the holes; calc.py is not actually in the folder)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The refutation needs two conditions at once (cut holes AND no core or chaplet traces), which makes it hard to trigger. Core-hole casting would also be true of any hollow function, so a positive result doesn't separate masterpiece from tool or symbol. | GAPS; FACT 8
DEFECT | MINOR | "Rough interiors" rests on an unverified wiki claim, and "rich houses" is not in the findspot data. | observatory.wiki; FACT 14
FIT | 10 of 17 | It fits the hollow form, a hole in every face, the lack of a scheme, the size range, the lack of wear, the silence of texts, Kortessem's repair and the deposit types.
SURVIVES | Holes as core prints or core-removal openings is the best mechanical account of why every face is pierced, and CT on Bonn or Tongeren pieces is feasible.
KNOWN | yes | Rüdiger Schwarz (Saalburg), masterpiece and test-piece reading (Wikipedia; mentalfloss); "production holes" wording in Guggenberger & Leach 2025

NEW FACT | A 4th-c. silvered-lead dodecahedron from Saint-Pierre Cathedral, Geneva (excavated 1982), carries the Latin zodiac names on its 12 faces, so FACT 19 holds only for the hollow bronze type | ge.ch/actualite/objets-archeologiques-episode1-3-11-2021
NEW FACT | A Roman icosahedron from Arloff, long misclassified as a dodecahedron, is in the LVR-LandesMuseum Bonn; the polyhedral family is wider than the dodecahedron | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_dodecahedron
NEW FACT | The Gellep bone rod (~15 cm × 3 cm) was too decayed to recover, so the sceptre link cannot be re-examined | livescience (above)
NEW FACT | A knobbed regular dodecahedron always rests on 5 knobs around one face, never on 3 (convex-hull check) | own python calculation
