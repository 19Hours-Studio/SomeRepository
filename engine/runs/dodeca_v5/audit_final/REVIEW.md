=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Tallow-candle draught shade
DEFECT | MAJOR | A flame burning inside a closed 0.6–1.6 mm shell should leave soot, tallow and heat tint, but none is reported on any of about 130 pieces; the only residue is a single wax report that cannot be traced to a piece and that sceptics read as casting residue. | FACT 20, FACT 21, FACT 7
DEFECT | MAJOR | The lighting-economics argument fails on dates and map. In Britain oil lamps "fell out of use quickly" after the 1st century, so candles were already normal before the dodecahedra appear. Candle-lit Raetia, Noricum and Pannonia should then have as many pieces as Gaul, yet about 90% lie inside the Gallic Empire area. | FACT 10, FACT 13; Colchester Collections record (cim-web.adlibhosting.com/ais6/Details/collect/181098)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Nothing in candle use requires opposite holes to match axis by axis, and the larger, unringed, sometimes oval pair gets only an ad hoc reading ("admitted a light"). The matching is real: recomputed exact p = 5/10395 = 0.0005 for Tongeren. | FACT 3, FACT 4, FACT 36
DEFECT | MAJOR | It does not say why a draught shade would be a costly thin-wall bronze casting with 20 knobs and engraved rings, when cheap clay or horn lanterns would do. It also does not say why such an object would go into rich graves and hoards. | FACT 1, FACT 13, FACT 14
DEFECT | MINOR | Light leaves only through the holes, and the flame sits 2–5 cm from a thin wall, so it would make a poor light and the casing would overheat. | FACT 5, FACT 7
FIT | 8 of 16 | Explains the graded holes, the size range and the domestic contexts, but not the paired axes, the map or the date.
SURVIVES | The GC-MS lipid and soot test is feasible, and a negative result on more than 10 pieces would refute it.
KNOWN | yes | The candle-holder theory comes from the wax report (bigthink.com/strange-maps/roman-dodecahedrons; Wikipedia).
NEW FACT | In Roman Britain tallow candles were the normal light and oil lamps were concentrated in the 1st century, at Colchester and London | cim-web.adlibhosting.com/ais6/Details/collect/181098

IDEA 3: Fumigation cage for aromatics
DEFECT | MAJOR | Burning an ember inside should leave soot, charred resin and heat tint, yet none is reported. "Use causes no wear" sidesteps the point, because the expected traces are chemical, not abrasion. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | Fumigation was common across the empire and had its own vessels (thymiateria). Calling this a "regional form" does not explain why none occur around the Mediterranean or why 90% lie in the Gallic Empire area. | FACT 10, FACT 11
DEFECT | MAJOR | It leaves the paired axes, the ten ringed holes and the special unringed pair as "ornament", although the ventilation logic needs only holes that differ. | FACT 3, FACT 4, FACT 36
DEFECT | MINOR | The "cooler grips" claim is wrong physically: the knobs are the same conductive copper alloy, joined to a wall of about 1 mm, so they heat with the shell. Small embers would also drop through the bottom hole, which can be up to 28 mm. | FACT 7, FACT 2
DEFECT | MINOR | Calling the wax a residue of fumigation is a stretch, because wax is not resin. | FACT 21
FIT | 9 of 16 | Explains the perforated hollow, the knobs as stand-offs and the mixed contexts; fails on the map and the hole system.
SURVIVES | The GC-MS test for resin and soot on more than 10 interiors is feasible and would clearly refute it.
KNOWN | partly | Popular overviews list a "candleholder or incense burner" (observatory.wiki/What_Were_the_Dodecahedrons_of_the_Roman_Era_For%3F/info). A 2026 Zenodo preprint proposes a "vault for volatile aromatics" (zenodo.org/records/21744442).

IDEA 7: Native priest's radiate sceptre
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Slovak fragments come from Germanic settlements outside the empire, and others from Pannonian, Moesian and fort contexts, none of which fits a Gaulish or British native priesthood. | FACT 12, FACT 15
DEFECT | MAJOR | A sprig in each hole is unsupported: no plant or other organic residue has been reported, holes of 28–40 mm suit branches rather than sprigs, and random stems would not produce matched axes. | FACT 2, FACT 22, FACT 36
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Knobs stopped it rolling" adds nothing, because a dodecahedron does not roll; it rests on five knobs. | FACT 34
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Gellep rod lay beside the piece, not through it, and was not recovered. The grave was a woman's, and the priestly reading is unsupported. | FACT 17, FACT 33 (SOFT)
DEFECT | MINOR | Gutuatri are attested by inscription at Autun in central Gaul, not in Britain or on the Rhine, where most finds are. | Wikipedia "Anvallus"
FIT | 10 of 16 | Explains the axis and the two five-fold crowns geometrically, plus the silence of art; fails on the eastern finds and on pairing.
SURVIVES | The geometry holds: an axis through opposite faces leaves two rings of five faces.
KNOWN | partly | The sceptre head is known (Tongeren museum); the sprig and priestly-crown version appears new.

IDEA 8: Unwritten twelve-face lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | Because opposite holes match, the faces read in two opposite positions look nearly the same (Tongeren 13.8/14.0, 20.3/20.5). That leaves about six distinguishable outcomes rather than twelve, and the "special" unringed pair reads as one outcome. | FACT 36, FACT 37
DEFECT | MAJOR | The knobs do not "make it rest on a face", since any dodecahedron does that. With knobs it rests on five points, as my hull check confirms. | FACT 34
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test cannot refute it. Throwing onto soft ground or cloth, with only occasional use, already predicts no flattening of the knobs, and the comparison piece (Norton Disney) is itself unworn. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Geneva zodiac die shows that dodecahedral lots in the region were inscribed, which undercuts "unwritten"; dice were also used across the whole empire, so the map is unexplained. | grandcurtius.be; FACT 11
DEFECT | MINOR | Sparavigna argues the object is a biased body and makes a poor die. | arxiv.org/pdf/1206.0946
FIT | 7 of 16 | Explains the lack of inscriptions and the variation within objects; fails on pairing, knobs and map.
SURVIVES | It does derive the absence of numbers from identifying faces by size.
KNOWN | yes | Heinevetter and Dornseiff on dodecahedral divination (archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeumdok/id/eprint/1865); the Geneva die as divination (Grand Curtius).
