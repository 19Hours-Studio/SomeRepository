ATTEMPT 1: Squarespace or Wix music template with a hero image, "Latest release" and a social-icon footer | Small labels and bands | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: Templates are sold as "sleek, professional", so every label looks the same and the name carries no identity. | [Capterra/Squarespace guides](https://jazzfuel.com/services/squarespace-websites/)
ATTEMPT 2: Black-and-white palette with a grid of square covers | Many labels, per Bandzoogle | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: It is the default starting point, so the page is only a wall of covers. Without text hierarchy, the label's stance gets lost. | [Bandzoogle](https://bandzoogle.com/blog/website-design-inspiration-best-record-label-sites)
ATTEMPT 3: Flash-era, "too artsy" navigation, with cryptic menus and hidden hamburger navigation | Older band and label sites | niche | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: Visitors cannot find music or contact details, and the style gets in the way of the three-click tasks. | [Kirkville](https://kirkville.com/five-mistakes-band-label-sites-make/); [Graphic Design Junction](https://graphicdesignjunction.com/2024/06/most-common-ux-mistakes/)
ATTEMPT 4: Site that is only a link-out to a Bandcamp page | Tiny labels | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: There is no label voice, news or demo policy, and nothing for press. | [Sakura Selections](https://sakuraselections.bandcamp.com)
ATTEMPT 5: Third-party embeds (Spotify, Bandcamp, YouTube) as the only player | Squarespace and WordPress labels | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: Embeds help engagement but look identical everywhere and load heavy scripts. The sample site can't use real audio anyway, so use a clearly marked placeholder. | [Sonicbids](https://blog.sonicbids.com/build-music-websites-for-bands)
ATTEMPT 6: News section left dated or empty | Labels with little upkeep | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: It reads as an inactive label. Sample posts need plausible dates, but must be marked as placeholder. | [otherrecordlabels.com](https://www.otherrecordlabels.com/record-label-websites)
ATTEMPT 7: "Send us your demo" as a bare email address with no rules | Many labels | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: It produces attachments and mismatched genres. It is better to state format, track count, link type, reply time and whether the label accepts unsolicited demos. | [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2017/02/5-steps-for-sending-a-perfect-demo-submission.html)
ATTEMPT 8: One shared contact@ inbox | Small labels | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: Demos, press and bookings get mixed. Split by purpose, as Sakura Selections does. | [Sakura Selections](https://sakuraselections.bandcamp.com)
ATTEMPT 9: Name-driven imagery (Nvasion Records' aliens and green) | Genre labels | niche | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: It is memorable but literal, and risks cliché. For «Порог», the literal door, a doorway silhouette or arrow is the obvious reading, so the form needs a quieter variation. | [Bandzoogle](https://bandzoogle.com/blog/website-design-inspiration-best-record-label-sites)
ATTEMPT 10: Release pages with only a cover and a stream button | Streaming-first labels | standard | WHY IT FALLS FLAT: They leave out catalogue number, format, credits, date and ℗© line, the details that mark an independent label. | [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/81ea1cab-61e5-350a-b9fa-9030c8ac3fd8)

LESSONS:
- The page must first be useful: music, artists, news, a demo policy and a contact address, each within three clicks.
- Real catalogue detail (numbers like ПОР-001, format, run size, credits, ℗© line) is what separates it from a template. Mark it all as placeholder.
- The name «Порог» should shape structure and typography (steps, thresholds, a frame or opening), not a literal door picture.
- The sources are weak on design critique and on Russian label practice. Treat the conventions as defaults, not rules.
