**What I checked**
- **Reproducibility:** I re-ran all of A's scripts (`crease_model.py`, `tension_heat.py`) and B's six round-3 scripts. Every output matches its committed `.out` file exactly, so neither answer's figures are made up or stale.
- **Strain arithmetic:** the basic figures in both are correct. A's 70 µm UTG at R = 4 mm gives 0.875% strain, about 630 MPa. B's 20 µm UTG gives a core tension of about 700·3/14 ≈ 150 MPa.

**What decided it**
1. **A rests on the assumption that the glass takes no set at all.** A says "glass at room temperature does not creep" and gives the glass a set fraction of exactly 0. A 2024 PolyU paper (*Extreme Mechanics Letters* 71, 102219) reports permanent creases in folded, ion-exchanged ultrathin glass, and says recovery gets worse with longer holds and repeated folding. In A's own model the glass carries 79% of the bending stiffness, so a glass set fraction of just 1e-3 gives about 9 µm depth and 1.9 mrad slope. That is roughly 5× over A's own targets of 2 µm and 0.4 mrad, before A's tensioning idea is counted. B cites the same paper, puts glass set into its budget, works out the tolerable limit (≤ 2.3e-3), and makes it the kill criterion of a cheap mandrel test.
2. **A's bar for "invisible" is probably too loose.** A's 0.4 mrad slope target bends a reflected ray by about 2.8 arcmin. Human vernier acuity is around 10 arcsec (my figure, not checked against a source), so a kink that size in the reflection of a straight edge could likely be seen. B's 0.024 mrad (about 10″) target fits the asker's demand of "actually invisible, not less visible" much better.
3. **B's error budget is more complete and more honest.** It also counts gravity sag, air pressure, the kinks where the free span meets the hinge, frame bow and thermal bow. It openly reports that its nominal case passes by only 18% and its pessimistic case fails by 5×.

**Where A is better**
- A's design is far more plausible as a phone. It uses 7–14 MPa of added glass stress, a thicker teardrop and a modest tensioning cam.
- B's design needs 600 N of tension, a sustained 244 MPa core tension in 20 µm glass, piezo-servoed gas-cushion support, and hinge seats repeatable to 0.135 µm. That is instrument-grade hardware, and it pushes the folded spine to about 14–15 mm.
- B is also cluttered with point-by-point replies to an earlier reviewer, which the asker never asked for.
- A is cleaner and its comparison with Apple, Samsung and Oppo is just as good.

On the asker's hard constraint, though, A's main claim falls once glass set is counted. B's claim survives with clearly stated, testable conditions.
