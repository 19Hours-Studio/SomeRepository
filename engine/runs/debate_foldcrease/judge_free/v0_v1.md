**B is clearly the better answer.** Both answers come from the same line of work: B is a revision of A, written after a critique of A's main design. I reran that critique and it holds up.

**What I checked**

1. **A's main claim fails when the layers are modelled as bonded.** A predicts a leftover dent of 0.3–1.7 µm, but its model treats the glass, the adhesive and the titanium foil as if they bend independently. A's own notes say the soft adhesive is sheared about 6× in the fold. A never connects that to the crease. If the adhesive keeps even part of that shear after opening, it pulls the glass and foil like a bimetal strip and curls the stack. I ran the critic's bonded-layer model (`critic_oca_coupled2.py`) on a coarser grid, because the full run timed out. For A's stack at a 4 mm radius:

   | Adhesive stiffness G | Set fraction | Depth, no tension | Depth with A's 1000 N/m tension |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 10⁴ Pa | 2% | 4.2 µm | 0.67 µm |
   | 10⁴ Pa | 10% | 21 µm | 3.3 µm |
   | 10⁵ Pa | 2% | 22.6 µm | 4.2 µm |
   | 10⁵ Pa | 10% | 113 µm | 20.8 µm |

   That is roughly 10–60× worse than A states. The crease would be visible by A's own target (≤ 2 µm). Even with the tensioning hinge added, half the cases miss the target.

2. **A's titanium foil also has a problem in the cold.** When the adhesive stiffens in the cold, the same model adds about 300–680 MPa to the foil's 500 MPa bending stress. That brings it close to or past yield, so the foil itself could take a permanent bend.

3. **B removes the cause instead of tuning around it.** B uses a glass / panel / glass sandwich joined by thin, rigid adhesive, with no metal bonded across the fold. Nothing in it can slip, and the display layer sits at the centre of the bend. I hand-checked its strain numbers and they are right: 0.68% and 490 MPa in the glass at 5 mm, 0.22% in the panel.

4. **B's own figures:**
   - Without tension, B predicts 3–13 µm, which fails its own ≤ 1 µm target. B says so openly and makes the tensioning spring a required part of the design.
   - With tension, it predicts 0.10–0.42 µm, and 1.1 µm in the pessimistic case.

   These are still model results with no prototype data.

**Where B is also stronger**
- **Defining invisible:** B measures the fold zone against a non-fold zone of the same screen, using the IEC 62715-6-22 test standard. It also uses published touch-detection thresholds (Louw et al. 2000).
- **Honesty about thresholds:** B admits that the 0.4 mrad slope target used in A could still be seen.
- **Glass failure:** it computes the scratch depth at which the glass would crack, which A doesn't.
- **Testing:** it adds a 10-day folded hold at room temperature and cold-opening tests. Its cheapest experiment has a clear kill criterion: if the untensioned sandwich strip exceeds 13 µm, the central claim fails.
- **Oppo:** it notes that Oppo's "Zero-Feel Crease" describes a visual effect, not physical absence.

**What B gives up**
- The spine is about 12–13 mm when folded.
- It needs a new glass-panel-glass lamination process.
- Drop and impact behaviour has not been quantified.

The two answers cover Apple, Samsung and Oppo about equally well.
