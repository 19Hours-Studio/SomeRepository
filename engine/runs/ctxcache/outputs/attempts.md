=== ATTEMPTS ===

I used all 15 searches. Every number below comes from the cited page's own claims, not from a replication. Arithmetic marked "mine" is my own. I did not search attempts 14 and 15.

ATTEMPT 1: Provider exact-prefix caching (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini) | Hosted APIs, 2024–25 | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Reads are billed at 0.1× and 5-minute writes at 1.25×, so the discount is large only up to the first differing token. Anthropic allows only 4 explicit breakpoints per request. OpenAI caches only prompts of 1024+ tokens, in 128-token steps. Gemini's own docs say implicit savings carry no guarantee. A mid-prompt edit forfeits all tokens after it. | [Helicone](https://docs.helicone.ai/gateway/concepts/prompt-caching), [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/prompt_caching_201), [Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching?lang=node)

ATTEMPT 2: Append-only prompt discipline (stable system prompt, deterministic JSON, no timestamps) | Manus, 2025 | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It is a convention, not a mechanism. Manus runs about 100:1 input to output tokens and prices cached Sonnet input at $0.30 vs $3 per million tokens. A single changed token still invalidates everything after it. It forbids exactly the middle edits, reorderings and tool-output replacements the question asks about. | [Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)

ATTEMPT 3: Self-hosted prefix caches (vLLM block hashing, SGLang RadixAttention) | Open source, 2023–25 | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Each vLLM block hash covers all earlier tokens, so any upstream change breaks every later block. Hit rates depend on prefix overlap. One vendor blog reports about 28% hit rate at 20% overlap and about 93% at 95% overlap (unverified). | [vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/design/prefix_caching.html), [SGLang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.07104)

ATTEMPT 4: Routing and keys to raise hits (OpenAI `prompt_cache_key`) | OpenAI, 2024–25 | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Hits also require landing on the same machine. One customer's hit rate rose from 60% to 87% with the key, which is anecdotal and still short of 100%. Routing cannot fix a changed prefix. | [OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/prompt_caching_201), [summary](https://growwstacks.com/blog/master-next-gen-ai-development-prompt-caching)

ATTEMPT 5: Paid retention (Anthropic 1-hour TTL, Gemini explicit caches) | Providers | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Retention extends only identical prefixes, and you pay for it. The 1-hour write costs 2× base input. Third-party-listed Gemini storage is $4.50 per million tokens per hour for 2.5 Pro, so a 100K-token cache costs about $0.45/hour (mine). | [Helicone](https://docs.helicone.ai/gateway/concepts/prompt-caching)

ATTEMPT 6: CacheBlend (reuse non-prefix chunk KV, recompute about 15% of tokens) | Chicago/Microsoft/LMCache, EuroSys '25 | niche (open source, server-side only) | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It gives 2.2–3.3× TTFT and 2.8–5× throughput, not about 100% savings. About 15% of tokens are always recomputed. If that were billed like a provider would, the cost would be about 0.85×0.1 + 0.15×1 ≈ 0.23 of full price (mine). It needs access to the KV tensors, so a closed API cannot offer it. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16444v3), [LMCache](https://blog.lmcache.ai/2025-03-31-eurosys)

ATTEMPT 7: EPIC / LegoLink (static recompute of the first k≤32 tokens of each chunk) | PKU/NJU/Huawei, ICML 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It reports up to 8× TTFT and 7× throughput with "negligible" loss. All figures are the authors' own, I found no independent replication, and it works only on a self-hosted vLLM fork. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15332v3)

ATTEMPT 8: Prompt Cache (schema-defined prompt modules with fixed positions) | Yale/Google, MLSys 2024 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It reports 8× (GPU) and 60× (CPU) lower TTFT, but only for text declared in advance as modules at fixed positions. Free-form edits and tool outputs fall outside that. Reused states also skip attention to other modules. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04934)

ATTEMPT 9: APE (shared prefix, attention temperature and scaling on independently encoded contexts) | CMU/NVIDIA, ICLR 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It is training-free but lossy. It keeps 98% of sequential accuracy on RAG and 93% on in-context learning, so 2–7% is lost. Its 4.5× end-to-end gain is at 128K context, and it needs model internals. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05431v1)

ATTEMPT 10: Block-Attention (block-wise attention, with fine-tuning) | ICLR 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: TTFT falls from 3638 ms to 45 ms at 32K tokens, which is the best speed result here. It needs fine-tuning, which the question rules out. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15355v1)

ATTEMPT 11: TurboRAG (offline chunk KV with reordered RoPE) | Moore Threads, EMNLP 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It reports up to 9.4× TTFT (8.6× average). The arXiv version fine-tunes the model and the EMNLP abstract does not mention it, so whether fine-tuning is needed is unresolved. Chunks must be precomputed offline, which suits a fixed corpus, not live tool outputs. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07590), [ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.emnlp-main.334)

ATTEMPT 12: KVLink (re-encoded positions plus trainable link tokens) | UCSB/Accenture, NeurIPS 2025 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It cuts TTFT by 60–90% (headline "up to" 96%) and improves QA about 4%. The link tokens must be trained, so it breaks the no-training constraint. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16002v3)

ATTEMPT 13: RAGCache (GPU/host-memory knowledge tree that is sensitive to document order) | PKU/ByteDance, 2024 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It is still prefix-based. It gives 1.2–4× TTFT over vLLM and 1.1–3.5× over SGLang, so reordered documents still miss. A later paper calls prefix caching "overly strict" when retrieved chunks vary. | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12457v1)

ATTEMPT 14: Cache everything on larger tiers (CPU/disk offload, KV compression) | Self-hosted | rejected for hosted APIs by cost | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Llama-3.1-70B needs about 320 KiB per token at FP16, so 100K tokens is about 33 GB (mine, from the layer and head counts). Storing KV for every chunk in every position is far too large. Trading storage for compute costs latency and money. | [size sources](https://tensorwave.com/blog/estimating-llm-inference-memory-requirements). I found no source this session for compression ratios or load times.

ATTEMPT 15: Client-side shrinking (summarising, truncating or pruning tool output) and semantic response caches | Common practice | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Not searched, so no number is given. Rewriting history itself changes the prefix and breaks the cache, and it changes content. | none

LESSONS:
- Reuse that is exact (prefix caching) needs identical tokens, so the discount stops at the first change. Every method that goes beyond this approximates attention, because chunk KV depends on all earlier tokens.
- The best training-free results keep about 93–98% of accuracy (APE) or still recompute about 15% of tokens (CacheBlend). Those are the 2–7% and 15% limits a new answer must beat. Nothing found reaches about 100% hits with zero quality change.
- Methods that reach 90%+ TTFT savings need fine-tuning or special tokens (Block-Attention, KVLink, possibly TurboRAG), which the constraints exclude.
- All the position-independent methods need the KV tensors, so they run on a self-hosted or provider-side stack. On a third-party API only client-side prompt layout is left (attempt 2). Providers expose no hook for approximate reuse.
- Evidence is thin: reported speedups are the authors' own, and I found no independent replication or multi-hop failure study. Any new proposal needs a quality test against full recomputation.
- Approximate reuse also changes outputs, and providers bill and promise exact semantics. That is a blocker for a provider-side rollout, which I infer and did not source.
