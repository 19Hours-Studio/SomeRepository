IDEA 1: Audit-calibrated chunk KV fusion
DEFECT | FATAL | The audit can almost never certify the flip budget it promises. A zero-flip pass in 148 audits happens only 23% of the time when the true flip rate is 1%, and 48% of the time when it is 0.5%. To certify an upper bound of 2% or less with flips actually present takes about 600 audits, or 30k requests at a 2% audit rate, at a 1% true rate. At a 1.5% true rate it takes 2,150 audits, or 107k requests. That is 4–15× the stated 7.4k-request window, so low-volume workloads never get calibrated. | My Python check: (1−q)^148 and Clopper-Pearson 95% bounds. Also, 0.98^148 = 0.0503 > 0.05, so the correct zero-failure n is 149, not 148.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "answer flip" metric is confounded by noise in full recompute itself. Two full-recompute runs of the same request differ because kernels are not batch-invariant. Long answers will differ from the reference in some token almost every time, so the flip rate needs a semantic judge, and that judge's error is not in the budget. Teacher-forced scoring under the reused KV measures likelihood shift, not flips. | [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference): temperature 0 is not deterministic on vLLM/SGLang. The "80 unique outputs in 1,000 runs" figure comes only from a secondary write-up, so I did not rely on it.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 3.7× gain over prefix caching is larger than the gain the selected method reports for itself. AgentKVShift reports 2–3.5× prefill speedup over *no reuse*, and prefix caching is already better than no reuse. It was also tested only on 3B–32B models on a single A100, not on a 70B FP8 model. | [AgentKVShift, arXiv 2607.21604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21604): 1003/271 = 3.70×, against a self-reported 2–3.5× over no reuse.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The audit overhead is left out of the 271 GPU-ms figure, and including it erases the margin over CacheBlend. With 1–2% of requests run as full recompute plus shadow (1,003–1,600 + 271 GPU-ms), the cost becomes 284–308 GPU-ms. That is 0.96–1.04× CacheBlend at 15% (295 GPU-ms) and 2.33–2.54× ContextPilot, not 2.6×. | My recomputation: 271 + a·(F + 271), with a = 1–2% and F = 1,003–1,600.
DEFECT | MAJOR | It cannot run on Anthropic or OpenAI APIs, because these do not accept or return KV. So it does nothing for the hosted-API half of the question. | Mechanism: the provider owns the KV cache.
DEFECT | MINOR | A 4× PCIe Gen4 NVMe array gives about 28 GB/s, below the stated 39 GB/s upper load. It needs Gen5 drives (about 56 GB/s) or a larger DRAM hot set, which costs about 335 TB of storage per 2B stored tokens. | 39e9 / 163,840 B = 238k tokens/s; 4 × 7 GB/s = 28 GB/s.
DEFECT | MINOR | The "CacheBlend loses 47.5% F1 at 10%" figure is unsourced. The CacheBlend paper reports drops within 0.02 F1 at about 15% on RAG. | [CacheBlend, arXiv 2405.16444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.16444)
SURVIVES | 160 KiB/token (2·80·8·128 B at FP8, Llama-3-70B GQA) reproduces. 148/0.02 = 7.4k reproduces. 720/271 = 2.66 and 1003/271 = 3.70 are arithmetically correct.
RATIO | 1.0 | Against CacheBlend at 15%, the best approximate-reuse practice, once the audit is costed (0.96–1.04×). It is about 2.4× against exact-match ContextPilot.
KNOWN | partly | [CacheBlend/LMCache](https://blog.lmcache.ai/en/2026/04/01/accelerating-openclaw-agents-with-cacheblend/), [AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21604) and [ContextPilot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03475) cover the reuse mechanisms. The shadow-audit loop is standard canary practice.
NEW FACT | ContextPilot reports about a 34% hit ratio against 7% for the baseline, and up to 3× lower prefill latency. Exact-match reordering is still far from 100%. | [ContextPilot supplement](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2026/file/b0131b6ee02a00b03fc3320176fec8f5-Supplemental-Conference.pdf)

IDEA 2: Floating depth-1 override ledger
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is a strawman. A competent operator keeps mutable state at the tail anyway, as in Manus-style todo.md recitation or append-only context. Re-sending the full 8k state each call costs about $0.093–0.101 against $0.0735, so the gain is 1.27–1.38×, not 4.6×. Against the coding-agent patch chain the idea itself says it costs the same (within 4–8%). | My calculation at Sonnet pricing ($3/M, 0.1× read, 1.25× write); [Manus context engineering](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Override depth held at 1" is false in an agent loop. The 20 intermediate state values stay in the cached history inside tool outputs and assistant replies, which the design never rewrites. The interference depth the model actually sees is therefore up to 20, not 1. | 20 edits across 40 calls in its own scenario.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The headline figures do not reproduce from the stated scenario. If inline costs $0.336 with front edits on 20 of 40 calls, output costs about $0.032. The no-edit floor would then be $0.077, which is above the claimed $0.0735. The implied ledger size comes out at −1.25k tokens. | My reconstruction: 150k × 0.1 × $3/M = $0.045 per read, 150k × 1.25 × $3/M = $0.5625 per rewrite.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The gain depends on the pricing scheme. OpenAI charges no write premium and discounts cache reads by 50–75%, so the same scenario falls to about 2.4× on input cost alone, before output and before the tail-state baseline. | My calculation at 0.25× read and 1.0× write: 93.75k vs 39k token-units.
DEFECT | MINOR | There is no test of the quality claim: no sample size and no stale-value error metric. | Absent from the text.
SURVIVES | Putting the ledger after the last breakpoint keeps the prefix byte-identical. Folding for free after TTL expiry is sound.
RATIO | 1.0 | Against the append-only patch chain, which it matches within 4–8%. It is at most about 1.3× against tail-placed state.
KNOWN | partly | Manus todo.md recitation at the end of context, and the appended-edit practice of Claude Code and Aider.

IDEA 3: Context-length-gated turn collapsing
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 150-turn gate-only figure of 2.02× breaks its own ceiling. When reads are 75% of cost and all reads are cut by k = 3, the maximum is 1/(0.25 + 0.25) = 2.00×. Turns before the 20–40k threshold are not folded, so my linear-growth model gives 1.95×. | My Python model.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The mechanism assumes every call after the threshold can be folded, but only read-only or idempotent tools qualify. At a 50% foldable share, 150 turns give 1.32× (1.50× with filtering) and 40 turns give 1.18× (1.23× with filtering). The early-stop rule cuts the effective k further. | My model with fold = 0.5.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline ignores best current practice. Anthropic's Programmatic Tool Calling already folds multi-tool steps into code and filters outputs before they enter context, and parallel tool calls already batch independent reads. The comparisons at 1.20× (CodeAct) and 0.83× (observation masking) are modelled, not measured. JetBrains measured observation masking at about half the cost of the raw agent. | [Anthropic, advanced tool use](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use); [Complexity Trap, arXiv 2508.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433)
DEFECT | MINOR | Writes with a 1-hour TTL cost 2× base, not 1.25×. That is a 60% write premium on long batches, which costs about 9–12% of total if writes are 15–20% of spend, and the text does not cost it. | [Anthropic prompt caching docs](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)
DEFECT | MINOR | The gate price C × 0.1 × p leaves out the script-writing output tokens (5× p on output). | Mechanism.
SURVIVES | 40-turn gate-only 1.48× is close to my 1.42× at a 50% read share.
RATIO | 1.1 | Against programmatic tool calling plus parallel calls, which already capture most of the same folding and filtering.
KNOWN | yes | [Anthropic Programmatic Tool Calling / code execution with MCP](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use), and CodeAct.

IDEA 4: Cost-optimal patch folding
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "≤2× of optimal" ski-rental guarantee does not hold here, because the buy price changes over time. Appended conversation and earlier-positioned patches keep raising the cost of rewriting from the earliest patched position. Counterexample: a 200-token patch near the tail has B₀ = 1.25k; over 200 turns, as the context grows faster than the rent accumulates, break-even never fires. The rent paid reaches 4k, which is 3.2× OPT and unbounded as the session lengthens. | My construction under its own cost model.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Against the append-only patch chain, folding only adds cost, so its value is quality and not price. Idea 2 itself puts patch-chain cost within 4–8% of this. | Folds are extra rewrites.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The ratio depends on the pricing scheme. The 120k copilot case has an upper bound of 4.1× on Anthropic, 2.0× on OpenAI at a 0.25× read rate, and 1.5× at a 0.5× read rate, so 2.66× cannot be reached on OpenAI. | My calculation: 30 reads × 120k plus 15 edits at a mean position of 60k.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 96.4% hit rate leaves out the cold write. Counting it gives about 90% for the 25k case. | 1 − 25k/250k.
SURVIVES | 1.52× for the 25k case sits under my 1.83× bound. Folding for free after TTL expiry is sound. The depth-cap trigger is enforceable for in-document sections.
RATIO | 0.95 | Against the append-only patch chain, the best current practice. It is 1.5–2.7× only against the inline-rewrite strawman.
KNOWN | partly | Appended diff edits in coding agents (Claude Code, Aider). Ski-rental break-even folding is a textbook result. It largely duplicates Idea 2.
