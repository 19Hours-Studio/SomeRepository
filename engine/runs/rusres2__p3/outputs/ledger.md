=== LEDGER ===
KIND: cost

Nornickel does not publish cost per tonne of Ni-eq. I found no figure, and the output data needed to derive one is not in the facts. The unit costs below are therefore capex or cost proxies. The cost splits are my estimates, anchored only on the brief's capital ≈37% and foundation losses of 1–2%. They are not reported figures. Group cash opex was $5.1bn in 2024 and $5.3bn in 2023 (F61, F62).

ARCHETYPE A (Talnakh/Oktyabrskoye rich and cuprous ore, underground, concentrator expansion on domestic equipment) | cost ≈1.8bn RUB per extra Mtpa of concentrator capacity (47bn RUB for +2.6 Mtpa, 2017). The next step is 10→18 Mtpa by 2030–31 with no capex published (F59, F349) | reserve grade for the Talnakh probable ore is about 0.94% Ni, 1.64% Cu and 5.2 g/t PGM (F1)
SHARES: energy 8 | labour 18 | inbound logistics 4 | outbound logistics 4 | processing 12 | capital 37 | other 17
LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes 33% of the cost
Timing: the 2017 upgrade is the benchmark. The third start-up complex has been switched entirely to Russian equipment, which resets the schedule (F60, F357). Nornickel lost about half its spare-parts suppliers, and 2023 output dipped during the fleet switch (F63, F64). The installed underground fleet is Western. The BelAZ-75855 truck is still on factory-ground tests (F184–186).

ARCHETYPE B (Norilsk-1 disseminated ore, 0.25% Ni, and Maslovskoye, 0.3% Ni with 5 g/t Pd; bulk mining plus pre-concentration by XRT sorting) | cost not published. Ore grade is a third to a half of Talnakh rich ore, so cost per Ni-eq tonne is higher than for A (F2, F7) | Selebi XRT rejects 15.2% of mass and claims about 98% recovery, but that is an unaudited release. The rejection may be a share of total feed, which gives ×1.156 and not ×1.094 (F350, F356). Sorting cut tin unit cost by about 10% at Renison (F331).
SHARES: energy 10 | labour 16 | inbound logistics 3 | outbound logistics 4 | processing 22 | capital 37 | other 8
LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes 33% of the cost
The second-largest term, processing, rises as grade falls. Maslovskoye has no investment decision or capex, and its GKZ approval dates from 2018 (F7).

ARCHETYPE C (Nadezhda smelter and Sulphur Programme, plus Talnakh concentrator availability) | Sulphur Programme: $2.5bn (2018) → $3.5bn (2019) → up to $4.3bn, a rise of 40–70%. Delay was at least 2 years, with foreign contractor withdrawal given as a cause. Stage 1 cost about 180bn RUB (~$2bn) and started October 2023 (F57, F58) | target is a 75% SO2 cut
SHARES: energy 14 | labour 14 | inbound logistics 6 | outbound logistics 2 | processing 18 | capital 37 | other 9
LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes 33% of the cost
No ore-type recovery figures for Nornickel were found. Polyus offers a comparison: group recovery was 82–83%, and Natalka lost recovery during ramp-up (F67, F287).

ARCHETYPE D (structures on Norilsk ground: thermometric boreholes, pile and strain monitoring, thermosyphons or adjustable foundations, maintenance) | foundation losses are 1–2% of full cost per tonne Ni-eq (the brief's figure, not sourced) | Nornickel's post-2020 budget for Norilsk infrastructure safety was reportedly 100bn RUB (F263). Norilsk pile bearing capacity is projected to fall by more than 55% around the 2040s (F89, press summary of the study). Thaw deepens about 1 cm/yr (F85).
SHARES (of the structure-loss cost): capital (replacement and new foundations) 50 | labour (inspection and repair) 20 | energy 2 | inbound logistics (materials) 13 | processing 0 | other 15
LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes 45% of the cost
Thaw is not the only cause of these losses:
- **Ambarnaya (2020):** Rostekhnadzor found no melting permafrost under the foundation. It found piles set in permafrost instead of bedrock, tank-bottom corrosion and poor maintenance (F257, F258).
- **Concentrator collapse (20 Feb 2021):** defects found in 2018 went unrepaired for 2.5 years (F264).
- **Share of deformed buildings:** about 35% in Norilsk and Talnakh in one study, and about 60% from an emergencies official. The two figures conflict (F267, F268).
- **Design standard:** SP 25.13330.2020 Amendment 3, effective 14 Jun 2026, lets designers account for ground-temperature dynamics over the whole life cycle. Before that, projected warming was not in the design assumptions (F92, F252).
- **Thermosyphon limits:** they work only in the cold season. The usable window has shrunk by almost a month in 30 years at Salekhard. Thermosyphon cooling is weakening on Qinghai–Tibet embankments (F205, F358, F321).

Comparators:
- **Raglan:** two 3 MW turbines cover about 10% of power and save about 4 million L of diesel a year. Total site use is about 50 million L a year (F312).
- **Aitik:** trolley-assist for SEK 300M cut diesel by 5,500 m³ a year (F328).
- **Kiruna:** the 1365 m level cost SEK 12.4bn. Its build time is disputed, from 4.5 to 10 years (F329).
- **Dudinka–Murmansk:** I did not build an archetype for it. Dudinka handled 4 Mt in 2022 against a 30-year maximum of 3.8 Mt, and RUB 26bn of upgrades are planned. No delivery cost per tonne was found (F166).

Where the problem sits: capital with contractors and equipment gone, then grade (processing cost per Ni-eq tonne rises with B), then maintenance and design faults in D, with thaw a smaller and unproven share. The 1–2% foundation share caps the direct cost gain from D. The main case for D is the avoided cost of collapse and spill. The Ambarnaya claim was 146bn RUB (F262).
