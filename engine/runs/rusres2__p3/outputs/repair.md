=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Gate Talnakh-3 on refill
MECHANISM: Anchor: the third Talnakh complex (TOF-3) is approved only together with a costed plan to refill the Norilsk concentrator (NOF) capacity it frees, and the pair is judged on full cost per added tonne of recovered Ni-eq. Ni-eq here counts PGM, not just Ni. The repaired refill is Norilsk-1 disseminated ore (+4 Mtpa, toward the 7 Mtpa already planned) plus a costed NOF life-extension package. That package keeps the new 9 Mtpa disseminated-ore concentrator deferred, and Maslovskoye comes second. In Ni-eq Maslovskoye is 2.66% (about 76% of its value is PGM), the same as Talnakh ore and 1.6× Norilsk-1, so the earlier "600k RUB per tonne Ni" ranking was wrong. Base case: the pair adds about 96 kt Ni-eq/yr at about $6,800/t, against $7,900/t for Nornickel's plan to build the new concentrator now (median ratio 0.88, range 0.76–1.00). If the Talnakh mines cannot supply extra ore, TOF-3 alone just moves ore and gains about 6 kt/yr from better recovery, at $26,800/t. The gate then reverses the decision: the pair costs $9,700/t and the plan $11,800/t. XRT sorting is kept only as an option once NOF is full. It pays only if the sorter keeps at least about 97% of the metal (+7–13 kt/yr at $7,400–10,200/t).
VALUE: The pair costs about 0.82–0.88× the best real alternative per added tonne of Ni-eq. Where TOF-3 would only move ore, it avoids a step costing 2.8× more per tonne, and it saves about 65 bn RUB of upfront capex (base case) while foreign equipment is unavailable.

IDEA 2
NAME: Availability as capacity
MECHANISM: Anchor: one parts registry pools spares across the Western-built underground fleet, ranks failure modes by Ni-eq tonnes lost, and sends the worst part families to domestic reverse-engineering and casting first. The stock is re-optimised every quarter from measured mean time between failures (MTBF). With 6 mines and a 9-month grey-import lead time, a pooled stock is 46–62% the size of separate site stocks for the same service. With the same budget, the chance a part is on hand when needed rises from 80% to 99.5% (Poisson model). Ranking by Ni-eq sends the first effort to the rich-ore fleets: one lost LHD-hour there costs about 6.3 t Ni-eq, against 1.2 t on Norilsk-1 ore (5.2×). That makes the programme 1.7× more effective than ranking by machine-hours. Base case: availability rises about 7 percentage points, output by about 29 kt Ni-eq/yr (3.6% of the group), at about $2,700 per added tonne. Gains count only while the mines, not the concentrators, are the limit (taken as 60%). It breaks even if domestic parts last at least 0.30× as long as the original parts.
VALUE: Each added tonne costs about $2,700, roughly 0.35× the $7,300–8,100 of a brownfield capacity stage. Group full cost falls about 2.6% within 6–18 months, which is real but not a 1.5× cut.

NOTES

**Idea 1: anchor kept** (conditional approval, pair-level cost per added tonne).
- **Numbers (scripts in `calc/`).**
  - NOF recovers 70.6–71.9% of Ni and Talnakh 83.2–87.9% (Nornickel AR2020, found by search). Cu and PGM recoveries are my assumptions; no source found.
  - Ore grades are from F1, F2 and F7. The group comes to about 789 kt Ni-eq. Full cost is about $10,260/t Ni-eq: $5.1bn opex (F62) plus capex at 37%. Group output is from my memory and needs checking; modelled revenue of $12.6bn matches reported revenue.
  - Input ranges: TOF-3 capex 70–130 bn RUB, NOF life extension 15–60 bn RUB, new 9 Mtpa section 60–130 bn RUB. None of these were found in sources.
- **Baselines.**
  - Nornickel's 2021 plan: a new disseminated-ore concentrator, with NOF retired (F359).
  - TOF-3 alone.
  - XRT-only, the cheapest option.
- **Timing.** TOF-3 runs from about the 2019 decision to 2030–31, about 11 years, against the 2017 modernisation's few years. Refill with Norilsk-1 lines up with the development to 730 m launched in 2024 (F360). Deferring the new concentrator removes exposure to the Sulphur-Programme-type slip of at least 2 years and +40–70% cost.
- **Consistency checks.**
  - NOF capacity of 8.5 Mtpa is assumed, not sourced. If it is 7.5 Mtpa, the Maslovskoye slot disappears.
  - The 4 Mtpa Norilsk-1 refill fits within the 7 Mtpa cap (F360).
  - The +2.8 Mtpa of new Talnakh ore is the swing factor (results above cover both cases).
  - Extra concentrate must stay within the Nadezhda SO2-capture capacity (not checked).
  - Pile check (approximate SP 25.13330 adfreeze values): 1 cm/yr deeper thaw cuts pile capacity by only 2.3% by 2050. Ground warming of 0.02–0.05 K/yr cuts it by 14–62%. That is consistent with F89's figure of more than 55%.
- **Hazards.** NOF is where the 2021 collapse happened, and that collapse came from defects left unrepaired for 2.5 years, not from thaw (F264). The life-extension package must therefore make the time to close a structural defect a tracked target of 6 months or less. Thermosyphons should go only where Nornickel's 2050 borehole forecast shows ground warming; their working season is already shrinking (F358, F321).
- **Deciding unknown and experiment.** The unknown is the NOF life-extension cost for 15+ years; the break-even is 90 bn RUB. Run a structural and thermometric survey: about 60 foundation points stratified by building and ground type, with thermistor strings at 10–15 points tied to the five 2022 boreholes (F9).
- **Prior art.** Nornickel's 2019 plan already pairs TOF-3 with freeing NOF. This idea adds the approval gate, valuing PGM in Ni-eq, and refurbishing NOF instead of replacing it.
- **Verdict: WORKS**, but modestly: about 1% on group full cost.

**Idea 2: anchor kept** (pooling, Ni-eq ranking, reverse-engineering the worst parts).
- **Numbers.**
  - Current availability 0.65–0.80 and the share of downtime spent waiting for parts (20–50%) are assumptions. No published availability figure (KTG) for Nornickel was found.
  - The output dip after the equipment switch is F64; the loss of half of suppliers is F63.
  - Rich-ore grades (3% Ni, 4% Cu, 6.8 g/t Pd+Pt) are assumed.
- **Baseline.** A brownfield stage on the Talnakh-2017 model: 47 bn RUB × inflation × 1.4–1.7 gives $7,300–8,100/t. TOF-3 alone is $6,660/t. Full BelAZ replacement is a further option, but its 45 t truck is still in factory tests (F185).
- **Sensitivity.** Across 729 combinations, the cost is $2,100–11,800/t (10th–90th percentile), median $3,200.
- **Consistency checks.** Gains count only while the mines are the limit. When TOF-3 arrives the plants gain slack, which helps this idea. Hoist capacity and smelter SO2 limits were not checked.
- **Hazards.** Reverse-engineered brake and steering parts are safety-critical, so leave them out or certify them under TR CU 010. There is also IP and sanctions exposure.
- **Deciding unknown and experiment.** The unknown is how long domestic parts last against the originals (break-even 0.30×). Run a paired field trial: about 30 domestic and 30 original parts of the top 3 families on matched LHDs for 6 months, logging hours to failure.
- **Prior art.** The Nornickel–BelAZ spares stockpile and criticality-ranked spares are known practice. Pooling across sites plus ranking by Ni-eq is the addition.
- **Verdict: WORKS** as the cheapest increment. It is **NICHE** if judged against the 1.5× target.
