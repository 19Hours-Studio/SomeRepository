For the producing Norilsk/Taimyr nickel-copper-PGM complex, which changes to mining or development order, processing of lower-grade and disseminated ore, equipment substitution, or foundation design and monitoring would cut full cost per tonne of nickel-equivalent delivered and raise recovery and structure life? The comparison is the current Norilsk practice (Talnakh concentrator expansion, Sulphur Programme), Raglan and Kiruna/Aitik. It must not overstate thaw where engineering faults (Ambarnaya, the 2021 concentrator collapse) were the cause.
Cases: Talnakh/Oktyabrskoye rich and cuprous ore; Norilsk-1 disseminated ore; Maslovskoye (low grade, rich in PGM); Nadezhda and Talnakh processing; Dudinka-Murmansk delivery; ageing industrial and residential structures on Norilsk ground.
A good answer: (a) full cost per tonne of nickel-equivalent delivered, where capital is about 37% of the total and foundation losses about 1–2%; (b) years from investment decision to added output or capacity, against Talnakh modernisation (47bn RUB for +34%) and the Sulphur Programme's slip of at least 2 years; (c) recovery for each ore type, plant availability, and design life of foundations against the 1 cm/yr deepening of thaw and Nornickel's thermometric borehole forecasts to 2050.
The binding constraint to attack: Sustaining and processing capital with foreign contractors and equipment gone (Sulphur Programme cost +40–70%), falling grade as Maslovskoye and disseminated ore come in, and the life of structures where maintenance and design faults count as much as thaw.
This is part 3 of 3 of a larger question (context, not instructions): Russia wants to develop and extract minerals and energy in its permafrost regions at the lowest full cost, in the shortest time from investment decision to first production, and with the highest effectiveness. The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: Availability as capacity
MECHANISM: Anchor: one parts registry pools spares across the Western-built underground fleet, ranks failure modes by Ni-eq tonnes lost, and sends the worst part families to domestic reverse-engineering and casting first. The stock is re-optimised every quarter from measured mean time between failures (MTBF). With 6 mines and a 9-month grey-import lead time, a pooled stock is 46–62% the size of separate site stocks for the same service. With the same budget, the chance a part is on hand when needed rises from 80% to 99.5% (Poisson model). Ranking by Ni-eq sends the first effort to the rich-ore fleets: one lost LHD-hour there costs about 6.3 t Ni-eq, against 1.2 t on Norilsk-1 ore (5.2×). That makes the programme 1.7× more effective than ranking by machine-hours. Base case: availability rises about 7 percentage points, output by about 29 kt Ni-eq/yr (3.6% of the group), at about $2,700 per added tonne. Gains count only while the mines, not the concentrators, are the limit (taken as 60%). It breaks even if domestic parts last at least 0.30× as long as the original parts.
VALUE: Each added tonne costs about $2,700, roughly 0.35× the $7,300–8,100 of a brownfield capacity stage. Group full cost falls about 2.6% within 6–18 months, which is real but not a 1.5× cut.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The headline "80% to 99.5% at the same budget" does not come out of a Poisson model. For 6 sites, each held at 80% and pooled with the same total stock, the pooled service is 77–95%. (My python check (scipy, λ solved so each site sits at exactly 80%, S = 1–11) gives 0.770, 0.860, 0.894 … 0.949. Even at the most favourable integer case (λ=8, S=10) a site at 81.6% pools to only 96.1%.)
- MAJOR: The "break-even at 0.30× part life" ignores change-out downtime. Parts that last 0.3× as long mean 3.3× as many replacements. Each one costs a repair time that pooled stock does not remove. (Extra downtime = d·(1/0.3 − 1) = 2.33·d. If change-outs of the targeted part families already cost d ≥ 3 percentage points of hours, the whole +7-point gain is gone. That points to a break-even life nearer 0.6–0.8×. (Here d is my assumption, but the idea leaves the term out entirely.))
- MAJOR: The comparison ignores what Nornickel is actually doing: replacing the Western fleet rather than keeping it running. The registry's base shrinks every year, and grey-import parts compete with Chinese or Belarusian OEM supply. (The BelAZ–Nornickel joint venture for underground machines was agreed in September 2026. MoAZ-4076 LHDs and 6 MoAZ-75851 trucks are on trial in Norilsk ([Moscow Times](https://ru.themoscowtimes.com/2026/09/07/nornikel-i-belaz-dogovorilis-o-sozdaniya-sp-dlya-proizvodstva-podzemnoy-tekhniki-a205476)). The $2,700 per tonne is a gain that fades over a few years, while the $7,300–8,100 brownfield figure buys capacity for 20 years or more, so the 0.35× ratio compares unlike things.)
- MAJOR: The decisive inputs are assumed, not measured: the 9-month lead time, the 7-point availability gain, mines being the limit 60% of the time, and 1.7× effectiveness. Hoisting, backfill and ventilation limits at the Talnakh shafts would cut how much extra LHD time becomes extra ore. (I found no published lead times or availability figures (KTG) for Nornickel's fleet. At 0.3 instead of 0.6 for mines being the limit, +29 kt drops to about 15 kt, and $2,700/t rises to about $5,400/t.)
- SURVIVES: The loss per LHD-hour holds: 6.3 t means about 110 t/h at 6.5% Ni-eq and 0.87 recovery, which is plausible. The 5.2× ratio to Norilsk-1 also holds (my range 4.9–5.7× including recovery). The group total of about 806 kt Ni-eq matches 2026-level prices (my figure is 799 kt before Rh, Au and Co).
- RATIO: 1.01 | After correcting pooling (to about 95%), the share of time mines are the limit, change-out downtime and the shrinking Western fleet, the group full-cost cut is about 1%, not 2.6%.
- KNOWN: partly | Nornickel already buys centrally, plans to replace its fleet with suppliers from "friendly countries" (2022), and has the BelAZ joint venture (2026). Pooling spares by criticality across a fleet is standard practice (multi-echelon / METRIC models).

IDEA 2
NAME: Gate Talnakh-3 on refill
MECHANISM: Anchor: the third Talnakh complex (TOF-3) is approved only together with a costed plan to refill the Norilsk concentrator (NOF) capacity it frees, and the pair is judged on full cost per added tonne of recovered Ni-eq. Ni-eq here counts PGM, not just Ni. The repaired refill is Norilsk-1 disseminated ore (+4 Mtpa, toward the 7 Mtpa already planned) plus a costed NOF life-extension package. That package keeps the new 9 Mtpa disseminated-ore concentrator deferred, and Maslovskoye comes second. In Ni-eq Maslovskoye is 2.66% (about 76% of its value is PGM), the same as Talnakh ore and 1.6× Norilsk-1, so the earlier "600k RUB per tonne Ni" ranking was wrong. Base case: the pair adds about 96 kt Ni-eq/yr at about $6,800/t, against $7,900/t for Nornickel's plan to build the new concentrator now (median ratio 0.88, range 0.76–1.00). If the Talnakh mines cannot supply extra ore, TOF-3 alone just moves ore and gains about 6 kt/yr from better recovery, at $26,800/t. The gate then reverses the decision: the pair costs $9,700/t and the plan $11,800/t. XRT sorting is kept only as an option once NOF is full. It pays only if the sorter keeps at least about 97% of the metal (+7–13 kt/yr at $7,400–10,200/t).
VALUE: The pair costs about 0.82–0.88× the best real alternative per added tonne of Ni-eq. Where TOF-3 would only move ore, it avoids a step costing 2.8× more per tonne, and it saves about 65 bn RUB of upfront capex (base case) while foreign equipment is unavailable.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The comparison case is wrong: Nornickel already ties TOF-3 commissioning to its ore-mining forecast and already plans to refill NOF with South Cluster ore. The "gate" is therefore current practice, and the $7,900/t "build the new concentrator now" case is a 2021 plan that the NOF reconstruction route has replaced. (The 2023 annual report says TOF-3 commissioning "is being clarified in line with the forecast ore-mining schedule" ([AR2023 South Cluster](https://ar2023.nornickel.ru/pdf/ar/ru/strategic-report_strategic-investment-projects_south-cluster.pdf)). NOF capacity "will be freed up for the treatment of the feed from the South Cluster project" ([AR2020](https://ar2020.nornickel.ru/en/strategic-report/key-investment-projects/processing.html)). NOF reconstruction is listed as a stage of the South Cluster ([fedpress](https://fedpress.ru/news/24/economy/2681340)). Against the real plan the ratio is about 1.0, not 0.82–0.88.)
- MAJOR: Maslovskoye at 2.66% Ni-eq with 76% from PGM only holds at high PGM prices. "The same as Talnakh ore" is true only for the average of the whole Talnakh field; rich ore is 2.2–3× higher. (My python check, using Maslovskoye at 0.3% Ni, 0.5% Cu, 5.0 g/t Pd, 2.0 g/t Pt ([AR2019](https://ar2019.nornickel.ru/en/business-overview/mineral-base/growth-projects.html)), gives 1.97% Ni-eq with 66% PGM at 2024 average prices, and 2.81% with 72% PGM only at about $1,300 Pd and $1,600 Pt. Talnakh rich ore (2.66% Ni, 3.26% Cu, 8.0 g/t PGM) comes out at 5.9–7.1%. The 2.66% figure equals the rich-ore Ni grade, which suggests the two were mixed up.)
- MAJOR: The refill feed is lower-grade and is recovered at a low rate. The proven Norilsk-1 reserve covers only about 4–5 years at 7 Mtpa, after which the feed is probable ore at a lower grade. The idea's 1.6× ratio uses proven-reserve grades, and none of its per-tonne costs carry NOF's recovery of about 71% Ni. (Proven: 32 Mt at 0.25% Ni and 4.56 g/t PGM. Probable (2022 statement): 104 Mt at 0.22% Ni and 3.85 g/t. Probable ore is 1.09–1.52% Ni-eq, against 1.29–1.81% for proven ([AR2023](https://ar2023.nornickel.ru/en/business-overview.1.html)). NOF Ni recovery fell from 71.9% to 70.6% over 2018–2020, against 87.9% at TOF ([AR2020](https://ar2020.nornickel.ru/en/business-overview/operational-performance/norilsk.html)).)
- MAJOR: The "costed NOF life-extension package" is an assumed figure for the district's oldest plant. Part of that plant collapsed in February 2021 during repair work to strengthen the structure: 3 killed, blamed on safety violations, not on thaw. Deferring the 9 Mtpa concentrator moves capex later in time rather than saving it, so the "65 bn RUB saved" is really a timing gain in NPV. (Ore-transfer building collapse at NOF on 20 Feb 2021 ([ArcticToday](https://www.arctictoday.com/three-people-die-in-partial-collapse-of-nornickels-arctic-processing-plant)). Earlier engineering literature puts NOF fixed-asset wear above 50% ([SPMI](https://pmi.spmi.ru/pmi/article/download/8479/6404)).)
- MAJOR: The years from decision to output have slipped far beyond anything the idea plans for. The 96 kt/yr cannot start before TOF-3 does, so the idea's own decision-to-output metric gets worse, not better. (TOF-3 was due in 2023 (AR2018/2019), then 2028 ([Interfax, Feb 2025](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/109729/)), and is now 2030–31 ([www1.ru, Mar 2026](https://www1.ru/news/2026/03/06/tretii-puskovoi-kompleks-talnaxskoi-obogatitelnoi-fabriki-v-norilske-postroiat-polnostiu-na-otecestvennom-oborudovanii.html)). That is 7–8 years of slip, against the ≥2 years given for the Sulphur Programme.)
- SURVIVES: The Maslovskoye/Norilsk-1 Ni-eq ratio of about 1.5–1.6× holds at every price set I tried (1.53–1.55×). Maslovskoye coming second behind Norilsk-1 is sound.
- RATIO: 1.00 | Once the comparison is Nornickel's actual sequence (TOF-3 timed to mine output, then NOF refilled with South Cluster ore), the idea adds no new step. The cost difference falls within the grade and PGM-price spread.
- KNOWN: yes | Nornickel South Cluster / Medvezhy Ruchey: TOF-3 to 18 Mtpa, all Talnakh ore to TOF, NOF refilled with Norilsk-1 ore ([AR2021 strategic projects](https://ar2021.nornickel.ru/en/strategic-report/main-strategic-projects.html)).
