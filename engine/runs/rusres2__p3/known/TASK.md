THE 4 IDEAS:
IDEA 1
NAME: Pair Talnakh expansion with backfill
MECHANISM: The Talnakh third complex is approved only together with a plan to refill the Norilsk concentrator capacity it frees. That plan is XRT-sorted Norilsk-1 disseminated ore, and later Maslovskoye ore. Capex is then computed per added tonne of contained Ni for the pair, not per tonne of ore. The closest earlier attempt is the 2017 Talnakh upgrade, which added capacity at one plant without counting the other plant's ore loss. The same booking error sank an earlier answer, and this pairing avoids it. The deciding quantity is Ni recovery from sorted disseminated ore at the old plant: XRT raises feed grade only about 1.09–1.16× (F350, F356, unaudited).
VALUE: It turns a 14–24bn RUB step that adds almost nothing into real added Ni-eq, ranked honestly against Maslovskoye's roughly 600k RUB per annual t Ni.

IDEA 2
NAME: Freed Norilsk concentrator, disseminated feed
MECHANISM: Talnakh's expansion to 18 Mtpa takes all Talnakh ore (F59), which frees capacity at the Norilsk concentrator (7.6 Mt in 2020). That capacity is refilled with Norilsk-1 and Maslovskoye ore instead of building a new plant. A blend is chosen each quarter to keep flotation feed in the range the existing circuits have recovered. The closest attempt is the 2017 Talnakh upgrade at 18bn RUB/Mtpa. It needed new capacity, and this idea needs none. The deciding quantity is the incremental cash margin per tonne of feed, which is ore value × recovery minus mining and processing cost, while concentrator capital stays sunk.
VALUE: Lower-grade ore enters production with almost no concentrator capital, removing much of the 37% capital share of Ni-eq cost on this path, and the lead time is set by mine development, not plant build.

IDEA 3
NAME: Availability as capacity
MECHANISM: A fleet-wide parts registry pools spares across the Western installed base, ranks failure modes by lost Ni-eq tonnes, and sends the worst parts first to domestic reverse-engineering and casting. Spares stock is re-optimised quarterly from measured mean time between failures. The closest earlier attempt is wholesale fleet replacement with BelAZ, whose 45 t truck is still on factory-ground tests and which caused the 2023 output dip. This idea instead keeps the proven fleet running while substitution matures. The deciding quantity is the gain in concentrator and mine availability in percentage points; each point at 10 Mtpa is about 0.1 Mtpa of capacity with no new build.
VALUE: Delivers added output within months rather than the 3–4 years of a new concentrator stage, and cuts the schedule risk from lost suppliers.

IDEA 4
NAME: Defect-to-repair deadline register
MECHANISM: Every defect found at the reused Norilsk concentrator and its tailings line is logged with a mandatory repair deadline set by structural risk. The register is fed by pile strain gauges and the thermometric boreholes, and any lapse is escalated to Rostekhnadzor. Thaw readings change foundation design only where the boreholes show warming. The closest attempt is post-spill monitoring (F263), which detected faults but did not fix them, as with the 2.5-year unrepaired defects behind the 2021 collapse. The deciding quantity is the median time from defect detection to repair, which should fall from about 30 months to under 6.
VALUE: The plant whose reuse saves the capital in disseminated-ore production lasts its design life. The register targets the collapse and spill losses that actually occurred (Ambarnaya claim 146bn RUB) rather than overstating thaw.
