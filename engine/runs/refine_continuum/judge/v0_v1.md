**Answer B is the better one, by a clear margin.**

**Where they agree.** Both pick the same core idea. Model weights sit in SRAM that is hybrid-bonded under the compute die, and HBM holds only the KV cache. Both target MoE decode, report tokens per MW or kW at a per-user speed floor, give kill criteria, and admit training is not a win. A is not wrong. It is compact and honest, and it gives a sensible deciding test: kill the design if a fabric traversal takes more than 3 µs or a layer more than 12 µs.

**Where B is stronger, for someone who would act on it:**

- **Workload and metrics.** B states its target workload precisely: DeepSeek-V4-Pro's config at 128K context, also checked at 8K and 1M. It explains why each metric defines "best", and its metric table includes cost per token and tokens per HBM stack.
- **Named blocks with sourced numbers.** B names its blocks (C-Die, W-Tier, K-Mem, Link) and derives each figure from a basis. The SRAM capacity chain runs from macro density through ECC, keep-out and repair derating to usable GB. The 2.0 kW package power is broken into its parts. The I/O bond count per tile is checked.
- **Roofline arithmetic that checks out.** I verified B's per-stage figures:
  - 14.3 GB of weights at 2 × 256 TB/s is about 28 µs.
  - 256 users × 1.69 MB of KV at 20 TB/s is about 22 µs.
  - The resulting stage time gives the stated tokens/s per user.
- **Breadth of comparison.** B compares against Rubin, MI455X, TPU 8i, Ironwood, Rubin + LPX and Cerebras CS-4. It covers several per-user speed tiers and three context lengths, and states where Continuum's lead is small: 1.13× at 8K, roughly equal on prefill, and behind CS-4 on peak single-stream speed. A compares against fewer designs and fewer operating points.
- **Sensitivity and tests.** B's sensitivity table shows exactly how the claim collapses, for example a 0.8× result at 128 TB/s of SRAM bandwidth or 1.0× at 20 µs stage latency. Its six tests each name a tool and a kill criterion.
- **Risks and facts.** B quantifies supply risk (SoIC bonding volume, CoWoS, HBM stacks per GW), yield, thermals and IR drop. It also separates facts, assumptions and model outputs explicitly.
- **Self-correction.** B corrects weaknesses that appear in A's approach. A's "match the ridge point to MLA" rationale no longer holds for V4-style compressed attention, and B is also more careful about its own "GPUs cannot reach this" claims.

B's main weakness is that it builds on many guessed power figures. It flags these and warns the reader to trust the ratios, not the absolute numbers.

WINNER: B
MARGIN: clearly

Its blocks are derived from stated bases, and its roofline arithmetic is checkable and holds up.
