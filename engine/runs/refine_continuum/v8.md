# Continuum-7: a weight-stationary 3D-SRAM decode accelerator for frontier MoE, built for the interactivity tier GPUs can't reach

## 0. Verdict

### 0.1 What changed this round
The workload is DeepSeek-V4-Pro decode at 128K context, with draft acceptance p = 0.8. Each cell is output tok/s per provisioned W, as a ratio to the best Rubin setup at that per-user speed floor. Rubin chooses its own voltage, speculation depth and layout in every cell. "GPU can't" means no Rubin configuration reaches that speed.

Continuum itself stays at **52.4 tok/s/W up to 1,158 tok/s/user**. It falls to 47.6 at ≥1,500 and 43.2 at ≥2,000 (`run8b.txt`).

**Why the number rose.** No hardware changed.
- Round 7 gave GPUs a per-layer latency 14× lower than the best measured GPU result on a large MoE.
- Replacing that assumption with measurements, for GPUs and for static-schedule chips alike, raises the honest bulk gain from 1.17× to 1.43×. It also turns the speed tier from "1.6–2.5× better" into "uncontested by GPUs".
- The symmetric case stays in the table as the conservative bound. It is what Continuum gets if GPU software closes a 14× latency gap.

**Levers I tried that did not raise the ratio:**
- **More packages per layer (g = 8 or 12):** −14% and −38% at every tier, because collectives and attention-weight re-reads grow (`run8c.txt`). g = 6 stays optimal.
- **FP4 core attention for every system:** raises Continuum from 52.4 to 58.1 tok/s/W, but Rubin also rises from 44.9 to 52.4. The ratio falls to 1.11 at ≥200 (symmetric) and is unchanged at 1.42 (calibrated) (`run8d.txt`). It is worth doing on any hardware, but it is not an edge for Continuum.

**The lever that did help: prefill on the pod (§4.4).** Round 7 left prefill on GPUs and charged no prefill energy. That overstated the deployable gain. With 3 uncached prompt tokens per output token, GPU prefill dilutes 1.17× to **1.05×**. Running chunked prefill on the Continuum pod restores **1.17×** (1.25× calibrated).

**Bottom line.**
- Continuum-7 is a credible best-in-class design for one job: serving frontier sparse MoE models (≥1T parameters) at 500–1,150 tok/s/user, at the energy per token GPUs need for 200–300 tok/s/user.
- In bulk serving it gains 1.3–1.4× on the same node against shipping Rubin. It loses to Rubin Ultra if Nvidia's 600 kW figure holds at full clocks.
- It is **not** shown to be best overall. The realisation fraction (§0.3) is unmeasured, and so is everything else that rests on Continuum's model.

### 0.3 The two unknowns that decide it

**Latency realisation.** This is now partly measured, at the level of the machine class (§2.1).
- Static-schedule SRAM machines (LPX, Cerebras) show per-layer step times of ≤ 5–27 µs, in line with Continuum's assumption.
- GPUs on large MoE show ~140–240 µs.
- Continuum itself is unmeasured. Kill test 1 decides it.

**Throughput realisation.** This is unmeasured.
- GPUs realise ~0.19 of my model on InferenceX V4-Pro (`calib7.txt`; unchanged at τ = 140 µs, `run8e.txt`).
- Break-even: Continuum must realise ≥ 70% of the GPUs' fraction (calibrated) or ≥ 85% (symmetric) at ≥200.
- In the speed tier no GPU baseline exists. There the competitor is Rubin+LPX, and break-even is ~15% of LPX's realisation.

## 1. Target workloads and metrics
- **Workload:** decode, plus optionally prefill (§4.4), of DeepSeek-V4-Pro:
  - 1.6T total / 49B active parameters, 61 layers;
  - 384 experts, top-6, plus 1 shared;
  - compressed sparse attention (CSA) on 30 layers, 128×-compressed attention on 31;
  - FP8 KV cache.
- **Conditions:** 128K and 1M context; p = 0.8 (code and tools) or 0.6 (prose).
- **Stress case:** a 6.2T model with the same active parameters.
- **Out of scope:** training; edge; models under ~200B, where SRAM-only systems win.

| Metric | Why it defines "best" |
|---|---|
| Output tok/s per provisioned MW at a per-user speed floor (200…2,000) | Sites are power-limited, and the floor prevents gaming at low speed. Every system picks its own draft depth k (0–7), microbatch count m (1–4), layout and voltage (0.55–0.75 V plus vendor Max-Q). |
| The same metric end to end, with prefill energy charged | What a buyer actually pays |
| Max tok/s/user; tok/s per HBM stack | The agent/reasoning speed tier; HBM is supply-limited through 2027 |

**Gate.** Every system gets one voltage/frequency law and one rule for how chip power savings reach the slot. Latency per layer is either symmetric (bound) or **taken from measurements of its machine class** (headline).

## 3. Architecture (hardware unchanged from round 7)

### 3.1 Package blocks
- **C-Die × 4** (N2, ~750 mm², 144 tiles each):
  - each tile: a ~111 TF FP4 tensor engine plus an FP32 vector/softmax unit;
  - 64 PF FP4 / 32 PF FP8 dense at 0.75 V; **40.6 / 20.3 PF at the 0.55 V operating point** (f = 0.635 f0 from the alpha-power law: Vt 0.30 V, α 1.3);
  - a zero-skip front end assumed equal to Rubin's (×1.43). With zero-skip off on both: 1.17 / 1.48 / 2.01× (symmetric).
- **W-Tier × 8** (N4-class SRAM, hybrid-bonded under the C-Dies):
  - 18.4 GB of expert and attention weights;
  - 512 TB/s at f0, clocked with compute (325 TB/s at 0.55 V), at 0.15 pJ/bit.
- **K-Mem:** 4 × HBM4 16-high, 192 GB at 10 TB/s, KV cache only.
- **Link:** 64 × 224G lanes per direction, 1.8 TB/s (85% usable), 144 W. It takes 10.8 µs of a 12.5 µs microbatch at the operating point.
- **Host:** one CPU per 8 packages (50 W per slot). One 800G NIC per package for KV ingress, ~0.4 Tb/s at ≥200.

**Thermal and packaging:** ~0.4 W/mm² of compute at 0.55 V, about a third of a GPU's. ~580 A per C-Die. A ~5-reticle CoWoS-L interposer, the same class as Rubin Ultra.

### 3.2 Mapping (one change this round)
- **Layer groups:**
  - g = 6 packages per layer: 373 packages plus 11 spares per 1.6T instance;
  - g = 3 (187 packages) gives the same ratio at ≤500 tok/s/user;
  - g = 8 and g = 12 are worse at every tier.
- **Expert placement:** each expert's intermediate dimension is split g ways, then over tiles.
- **Attention:** data-parallel, re-reading 0.43 GB of attention/shared weights per microbatch.
- **Microbatching:** m = 2–3; cycle T = max(m·c, c + τ).
- **New: chunked prefill on the pod.** Weights are already resident and prefill is compute-bound, so a compile-time schedule time-slices prefill chunks between decode cycles at the same 0.55 V point. KV is written to K-Mem directly. The 800G NIC is no longer needed for KV ingress, but I keep it for disaggregated deployments.

| Data | Format |
|---|---|
| Expert weights | NVFP4, zero-compressed |
| Attention, shared expert, drafter | FP8 |
| Indexer | FP4 |
| Accumulation and softmax | FP32 |

### 4.2 Hand checks

**C7:**
- Time per position per layer: 1.655 GFLOP linear ÷ (64 PF × 0.635 × 1.43), plus 0.297 GFLOP attention ÷ (32 PF × 0.635), plus 0.26 GFLOP indexer ÷ (64 PF × 0.635) = 4.95e-8 s.
- Per microbatch: 814 positions × 4.95e-8 ÷ 6 packages × 1.12 imbalance ÷ 0.6 efficiency = **12.5 µs** ✓
- Speed: 1.8 tokens ÷ (62 × 25.1 µs) = **1,157 tok/s/user** ✓
- Efficiency: 0.85 × 50,468 users × 1,158 ÷ (373 × 2,542 W) = **52.4 tok/s/W** ✓

**Rubin, calibrated:**
- k = 3 accepts 1 + 0.8 + 0.64 + 0.512 = 2.95 tokens per step.
- At 229 tok/s/user that is T = 2.95 / (229 × 62) = 208 µs.
- τ at 0.55 V = 137 + 3/0.635 = 141.7 µs, which leaves c ≈ 66 µs for weight reads and compute ✓
- To reach 500 tok/s/user even at k = 7 (4.2 tokens per step), T must be ≤ 135 µs. That is below τ alone, so **"GPU can't" follows from arithmetic, not from the model's details.**

**Where the bulk 1.17× comes from (symmetric):**
- C7: 40.6 × 1.43 ÷ 2.542 kW = 22.8 PF/kW.
- Rubin: 25.8 × 1.43 ÷ 1.981 kW = 18.6 PF/kW.
- Ratio 1.23, times the imbalance penalty 1.07/1.12 = 1.17.
- At the same node this is 1.06, which is MAC-energy-bound and physically a tie. Calibrated latency lifts it to 1.43 / 1.30 only because Rubin must speculate deeper and run less batch to stay under its latency.

## 6. Software stack and programming model
- **Front end:** PyTorch (`torch.export`) and JAX (StableHLO) → MLIR → **static spatial schedule**. The schedule covers:
  - layer-group placement (g);
  - expert slicing and hot-expert replication;
  - m, k and the voltage point;
  - **interleaving of prefill chunks**;
  - fixed-size collectives.
  
  Compiling a model takes hours, offline.
- **Kernels:** a tile DSL (block-matmul, softmax, gather) for CSA, the FP4 indexer and MLA. No warp/thread model.
- **Serving:** a vLLM/SGLang backend with continuous batching at stage boundaries, paged KV in K-Mem and a built-in drafter. Prefill runs on the pod by default, or on GPUs if disaggregated.
- **Existing models:** checkpoints convert by NVFP4 post-training quantization; V4-Pro and gpt-oss need none. Models of ≤200B waste a pod. Training and dynamic control flow stay on GPUs.
- **Maturity risk:** GPU software for V4 improved 2.1–2.6× in two months, and Continuum's stack starts from zero. Static scheduling is the reason the latency class differs, but the compiler is unbuilt.

## 7. Where Continuum loses or ties
1. **Rubin Ultra at Nvidia's 600 kW with full clocks and undervolt:** loses 14–25% at bulk.
2. **Symmetric latency at bulk on the same node:** a tie (1.06×), and a loss at η = 0.5.
3. **Ultra at 1M context, bulk, symmetric latency:** 0.90×.
4. **SoIC and logic area:** 68k SoIC passes per GW; ~1.4× Rubin's leading-edge area per GW.
5. **Deployment unit:** 187–373 packages per instance; no training.
6. **Calendar:** 2028 at the earliest. Nvidia's 2028 Feynman generation is unspecified. A GPU with ≤40 µs per-layer latency would shrink the speed-tier lead to 1.4–2×.

## 9. Risks and physical, process and supply limits
- **SoIC:** 4.6–6.8 months of 2026 capacity per GW; new capacity costs ~$7B per 10k wafers/month.
- **CoWoS-L:** ~5 reticles. If the Kyber slip report is true, that size class is also hard for Nvidia.
- **HBM:** 1.57M 16-high stacks per GW; 16-high volume is unconfirmed (12-high fallback loses ≤5%).
- **Low voltage:** 0.55 V needs adaptive clocking; SRAM sits on a separate rail at ≥0.65 V.
- **Yield:** a 750 mm² N2 die is ~47% raw and ~65% with harvesting. Bonds are 98–99% each, so known-good-die testing is mandatory. 11 spares per pod.
- **Workload drift:** fewer, larger experts or dense attention erode the design.
