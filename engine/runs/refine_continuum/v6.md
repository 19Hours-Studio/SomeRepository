I've re-run the model with three fairness fixes and two design changes. The result is a modest gain at moderate speeds and a larger one above 1,000 tok/s/user. Continuum still does not clearly beat Rubin Ultra, its likely rival when it could ship.

# Continuum-6: a weight-stationary 3D-SRAM decode accelerator for frontier MoE

## 0. Verdict

**What changed since the last round, and the honest result.** Everything is on DeepSeek-V4-Pro at 128K context, with draft acceptance p = 0.8. Every system gets an equal per-layer latency τ = 10 µs. Cells are output tokens/s per provisioned watt, as a ratio to the best Rubin setup (`round6/run6.txt`, `run6c.txt`).

| Step | ≥200 tok/s/user | ≥500 | ≥1,000 | ≥1,500 | ≥2,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuum-5, last round's headline | 0.99 | 1.03 | 1.03 | 1.01 | 0.94 |
| Fix 1: attention is data-parallel, so each package must read *all* attention weights (last round split them) | 0.99 | 1.03 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.94 |
| Fix 2: Continuum gets the same Max-Q power profile Rubin already had | 1.04 | 1.08 | 1.04 | 1.01 | 0.94 |
| **Design change: 6 packages per layer instead of 3 (373-package pod)** | **1.04** | **1.08** | **1.27** | **1.51** | **1.91** |
| Fix 3: Rubin may also spread experts across 2 or 4 NVL72 racks | 1.04 | 1.08 | 1.27 | 1.51 | 1.91 |
| Same, at 1M context | 1.13 | 1.13 | 1.29 | 1.53 | 2.02 |

- Fixes 1–3 are corrections in Rubin's favour or neutral. Fix 3 does not help Rubin at 128K: cross-rack expert traffic over 0.2 TB/s networking makes it 2.5–3.5× worse than a single rack. At 1M context it lowers Continuum's ratio from 1.74 to 1.53 at ≥1,500 and from 4.00 to 2.02 at ≥2,000.
- **Where the gain comes from (§4.1).** A token-step must read every layer's expert weights, about 14.3 GB per layer. Both machines read one layer's weights at nearly the same rate: NVL72's HBM gives 1.58 PB/s; 6 Continuum packages' SRAM gives 1.54 PB/s. But Rubin's 72 GPUs read all 62 layers one after another. Continuum's 62 layer groups read at the same time, on separate silicon.
  - That is **95 PB/s of weight bandwidth for 0.91 MW, against 1.58 PB/s for 0.19 MW: 12.6× more per watt.**
  - So Continuum reaches 1,000–1,300 tok/s/user with one draft token (k = 1). That wastes 1.11 FLOPs per accepted token.
  - Rubin needs four draft tokens (k = 4), which wastes 1.49. With k = 1, Rubin reaches 1,000 tok/s/user at only 4.9 tok/s/W, because it is HBM-bound.
  - 1.49 ÷ 1.11 = 1.34. Effective FLOPs per provisioned watt are equal (17.2 vs 17.4 PF/kW). The model's answer is 1.27.
- **Below ~500 tok/s/user it is a tie (1.04–1.08×).** Both designs are compute-bound, and memory hierarchy does not change FLOPs per joule.

- it ties at moderate speeds;
- it likely loses to Rubin Ultra at ≤500 tok/s/user if Nvidia's 600 kW Kyber figure is real;
- every Continuum number is model output, with no silicon.

## 1. Target workloads and metrics

**Primary workload: decode of DeepSeek-V4-Pro.**
- 1.6T total / 49B active parameters, 61 layers, hidden size 7168.
- 384 routed experts (top-6) plus 1 shared; expert FFN size 3072.
- One 512-dim latent KV head.
- Compressed sparse attention (4× compression, top-1,024 selection, FP4 indexer) on 30 layers; 128× compressed attention on 31 layers; 128-token window.
- KV cache in FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions.

**Conditions and scope.**
- Context: 128K and 1M.
- Speculation: a one-layer block drafter; geometric acceptance p = 0.8 (code and tools) or 0.6 (prose).
- Stress cases: 3.1T and 6.2T models with the same active parameters.
- Out of scope: training; edge; models under ~200B, which fit SRAM-only systems (LPX, Cerebras) and where those win.

**Metrics.**

| Metric | Why it defines "best" here |
|---|---|
| Output tok/s per provisioned MW at a per-user speed floor (200…2,000 tok/s/user) | Sites are power-limited, and the floor stops a chip looking efficient at 5 tok/s/user. Each system chooses its own draft depth k (0–7), microbatch count m (1–4), power profile, tensor-parallel width (Rubin) and layer-group width (Continuum). |
| Output tok/s per HBM stack, and per DRAM-wafer-equivalent GB | HBM4 is supply-limited through 2027. TrendForce puts HBM at ~22–30% of DRAM wafer input for ~9–13% of bits. |
| Max tok/s/user | The speed-tier race: coding agents, reasoning. |
| Cost per token | Rough only (§4.4). |

**Gate.** A gain counts only if it holds at equal per-layer latency τ, with the same power-overhead method, the same power-profile menu, and Rubin allowed its best parallel layout, including multiple racks.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Package blocks (unchanged hardware since Continuum-5 except the power profile)

**C-Die (compute)**
- 2 per package on N2, ~750 mm² each, 144 tiles each.
- Each tile: a ~111 TF FP4 tensor engine plus an FP32 vector/softmax unit.
- **32 PF FP4 / 16 PF FP8 dense per package.**
- Zero-skip front end: offline-compressed weight zeros plus run-time activation zeros. Assumed to give the same ≤1.43× as Rubin's 35 → 50 PF. With zero-skip off on both, the round-5 ratio was 0.89–1.08×.
- Compute energy: 0.040 pJ/FLOP (N2) against an assumed 0.050 for Rubin (N3P). With 0.050 for both, the ratios fall ×0.89 (§8).

**W-Tier (weight SRAM)**
- 2 N4-class SRAM-only tiers under each C-Die, hybrid-bonded.
- **9.2 GB and 256 TB/s per package at 0.15 pJ/bit**, 307 W at full rate.
- About 4.7k signal bonds per tile at 1.5 Gb/s on a 9 µm pitch.

**K-Mem (KV cache only)**
- 3 × HBM4 16-high, 48 GB each: 144 GB at ~7.5 TB/s.
- Fallback until 16-high ships: 3 × 36 GB 12-high.

**Link**
- 64 lanes of 224G per direction (1.8 TB/s, 85% usable).
- Never binds: 7.1 µs of link time against an 11.4 µs microbatch at ≥1,000.

**Power profiles (new: Max-Q parity)**
- Max-P slot: 2.78 kW. Package 2,261 W (compute 1,320, W-Tier 307, HBM 240, links 144, leakage/other 250), plus host share 100, NIC 60, switch plane 30, conversion and cooling 330.
- **Max-Q slot: 2.44 kW at compute × 0.92.** It is defined exactly as for Rubin:
  - chip saves 29% of compute power plus 10% of leakage;
  - the rack sees 0.83 of the chip saving, as Rubin goes from 2.3 → 1.8 kW per chip but 220 → 190 kW per rack.
- Continuum's per-slot overhead is ~0.5 kW against Rubin's ~0.8 kW, because there are no NVSwitch trays and fewer CPUs.

**Thermal and packaging**
- ~1.05–1.2 W/mm², GPU-class, so no thermal advantage.
- ~700 A per C-Die passes through TSVs in two tiers.
- 2 SoIC stacks plus 3 HBM sites on a CoWoS-L interposer of ~2.7 reticles.

### 3.2 Mapping: the design change

**Layer groups of g = 6 packages.**
- Each expert's intermediate dimension is split 6 ways (512 columns per package), then over r = 16 tiles: **32 columns per tile**.
- Tile load imbalance at the operating points: 1.10–1.13 (`round5/imbal5.txt`; positions per package are about the same as last round).
- Attention is data-parallel across users. Each package holds the full 0.43 GB of attention and shared weights, and re-reads them every microbatch: 1.7 µs, now included.
- The compiler chooses g ∈ {3, 4, 6} per deployment. It picks g = 6 at ≥1,000 tok/s/user and g = 3 is equal at ≤500.

**Pod**
- 62 stages × 6 packages + 1 LM-head package = **373 active packages + 11 spares**, in 6 liquid-cooled racks of 64.
- ~0.91 MW active, ~0.94 MW provisioned with spares. Spares cost ×0.97, not included in the ratios; NVL72 is also counted without fleet spares.
- SRAM is only 27% full. **The design spends SRAM capacity to buy SRAM bandwidth per layer.**
- The free capacity holds hot-expert replicas, used for load balancing.

**Microbatching**
- m = 2: each stage computes one microbatch while the other's collective and dependency latency are in flight.
- Cycle T = max(2c, c + τ).

| Data | Format |
|---|---|
| Expert weights | NVFP4 (E2M1, E4M3 scale per 16 values, FP32 tensor scale), zero-compressed |
| Attention, shared expert, drafter | FP8 |
| Indexer | FP4 |

**Hand check, C-6 at ≥1,000**
- Positions per microbatch: 268 users × 2 = 536.
- Per position per layer: linear 1.655 GFLOP ÷ (32 × 0.92 × 1.43 PF); attention 0.297 GFLOP ÷ (16 × 0.92 PF); indexer 0.26 GFLOP ÷ (32 × 0.92 PF). Total 6.83e-8 s.
- 536 × 6.83e-8 ÷ 6 packages × 1.12 imbalance ÷ 0.6 efficiency = **11.4 µs** ✓
- Speed: T = 2 × 11.4 = 22.8 µs, so 1.8 accepted tokens ÷ (62 × 22.8 µs) = **1,273 tok/s/user** ✓
- Efficiency: 0.85 × 33.2k users × 1,273 tok/s ÷ (373 × 2.44 kW) = **39.5 tok/s/W** ✓

**Why Rubin can't use k = 1 at ≥1,000**
- At 1,000 tok/s/user with k = 1, T must be ≤ 29 µs, so c ≤ 14.5 µs per layer.
- The expert-weight floor alone is 14.27 GB ÷ 1.58 PB/s = 9.0 µs per layer, per microbatch, whatever the batch size.
- Data-parallel attention adds 0.43 GB ÷ 22 TB/s = 19.5 µs. Tensor parallelism 8 cuts that but replicates KV reads eightfold.
- Best k = 1 point ≥1,000: **4.9 tok/s/W**. Rubin's optimum is k = 4 at 31.0.

## 6. Software stack and programming model

**Front end**
- PyTorch (`torch.export`) and JAX (StableHLO) graphs are lowered through an MLIR compiler.
- The compiler produces a **static spatial schedule**: layer-to-group placement (g), expert slicing over tiles, hot-expert replication from a routing profile, microbatch count, and fixed-size collectives.
- Recompiling for a new model is an offline step, estimated in hours.

**Kernels**
- A tile-level DSL (Triton-like: block-matmul, softmax, gather) for custom attention variants such as compressed sparse attention, the FP4 indexer and MLA.
- No warp/thread model; tiles are scheduled at compile time.

**Serving**
- A vLLM/SGLang backend plugin: OpenAI-compatible API, continuous batching at stage boundaries, paged KV in K-Mem, built-in speculative decoding (block drafter on stage 62).
- Dynamic per-step shapes are handled by batch-size buckets.

**Existing models**
- Any MoE or dense transformer checkpoint converts by post-training quantization to NVFP4. Models already served in NVFP4 (V4-Pro, gpt-oss) need no new quantization.
- Models that do not fill a pod (≤200B) run, but waste the pod; LPX or Cerebras are better there.
- Training, fine-tuning and dynamic-control-flow models stay on GPUs.

**Maturity risk.** GPU serving software improved ~5× on V4 in 43 days (SemiAnalysis). Continuum starts from zero, and the §0.2 realisation fraction depends on this stack.

## 7. Where Continuum loses or ties

1. **≤500 tok/s/user against Rubin:** 1.04–1.08×, within model error. Physically it is a tie: both are compute-bound.
2. **Rubin Ultra, if its 600 kW rack holds:** loses by 15–32% at 128K, and by 16–28% at ≤1,000 at 1M. Even the 4-die A16 variant is 0.74–1.12×.
3. **Deployment unit:** ~0.9 MW and 373 packages per 1.6T model instance at g = 6 (or 187 packages at g = 3, at ≤500 tok/s/user). A 6.2T model needs ~870 packages at g = 6.
4. **SRAM capacity is 73% idle at g = 6.** A cheaper bandwidth-rich, capacity-poor tier is a future SKU.
5. **NVFP4 KV on every system** lowers ratios at 128K by ~0–8%.
6. **Peak single-stream speed:** CS-4 and Ultra are modelled higher.
7. **Training:** not supported.
8. **Software maturity** and **calendar** (2028 at the earliest, without silicon).

| HBM scarcer than SoIC for the buyer | HBM plentiful | The per-stack advantage stops mattering; 1.27× tokens/MW remains |
| Sparse MoE with compressed attention remains the frontier | Dense long-context attention | KV-bound: 7.5 vs 22 TB/s per package, so Continuum loses |
| Rubin Ultra slot ≥5.7 kW, or Kyber slips | 4.2 kW on time | Continuum is behind its calendar rival |

## 9. Risks and physical, process and supply limits

- **SoIC:** ~1.5–2.3 months of reported 2026 capacity per GW, most of it committed to others. New capacity is reported at ~$7B per 10k wafers/month.
- **CoWoS-L:** 2.7 reticles (Continuum-6) or ~5 reticles (6X), similar to Rubin and Rubin Ultra.
- **HBM:** 1.23M 16-high stacks per GW (zero for Continuum-G); 16-high volume is unconfirmed.
- **GDDR7 (G variant):** 48 devices per package is 1.5× the device count reported for Rubin CPX. Board area and signal integrity are a risk.
- **Thermal and power delivery:**
  - ~1.05–1.2 W/mm²;
  - ~700 A per C-Die through TSV-perforated tiers;
  - SRAM retention at high temperature;
  - no backside power until A16.
- **Yield:** a 750 mm² N2 die yields ~47% raw, ~65% with tile harvesting. Each bond interface is 98–99%. Known-good-die testing before bonding is mandatory.
- **Pipeline fragility:** one failed package stalls 1/373 of the pipeline, so 11 spares and a remap plane are needed (×0.97 if provisioned).
- **Workload drift:**
  - better drafters (p ≥ 0.9) cut Rubin's speculation waste and shrink the ≥1,000 gain;
  - fewer, larger experts cut the HBM advantage;
  - dense attention breaks the design.
