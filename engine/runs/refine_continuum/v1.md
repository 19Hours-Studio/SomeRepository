# Continuum: a weight-stationary 3D-SRAM inference accelerator for frontier MoE decode

## 0. Verdict

Continuum is an inference chip. It is not a training chip and not an edge part. It is built for one job: interactive decode of trillion-parameter-class MoE models at long context. The reference is DeepSeek-V4-Pro: 1.6T total parameters, 49B active, a hybrid compressed-sparse attention, and contexts from 128K to 1M.

The design idea is to put the whole model's weights into SRAM that is hybrid-bonded under the compute die, spread across a 128-package pod. HBM then holds only the KV cache.

In my analytical model, at 128K context Continuum beats every current design on tokens/s per provisioned MW at ≥200 tok/s per user:

| Comparison | Continuum's lead |
|---|---|
| Rubin NVL72, optimistic software | 1.6× |
| MI455X | 1.8× |
| TPU 8i | 3.1× |
| Ironwood | 5.4× |
| Rubin + Groq-3 LPX | 8× |
| Cerebras CS-4 | 9–13× |

At ≥1,000 tok/s/user it is 2.4–3.5× better than the only other designs that reach that speed (wafer-scale and LPU).

It loses or only ties in these cases:
- **Low-interactivity, short-context throughput:** about 1.1–1.3× against the best HBM designs, which is inside my error bars.
- **Prefill:** parity per MW, and 0.69× Rubin per package.
- **Peak single-stream speed:** below CS-4 at equal fabric latency.
- **Training:** I make no claim.

So "best in the world" holds only for the metric and workload defined in §1, and only if the three conditions in §7 hold. All Continuum numbers are model outputs, not measurements.

This version also corrects an earlier draft:
- That draft targeted DeepSeek-V3 dense-MLA attention. V4's CSA/HCA attention cuts attention FLOPs per token at 128K by roughly 60× (my count), which removes that draft's "match the ridge point to MLA" rationale.
- Its 10 GB SRAM figure did not fit the stated die count.
- Its "GPUs cannot reach the speed" claims were stronger than the data supports.

## 1. Target workload and metrics

**Workload.**
- **Primary:** decode of DeepSeek-V4-Pro-class MoE.
  - Config: 61 layers, hidden size 7168, 384 routed experts with top-6 plus 1 shared expert, expert FFN size 3072.
  - Attention: CSA at m=4 with top-1024 selection, HCA at m=128, a 128-token window, KV in FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions.
  - Context: 128K, also checked at 8K and 1M.
- **Secondary:** a 2K-token prefill per 1K output tokens, which models an agentic turn over a cached 128K prefix.
- **Out of scope:** training, edge, and small models.

**Metrics.**

| Metric | Why it defines "best" |
|---|---|
| **Primary:** tok/s per provisioned MW at a per-user speed floor (≥50, 100, 200, 400, 1000, 2000 tok/s/user) | Sites are power-limited, and power is bought as provisioned capacity. Nvidia also sells Vera Rubin + LPX on "35× throughput per megawatt". A per-user speed floor is needed because any chip looks efficient at 5 tok/s/user. |
| Activity energy, tok/J | Checks that the provisioned-power figure is physically consistent. |
| Tokens/s per HBM stack | HBM4 is the scarcest input in 2026. |
| Maximum tok/s/user | The latency tier, where Cerebras and Groq compete. |
| Cost per token | Capex plus power. Only a rough estimate, because prices are not public. |

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Package (named blocks)

| Block | Spec | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **C-Die** (compute) | 2 per package, N2, ~750 mm². 144 tiles per die; each tile is a ~83 TF FP4 tensor engine with a vector/softmax unit. **12 PF FP4 / 6 PF FP8 dense per die, 24 / 12 PF per package.** | Rubin gets ~17.5 PF per N3P die, so 12 PF on N2 is conservative. The spare area goes to bond landing, TSV keep-outs and links. |
| **W-Tier** (weight SRAM) | 2 tiers under each C-Die, so 4 per package. Each is an N4-class, ~750 mm² SRAM-only die, face-to-back hybrid-bonded (SoIC) at ~9 µm pitch. **Usable 2.3 GB per tier, 9.2 GB per package.** | 31.8 Mb/mm² macro × 0.89 (ECC) × 0.90 (TSV/power keep-out) × 0.97 (repair) gives ~24.7 Mb/mm². Times 750 mm², that is 2.3 GB. |
| W-Tier bandwidth | Each tile reads the 32 MB stacked directly under it. **256 TB/s per package (0.89 TB/s per tile) at 0.15 pJ/bit** (range 0.08–0.3). | The macro array could deliver about 500 TB/s per tier, so the cap is power, not bonds: 256 TB/s × 8 × 0.15 pJ = 307 W. A tile needs ~4.7k signal bonds, about 0.4 mm² of a ~5 mm² tile. |
| **K-Mem** (KV only) | 4 × HBM4 12-high: 144 GB, ~10 TB/s | JEDEC HBM4 has a 2048-bit interface; vendors quote 2–3.3 TB/s per stack. |
| **Link** | 64 lanes of 224G PAM4 per direction (1.8 TB/s): 32 lanes direct to the next pipeline stage, 16 to the partner package, 16 to a switched plane. | Direct copper hops of ~0.3–0.5 µs; a switch adds ~250 ns (Tomahawk Ultra class). |
| Packaging | 2 SoIC stacks + 4 HBM4 on CoWoS-L, about a 3-reticle interposer | Fewer HBM sites than Rubin's 8 |
| Power | Peak ~2.0 kW (compute 960 W, W-Tier 307 W, HBM 320 W, links 144 W, leakage/clock 250 W). TDP 1.8 kW; **provisioned slot 2.1 kW.** At the reference operating point the activity model gives ~1.37 kW. | §4 |
| Thermal | C-Die on top, against the cold plate, as in AMD's 9800X3D. Hottest die runs at ~0.6–0.9 W/mm², versus ~1.1+ W/mm² for current GPUs. Power reaches the C-Die through TSVs in both W-Tiers. | IR drop and thermal behaviour need simulation (§8) |

**Pod:** 128 packages (123 active, 5 spare) in 2 liquid-cooled racks of about 135 kW each. The pod has 1.18 TB of SRAM and 18 TB of HBM.

### 3.2 Mapping: why 123 packages and no all-to-all

- One V4-Pro layer needs 14.65 GB: 14.3 GB of NVFP4 experts plus ~0.43 GB of FP8 attention and shared expert. That is two packages per layer, and 61 layers × 2 + 1 for the LM head gives 123 packages.
- **Within a stage pair:**
  - Each expert is tensor-sliced in half across the two packages, so there is no expert-load imbalance between them.
  - Attention is data-parallel: each package holds half the users' KV in its own HBM.
  - The pair exchanges FP8 attention outputs, about 0.9 MB at microbatch 256, which is ~2 µs on 16 lanes.
- **Between stages:** activations pass point-to-point, 61 hops per token. There is no global expert all-to-all. DeepEP's measured dispatch and combine take 77–369 µs per layer over InfiniBand, and this mapping removes that cost.
- **Sizing:** the fabric is sized for serialization latency, not average bandwidth. Average traffic is ~240 GB/s per package; peak per-stage transfers are 3.7 MB of BF16 partial sums, about 4 µs at 0.9 TB/s, overlapped with the next stage's attention.
- **Spares:** any stage can be remapped onto a spare package through the switched plane.

### 3.3 Numerics

| Data | Format |
|---|---|
| Expert weights | NVFP4 (E2M1 values, E4M3 scale per 16 values, FP32 tensor scale), 4.5 bits per value |
| Attention and shared-expert weights | FP8 |
| Matmul activations | FP8 |
| Accumulation and softmax | FP32 |
| KV cache | FP8, with BF16 RoPE dimensions (V4's own format) |
| Lightning indexer | FP4 (as V4 specifies) |
| BF16 | Quarter rate; no FP64 |

V4-Pro is already served at FP4 on GB300 in the InferenceMAX runs, so this format choice does not cost Continuum any accuracy relative to GPUs.

## 4. Sanity arithmetic

All of this comes from `model.py`. Per token at 128K context:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Linear FLOPs | 101 GFLOP |
| Attention FLOPs | 35 GFLOP |
| KV read per step | 103 MB |
| KV resident per user | 0.65 GB |

My structural KV count gives 4.9 KB per token. vLLM reports 9.62 GiB per sequence at 1M tokens in BF16, which is about 5.5 KB per token in FP8. The two agree within about 10%.

- **Weight-read roofline, per stage at microbatch b = 256:**
  - At b = 256, ~377 of the 384 experts are touched. That is 14.3 GB read at 2 × 256 TB/s, or **28 µs**.
  - KV: 256 users × 1.69 MB at 2 × 10 TB/s = **22 µs**.
  - Compute: 256 × 2.2 GFLOP at 60% of 48 PF = **20 µs**.
  - Stage time = 28 µs + τ (3 µs) = 31 µs. That gives **525 tok/s/user** (61 stages) and **8.2M tok/s per pod**, or 66.7k tok/s per package, for 15.6k concurrent users.
  - Batch is capped by HBM capacity (KV), not by SRAM.
- **Why HBM holds only KV:**
  - V4 attention at 128K runs at about 340 FLOP/byte, so it is HBM-bound on every chip. Continuum's HBM time (22 µs) and SRAM time (28 µs) are deliberately balanced.
  - The 26 GB of FP8 attention weights that a data-parallel GPU must re-read every step never touch HBM in Continuum.
- **Energy per token (activity model):**

  | Design | mJ/token | Breakdown (mJ) | tok/J |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Continuum | 20.6 | SRAM 4.1, HBM 2.9, FLOPs 6.8, links 0.1, static 6.7 | 48 |
  | Rubin, same tier | 31.3 | HBM 7.7, FLOPs 6.8, links 0.3, static 16.5 | 32 |

  Inputs: FP4 0.04 pJ/FLOP and FP8 0.08 pJ/FLOP all-in, SRAM 0.15 pJ/bit, HBM4 3.5 pJ/bit, links 5 pJ/bit. For scale, Rubin's 35 PF at ~2 kW bounds whole-chip energy at ≤0.06 pJ/FLOP.
- **Weight-holding SRAM per kW:** Continuum 4.4 GB/kW, CS-4 ~1.0, LP30 0.8. Holding the 0.9 TB model in SRAM therefore costs about 0.26 MW on Continuum, about 1.2 MW on LPX (1,952 LP30s) and about 0.9 MW or more on CS-4. This single number explains most of §5.
- **Cost (assumptions):**
  - N2 C-Die about $650–700 after yield; N4 W-Tier about $270.
  - SRAM costs about $120/GB against HBM4 at about $15–20/GB (price not public). The pod's SRAM is about $140k, roughly 3% of a $4–5M pod.
  - Package BOM is about $6.5–8k, comparable to Rubin's 8 HBM4 stacks alone.
  - With capex per MW similar to Rubin's (±30%), cost per token follows tok/s per MW within about ±30%.

## 6. Where Continuum loses or ties

1. **Throughput-tier serving (≤100 tok/s/user):** against MI455X and TPU 8i it leads by only 1.2–1.3×. That is within the uncertainty of the guessed TPU and AMD power figures, so Continuum is not clearly best here.
2. **Prefill:** it ties per MW and is 0.69× Rubin per package. An end-to-end agentic mix compresses the decode lead to about 1.3× at ≥200 tok/s/user.
3. **Peak single-stream speed:** at equal τ, CS-4 reaches 5.2k tok/s/user against Continuum's 3.8k, because a wafer's on-chip bandwidth is about 170× higher per chip. On small models (≤70B), LPX and Cerebras are likely just as good; I make no claim there.
4. **Model-size cliff:** a model above about 1.1 TB of weights needs more packages. A 3.2T model needs about 245 packages, which is still single-hop on 512-port switches. Per-MW efficiency holds in the model (32.3M), but the deployment unit doubles. Small models waste most of the pod.
5. **Training:** not a target. A Continuum-T variant (the same C-Die on a plain base die with 8 × HBM4) would at best reach parity per MW and about 0.7× Rubin per package. Train on GPUs or TPU 8t.
6. **Software maturity:** GPUs gained about 5× on V4 within 43 days (SGLang on GB300). Each new attention variant requires new compiler kernels on Continuum.

| Provisioned power ≤ 2.1 kW per package | 1.6× | 1.3× at 2.6 kW |
| 4 HBM4 stacks per package | 1.6× | 6 stacks: 1.8× (but more HBM). 2 stacks: 1.0× |

The first two rows are the design. If either fails, Continuum is just another inference chip.

## 8. Risks and physical, process and supply limits

- **SoIC hybrid-bonding capacity (the largest supply risk):** each package needs 4 die-to-wafer bonds of ~750 mm² dies. One GW of Continuum is about 3,700 pods and about 1.8M tier bonds. TSMC does not publish SoIC capacity, and today it serves X3D and MI300-class volumes. **Mitigation:** W-Tiers use N4-class wafers, which are not N2-constrained, and are tested as known-good dies before bonding.
- **CoWoS-L:** Continuum needs about 3 reticles per package against about 4 for Rubin. Per pod it uses about 1.3× more interposer area than an NVL72, but delivers 1.6× more tokens per MW. Supply remains tight (10–20% short in 2026).
- **HBM4:** 1.8M stacks per GW, against about 2.6M for Rubin, and 2.2× more tokens per stack. This is a real advantage while HBM is sold out.
- **Thermal and IR drop:** about 700 A per stack must pass through two TSV-perforated tiers. SRAM leakage of about 9 GB at high temperature (~100–200 W) is a retention and power risk. N2 has no backside power delivery; A16 would help.
- **Yield:**
  - A 750 mm² N2 die at D0 ≈ 0.1/cm² yields about 47% raw, or about 65% with tile harvesting.
  - SRAM tiers yield about 90% after repair. Bond yield is about 98–99% per interface.
  - Assembly with 6 sites is about 95%.
  - Stacking adds cost but is not a showstopper, given known-good-die testing.
- **Pipeline fragility:** one failed package stalls 1/61 of the pipeline. The 5 spares and the remap path through the switched plane are mandatory.
- **Workload drift:** the design bets on top-k sparse and compressed attention, as in V4. A return to dense long-context attention would push decode back to HBM bandwidth, where Continuum has less (10 TB/s against Rubin's 22). A shift to models above 2 TB of weights doubles the deployment unit.

## 9. Software and programming model

- **Path:** PyTorch or JAX → `torch.export` / StableHLO → Continuum compiler. The compiler:
  - assigns layers to stages;
  - slices experts across each pair;
  - quantizes to NVFP4, or keeps the vendor's FP4 checkpoint;
  - emits **static** per-stage schedules for compute, W-Tier reads and link slots, in the style of Groq/TPU;
  - pages KV into HBM in fixed blocks.
- **Dynamic MoE routing:** routing is resolved inside a stage. Because experts are sliced across the pair, routing changes only which W-Tier rows are read, not which package does the work. The schedule handles this with a capacity factor and no runtime collectives.
- **Serving:** a vLLM/SGLang out-of-tree platform plugin, the same mechanism TPU and Ascend use. It provides continuous batching into fixed microbatch slots, prefix caching in HBM and host memory, and MTP/speculative decoding. MTP raises everyone's per-user speed but helps Continuum most, because it is latency-bound.
- **What breaks:**
  - Custom CUDA/Triton kernels do not port.
  - Dynamic shapes are padded into buckets.
  - Every new attention variant (such as CSA/HCA) needs compiler support before it runs.
  - Existing checkpoints run without retraining if they are already FP4/FP8; otherwise they need post-training quantization with an accuracy gate.

## 10. Tests that could prove the design wrong

| # | Test | Tool | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stage latency: 224G link plus a Tomahawk-Ultra-class switch, messages from 64 B to 4 MB, pair exchange and partial-sum hand-off | FPGA or test-chip loopback; ASTRA-sim | τ > 12 µs per stage (the lead falls to ≤1.2×) or traversal > 3 µs |
| 2 | W-Tier energy and bandwidth for 0.58 Mb macros at N4/N5 with a 3D bond path | CACTI/DESTINY + Accelergy; ISSCC macro data | > 0.3 pJ/bit or < 150 TB/s per package |
| 3 | Thermal and IR drop with the C-Die over 2 tiers at 0.9 W/mm² | HotSpot or 3D-ICE; PDN IR solver | Tj > 105 °C, or IR drop > 5% at the tile, or SRAM retention failures |
| 4 | End-to-end pipeline using real V4 routing traces (expert skew, bubbles, MTP) | LLMCompass or event simulator; Timeloop for tiles | Achieved < 60% of `model.py`'s operating point |
| 5 | Model calibration: rerun `model.py` for GB300 and B200 against InferenceMAX with prefill included | Python | If the model over-predicts GPUs by X, derate Continuum by X; drop the claim if the ≥200 lead is < 1.3× |
| 6 | FP4 accuracy at 128K–1M (RULER, LongBench v2), needed only if the checkpoint is not natively FP4 | GPU cluster | > 1 point loss against the FP8 baseline |

None of these could run here: there is no silicon, and model downloads are blocked. Everything above is analytical.
