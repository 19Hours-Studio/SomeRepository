# Continuum: a weight-stationary, 3D-SRAM inference accelerator for interactive frontier-MoE decode

## 0. Verdict

In my analytical model, Continuum is the best design in one specific regime: **frontier MoE models served at ≥ ~600 tok/s per user, at 128K–1M context.**

- **Interactive tiers.** At those speeds it leads the strongest GPU (Rubin, with aggressive 8 µs/layer software) by 1.3–4.4× in tokens/s per provisioned MW. It leads SRAM-only designs (Groq-3 LPX, Cerebras CS-4) by 9–17× at ≥1,000 tok/s/user.
- **Throughput tiers.** At ≤500 tok/s/user it only matches Rubin: 1.07–1.21× if Rubin's dense FP4 rate is 35 PF, and 0.90–0.95× if the 50 PF figure is dense-equivalent. That is a tie within the model's error, so I do not claim to be best there.
- **Peak single-stream speed.** It loses to CS-4: 4.8k against 7.8k tok/s/user.
- **Training and edge.** I make no claim.

Everything Continuum-side is model output, not measurement. The claim holds only if the three conditions in §7 hold.

**What changed from the previous draft.** Its headline "1.6× vs Rubin at every tier" does not survive re-checking. Most of that lead came from four modelling errors:

1. A power-of-two batch grid that left GPUs far from their best batch size.
2. No multi-token prediction (MTP). DeepSeek-V4 ships an MTP layer, and every serious deployment uses it.
3. Giving Continuum 3 µs per layer while GPUs got 15 µs.
4. Using 62 PF dense for each CS-4 wafer. The 250 PF figure is sparse; independent analysis puts dense at ~25 PF.

I fixed all four, applied the same 0.85 system derate to every design, and found that the round-1 Continuum was *compute-starved* once MTP is used. The design therefore changed in two ways:
- compute rises from 24 to 32 PF FP4 per package;
- the default mapping spreads each layer over **3 packages instead of 2**. This costs nothing in tokens per MW and roughly triples the throughput available at ≥1,000 tok/s/user.

## 1. Target workload and metrics

**Workload**
- **Primary:** decode of a DeepSeek-V4-Pro-class MoE.
  - 1.6T total parameters, 49B active, 61 layers, hidden size 7168.
  - 384 routed experts (top-6) plus 1 shared expert; expert FFN size 3072; the first 3 MoE layers are hash-routed.
  - Attention: 128 query heads of dimension 512, one shared KV head.
  - Layer split: CSA (compression 4, top-1024) and HCA (compression 128), roughly 30 and 31 layers, with a 128-token window.
  - KV in FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions; MTP with 1 draft token.
  - Context 128K, also checked at 8K and 1M.
- **Secondary:** an agentic turn, i.e. 2K new prefill tokens per 1K output tokens over a cached 128K prefix.
- **Out of scope:** training, edge, and models under ~200B parameters, where LPX and Cerebras already fit in SRAM.

**Metrics.** "Best" is ranked by the primary metric. The others are consistency checks.

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **Primary: output tok/s per provisioned MW at a per-user speed floor** (50 … 3,000 tok/s/user) | Data-center sites are power-limited and buy provisioned capacity. Nvidia itself sells LPX on "throughput per MW". Without a speed floor, any chip looks efficient at 5 tok/s/user. |
| Activity energy, tok/J | Physical consistency check on the power figures |
| Tokens/s per HBM stack | HBM4 is the scarcest input in 2026 |
| Maximum tok/s per user | The latency-tier race (Cerebras, Groq) |
| Cost per token | Only a rough estimate: prices are not public, and capex per MW is assumed similar (±30%) |

I chose the interactive tier as the target because reasoning and agent workloads multiply the number of output tokens per answer. At 1,000 tok/s/user, a 20K-token chain of thought takes 20 s instead of 100 s. The throughput tier is already well served by GPUs.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Package blocks

**C-Die (compute)**
- 2 per package, each ~750 mm² on N2, 144 tiles per die (288 per package).
- Each tile has ~111 TF FP4 of tensor engine plus an FP32 vector/softmax unit.
- **32 PF FP4 / 16 PF FP8 dense per package.**
- Basis: Rubin is 35 PF (or 50) in about 2 × 830 mm² on N3P. Matching that in less area on N2 is plausible, partly because the C-Die needs only 4 HBM PHYs and no large L2 cache.
- Compute power: 32 PF × 0.04 pJ/FLOP = 1.28 kW. N2 is about 25% lower power than N3. Rubin's whole chip is bounded by about 0.05–0.06 pJ/FLOP all-in.

**Tensor-engine dataflow: activation-stationary, weight-streaming**
- At the operating point, each expert slice sees only about 4–16 tokens per stage. Weights are used once per stage, so the engine holds activations in registers and streams weights through.
- Per tile, weights arrive at 0.89 TB/s, or 1.58 T FP4 weights/s. The ridge point is therefore ~35 tokens per expert. Below that the tile is W-Tier-bound by design, and the roofline in §4 is set by bandwidth, not by peak FLOPs.

**W-Tier (weight SRAM)**
- 2 tiers under each C-Die, so 4 per package. Each is an N4-class, ~750 mm² SRAM-only die, hybrid-bonded face-to-back (SoIC) at ~9 µm pitch.
- **Usable capacity: 2.3 GB per tier, 9.2 GB per package, ~32 MB under each tile.**
- Derivation: 31.8 Mb/mm² × 0.89 (ECC) × 0.90 (TSV keep-out) × 0.97 (repair) ≈ 24.7 Mb/mm², times 750 mm².

**W-Tier bandwidth**
- **256 TB/s per package at 0.15 pJ/bit** (range 0.08–0.3), which is 307 W.
- The macro arrays could deliver about 500 TB/s per tier; the cap is set by power, not by the bonds.
- Per tile this needs ~4.7k signal bonds at ~1.5 Gb/s each, which take about 0.4 mm² of a ~5 mm² tile.

**K-Mem (KV only)**
- 4 × HBM4 12-high: 144 GB at ~10 TB/s. Weights never touch HBM.

**Link**
- 64 lanes of 224G PAM4 per direction, 1.8 TB/s per direction, about the same as NVLink 6 per GPU.
- Allocation: 2 × 12 lanes to the two packages in the same layer group, 30 lanes to the next stage, 10 lanes to a switched plane.

**Power**

| Item | W |
|---|---|
| Compute | 1,280 |
| W-Tier | 307 |
| HBM | 320 |
| Links | 144 |
| Leakage, clock, other | 250 |
| **Peak** | **~2.3 kW** |

- TDP is 2.1 kW. **The provisioned slot is 2.45 kW**, which includes a 0.35 kW share of hosts, switches and power conversion. Rubin's slot likewise includes its share of the Vera CPU and NVSwitch.
- At the ≥200 tok/s/user operating point, activity energy is about 1.26 kW per package.

**Thermal**
- The C-Die sits on top, against the cold plate, as in the 9800X3D. The stack runs at about 0.85 W/mm² (C-Die) plus 0.2 W/mm² (tiers), roughly **1.05 W/mm²**. That is about the same as current GPUs, so there is no thermal advantage, and the previous draft's claim of one was wrong.
- About 700 A per C-Die reaches it through TSVs in two W-Tiers.

**Packaging**
- 2 SoIC stacks plus 4 HBM4 sites on CoWoS-L, an interposer of about 3 reticles (Rubin uses about 4).

**Pod**
- **192 packages (184 active, 8 spare) in 3 liquid-cooled racks of ~157 kW, about 470 kW in total.**
- 1.77 TB of SRAM and 27.6 TB of HBM.

### 3.2 Mapping: 3 packages per layer, point-to-point, no global all-to-all

- **Layer placement.** One V4-Pro layer is 14.65 GB: 14.3 GB of NVFP4 experts plus ~0.43 GB of FP8 attention and shared expert. With g = 3 packages per layer, 61 × 3 + 1 (LM head) = 184 packages. SRAM is then 53% full.
- **Model size.** The same pod at g = 2 holds models up to about 1.6 TB of weights (~2.8T parameters at 4.5 bits). The compiler picks g. In the model, g = 2 and g = 3 give the same tok/s/MW at throughput tiers (31.9M vs 32.0M), but g = 3 gives **28.7M against 10.1M at ≥1,000 tok/s/user**. Spreading a layer triples its weight-read bandwidth while leaving packages per MW unchanged.
- **Within a layer group (3 packages):**
  - Each expert is tensor-sliced into thirds of its intermediate dimension, so there is no expert-load imbalance between packages.
  - Attention is data-parallel: each package holds one third of the users' KV.
  - Post-attention hidden states (FP8) are all-gathered within the group.
  - BF16 FFN partial sums are reduce-scattered to the next group.
- **Traffic arithmetic at the ≥200 tier.** The microbatch is 539 users × 2 MTP positions. That means ~5.2 MB of all-gather plus ~15.4 MB of partial sums leaving each package per stage, about 11.5 µs of link time against a ~52 µs compute-bound stage (22% utilization).
- **Exposed latency τ.** Overlap is achieved by chunking the microbatch, so the exposed τ is the last chunk plus link latency. **Base τ = 5 µs, conservative 8 µs.** GPUs do two all-to-alls (dispatch and combine) across 72 endpoints per layer. Continuum makes the same number of traversals, but they are point-to-point and statically scheduled.
- **Spares.** Any stage can be remapped onto a spare package through the switched plane.

### 3.3 Numerics

| Data | Format |
|---|---|
| Expert weights | NVFP4: E2M1 values, an E4M3 scale per 16 values, FP32 tensor scale; 4.5 bits per value |
| Attention and shared-expert weights | FP8 |
| Matmul activations | FP8 |
| Accumulation, softmax, partial sums | FP32 accumulation and softmax; partial sums sent as BF16 |
| KV cache | FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions (V4's own format) |
| Indexer | FP4 (as V4 specifies) |
| BF16 | Quarter rate; no FP64 |

V4-Pro is already served at FP4 on GB300, so this format choice costs no accuracy relative to GPUs.

## 4. Sanity arithmetic (`model2.py`)

**Per token at 128K context**
- 101 GFLOP of linear work and 34.5 GFLOP of attention.
- 103 MB of KV read per step.
- 0.65 GB of KV resident per user. That is 4.9 KB per token; vLLM reports 9.62 GiB per sequence at 1M tokens in BF16, which is about 5.5 KB per token in FP8.

**Continuum stage roofline at the ≥200 tier (128K, MTP, g = 3, b = 539 users per stage)**

| Component | Time per stage |
|---|---|
| Weights: all experts touched, 4.9 GB per package at 256 TB/s | 19 µs |
| KV: 539 × 1.69 MB / 3 packages at 10 TB/s | 30 µs |
| Compute: 1,078 positions × (101 GF at FP4 + 34.5 GF at FP8) / 61 layers / 3 packages, at 60% of peak | **52 µs (binding)** |

The stage takes 52 µs + τ. Results: **516 tok/s/user, 32.9k concurrent users, 78k tok/s per package.** KV occupies 117 GB of the 130 GB usable HBM.

**At the ≥1,000 tier (b = 233)**
- W-Tier 19 µs and compute 22.5 µs are balanced; the stage is about 27.5 µs including τ.
- Results: 1,071 tok/s/user and 70k tok/s per package.
- Above about 1,100 tok/s/user, reading the full expert set exceeds the stage budget and the throughput falls off a cliff, from 28.7M at 1,000 to 7.7M tok/s/MW at 1,500. g = 4 moves the cliff only a little.

**Structural ratios (pod level)**

| Design | Weight-read bandwidth per MW | Weight-holding SRAM per kW |
|---|---|---|
| Continuum | 184 × 256 TB/s ÷ 0.47 MW ≈ **100 PB/s/MW** | 3.8 GB/kW |
| Rubin (HBM) | 72 × 22 TB/s ÷ 0.22 MW ≈ 7.2 PB/s/MW | n/a |
| LPX | ~240 PB/s/MW | 0.8 GB/kW |
| CS-4 | ~1,000 PB/s/MW | 1.0 GB/kW |

Continuum sits in the middle: it has a fraction of the SRAM-only designs' bandwidth density, but 4–5× their capacity per kW. Holding a 0.9 TB model therefore costs about 0.24 MW of pod instead of 1–1.2 MW. These two ratios explain §5:
- **Against GPUs:** Continuum wins wherever reading weights dominates the step, i.e. at high interactivity.
- **Against SRAM-only designs:** it wins whenever the model does not fit in a few chips.
- **Where it ties:** in the compute-bound regime both Continuum and Rubin run at 11–13 PF/kW, so they draw level.

**Activity energy (128K, MTP)**

| ≥200 | Continuum | 16.1 | SRAM 1.1, HBM 1.6, FLOPs 7.6, static 5.7 | **62** |

Energy inputs: FP4 0.04 and FP8 0.08 pJ/FLOP; SRAM 0.15, HBM4 3.5 and links 5 pJ/bit; static 450 W per package and 1 kW per GPU slot. All of these are assumptions.

**Cost (assumptions)**
- Per package:
  - 2 N2 C-Dies at ~$700 each after yield;
  - 4 N4 W-Tiers at ~$270 each;
  - 4 HBM4 stacks at ~$550–700 each;
  - CoWoS-L, SoIC bonding and test at ~$1.5k.
- That totals **~$6.5–7.5k per package**, against Rubin's 8 HBM4 stacks alone at about $4.5–5.5k.
- SRAM is about $120/GB against HBM4 at about $15–20/GB (not public). The pod's SRAM costs about $210k, roughly 3–4% of the pod.
- Packages per MW are similar to GPUs, so cost per token tracks tok/s per MW within about ±30%.

## 6. Where Continuum loses or ties

1. **Throughput tier (≤500 tok/s/user).** About 1.1–1.2× against Rubin and 0.90–0.95× against Rubin-50, which is a tie inside the error bars. Here both designs are compute-bound at about 11–13 PF/kW, and no memory hierarchy changes that. MI455X and the TPUs trail Continuum by 1.4–1.6×, but their power figures are guesses.
2. **Prefill.** Parity to 1.14× against Rubin, and 0.90× against Rubin-50. Prefill-heavy fleets should run on GPUs or a Rubin CPX-style prefill tier.
3. **Peak single-stream speed.** CS-4 reaches 7.8k against Continuum's 4.8k tok/s/user, and LPX and Cerebras are likely as good or better on models that fit in a few chips (≤ ~200B). The ≥2,000 tier is a narrow win (3× against CS-4) only because a 1.6T model needs about 21 wafers.
4. **Short context (8K).** The lead narrows, and Rubin-50 wins the throughput tiers.
5. **Deployment unit.** One pod is about 470 kW and 192 packages. Small models waste most of it, and models above about 1.6 TB of weights need a second pod or a deeper pipeline.
6. **Training.** Not a target. A variant with the same C-Die on plain silicon plus 8 × HBM4 would at best reach parity per MW. Train on GPUs or TPU 8t.
7. **Software maturity.** GPUs gained about 5× on V4 in 43 days. Continuum would start that curve from zero.

## 8. Risks and physical, process and supply limits

- **SoIC hybrid-bonding capacity (the largest supply risk).**
  - 1 GW of Continuum is about 408k packages, or about 1.6M tier bonds of ~750 mm² dies. TSMC does not publish SoIC capacity, which today serves X3D and MI300-class volumes.
  - Mitigations: W-Tiers on N4 (not N2-constrained), tested as known-good dies before bonding. A 1-tier variant (4.6 GB per package at g = 4, 245 packages) halves bond demand and eases IR drop, at the same tok/s/MW in the model but about 33% more packages and HBM stacks.
- **CoWoS-L.** About 3 reticles per package against about 4 for Rubin, so per GW the interposer area is similar (~1.2M vs ~1.3M reticle-equivalents). Supply is 10–20% short in 2026.
- **HBM4.** About 1.6M stacks per GW against about 2.6M for Rubin, with 1.8–7× more tokens per stack. This is a real advantage while HBM is sold out.
- **Thermal and IR drop.**
  - About 1.05 W/mm², with roughly 700 A per C-Die passing through two TSV-perforated tiers.
  - SRAM leakage at high temperature (~100–200 W across 9.2 GB) risks both retention and power.
  - N2 has no backside power delivery; A16 would help.
- **Yield.**
  - A 750 mm² N2 die at D0 ≈ 0.1/cm² yields about 47% raw, or about 65% with tile harvesting.
  - SRAM tiers yield about 90% after repair, and each bond interface about 98–99%.
  - A package with a dead tier still works: the compiler sets g ≥ 3 anyway, so 6.9 GB per package is enough.
- **Pipeline fragility.** One failed package stalls 1/183 of the pipeline. The spares and the switched-plane remap path are mandatory.
- **Workload drift.** The design bets on sparse or compressed attention (V4 CSA/HCA). Dense long-context attention would make decode HBM-bound, and Continuum has 10 TB/s against Rubin's 22. More active parameters per token shifts work to compute, where Continuum only ties. Models above 1.6 TB of weights double the deployment unit.

## 9. Software and programming model

- **Path:** PyTorch or JAX → `torch.export` / StableHLO → Continuum compiler. The compiler:
  - chooses g and assigns layers to stage groups;
  - slices experts into thirds;
  - quantizes to NVFP4, or keeps the vendor's FP4 checkpoint;
  - emits **static** per-stage schedules for tiles, W-Tier reads, HBM KV pages and link slots (Groq/TPU-style);
  - chunks microbatches so link transfers overlap compute.
- **Dynamic MoE routing.** Routing is resolved inside a stage. Because every expert is sliced across all three packages, a routing decision changes only which W-Tier rows each tile reads, not which package does the work. There are no runtime collectives. Hash-routed layers are trivial.
- **Kernels.** Expert GEMMs are activation-stationary and weight-streaming with M ≈ 4–40. Attention runs on the vector units from HBM-paged KV. The indexer runs in FP4.
- **Serving.** A vLLM/SGLang out-of-tree platform plugin, the same mechanism TPU and Ascend use. It provides:
  - continuous batching into fixed microbatch slots;
  - prefix caching in HBM and host memory;
  - MTP and speculative decoding. MTP is assumed in all the headline numbers; without it the throughput-tier lead is 1.2–1.3× instead of 1.1×.
- **What breaks.**
  - Custom CUDA/Triton kernels do not port.
  - Dynamic shapes are padded into buckets.
  - Each new attention variant needs compiler support before it runs.
  - FP4/FP8 checkpoints run unchanged; others need post-training quantization with an accuracy gate.

## 10. Tests that could prove the design wrong

| # | Test | Tool | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exposed per-stage latency: chunked all-gather and reduce-scatter across 3 packages over 224G copper and a Tomahawk-Ultra-class switch | FPGA or test-chip loopback; ASTRA-sim | τ > 12 µs |
| 2 | W-Tier bandwidth and energy: 0.58 Mb macros at N4/N5 with a 3D bond path | CACTI/DESTINY + Accelergy; ISSCC macro data | < 180 TB/s or > 0.3 pJ/bit per package |
| 3 | Weight-streaming tile efficiency at M = 4–40, FP4 with E4M3 scales | Timeloop + RTL of one tile | η < 0.5 (Continuum then loses the throughput tier) |
| 4 | Thermal and IR drop for the C-Die over 2 tiers at ~1.05 W/mm² | HotSpot / 3D-ICE; PDN solver | Tj > 105 °C, IR drop > 5%, or retention failures |
| 5 | Model calibration: does InferenceX count output or total tokens? Rerun GB300 and B200 at several interactivity points | Python + InferenceX raw data | If the model is > 2× optimistic for GPUs at ≥200 tok/s/user after the token basis is fixed, re-derive τ and derates for every system |
| 6 | End-to-end pipeline with real V4 routing traces (expert skew, MTP acceptance, sequence-length variance across microbatches) | Event simulator or LLMCompass | Achieved < 70% of `model2.py`'s operating point (equivalent to a derate below 0.6) |
| 7 | FP4 accuracy at 128K–1M (RULER, LongBench v2), needed only if the checkpoint is not natively FP4 | GPU cluster | > 1 point loss against FP8 |

None of these could be run here: there is no silicon, and model downloads are blocked. Everything above is analytical.
