I've finished the analysis and am writing up the revised answer.

Continuum is a 3D-SRAM inference accelerator for serving large mixture-of-experts (MoE) models: model weights sit in SRAM stacked under the compute dies, and HBM holds only the KV cache. I re-ran the comparison with the same accounting rules for every chip. On its home workload (a 1.6T-parameter model at 128K context) it **ties Nvidia Rubin on tokens per provisioned MW: 0.94–1.03×**. The previous answer's 1.3–1.6× lead came almost entirely from accounting that favoured Continuum. Four honest gains remain or were created by design changes:

- **2.9× more tokens per HBM stack** at equal tokens per MW. Where HBM supply, not power, limits a site, that means up to 2.9× more tokens from the site.
- **1.1–2.3× at 1M-token context and 1,000–2,000 tok/s/user.**
- **1.2–1.3× when speculative drafts are accepted less often**, as on prose.
- **1.6–3.2× against every non-Nvidia design** and against Nvidia's own Rubin+LPX.

The large lead over Rubin seen in earlier rounds survives only if GPU per-layer latency stays near today's measured level. Even then it applies only above ~1,000 tok/s/user. This is not "best in the world" in general. There is no silicon, and every Continuum number is model output.

### 0.1 What the fair re-accounting found

| **7. Design change:** Continuum gets zero-skip, Rubin's "adaptive compression" | 0.97 | 1.01 | 1.00 | 0.99 |
| **8. Design change:** 3 × 48 GB 16-high HBM4 instead of 4 × 36 GB (primary SKU) | **0.99** | **1.03** | **1.03** | **1.01** |

**Reading the steps.**
- **Steps 2, 3 and 5 removed the old lead.** Round 4 gave Continuum a 0.15 kW overhead per slot against Rubin's ~0.76–0.84 kW. It also left Rubin at Max-P and did not let GPUs overlap two microbatches.
- **Microbatch overlap matters most.** With two microbatches in flight, a GPU hides its per-layer latency behind the other microbatch's compute. **τ then stops mattering for throughput below ~1,500 tok/s/user, for both designs.** The old "decisive bet" on τ only matters in the top speed tiers.
- **Zero-skip is symmetric.** Rubin's 50 PF figure is dense 35 PF plus run-time zero skipping. Continuum now has an equivalent engine; its weights are static, so they can also be compressed offline. With zero-skip on neither, the ratio is 0.89–1.08 (`run5.txt` 7b). The tie does not depend on whether 50 PF is reachable.
- **Weight zeros alone justify only part of 50 PF.** Synthetic NVFP4 weights quantize only 7–13% of codes to zero, worth ≤1.07–1.15× (`zeros5.txt`). Reaching 1.43× needs activation zeros too. Rubin's 50 PF is therefore an upper bound, applied to both.

**The physics.** With best speculation depth and microbatch overlap, both designs are compute-bound. Tokens per MW then reduce to (FLOPs per provisioned joule) ÷ (FLOPs per accepted token):

| Quantity | Rubin, Max-Q | Continuum-5 |
|---|---|---|
| Effective FLOPs per provisioned kW | 17.4 PF/kW (50 × 0.92 / 2.64) | 16.5 PF/kW (32 × 1.43 / 2.78) |
| Speculation waste, (k+1) ÷ expected accepted tokens | k = 2–4: 1.23–1.49 | k = 1–2: 1.11–1.23 |

- The two effects cancel.
- **No memory hierarchy changes this.** Weight reads are 10–30% of activity energy for both designs (`energy5.txt`).

### 0.3 Defensible claim

- **What Continuum-5 is:** an HBM-frugal frontier-MoE decode engine.
  - It matches Rubin on tokens per MW for 1.6T models at 128K.
  - It does so with **2.9× fewer HBM stacks per token** (2.1× fewer DRAM bits).
  - It pulls ahead at 1M context and ≥1,000 tok/s/user, on low-acceptance traffic, and on sparser models.
- **What it is not:**
  - a general Rubin-beater;
  - a training chip;
  - a better choice than LPX or Cerebras for models under ~200B parameters.
- **Same-generation caveat.** Continuum uses N2 plus HBM4, the same technology generation as Rubin (N3P plus HBM4). But with no silicon, it could not ship before about 2028, and its calendar-year rival would be Rubin Ultra or Feynman. I could not model those, as no usable specifications exist.

### 0.4 Calibration warning

- **Measured Rubin.** At MLPerf Inference v6.1 (preview), DeepSeek-R1 Interactive, VR200 NVL72 is reported at 652,750 tok/s, or 9.07k per GPU. GB300 NVL72 is reported at 253–260k.
- **My model is 3–11× above that measurement**, depending on average decode context (2.8K → 16K), which is not published. Interactive TPOT is assumed at ~16 ms (`calib5.txt`).
- **The model is, if anything, generous to Rubin.** Its Rubin/GB300 ratio is 3.0–3.25× against 2.54× measured.
- **Implication.** Absolute tokens per watt in this answer are ceilings. Every ratio assumes Continuum realises the same fraction of its ceiling as GPUs do. That is unmeasured (§10, test 2).

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Package blocks

**C-Die (compute)**
- 2 per package on N2, ~750 mm² each, 144 tiles per die (288 per package).
- Each tile: a ~111 TF FP4 tensor engine plus an FP32 vector/softmax unit.
- **32 PF FP4 / 16 PF FP8 dense per package.**
- **New: zero-skip front end.** Each tile skips zero weight codes using offline compression metadata, and zero activations in flight. Assumed to give the same up-to-1.43× as Rubin, at +40 W.
- Attention runs on the tensor engines. The FP4 indexer runs at the FP4 rate.
- Compute energy assumption: 0.040 pJ/FLOP on N2, against an assumed 0.050 for Rubin on N3P. This rests on TSMC's ~25–30% iso-performance power claim for N2 and is unmeasured.

**W-Tier (weight SRAM)**
- 2 SRAM-only N4-class tiers under each C-Die (4 per package), hybrid-bonded face-to-back.
- Capacity: **9.2 GB per package**.
  - Derivation: 31.8 Mb/mm² × 0.89 (ECC) × 0.90 (TSV keep-out) × 0.97 (repair).
- Bandwidth and energy: **256 TB/s at 0.15 pJ/bit**, 307 W at full rate.
  - Energy per bit: macro 0.03–0.05 pJ + ~1 mm of wire 0.04 pJ + bond 0.01 pJ.
  - Bonds: ~4.7k signal bonds per tile at 1.5 Gb/s, ~0.4 mm² of a ~5 mm² tile at 9 µm pitch.
- **Correction to round 4.** With m = 2 interleaving, the W-Tier binds at ≥1,000 tok/s/user, because every microbatch re-reads all touched experts (§4). It is no longer "over-provisioned". Doubling it would gain only ~7% (stage time 38.2 → 35.8 µs), since compute (17.9 µs) is close behind.

**K-Mem (KV cache only) — changed**
- **3 × HBM4 16-high, 48 GB each: 144 GB at ~7.5 TB/s.**
- Fallback until 16-high ships: 3 × 36 GB 12-high.
  - Tokens per MW 0.90–1.03× against 0.99–1.03× for the primary.
  - Same 2.9× tokens per stack at ≥1,000.
- Weights never touch HBM.

**Link**
- 64 lanes of 224G per direction: 1.8 TB/s, 85% usable.
- Allocation: within the layer group, to the next stage, and a spare/remap plane.
- Never binds: 5.7–6.7 µs of link time against a 19–21 µs microbatch cycle.

|---|---|
| Compute plus zero-skip | 1,320 |
| W-Tier | 307 |
| HBM (3 stacks) | 240 |
| Links | 144 |
| Leakage, clock, other | 250 |

- The same method gives Rubin 2.64 kW at Max-Q and 3.06 kW at Max-P.
- Continuum's overhead is ~0.5 kW against Rubin's ~0.8 kW. That is because there are no NVSwitch trays and fewer CPUs, not because its accounting is lighter.
- If Continuum's slot is 3.06 kW instead, its ratios fall by ×0.91.

**Thermal**
- ~1.05–1.2 W/mm², GPU-class, so **no thermal advantage**.
- ~700 A per C-Die passes through TSVs in two tiers.

**Packaging**
- 2 SoIC stacks plus 3 HBM sites on CoWoS-L, an interposer of ~2.7 reticles.

**Pod**
- 192 packages: 187 active and 5 spare. 3 liquid-cooled racks, ~530 kW provisioned.
- 1.77 TB of SRAM and 27.6 TB of HBM.

### 3.2 Mapping

**Layer placement**
- 3 packages per layer group. One layer is 14.65 GB, so SRAM is 53% full.
- 62 stage groups (61 layers plus the drafter) and 1 LM-head package.

**Within a group**
- Each expert is sliced into thirds of its intermediate dimension, one third per package.
- Attention is data-parallel across users.
- Fixed-size all-gather and reduce-scatter, compile-time scheduled.

**Within a package (new)**
- Each expert's package slice is spread over r = 16 tiles, 64 columns per tile.
- Load imbalance across 288 tiles at the operating points is 1.10–1.15. This holds even under heavily skewed expert popularity (lognormal σ = 1), because each tile carries pieces of ~21 experts.
- With r = 4 it would be 1.3–2.3, a risk the earlier rounds missed.
- The 47% free SRAM is used to replicate hot experts, an EPLB-style balancer that runs offline.
- A GPU with expert parallelism over 72 GPUs and 32 redundant experts measures 1.06–1.09 at σ ≤ 0.5 and 1.29–1.46 at σ = 1 (`imbal5.txt`). Both designs get their σ = 0.5 values in the model.

**Microbatching**
- m = 2 microbatches per stage. Each stage runs one microbatch while the other's link transfer and dependency latency are in flight.
- Users in flight = 62 × m × b, limited by HBM capacity.

- **The constraint moves to SoIC.** 1 GW is ~360k packages: 720k C-Dies, each bonded to 2 tiers. That is ~20k wafer-bonding passes, about **1.4–2.1 months of TSMC's entire reported 2026 SoIC capacity**.

- **The gain is real only if HBM is scarcer than SoIC for the buyer.**

### 6.2 Long context, low acceptance, sparser models

All at equal τ (`run5.txt` §B):

| Regime | Ratio vs Rubin |
|---|---|
| 1.6T, 1M, p = 0.8 | 1.12 / 1.41 / 2.29 at ≥1,000 / ≥1,500 / ≥2,000 |
| 1.6T, 128K, p = 0.6 | 1.27 / 1.20 / 1.34 at ≥500 / ≥1,000 / ≥1,500 |
| 1.6T, 1M, p = 0.6 | 2.14 at ≥1,000; Rubin near its ceiling beyond |
| 6.2T, 128K, p = 0.8 | 1.08 / 1.20 at ≥500 / ≥1,000 |

- For the 6.2T model at ≥1,500, Rubin NVL72 cannot reach the tier: streaming 3.5 TB of experts at 1.58 PB/s takes ≥2.2 ms per step.
- I did not model a two-rack scale-out expert-parallel configuration, and Rubin Ultra NVL576 (2027) would likely close this gap. **I do not claim the 15–40× figures.**

### 6.3 What transfers to GPUs today (gain for the asker without Continuum)

- **Microbatch overlap.** In the model, two-batch overlap takes Rubin from 26.9 to 37.9 tok/s/W at ≥200, at τ = 20 µs, roughly the GPU central case in round 4. It also removes per-layer latency as a throughput factor below ~1,500 tok/s/user.
- **FP4 indexer and NVFP4 KV.** Rubin at 128K and ≥200 goes 37.9 → 42.0 (+11%).
- **Max-Q** is Rubin's better perf-per-watt profile at most tiers, if compute is ≥92% of Max-P (an assumption).

## 7. Where Continuum loses or ties

1. **Home workload (1.6T, 128K, p = 0.8) against Rubin:** 0.94–1.03×, a tie. It loses 6% at ≥2,000 if GPU τ reaches 10 µs.
2. **With NVFP4 KV on every system:** 0.90–1.02× at 128K.
3. **Prefill:** a tie.
4. **Peak single-stream speed:** CS-4 is modelled higher (5.7k against 5.3k tok/s/user). For models ≤200B, LPX and Cerebras win outright.
5. **Weight-read energy per token** is higher than Rubin's (§4).
6. **Deployment unit:** ~530 kW per pod. Models above ~1.6 TB of weights need more packages (435 for 6.2T).
7. **Training:** not a target.
8. **Software maturity:** GPU software gained ~5× on V4 in 43 days. Continuum would start from zero.
9. **Calendar:** Continuum would ship around 2028, against Rubin Ultra or Feynman, which are not modelled.

## 8. What has to be true

| Assumption (base) | If wrong | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Continuum realises the same fraction of its modelled ceiling as GPUs (GPUs realise ~1/3–1/11 of theirs) | Lower fraction | Every ratio scales down by the same factor. **This is now the decisive unknown.** |
| Tile efficiency η = 0.6 at 10–17 positions per expert slice; tile imbalance 1.12 | η = 0.45 | Compute-bound points ×~0.75, so it loses to Rubin by ~25% |
| Compute energy 0.040 pJ/FLOP (N2) | 0.050 (equal to Rubin) | Tokens per MW ×~0.88 |
| Provisioned slot 2.78 kW | 3.06 kW | ×0.91 |
| Zero-skip gain equal on both | Continuum gets weight-only skip (≤1.15×) and Rubin gets 1.43× | Ratio ~0.8–0.85 |
| 16-high HBM4 48 GB available | 12-high only | Tokens per MW 0.90–1.03× at 128K and 0.86–0.98× at 1M ≤1,000; tokens per stack unchanged at ≥1,000 |
| HBM stays scarcer than SoIC | HBM plentiful | The 2.9× per-stack gain stops mattering and the result is a tie |
| Models stay sparse MoE with compressed attention | Dense long-context attention | Decode becomes HBM-bound: Continuum has 7.5 TB/s against Rubin's 22, so it loses |

## 9. Risks and physical, process and supply limits

- **SoIC** (largest supply risk): ~1.4–2.1 months of 2026 capacity per GW, most of it already committed to others. Supply-chain reports put new capacity at ~$6.8–7B per 10k wafers per month.
- **CoWoS-L:** ~2.7-reticle interposer, about the same wafer demand per GW as Rubin.
- **HBM:** 1.08M 16-high stacks per GW against 3.03M for Rubin. 16-high volume availability is unconfirmed.
- **Thermal and power delivery:** ~1.05–1.2 W/mm²; ~700 A per C-Die through TSV-perforated tiers; SRAM retention at high temperature; no backside power on N2 (A16 would help).
- **Yield:**
  - A 750 mm² N2 die yields ~47% raw, ~65% with tile harvesting.
  - Tiers ~90% after repair; each bond interface 98–99%.
  - Known-good-die test before bonding.
- **Pipeline fragility:** one failed package stalls 1/187 of the pipeline, so the 5 spares and the remap plane are mandatory.
- **Weight-stationary imbalance:** each expert must be spread over ≥16 tiles. Narrow 64-column slices put the η = 0.6 assumption at risk.
- **Workload drift:**
  - Better drafters (p ≈ 0.9) or more active parameters push both designs further into compute-bound territory, where they tie.
  - Fewer, larger experts reduce the HBM advantage.

| # | Test | Tool | Kill criterion |
