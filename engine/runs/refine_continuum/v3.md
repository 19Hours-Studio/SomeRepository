# Continuum: a weight-stationary, 3D-SRAM inference accelerator for long-context, interactive frontier-MoE decode

## 0. Verdict (round 3)

**The round-2 headline does not survive a fair comparison.** Round 2 claimed a 4.4× lead over Rubin at ≥1,000 tok/s/user. That lead came from limiting every GPU to one draft token (MTP-1). This round fixes that:

- Every system picks its own best speculation depth k (0–7) at each speed tier.
- GPUs, TPUs and Continuum get the same per-layer exposed latency τ (8 µs).
- The cost of the draft layer is now counted.

With those fixes, deep drafting (DSpark/DFlash-style, about 3–4 tokens accepted per step) turns the GPU's weight-bound interactive regime into a compute-bound one. That removes most of Continuum's lead.

**Corrected model result** (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 128K context, equal τ = 8 µs, best k for each system, output tok/s per provisioned MW):

| Draft acceptance p | Continuum vs Rubin at 35 PF dense | Continuum vs Rubin if 50 PF is usable |
|---|---|---|
| 0.8 (code, tools, JSON) | 1.07–1.26× | 0.90–1.02× |
| 0.6 (open prose) | 1.07–1.40× at ≤1,000 tok/s/user | 0.90–1.24× |

| Comparison | Continuum's ratio |
|---|---|
| vs MI455X | 1.3–1.6× |
| vs TPU 8i | 1.4–3.6× |
| vs Rubin + Groq-3 LPX | 3.1–3.6× |
| vs Cerebras CS-4 | 12–18× |
| Peak single-stream speed vs CS-4 | Loses: 6.2k vs 11.3k tok/s/user |
| 1M context vs Rubin | 1.04–1.65× |
| 8K context vs Rubin-50 | Loses: 0.84–0.87× |

**Where the design wins robustly:**

- **Output tokens per HBM stack:** 1.4–1.9× Rubin.
- **Activity energy:** 1.25–1.6× better tok/J. Static-power assumptions dominate this figure.

**Defensible claim.** Continuum is the strongest design I can put numbers to for HBM-constrained, long-context (≥128K), interactive (≥800 tok/s/user) serving of frontier MoE models. Its lead there is 10–90%, not 4×.

- It is **not** shown to be best in the world.
- Against Rubin it is roughly a tie at throughput tiers and on short context.
- The lead disappears if per-layer exposed latency exceeds ~12 µs, or if weight-streaming tiles reach less than ~55% of peak.
- Every Continuum number is model output, and the GPU model itself does not match one InferenceX data point (§5.4).

**Why the lead is bounded (physics, not tuning).** Drafting k tokens spreads each weight read over k+1 positions. The price is verifying rejected drafts: (k+1)/E[accepted] times the FLOPs per output token. At p = 0.8 and k = 3 that is 1.36×. Continuum reads weights cheaply, so it stays at k = 1–2 and pays about 1.1–1.2×. Once everyone is compute-bound, tok/MW tracks FLOPs per watt, and Continuum and Rubin are both at about 11–13 PF/kW. So:

> Continuum's interactive lead ≈ (GPU speculation waste ÷ Continuum speculation waste) × (static-power ratio) ≈ 1.1–1.4×.

It grows only where drafts are rejected often (prose, high-temperature sampling) or where KV reads dominate (1M context).

## 1. Target workload and metrics

**Workload**

- **Primary:** decode of a DeepSeek-V4-Pro-class MoE.
  - 1.6T total parameters, 49B active, 61 layers, hidden size 7168.
  - 384 routed experts (top-6) plus 1 shared expert; expert FFN size 3072.
  - Attention: 128 query heads × 512, one shared KV head.
  - CSA (compression 4, top-1024) / HCA (compression 128) split ~30/31, with a 128-token window.
  - KV in FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions.
  - Context 128K (also 8K and 1M).
- **Speculation:** a drafter costing one MoE layer (weights, FLOPs, latency), depth k = 0–7, geometric acceptance p = 0.8 or 0.6.
  - Reported acceptance lengths are 3.1–3.85 at depth 5. That fits p ≈ 0.8 on predictable output. Depth-1 MTP gains only ~0–10% on free prose ([DSpark](https://www.remio.ai/post/deepseek-releases-dspark-speculative-decoding-framework-to-speed-up-deepseek-v4-generation-by-60-85), [HyperDFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26744), [DGX Spark MTP](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-deepseek-v4-flash-mtp-workload-asymmetry)).
- **Secondary:** agentic turns, i.e. 2K new prefill tokens per 1K output tokens over a cached 128K prefix.
- **Out of scope:**
  - Training and edge.
  - Models under ~200B parameters, where LPX and Cerebras already fit in SRAM. Nvidia's 3,400 tok/s/user LPX result is on Gemma 4 31B ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Groq-3-LPX-Now-in-Full-Production-With-World-Class-Speed-for-Agentic-AI/default.aspx), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-says-its-groq-3-lpx-is-four-times-faster-than-cerebras-but-the-math-is-more-complicated/)).

**Metrics**

| Metric | Role |
|---|---|
| **Output tok/s per provisioned MW at a per-user speed floor** (100 … 3,000 tok/s/user), each system at its own best speculation depth | Primary. Sites are power-limited. Without a speed floor, any chip looks efficient at 5 tok/s/user. Without best-k, the comparison favours whichever chip reads weights cheapest. |
| Output tok/s per HBM stack | HBM4 is the scarcest input in 2026 |
| Activity energy (tok/J) | Physical consistency check |
| Max tok/s per user | The latency-tier race |
| Cost per token | Only a rough estimate: prices are not public, and capex per MW is assumed similar within ±30% |

**Why interactive long context.** Reasoning and agent workloads multiply output tokens per answer: a 20K-token chain of thought takes 20 s at 1,000 tok/s/user and 100 s at 200. Agents also carry 100K+ contexts.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Package blocks

**C-Die (compute)**

- 2 per package, ~750 mm² each on N2. 144 tiles per die, 288 per package.
- Each tile has ~111 TF FP4 of tensor engine plus an FP32 vector/softmax unit.
- **32 PF FP4 / 16 PF FP8 dense per package.**
- Attention runs on the tensor engines. All 128 query heads share one KV head, so each KV entry feeds a GEMM with M = 128 × positions.
- Compute energy is assumed at 0.04 pJ/FLOP, giving 1.28 kW.
- Basis: Rubin's whole chip runs at ~0.05–0.065 pJ/FLOP all-in on N3P, and N2 is about 25% lower power.

**Tensor-engine dataflow**

- Activations stay in registers and weights stream past them.
- At the operating point each expert slice sees ~4–40 positions per stage.
- Per tile, the W-Tier feeds 0.89 TB/s, or 1.58 T FP4 weights/s. The ridge is ~35 positions per expert, so tiles below that are W-Tier-bound by design.

**W-Tier (weight SRAM)**

- 2 tiers under each C-Die, 4 per package. Each is an N4-class, ~750 mm² SRAM-only die, hybrid-bonded face-to-back (SoIC) at ~9 µm pitch.
- **Usable: 2.3 GB per tier, 9.2 GB per package, ~32 MB under each tile.**
  - Derivation: 31.8 Mb/mm² × 0.89 (ECC) × 0.90 (TSV keep-out) × 0.97 (repair) ≈ 24.7 Mb/mm².
- **Bandwidth: 256 TB/s per package at 0.15 pJ/bit** (range 0.08–0.3), which is 307 W. Power sets that cap, not the bonds.
- Energy estimate per bit:
  - macro read ~0.03–0.05 pJ;
  - ~1 mm of wire at ~0.04 pJ/bit/mm;
  - bond ~0.01 pJ.
- Bonds: ~4.7k signal bonds per tile at 1.5 Gb/s, taking ~0.4 mm² of a ~5 mm² tile.

**K-Mem (KV cache only)**

- 4 × HBM4 12-high: 144 GB at ~10 TB/s. Weights never touch HBM.

**Link**

- 64 lanes of 224G per direction, 1.8 TB/s per direction.
- Allocation: 2 × 12 lanes within the layer group, 30 lanes to the next stage, 10 lanes to a switched plane used for spare remapping.

**Power**

| Item | W |
|---|---|
| Compute | 1,280 |
| W-Tier | 307 |
| HBM | 320 |
| Links | 144 |
| Leakage, clock, other | 250 |
| **Peak** | **~2.3 kW** |

- TDP is 2.1 kW.
- **The provisioned slot is 2.45 kW**, including the share of hosts, switches and power conversion.

**Thermal**

- The C-Die sits on top, against the cold plate. The stack runs at ~1.05 W/mm², about the same as GPUs, so there is **no thermal advantage**.
- About 700 A per C-Die reaches it through TSVs in two tiers.

**Packaging**

- 2 SoIC stacks plus 4 HBM4 sites on CoWoS-L, an interposer of ~3 reticles (Rubin uses ~4).

**Pod**

- **192 packages: 187 active (62 layers incl. drafter × 3, plus 1 for the LM head) and 5 spare.**
- 3 liquid-cooled racks, ~470 kW in total.
- 1.77 TB of SRAM and 27.6 TB of HBM.

### 3.2 Mapping: 3 packages per layer, point-to-point

**Layer placement**

- One V4-Pro layer is 14.65 GB: 14.3 GB of NVFP4 experts plus ~0.43 GB of FP8 attention and shared expert.
- At g = 3 packages per layer, SRAM is 53% full.
- At g = 2 the same pod holds up to ~1.6 TB of weights.

**Within a layer group**

- Each expert is sliced into thirds of its intermediate dimension, so there is no expert-load imbalance between packages.
- Attention is data-parallel: each package holds 1/3 of the users' KV.
- FP8 hidden states are all-gathered within the group.
- BF16 partial sums are reduce-scattered to the next group.

**Traffic and latency**

- Traffic at the ≥200 tier is ~20.6 MB out per package per stage. That is ~11.5 µs of link time inside a ~52 µs compute-bound stage, overlapped by chunking.
- **Exposed τ: 5 µs is the design goal. 8 µs is used in all headline comparisons, the same as the GPUs get.**
- Is the 5 µs goal plausible? Continuum's traffic is point-to-point and statically scheduled, whereas GPUs do dispatch and combine all-to-alls over 72 endpoints. But I have no measurement, so the advantage is not credited (§7).

**Why g = 3**

- Spreading a layer over more packages multiplies its weight-read bandwidth without changing packages per MW.
- With MTP-1, g = 3 gives 2.8× the tok/MW of g = 2 at ≥1,000 tok/s/user, and the same at throughput tiers.

### 3.3 Numerics

| Data | Format |
|---|---|
| Expert weights | NVFP4 (E2M1, E4M3 scale per 16 values, FP32 tensor scale; 4.5 bits per value) |
| Attention, shared expert, drafter | FP8 |
| Matmul activations | FP8 |
| Accumulation, softmax | FP32 |
| Partial sums on links | BF16 |
| KV cache | FP8 with BF16 RoPE dimensions |
| Indexer | FP4 |
| BF16 | Quarter rate; no FP64 |

V4-Pro is already served at FP4 on GB300, so the format choice costs no accuracy relative to GPUs.

### 3.4 Alternatives considered and rejected

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **All-SRAM (LPX, Cerebras)** | 0.8–1.0 GB of SRAM per kW against Continuum's 3.8. Holding a 0.9 TB model takes 1–1.2 MW of SRAM-only silicon against ~0.24 MW of Continuum. It loses 3–18× on tok/MW for 1.6T models. |
| **HBM-only GPU with deeper speculation** (i.e. Rubin) | This is the strong competitor. It now ties at throughput tiers. Its remaining handicaps are speculation waste, weight re-reads from HBM at 3.5 pJ/bit, and 8 HBM stacks per package. |
| **DRAM hybrid-bonded to logic** | ~10× SRAM density, so a whole layer fits in one package. Rejected for 2026 because there is no production DRAM-on-logic process with hybrid-bond pitch and >100 TB/s per package. It is the natural Continuum-2 if one appears. |
| **One W-Tier per C-Die (g = 4, 249 packages)** | Same or slightly better tok/MW up to 1,000 tok/s/user (§7), with half the bonds. It falls off at ≥1,500 tok/s/user. **This is the fallback SKU if SoIC capacity binds.** |

## 4. Sanity arithmetic (`round3/model3.py`)

**Per token at 128K**

- 101 GFLOP of linear work (plus ~1.6 for the drafter) and 34.5 GFLOP of attention.
- 103 MB of KV read per step.
- 0.65 GB of KV resident per user (~4.9 KB per token, consistent with vLLM's 9.62 GiB per 1M-token sequence in BF16).

**Stage at ≥200 tok/s/user** (k = 1, g = 3, b = 539 users per stage, 62 stages)

| Component | Time |
|---|---|
| Weights (all 384 experts touched): 4.9 GB per package at 256 TB/s | 19 µs |
| KV: 539 × 1.66 MB / 3 at 10 TB/s | ~30 µs |
| Compute: 1,078 positions × 135 GF / 62 / 3 at η = 0.6 | ~51 µs (binding) |

- With τ = 8 µs the stage is ~59 µs.
- Results: **485 tok/s/user, 33.4k concurrent users, 73.7k output tok/s per package.**
- KV occupies ~115 GB of the 130 GB usable HBM.

**Stage at ≥1,000 tok/s/user** (best k = 2, b = 203)

- W-Tier and compute are roughly balanced.
- Results: 1,056 tok/s/user and 60.4k tok/s per package.

### 5.3 Prefill and the agentic blend

- **Prefill** is compute-bound: 32 PF / 2.45 kW against 35 PF / 3.06 kW. That gives 1.14× against Rubin35 and **0.90× against Rubin50**.
- **Agentic blend** (2 prefill tokens per output token on the same fleet): Continuum ties Rubin within ±15%.
- A prefill-heavy fleet should run prefill on GPUs or a Rubin CPX-style tier.

### 5.4 Calibration (unresolved)

- **At ~50 tok/s/user:** the GB300 model at 8K/1K gives ~7.1k output tok/s/GPU. InferenceX reports ~7.5–9.4k, so they agree if InferenceX counts output tokens. The glossary suggests it does but does not say so.
- **At 126 tok/s/user:** InferenceX's 923 tok/s/GPU is ~7× *below* the model. Either real per-layer GPU overheads at interactive batch sizes are well above 40 µs, or that snapshot predates the 5× software gains.
- I do not credit this gap to Continuum. A pipeline of 187 packages may hide overheads of its own that the model does not capture.
- **Read the ratios, not the absolute numbers.**

## 6. Where Continuum loses or ties

1. **Against Rubin with 50 PF usable:** loses by 6–10% at ≤800 tok/s/user at 128K, and by 13–16% at 8K. It ties at ≥1,000.
2. **Against Rubin35 at throughput tiers:** about 1.07–1.12×. That is inside the model's error, so it counts as a tie.
3. **Prefill:** 0.90–1.14×, a tie.
4. **Peak single-stream speed:** CS-4 reaches 11.3k against 6.2k tok/s/user. On models ≤200B, LPX and Cerebras probably win outright.
5. **Very high tiers:** at ≥3,000 tok/s/user, everything except CS-4 collapses. Continuum is 0.9× Rubin there.
6. **Deployment unit:** about 470 kW and 192 packages. Small models waste most of it, and models above ~1.6 TB of weights need a second pod.
7. **Training:** not a target. A variant with the same C-Die and 8 × HBM4 would at best reach parity. Use GPUs or TPU 8t.
8. **Software maturity:** GPUs gained ~5× on V4 in 43 days. Continuum would start that curve from zero, and speculation research (DSpark, DFlash) is moving in the GPU's favour.

## 8. Risks and physical, process and supply limits

- **SoIC hybrid-bonding capacity (largest supply risk).**
  - 1 GW of Continuum is ~408k packages and ~1.6M bonded tiers of 750 mm² each.
  - TSMC does not publish SoIC capacity.
  - Mitigations: tiers on N4 tested as known-good dies before bonding; the 1-tier g = 4 SKU halves bond demand at about equal tok/MW below 1,000 tok/s/user.
- **CoWoS-L:** ~3 reticles per package against ~4 for Rubin, so per GW the interposer area is similar. Supply is 10–20% short.
- **HBM4:** ~1.6M stacks per GW (0.8M with the 2-stack SKU) against ~2.6M for Rubin, at 1.4–1.9× the tokens per stack. **This is Continuum's most robust advantage while HBM is sold out.**
- **Thermal and IR drop.**
  - ~1.05 W/mm², with ~700 A per C-Die through two TSV-perforated tiers.
  - SRAM leakage at high temperature: ~100–200 W across 9.2 GB.
  - N2 has no backside power delivery; A16 would help.
- **Yield.**
  - A 750 mm² N2 die at D0 ≈ 0.1/cm² yields ~47% raw, ~65% with tile harvesting.
  - Tiers yield ~90% after repair; each bond interface ~98–99%.
  - A package with one dead tier (6.9 GB) still works at g ≥ 3.
- **Pipeline fragility:** one failed package stalls 1/187 of the pipeline. The spares and the remap path through the switched plane are mandatory.
- **Workload drift.**
  - **Better drafters shrink Continuum's lead further.** If acceptance reaches p ≈ 0.9 on all text, the interactive advantage tends toward the static-power ratio (~1.1×).
  - Dense long-context attention would make decode HBM-bound, and Continuum has 10 TB/s against Rubin's 22.
  - More active parameters push work toward compute, where Continuum only ties.

## 9. Software and programming model

- **Path:** PyTorch or JAX → `torch.export` / StableHLO → Continuum compiler. The compiler:
  - chooses g and assigns layers (including the drafter) to stage groups;
  - slices experts into thirds;
  - keeps vendor FP4 checkpoints, or quantizes to NVFP4 behind an accuracy gate;
  - emits static per-stage schedules for tiles, W-Tier reads, HBM KV pages and link slots;
  - chunks microbatches so link transfers overlap compute.
- **Dynamic MoE routing** is resolved inside a stage. Every expert is sliced across all 3 packages, so a routing decision changes only which W-Tier rows each tile reads. There are no runtime collectives.
- **Speculation:**
  - The drafter is one more pipeline stage.
  - Speculation depth k is chosen per speed tier at runtime (k = 1–2 is typical, per §4).
  - Tree drafts are handled as extra positions inside a microbatch slot.
- **Serving:** a vLLM/SGLang out-of-tree platform plugin, the same mechanism TPU and Ascend use. It provides continuous batching into fixed microbatch slots, prefix caching in HBM and host memory, and speculative decoding.
- **What breaks:**
  - Custom CUDA/Triton kernels do not port.
  - Dynamic shapes are padded into buckets.
  - New attention variants need compiler support before they run.
  - Non-FP4/FP8 checkpoints need post-training quantization.

## 10. Tests that could prove the design wrong

| # | Test | Tool | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exposed per-stage latency: chunked all-gather and reduce-scatter across 3 packages plus a hop to the next stage | FPGA or test-chip loopback; ASTRA-sim | τ > 10 µs |
| 2 | Weight-streaming tile efficiency at M = 4–40, FP4 with E4M3 scales | Timeloop + RTL of one tile | η < 0.55 |
| 3 | W-Tier bandwidth and energy through a 3D bond path | CACTI/DESTINY + Accelergy; ISSCC macro data | < 180 TB/s or > 0.3 pJ/bit per package |
| 4 | Thermal and IR drop for the C-Die over 2 tiers | HotSpot / 3D-ICE; PDN solver | Tj > 105 °C, IR drop > 5%, or retention failures |
| 5 | **Deep-speculation GPU baseline:** measure Rubin/GB300 tok/s/GPU at 800–1,500 tok/s/user with DSpark-class drafters on real traffic, and fit the measured acceptance p | InferenceX spec-decode runs, or own runs | If GPUs reach ≥0.9× of `model3`'s Rubin envelope **and** real acceptance is ≥0.85, Continuum's interactive lead falls below 1.15× and the project is not worth taping out |
| 6 | Token-basis calibration: rerun GB300 and B200 at several interactivity points; resolve the 7× gap at 126 tok/s/user | Python + InferenceX raw data | Model > 2× optimistic for GPUs after fixing the token basis → re-derive τ and derates for every system |
| 7 | End-to-end pipeline with V4 routing traces (expert skew, sequence-length variance, speculation-acceptance variance across microbatches) | Event simulator or LLMCompass | < 70% of the `model3` operating point |
| 8 | FP4 accuracy at 128K–1M, only if the checkpoint is not natively FP4 | GPU cluster | > 1 point loss against FP8 |

None of these could be run here: there is no silicon, and model downloads are blocked. Everything above is analytical.
