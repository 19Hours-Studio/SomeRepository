Propose a new architecture for an AI accelerator chip, named "Continuum", that could credibly be called the best in the world. The asker has not said which workloads count: training, inference, or both. They have not said what "best" is measured by, or which kind of target (data center, edge, or other) the chip is for. A good answer therefore has to state its own target workloads and metrics, then defend the architecture against the current leading designs. Those designs include GPUs such as the Nvidia Rubin class, Google TPUs such as Ironwood, and SRAM-centric dataflow chips such as Cerebras WSE-3 and Groq LPU. The architecture should cover compute, memory hierarchy, interconnect, numerics, programming model, packaging and power. It should cite known physical, economic and manufacturing limits, and say what would need to be true for it to work. Reported 2026 reference points are listed in CONTEXT. Many are vendor or blog figures, and the sources disagree on some numbers.
A good answer: 
- The answer states explicit target workloads and a metric set. Examples are tokens/s per user, tokens per joule, cost per token, and training throughput. It says why those metrics define "best".
- It describes a coherent architecture with named blocks and quantified estimates. Estimates cover compute, on-chip and off-chip memory capacity and bandwidth, interconnect, power and die or package size. They are sanity-checked against roofline or bandwidth arithmetic.
- It compares quantitatively against at least three current designs (GPU, TPU, SRAM/wafer-scale) and says where Continuum wins, where it loses, and by how much.
- It names the main risks and the physical, process or supply limits (HBM supply, CoWoS packaging, power density, yield). It gives a falsifiable test or simulation plan that could prove the design wrong.
- It covers the software stack and programming model, and says what happens to existing models and frameworks.
- It separates established facts from the design's own assumptions and estimates.
- "Best in the world" is not asserted without evidence. Trade-offs are stated honestly.
Constraints: 
- The name is "Continuum".
- The answer is an architecture for an AI chip.
- Nothing else was specified. The answer must be a real, physically plausible design, not marketing text.

The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: Continuum-D: SRAM weights, LPDDR KV
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged. Weights of a frontier MoE stay resident in plain 2D SRAM across many standard-packaged dies, with no HBM and no CoWoS, and each user's KV cache lives in LPDDR attached to each die. Repaired sizing: - At NVFP4's 4.5 bits per weight the model is 377 GB, not 335 GB. That takes 333 N3 dies of 800 mm², each with 1.25 GB of SRAM (428 mm² at 31.8 Mib/mm² and 70% array efficiency), about 1.3 PF of FP8, and 48 GB of LPDDR6 at 1 TB/s (26 x24 channels). - The dies form 61 layer-groups that broadcast and then reduce, instead of one 333-stage pipeline. That cuts the hop floor from 233 µs to 85 µs per token, so the per-user ceiling is about 11k tok/s. - Each die keeps a copy of a neighbour's KV slice. Without it, losing one die would erase part of every user's cache. At 32K context with NVFP4 KV the system serves 414k tok/s and is compute-bound (LPDDR could carry 514k). Bill of materials is $0.81M at 117 kW, or $2.44M priced at a 3× markup. Energy is 0.19–0.20 J/token, about 9% worse than Rubin, so the original "40× less energy" claim is dropped.
VALUE: At 32K context and ≥1000 tok/s/user it serves tokens at $0.077/Mtok. That is 2.8× cheaper than Rubin NVL72 with wide expert parallelism ($0.214), about 5× cheaper than Rubin plus Groq 3 LPX (if an LPX rack costs ~$4M), and 10–19× cheaper than Cerebras. At ≤500 tok/s/user it only reaches parity (1.1×).
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The energy figure does not reproduce from the design's own numbers. 117 kW ÷ 414k tok/s = 0.283 J/token, not 0.19–0.20, so the design is about 1.5× worse than Rubin, not 9% worse. (check.py.)
- MAJOR: 16 TB of LPDDR6 (48 GB × 333) is sized for capacity the headline tier never uses. At ≥1000 tok/s/user and 414k tok/s only about 414 users are in flight, holding 268 GB of KV against 8 TB available after replication. At 2026 prices that LPDDR alone is $0.16–0.32M of the $0.81M BOM. (check.py; LPDDR $10–21/GB ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/mobile-dram-prices-expected-to-increase-by-100-quarter-over-quarter-as-long-term-agreements-now-getting-signed-at-prices-as-high-as-21-gb/amp/)).)
- MAJOR: The baselines are inconsistent. Rubin's $0.214 at ≥1000 tok/s/user for a 671B MoE is not sourced, and Blackwell-class records for DeepSeek-R1 are in the hundreds of tok/s/user. The 5× against LPX rests on an assumed $4M rack price. Cerebras is compared at its market token price against BOM×3, and Cerebras runs at 2–3× higher per-user speed. (LPX: 256 LPUs, 500 MB each, 128 GB SRAM per rack, so DeepSeek-V3 needs 3 racks; no price was published ([StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/nvidia-groq-3-lpx-everything-we-know)).)
- SURVIVES: 428 mm² for 1.25 GB SRAM (decimal GB), 416 GB total, LPDDR6 26×24 at 14.4 Gb/s = 1.12 TB/s, 0.65 GB KV per user at 32K NVFP4, 514k tok/s LPDDR ceiling, 11.8k tok/s hop ceiling, MLA stage time consistent with about 1,100 tok/s/user.
- RATIO: 1.5 | The claimed 2.8× after adding the LPDDR cost at $20/GB (if it is not in the BOM), 0.283 J/token, and a Rubin/LPX baseline that is unverified at this speed tier.
- KNOWN: yes | Groq multi-hundred-chip SRAM-resident deployments and Nvidia Groq 3 LPX (GTC 2026); d-Matrix Corsair, which combines SRAM with 256 GB of LPDDR ([Chips and Cheese](https://old.chipsandcheese.com/?p=34204)).

IDEA 2
NAME: Detached attention-stack memory nodes
MECHANISM: The anchor is kept: the custom HBM4 logic base die runs GQA decode attention (QKᵀ, softmax, AV at FP8) on KV read through the TSVs, and only partial outputs leave the stack. The repair takes the stack off the GPU interposer. It uses a JEDEC SPHBM4-style organic-substrate mount (standard published July 2026), with a narrow link in place of the 2048-bit host bus. Each 48 GB, 2.5 TB/s stack becomes a separate KV node with a 13–19 W attention base die. It serves 2 users at 128K context (FP8 KV) at 49 tok/s each and needs only 0.2 GB/s of decode link, because KV bytes read outnumber link bytes about 8,000 to 1 (plus ~2 GB/s to load KV after prefill). The claimed 2× energy saving does not hold: counting the GPU-side NoC, L2 and shared-memory path, it is 33% (5.34 → 3.58 pJ/bit). The real gain is that KV capacity and bandwidth grow without CoWoS or GPU silicon: one Rubin handles weights and FFN for about 1,200–1,500 users while 400–760 stacks hold their KV.
VALUE: Long-context (128K) GQA decode costs about 1.7× less per million tokens than the best baseline (Rubin-only: $0.177 vs $0.304 at cost of goods; 2.6× at market prices), and 1.5× less at 16K context.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The idea sells relief from the HBM shortage but uses 1.57× more HBM per delivered token/s than Rubin-only. It needs 48 GB of HBM for every 2 users at 49 tok/s, while Rubin serves about 11 users at about 84 tok/s from 288 GB. SPHBM4 avoids CoWoS but not the DRAM shortage. (check.py: 0.490 GB vs 0.312 GB per tok/s; 750 stacks = 36 TB of HBM per Rubin. [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/jedec-releases-new-sphbm4-standard-to-slash-ai-memory-costs-narrow-512-bit-interface-enables-dropping-expensive-interposers-for-organic-substrates) notes SPHBM4 does not relieve the DRAM shortage.)
- MAJOR: The baseline is dense-GQA full attention at 128K. A competent operator at 128K now runs sparse or latent attention (DSA top-2048, MLA, hybrid linear), which reads about 64× fewer KV bytes per token, so the stacks' bandwidth advantage mostly disappears. (2048/131072 = 1.6% of the dense KV read.)
- MAJOR: The fan-in at the GPU is ignored. 400–760 stacks at 0.26 GB/s each add up to 104–198 GB/s per Rubin, which is essentially the whole 1.6 Tb/s (200 GB/s) ConnectX-9. On top of that, each token needs 80 serial GPU↔stack round trips with a 255 µs budget each, and every round trip waits for the slowest of 760 nodes. (check.py: 760 × 98 tok/s × 2.66 MB = 198 GB/s.)
- MAJOR: The cost advantage is very sensitive to HBM price, because 36 TB of HBM sits behind each Rubin. HBM4 is forecast at about $1,300 per stack, or $36–53/GB, in 2027. (At $36/GB the idea costs about $0.257 vs $0.355 for Rubin (1.38×). At $53/GB it is $0.323 vs $0.397 (1.23×). ([Silicon Analysts/TrendForce](https://siliconanalysts.com/market/trendforce-lifts-2027-hbm-asp-forecast-to-121-hbm4-stack-seen-doubling-to-1-300-2026-10-01)))
- SURVIVES: 21.5 GB per user at 128K (Llama-70B-class FP8 KV), 2 users in 48 GB, 84% bandwidth efficiency for 49 tok/s, the 8,066:1 byte ratio, 0.26 GB/s link per stack, about 34 TFLOP/s FP8 per base die; SPHBM4 (JESD330-4, 13 Jul 2026, 512 data signals) is real.
- RATIO: 1.2 | The claimed 1.7× is re-priced at 2027 HBM4 ($36–53/GB) with 1.57× HBM per token. Against a sparse-attention baseline it would be about 1.0.
- KNOWN: partly | AttAcc (attention in HBM-PIM, ASPLOS 2024), SK hynix AiM and Samsung HBM-PIM; attention offload in Lamina (arXiv 2405.01814) and Step-3 AFD.

IDEA 3
NAME: Layer-pipelined LPDDR prefill dies
MECHANISM: The anchor is kept: a dense FP4/FP8 compute die with no HBM runs the compute-bound prefill phase, sends the KV to the decode tier over a plain NIC, and uses the same ISA and compiler as that tier. The kill test was right that one die holding a whole MoE model is memory-bound: on DeepSeek-V3 at 8K tokens per pass, each expert sees only 256 tokens, which gives 910 FLOP/B. The repair splits the model's layers across 8 dies. Each die (20 PF dense FP4, 800 mm², ~1.18 kW) keeps 47 GB of the 376 GB of NVFP4 weights in 64 GB of 1024-bit LPDDR5X (1.37 TB/s) and pushes a large batch of tokens through one layer before moving to the next (layer-major mega-batch). That makes a die compute-bound from about 45K tokens per pass. Only activations cross between dies (15–45 GB/s, against the 0.65 TB/s per die that expert-parallel all-to-all would need), so no NVLink-class scale-up fabric is required. KV egress is 2.4–5 GB/s per die, TTFT is 0.69 s at 8K prompts, and the design uses 0 GB of HBM and no CoWoS.
VALUE: All-in cost per prefill token comes out 1.27× cheaper than Rubin, 1.20× cheaper than the revived HBM4 Rubin CPX and 2.7× cheaper than B200, rising to about 1.45× vs Rubin if HBM4 reaches $32/GB. It also removes the ~1.26 GB of HBM Rubin needs per 1K prefill tok/s.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: "Compute-bound from 45K tokens per pass" leaves out activation traffic. A 45K-token mega-batch per layer is 323 MB in FP8 and 645 MB in BF16, which is far too large for on-chip memory, and the design budgets no SRAM. So every routed-expert token must read its input from LPDDR and write its output back. That traffic alone runs at 4,100–6,100 FLOP/B, while the die balance is 14,600 FLOP/B, so routed experts can never use more than 28–42% of the 20 PF at any batch size. (check.py: 2·3·7168·2048 FLOP ÷ (7168 B FP8 in + 7–14 KB out) = 4,096–6,144 FLOP/B; 20 PF ÷ 1.37 TB/s = 14,599 FLOP/B. Avoiding this would need about 0.3–0.65 GB of SRAM per die, roughly 115–230 mm² at N3, which is not in the 800 mm² die.)
- MAJOR: The cost case treats "0 GB HBM" as a memory saving, but 2026 LPDDR now costs as much per GB as HBM4 or more. (LPDDR5X contract price is about $10/GB, with Q2-26 forecasts of $19–21/GB ([Wccftech/SemiAnalysis](https://wccftech.com/mobile-dram-prices-expected-to-increase-by-100-quarter-over-quarter-as-long-term-agreements-now-getting-signed-at-prices-as-high-as-21-gb/amp/)). HBM4 12-Hi is about $550–600 per 36 GB, or $15–17/GB ([Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/data/hbm-pricing)). Memory cost per PF is $32–64 for the idea against $88 for Rubin at 50 PF, a saving of at most about $1–3k per Rubin-equivalent. That is not enough to deliver 1.27× once the 28–42% routed-expert cap applies.)
- MAJOR: The "revived HBM4 Rubin CPX" baseline (1.20×) is an unconfirmed analyst rumour. Nvidia pulled CPX at GTC 2026. ([TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/352213/nvidias-rubin-cpx-gpu-reborn-with-hbm4-memory-no-more-gddr7) and [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/nvidia-rubin-cpx-long-context-inference/) give the Kuo report of 168 GB HBM4 at 2.3 kW with a Q1-27 target, unconfirmed by Nvidia. The 2.7× against B200 compares with an older generation and is irrelevant.)
- SURVIVES: 377 GB NVFP4 and 47 GB per die; 1.37 TB/s for 1024b LPDDR5X-10.7; 256 tokens/expert giving 910 FLOP/B; activation crossing about 10 GB/s and KV egress about 3 GB/s per die at MFU 0.4 (inside the stated ranges); 1.48 W/mm², the same as Rubin.
- RATIO: 0.9 | 1.27× claimed, divided by about 1.3–1.6× throughput loss from the activation-bandwidth cap (routed experts are 44% of FLOPs), with LPDDR priced at $10–20/GB.
- KNOWN: partly | Prefill/decode disaggregation (DistServe, Splitwise) and Nvidia Rubin CPX, a GDDR7 prefill-only die announced in Sep 2025 ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/nvidia-rubin-cpx-long-context-inference/)).

IDEA 4
NAME: HBM-halved expert-stationary stack
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged: MoE expert weights stay fixed in hybrid-bonded SRAM layers, and HBM serves only the KV cache. The repair changes the job of the stack. It no longer sits on top of a full-HBM GPU, where it bought only 1.00–1.04×: with wide expert parallelism a Rubin package streams just 5.2 GB of weights per step, about 0.24 ms, which is around 1% of energy per token. Instead the stack replaces half the HBM. The repaired package: - 2 compute dies, each with 2–4 SRAM layers bonded underneath. The compute die sits on top, as in Zen 5 X3D, so heat goes straight to the lid. At the N5 bitcell that is 2.29 MB/mm², 6.9 GB per die with 4 layers, and 987 GB per 72-package rack. - 4 HBM4 stacks per package instead of 8: 11 TB/s and 144 GB, used only for KV. DeepSeek-V3 needs 2 SRAM layers per die and Kimi-K2 (1T) needs 3. In every tier tested for DeepSeek-V3, and for Kimi-K2 at a realistic 0.35 MFU, throughput equals the 8-stack Rubin. The stack adds about $4.3k per package; the 4 HBM4 stacks it saves are worth about $10–14k.
VALUE: Throughput per rack is the same, but the rack costs about 0.73–0.83× as much per token and uses 288 fewer HBM4 stacks (half), and HBM supply is the binding limit for 2026–28. It loses (0.61×) only for 1T-class models at high MFU and ≤300 tok/s/user, where HBM bandwidth for KV becomes the limit again.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The savings use an HBM price 4–6× above 2026 contract. "$10–14k for 4 stacks" means $2,500–3,500 per stack, or $69–97/GB. At the reported $550–600 per stack the package saves $2.2–2.4k but adds $4.3k, so cost per token rises. (check.py: net −$1.9k to −$2.1k per package at 2026 prices; +$0.9k at the 2027 forecast of $1,300/stack. Idea 1 in the same run treats $32/GB as the upside case, so the two ideas contradict each other. ([Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/data/hbm-pricing)))
- MAJOR: "Same throughput as 8-stack Rubin" holds only when attention MFU is ≤0.35. FlashMLA already reaches 660 TFLOPS, about 67% of peak, on H800. At that MFU, DeepSeek-V3 at 32K is KV-bandwidth-bound on 11 TB/s and throughput halves. (check.py: 18,589 tok/s with 8 stacks vs 9,554 with 4 stacks = 0.51×. ([FlashMLA](https://gittrend.io/repo/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA)))
- MAJOR: The result depends on MLA. Absorbed MLA runs at 484 FLOP/B (DeepSeek-V3) and 242 FLOP/B (Kimi-K2), but GQA-8 models run at about 16 FLOP/B, and the Rubin balance is ≥795 FLOP/B. So every GQA model is strictly bandwidth-bound, and halving HBM halves its decode throughput. (check.py.)
- MAJOR: KV capacity halves from about 283 GB to 144 GB, or 125 users at 32K FP8 instead of about 246. Every tier slower than about 76 tok/s/user is then capacity-bound at roughly 0.5×. (144 GB ÷ 1.15 GB per user.)
- MAJOR: The Zen 5 X3D precedent is 1 cache layer under a 71 mm², ~120 W CCD. This design needs 2–4 reticle-size (753 mm²) layers under a ~1 kW die, with about 1.4 kA passing through the TSVs of every layer. That has not been demonstrated, and SoIC hybrid bonding is its own capacity-limited TSMC line. The 4 remaining HBM stacks still need CoWoS. (6.9 GB ÷ 4 ÷ 2.29 MB/mm² = 753 mm² per layer.)
- SURVIVES: 753 mm² per layer, 994 GB per rack (claimed 987), 2 layers = 497 GB ≥ 377 GB for DeepSeek-V3, 3 layers = 745 GB ≥ 585 GB for Kimi-K2; weights streamed under wide EP are about 1% of the per-step budget.
- RATIO: 0.6 | Package cost goes up by $0.9–2.1k, throughput is 0.51–1.0× depending on attention MFU and model (MLA vs GQA), and is weighted toward optimized kernels.
- KNOWN: partly | AMD 3D V-Cache (SRAM under the CCD), Groq/Cerebras weight-stationary SRAM, d-Matrix 3D-stacked DRAM work.
