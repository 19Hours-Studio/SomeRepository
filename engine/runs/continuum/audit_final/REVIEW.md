IDEA 1: Layer-pipelined LPDDR prefill dies
DEFECT | FATAL | "Compute-bound from 45K tokens per pass" leaves out activation traffic. A 45K-token mega-batch per layer is 323 MB in FP8 and 645 MB in BF16, which is far too large for on-chip memory, and the design budgets no SRAM. So every routed-expert token must read its input from LPDDR and write its output back. That traffic alone runs at 4,100–6,100 FLOP/B, while the die balance is 14,600 FLOP/B, so routed experts can never use more than 28–42% of the 20 PF at any batch size. | check.py: 2·3·7168·2048 FLOP ÷ (7168 B FP8 in + 7–14 KB out) = 4,096–6,144 FLOP/B; 20 PF ÷ 1.37 TB/s = 14,599 FLOP/B. Avoiding this would need about 0.3–0.65 GB of SRAM per die, roughly 115–230 mm² at N3, which is not in the 800 mm² die.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The cost case treats "0 GB HBM" as a memory saving, but 2026 LPDDR now costs as much per GB as HBM4 or more. | LPDDR5X contract price is about $10/GB, with Q2-26 forecasts of $19–21/GB ([Wccftech/SemiAnalysis](https://wccftech.com/mobile-dram-prices-expected-to-increase-by-100-quarter-over-quarter-as-long-term-agreements-now-getting-signed-at-prices-as-high-as-21-gb/amp/)). Memory cost per PF is $32–64 for the idea against $88 for Rubin at 50 PF, a saving of at most about $1–3k per Rubin-equivalent. That is not enough to deliver 1.27× once the 28–42% routed-expert cap applies.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "revived HBM4 Rubin CPX" baseline (1.20×) is an unconfirmed analyst rumour. Nvidia pulled CPX at GTC 2026. | [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/352213/nvidias-rubin-cpx-gpu-reborn-with-hbm4-memory-no-more-gddr7) and [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/nvidia-rubin-cpx-long-context-inference/) give the Kuo report of 168 GB HBM4 at 2.3 kW with a Q1-27 target, unconfirmed by Nvidia. IDEA 2: Detached attention-stack memory nodes
DEFECT | MAJOR | The idea sells relief from the HBM shortage but uses 1.57× more HBM per delivered token/s than Rubin-only. It needs 48 GB of HBM for every 2 users at 49 tok/s, while Rubin serves about 11 users at about 84 tok/s from 288 GB. SPHBM4 avoids CoWoS but not the DRAM shortage. | check.py: 0.490 GB vs 0.312 GB per tok/s; 750 stacks = 36 TB of HBM per Rubin. [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/jedec-releases-new-sphbm4-standard-to-slash-ai-memory-costs-narrow-512-bit-interface-enables-dropping-expensive-interposers-for-organic-substrates) notes SPHBM4 does not relieve the DRAM shortage.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is dense-GQA full attention at 128K. A competent operator at 128K now runs sparse or latent attention (DSA top-2048, MLA, hybrid linear), which reads about 64× fewer KV bytes per token, so the stacks' bandwidth advantage mostly disappears. | 2048/131072 = 1.6% of the dense KV read.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The fan-in at the GPU is ignored. 400–760 stacks at 0.26 GB/s each add up to 104–198 GB/s per Rubin, which is essentially the whole 1.6 Tb/s (200 GB/s) ConnectX-9. On top of that, each token needs 80 serial GPU↔stack round trips with a 255 µs budget each, and every round trip waits for the slowest of 760 nodes. | check.py: 760 × 98 tok/s × 2.66 MB = 198 GB/s.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The cost advantage is very sensitive to HBM price, because 36 TB of HBM sits behind each Rubin. HBM4 is forecast at about $1,300 per stack, or $36–53/GB, in 2027. | At $36/GB the idea costs about $0.257 vs $0.355 for Rubin (1.38×). At $53/GB it is $0.323 vs $0.397 (1.23×). ([Silicon Analysts/TrendForce](https://siliconanalysts.com/market/trendforce-lifts-2027-hbm-asp-forecast-to-121-hbm4-stack-seen-doubling-to-1-300-2026-10-01))
DEFECT | MINOR | Power and thermal are understated. The quoted 13–19 W covers only the base die. Reading DRAM at 2.5 TB/s and 3.58 pJ/bit costs another 72 W per stack, and the heat from the hot logic die has to pass through 16 DRAM layers, which pushes them toward the 85 °C point where refresh doubles. | check.py: 2.5e12 × 8 × 3.58e-12 = 72 W.
DEFECT | MINOR | The counts do not close. At 2 users per stack, 400 stacks serve only 800 users at 128K, not 1,200. | 1,500 users need 750 stacks.
SURVIVES | 21.5 GB per user at 128K (Llama-70B-class FP8 KV), 2 users in 48 GB, 84% bandwidth efficiency for 49 tok/s, the 8,066:1 byte ratio, 0.26 GB/s link per stack, about 34 TFLOP/s FP8 per base die; SPHBM4 (JESD330-4, 13 Jul 2026, 512 data signals) is real.
RATIO | 1.2 | The claimed 1.7× is re-priced at 2027 HBM4 ($36–53/GB) with 1.57× HBM per token. Against a sparse-attention baseline it would be about 1.0.
KNOWN | partly | AttAcc (attention in HBM-PIM, ASPLOS 2024), SK hynix AiM and Samsung HBM-PIM; attention offload in Lamina (arXiv 2405.01814) and Step-3 AFD.
NEW FACT | SPHBM4 = JESD330-4, 22.4–46 GT/s, same DRAM dies as HBM4 | [01net/JEDEC](https://www.01net.it/new-jedec-sphbm4-standard-enables-hbm4-class-bandwidth-on-organic-substrates/)
