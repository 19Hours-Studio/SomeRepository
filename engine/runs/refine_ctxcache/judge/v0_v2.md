**Shared ground.** Both answers propose the same four ideas:
- client-side append-only errata with ski-rental compaction;
- KV deletion or splicing on your own serving stack;
- a segment cache with partial recompute;
- storage-aware retention.

Both are honest that only the client-side route stays exact.

**Where B goes further, in ways that change decisions:**
- **Bill decomposition.** It shows that at about 100% hits the bill is roughly 61% reads, 25% writes and 14% output. So "much cheaper" requires reading fewer tokens, not more hits. That reframes the goal usefully.
- **Ordering rule.** It gives a concrete layout rule (sort blocks by odds of change per token), checked by brute force against the naive rule. The step-by-step layout table goes from 47% to 98% hit rate, which tells a client-only user exactly what to do.
- **Operational levers A omits.** These are choosing the TTL by reuse interval and sharding OpenAI cache keys to avoid routing misses.
- **Ski-rental threshold.** B's formula uses 1.15·S, which correctly nets out the read cost already being paid. A uses 1.25.
- **Replay on real trajectories.** B's Idea 2 replays real SWE-agent trajectories. It shows that client-side elision every step can cost 1.79× the append-only bill, while a server-side splice brings it to 0.62×. A relies only on a synthetic replay.
- **RAG with a new document set per query.** B covers this case and quantifies it: 34–60% of document tokens via prefix reuse, about 96% via a segment cache.
- **Quality gate.** B adds a gate calibrated by shadow-mode sampling against a stated agreement target, plus a separate price for approximate hits.
- **Limits section.** B argues that exact reuse after an in-place middle change can save at most about 1/L of the suffix work, since only layer-0 K/V are independent of earlier tokens. That makes the "cannot reach 100%" claim rigorous rather than asserted.

**Where A holds up.** A is correct and concise, and its point that compaction is driven by the context-window limit rather than by cost is useful. Its coverage is narrower and less quantified, though, and its limits section is thin.

**B's flaw.** B opens with stray references to an "earlier answer's" files, which is noise but does not affect the substance.
