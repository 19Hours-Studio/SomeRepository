I rebuilt the analysis in round5 and replayed it on **42 real agent sessions**. The main correction is that the saving from clearing old tool results is **0.79×** on real traces, not the 0.43× the old synthetic model gave. # How to make cached context much cheaper, with hits near 100%

**Files** (in this directory):
- `calc.py` is stdlib-only and takes about 2.5 minutes with `python3 -I calc.py`. Its output is in `calc_out.txt`.
- `traces/c4/steps.json` comes from real data: per-step request message sizes and hashes from 42 SWE-agent runs with Claude Sonnet 4 on SWE-bench Verified (every 12th instance). They were taken from the public `swe-bench-submissions` S3 bucket. `traces/c4/pick.txt` lists the keys and `extract.py` reproduces the file.
- `cachediff.py` finds where one request breaks the cache relative to the one before it. `examples/` holds test pairs.

**Limits on the evidence:**
- No model weights could be downloaded, so every quality claim is either published or an untested hypothesis.
- Token counts are chars/4. Claude's tokenizer will differ, and code often comes out at about 3.5 chars per token.
- The tool schemas (assumed 1,500 tokens) are not in the stored queries.

## What changed from the previous version, and why

| Previous claim | Honest status now |
|---|---|
| Clearing old tool results cuts the bill to 0.43× | **0.79× on real traces** (§N). The synthetic model assumed 2,000-token observations; the real median is 159 tokens (p90 929) |
| "Never clear" is the baseline | The fair baseline is **what the agent actually sent**: append-only with `cache_control` on the last 2 messages, giving a 97.0% token hit rate. Anthropic's default context editing (trigger at 100k) **never fires** on these sessions (×1.000) |
| Layout and ordering raise the hit rate from 47% to 98% | That baseline was a strawman. Real agent traffic is already at **97%**. Hit-rate methods have at most about 3% of tokens left to win, and those tokens are mostly new |
| The odds/size ordering rule is new | It is the textbook adjacent-exchange ratio rule (as in sequential testing). The square-root clearing period is the EOQ formula. Folding errata back in is ski rental. All three are applied here, not invented |
| — | **New:** the published rolling observation masking costs **2.01×** under prompt caching. The same masking applied in batches costs **0.90×** (§N2) |
| — | **New:** a roofline model of the runner's GPU time on the same traces (§O). Even at 97% hits, **55% of GPU time is work on cached context**, mostly decode reads of the KV cache |

**Bottom line.** On real agent traffic the hit rate is already near its ceiling. A large further saving comes only from **reading fewer cached tokens** and **making each cached token cheaper to read**. A higher hit rate cannot deliver it.

## Short answer, by who you are

| You are | Best measured gain over current best practice | Exact? |
|---|---|---|
| **API user (agent)** | Batched clearing: keep 3, every 9 steps → **0.79×** total bill (long sessions: 0.74×). Keep 10, every 18 steps → **0.90×**, which keeps more context at every step than the published rolling window of 10. Plus the TTL, warm-up, routing and batch rules (§0) | Exact KV. Content is dropped on purpose |
| **Self-hosting or provider (the asker, per the notes)** | GPU time: periodic clearing **0.84×** (exact); server splice **0.70×**; splice plus 4-bit KV **0.60×**. Baseline is prefix caching with FP8 KV | Splice and 4-bit are approximate |
| **RAG or shared-memory serving, new document combination per query** | The only regime where non-prefix reuse matters. Exact prefix reuse covers 34–60% of document tokens; a segment cache covers ~96%, approximately (Idea 3) | Approximate, gated |

**Key fact 1.** No exact method can reuse KV after a token that changed in place. From layer 1 on, every later token's K/V depend on the changed token. An exact method can therefore save at most about 1/L of the suffix work: 1/80 for a 70B model. "Near 100% and exact" after a middle change requires the client to stop changing content in place, by appending errata instead.

**Key fact 2.** At ~100% hits the bill is reads, plus writes of genuinely new tokens, plus output. On the real traces that is **56% / 21% / 23%**. Only fewer read tokens or a lower price per token moves it.

## §N. Real traces: what the cache actually sees (`calc.py` §N, N2)

**The sessions.** 42 sessions, median 55.5 steps (max 121), median final context 41.6k tokens (max 111k). 2,441 of 2,458 step transitions are strictly append-only. The other 17 are re-queries that replace the last turn, which is cheap.

**Policies on Anthropic-style multipliers** (write 1.25×, read 0.1×, output 5×). Bills are total bills relative to "as sent".

| Policy | Bill | Hit rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| As sent (append-only) | 1.000× | 97.0% | Current best practice |
| Anthropic context editing, default (100k trigger, keep 3) | 1.000× | — | Never triggers here |
| Same with a 30k trigger | 0.999× | — | Re-clearing every step undoes the saving |
| Clear old tool results every step, keep 3 | 1.033× | — | Each step rewrites the last 3 turns |
| **Clear every 9 steps, keep 3** | **0.791×** | 91.6% | Best; the square-root rule predicts T=9 |
| Clear every 9 steps, keep 3, sessions ≥80 steps (7) | **0.739×** | — | Gain grows with session length |
| Keep 1, every step | 0.659× | — | Rewrite is tiny, but keeps very little context |
| Keep 10, every step (rolling, Complexity-Trap style) | **2.010×** | — | Rewrites 10 turns per step |
| **Keep 10, every 18 steps** | **0.900×** | — | Always shows ≥10 recent observations |
| Clear only results >500 tokens, keep 3 (best T=3) | 0.816× | — | Size filtering doesn't beat age-based clearing |
| Server splice, keep 3 (runner only, Idea 2) | 0.680× | 93.1% | No rewrite cost; approximate |

**Rule for the period.** T* ≈ √(2·w·K / (r·g)) (EOQ), where:
- K is the number of tokens rewritten per clear (the kept window);
- g is the number of clearable tokens added per step;
- w = 1.25 and r = 0.1 are the write and read multipliers.

With the real g = 531 and K ≈ 1,833, it predicts **9**, and the replay's best is **9**.

**How to run it on Anthropic.**
- Clear on the client and keep the placeholders, so the history you store stays append-only between clears.
- Put one explicit breakpoint at the start of the not-yet-cleared region. That boundary stays fixed between clears, so its entry is read (and its TTL refreshed) every step and is still there at the next clear.
- Without this breakpoint, the next clear point is ~24 blocks back, beyond the ~20-block lookback, and you lose everything back to the system prompt.
- I could not confirm from the docs whether server-side `clear_at_least` produces this batching when the full history is re-sent each turn. Client-side clearing is fully under your control. Verify either way with `cache_read_input_tokens`.

**Quality.**
- *The Complexity Trap* (arXiv 2508.21433, SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified) reports that observation masking matches or slightly beats the raw agent, for example Qwen3-Coder 480B going from 53.8% to 54.8%. It reports about half the cost.
  - A third-party summary gives its window as M=10; I could not confirm that in the paper text.
  - I also found no statement on whether its costs include caching discounts.
  - On these traces the rolling form would cost 2.01×, so **its cost result does not transfer to cached APIs as is.**
- Batched clearing with keep = M shows, at every step, a **superset** of what rolling masking with M shows: between M and M+T recent observations. So the published quality result plausibly carries over to the 0.90× setting. More context can occasionally hurt, so A/B it.
- Keep 3 (0.79×) and keep 1 (0.66×) are below any published window and are untested.
- **Test:** SWE-bench Verified resolve rate for keep ∈ {1, 3, 10} × batched versus rolling, plus cost from the usage fields.

## §O. The runner's cost: where GPU time goes at 97% hits (`calc.py` §O)

**Assumptions** (roofline; replace them with your own measurements):
- a Llama-3-70B-class dense model on 8×H100;
- FP8 weights and FP8 KV (160 KiB per token) as the best-practice baseline;
- prefill at 45% MFU;
- decode at 70% of HBM bandwidth with 32 concurrent sequences;
- KV resident between steps.

**Baseline:**
- No prefix cache would cost **11.4×** the prefix-cached baseline.
- In the baseline, prefill is 36% and decode 64% of GPU time.
- **55% of GPU time is work on cached tokens**: new tokens attending to cached keys, plus decode reads of the cached KV.
- That is about 8 node-seconds, or $0.035, per session at $2 per GPU-hour.

| Change on the runner (same traces) | GPU time | Exact? |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic clearing, keep 3, T=9 | **0.841×** (prefill 1.16×, decode 0.67×) | Yes |
| Keep 1, every step | 0.678× | Yes, but less context |
| Server splice, keep 3 | 0.697× | Approximate |
| 4-bit KV instead of FP8, no clearing | 0.781× | Approximate |
| Periodic clearing + 4-bit KV | 0.729× | Approximate (KV) |
| **Splice + 4-bit KV** | **0.598×** | Approximate |

**What this means for the runner:**
- After prefix caching, the dominant cost of reused context is **decode-time KV traffic, which grows with context × output tokens**. Hit rate is not the lever.
- Per your earlier run, KV compression bottoms out at ~2.6–3 bits without retraining, so 4-bit is near the useful limit.
- When many concurrent decodes share a long prefix (parallel subagents, best-of-N), shared-prefix attention reads that prefix once per batch. This is FlashInfer's cascade kernel; vLLM V1 has a heuristic cascade path. These traces have almost no shared prefix across sessions, so I don't claim a gain here. Measure it on your own fan-out traffic.

**Measure the hit rate from usage fields:**
- Anthropic: `cache_read_input_tokens / (cache_read + cache_creation + input_tokens)`.
- OpenAI: `cached_tokens / prompt_tokens`. From GPT-5.6 on, OpenAI also reports `cache_write_tokens` and bills writes at 1.25×.

| Lever | Effect (`calc.py`) | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **1-hour TTL (§G)** | A context reused N times at 6–60-minute gaps costs 1.25·(N+1) per token with a 5-minute TTL, against 2 + 0.1·N with a 1-hour TTL. At N=5 that is 7.5× against 2.5× | Gaps longer than 5 minutes |
| **Warm-up before fan-out (§K)** | Per Anthropic's docs, an entry is usable only after the first response begins, so concurrent requests all write. One request first (`max_tokens=1`) makes the shared prefix 1.9× cheaper at N=2, 5.8× at N=10, 10.2× at N=50 | Costs one extra time-to-first-token |

## Idea 1: Cache-stable prompt layout (client only, exact)

**Where it runs and what it costs:** any API. No extra memory or storage, and latency is the same or lower.

**Cases covered:**
- **Middle edit, insertion or changed tool output:** append a short erratum instead, such as `[supersedes tool_result #17]: …`. A 500-token erratum in a 74k prompt keeps ~99.3% hits on that turn.
- **Reordering of documents already seen:** serialize in canonical order (sorted by hash or popularity). The reorder then costs nothing.
- **Re-serialization:** normalize timestamps, IDs and JSON key order.

**Rules:**
- *Order blocks* by pᵢ / ((1−pᵢ)·sᵢ), ascending, where pᵢ is the chance block i changes and sᵢ its size. This is the textbook exchange rule. A brute-force check over 3,000 random layouts found it optimal every time; the plain p/s rule was suboptimal 59% of the time, by up to 17%.
- *Volatile tail:* put the to-do list, memory and current state after the conversation, below the last breakpoint. It is then billed at 1.0× rather than written at 1.25× (§J).
- *Fold errata back in* after T* = (1.15·S − 1.25·e)/(0.1·e) turns, where e is the erratum size and S the number of tokens after the edit. At most 2× the best schedule chosen in hindsight.
- *Clearing:* see §N.

**Hit rate:**
- Real agent traffic is already at 97%, so here the value of Idea 1 is mainly **avoiding breaks**, not raising the average.
- In the synthetic 6-block agent (§C), a volatile tail plus errata plus diff-only re-sending gives 98.0%, against 93.7% for the practitioner layout with the volatile tail. On input cost that is (0.1h + 1.25(1−h)): 0.173 → 0.123, so **1.4× cheaper**.
- The 47% "authoring order" figure is not a fair baseline.

**Traps measured:**
- **20-block lookback.** An edit 50 blocks back with a single end breakpoint loses everything, including a 13k system prompt (§J). Use up to 4 explicit breakpoints.
- **Templates that strip reasoning** (Qwen3, gpt-oss style). They cost 1.27× per input token at 5 tool steps per query and 1.10× at 20 (§H). xAI's docs name dropped reasoning as the top cause of misses.
  - The runner fixes this exactly with a template that keeps reasoning, or by sending token IDs. vLLM has fixed gpt-oss double-dropping (per its forum); I did not verify the PR.
  - Quality of kept reasoning must be A/B tested.
- **`cachediff.py prev.json next.json`.** Reports the first differing block, a likely cause (timestamps, IDs, key order, reordered lines, reasoning removed, real edit), how many unchanged tokens get recomputed, and a lookback warning. On `examples/`, a one-character tool-output change at message 4 of 61 leaves 11% cacheable and costs 11.2× a full hit.

**RAG with a different document set per query (§E, synthetic Zipf).** 2,000 documents of 500 tokens, 5 per query, popularity s = 0.8–1.2.
- Popularity order: 63–77% total hits.
- Relevance order: 55–63%.
- A greedy radix walk adds ≤0.4 points under i.i.d. popularity.

**Quality.** The KV is exact for the prompt you send. The risks are in the prompt itself: stale text next to its correction, popularity order instead of relevance order, and kept reasoning being out of distribution. Tests on a real model: a needle test where a superseded fact sits next to its erratum, SWE-bench with errata versus in-place edits, and RAG QA under relevance versus popularity order.

**Prior art.** Manus and Anthropic advise append-only context with stable content first. *Don't Break the Cache* reports 41–80% savings. RAGCache, RAGBoost and CacheWeaver reorder documents for reuse. Anthropic documents `clear_at_least`.

**What this adds:**
- the real-trace replay;
- the fair baseline (default context editing has no effect here);
- the finding that rolling masking costs 2.0× under caching and that batching fixes it;
- the EOQ period validated on real data (9 = 9);
- the breakpoint placement that makes batched clearing work on Anthropic.

## Idea 2: Delete and splice KV in place (own stack or provider, approximate)

**Mechanism.** An `edit_span` operation:
1. Drop the span's paged KV blocks.
2. Re-rotate the RoPE keys after the span, or leave a gap in position IDs.
3. Prefill only the replacement.
4. On sampled requests, compare against exact recomputation of the suffix (the gate from Idea 3).

**Cost.** No extra memory, since freed blocks return to the pool. Re-rotation is O(suffix) element-wise work. Compared with periodic clearing, it removes the rewrite cost: on the real traces, **0.68× of the bill and 0.70× of GPU time, against 0.79× and 0.84×** for the exact periodic form.

**Cases.** Clearing old tool results, replacing a tool output, and stripping reasoning without re-rendering.

**Quality.** Not exact. Later tokens keep what they absorbed from the deleted span. Leyline reports splices that track the full-context baseline on MLA models; KV-Editable and PatchKV study the same idea. These come from abstracts and were not reproduced here.

**Tests against exact recomputation on these 42 traces:**
- KL divergence and top-1 agreement of next-token distributions;
- resolve rate for splice versus exact periodic clearing;
- a "deleted wrong fact" probe.

**Decision rule.** Splice gains only about 0.11× on the bill over the exact periodic form. Ship it only if the gate shows top-1 agreement of ≥99%.

## Idea 3: Content-addressed segment cache with a shadow-calibrated gate (own stack or provider, approximate)

**Mechanism.**
1. Cache each document, tool output or memory unit by content hash, prefilled after the shared system prompt (APE style, no training needed).
2. Re-rotate a segment's keys when it appears at a new position.
3. Fix up cross-segment attention with one of:
   - recompute the ~15% most-deviating tokens (CacheBlend);
   - recompute the first tokens of each chunk (EPIC);
   - apply an offset correction (AgentKVShift, 10–30% of the cache refreshed; KVCOMM uses a similar offset idea).
4. **Gate:** run exact prefill in shadow on 1% of traffic. Fit a threshold on a cheap deviation signal so that top-1 agreement over the first 64 generated tokens is ≥99%. Requests above the threshold fall back to the exact path.
5. Charge two prices: an exact hit and a cheaper approximate hit.

**Cases.** Reordering, insertions, changed segments, and RAG with a new document set per query.

**Where it pays.** RAG and shared agent memory only. In the agent traces almost nothing breaks in the middle (17 of 2,458 transitions, all at the tail).

**Coverage and saving.** In the Zipf replay, segment coverage reaches ~96% of document tokens, against 34–60% for exact prefix reuse.

Compute saving per document token, counting the 1% shadow traffic (load 0.1, refresh 0.15):

| Gate fallback rate | 0% | 10% | 30% | 50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saving, s = 0.8 | 2.40× | 2.10× | 1.68× | 1.40× |
| Saving, s = 1.2 | 1.60× | 1.51× | 1.35× | 1.22× |

Published prefill speedups (authors' own numbers): CacheBlend 2.2–3.3× time to first token, EPIC up to 8×, AgentKVShift 2–3.5×.

**Storage:** one KV copy per unique segment, 160 KiB per token in FP8 for a 70B model.

**Quality.** Close to full recomputation on single-hop RAG. All methods leave a gap on multi-hop and cross-chunk reasoning (Fusion-RAG; the AgentKVShift paper says so too).

Tests:
- 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue and LongBench against exact prefill;
- KVShareArena;
- the gate's fallback rate in production, which is the real cost driver.

**What this adds.** The gate calibrated by shadow sampling, a separate approximate-hit price, and the saving-versus-fallback curve.

## Idea 4: Price retention against recompute cost (own stack or provider, exact)

Keep KV in a tier only while expected reuses × recompute cost exceed storage cost × time (§F, assumed prices).

**Recompute cost:** $0.19–0.38 per million tokens for 8k–128k contexts.

**Retention break-even (BF16; FP8 doubles these windows):**

| Tier | Keep only if reused within |
|---|---|
| GPU memory (HBM) | 1.4 min |
| CPU memory (DRAM) | 6.9 min |
| NVMe SSD | 2.6 h |

**Bandwidth check.** One NVMe drive loads ~21k tokens/s in BF16, which is no faster than recomputing. Four drives or RDMA do help. This is consistent with the ~1.2× gain from tiering in your earlier run.

**Effect.** Hits become affordable for long-tail sessions, but no new hits appear after middle changes. Quality is unaffected.

## What cannot reach ~100%, or a large gain, under these constraints

- **Exact hits after an in-place change:** at most 1/L of the suffix work can be saved, as above.
- **Hit rate as the lever on agent traffic:** it is already 97%. The remaining "misses" are genuinely new tokens.
- **New document combinations per query:** exact reuse is capped at 34–60% of document tokens. The ~96% segment coverage is approximate and weakest on multi-hop reasoning.
- **Big exact savings on short sessions:** with real observation sizes, exact clearing gives 0.79–0.90×, not 0.4×. Larger cuts need approximation (splice, 4-bit KV: 0.60× of GPU time) or dropping more context (keep 1: 0.66×), each with an untested quality cost.
- **Reaching the 0.2–0.4 range the asker may hope for:** per the replay, that only appears in much longer sessions with large observations (the synthetic 2k-token case).
- **Concurrent first requests, prompts below the minimum, history-rewriting templates on APIs you don't control.**
