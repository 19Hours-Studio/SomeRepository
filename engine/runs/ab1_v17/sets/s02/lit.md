L03 | PARTIAL | closest work: Baarda data snooping and reliability/redundancy theory in geodetic least-squares adjustment (1968/1977; review "A half century of Baarda's concept of reliability" https://agrimensura.florianopolis.ifsc.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/A-half-century-of-Baarda-s-concept-of-reliability-a-review-new-perspectives-and-applications.pdf) ; ConCoRD: Enhancing Self-Consistency and Performance of Pre-Trained Language Models through Natural Language Inference (2022) https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11875 (also Belief Tree Propagation (2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06950v2) | what is genuinely new, in one sentence: Applying closed-loop weighted least-squares misclosure spreading, normalized-residual blunder naming, and per-claim redundancy numbers (near-zero means structurally unverifiable) to an LLM's own quantitative claims, whereas existing LLM work uses MaxSAT or tree/factor-graph inference without redundancy or reliability measures.
L04 | PARTIAL | closest work: Desroziers et al., Diagnosis of observation, background and analysis error statistics in observation space (2005) https://bibliotheque.meteo.fr/pub/DOC00017306-diagnosis-observation-background-and-analysis-erro.html ; Knowledge-Based Trust: Estimating the Trustworthiness of Web Sources (2015) https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.03519 (Kalman-style LLM work: Filtering Beats Fine-Tuning: A Bayesian Kalman View of In-Context Learning (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06100v1) | what is genuinely new, in one sentence: Using Desroziers identities as label-free estimators of per-source and per-model-region error covariance to set the gain for both belief and parameter updates in an LLM-like system; data assimilation and unsupervised source-reliability estimation each exist, and the Kalman-LLM papers I found use labels or a different formulation.
L05 | PARTIAL | closest work: Future-as-Label: Scalable Supervision from Real-World Outcomes (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06336v1 ; Outcome-based Reinforcement Learning to Predict the Future (2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17989 (also Scaling Open-Ended Reasoning to Predict the Future (2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.25070) | what is genuinely new, in one sentence: The core idea of training only on matured predictions scored against later real outcomes is already published, so the new part is the hash-committed frozen deterministic resolver, the escrow ledger with per-domain and per-horizon calibration, and resolver retirement for drift or ambiguity.
L06 | PARTIAL | closest work: Can LLMs Learn by Teaching for Better Reasoning? A Preliminary Study (2024) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14629 ; Can Language Models Teach Weaker Agents? Teacher Explanations Improve Students via Personalization (2023) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09299 (also Sakana RLT https://sakana.ai/rlt/ and Schmidhuber's Driven by Compression Progress (2009) https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.4360) | what is genuinely new, in one sentence: Scoring a claim by the gain it gives a freshly initialized student on held-out raw unlabeled observations is new, since the prior work rewards teacher output by student accuracy on labeled exam or answer tasks, but the teacher-rewarded-by-student-gain loop itself is established.
NOTE: Four of the six ideas (L01-L04) are cross-domain transplants of mature estimation methods (standard addition, turbo/EXIT decoding, geodetic adjustment, data assimilation), and my searches found no LLM-specific prior work for them; the novelty is the transfer and the specific wiring, not the underlying math. The other two (L05, L06) have direct recent LLM precedents (outcome-based forecasting RL, learning-by-teaching), so their cores are largely known and only the commit/resolver machinery or the raw-data student target is extra. All verdicts rest on a handful of standard web searches per idea, so the absence of LLM-specific hits for L01-L04 does not prove absence, and the key open question for each is whether the classical assumptions (linearity, Gaussian errors, measurable extrinsic information) hold for LLM outputs.
