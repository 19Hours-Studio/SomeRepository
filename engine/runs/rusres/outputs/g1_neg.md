**Conventional first eight:** stronger piles or norms, thermosyphons, floating nuclear power, wind-diesel hybrids, more Arc7 ships and icebreakers, domestic equipment substitution, autonomous haulage, tax relief.
**Shared paradigm:** these fight cold, thaw and distance by building more or bigger infrastructure while keeping the ground frozen.

Targets are chosen for their ceilings: LNG capital (A), Norilsk capital (B), and foundations across all archetypes. I claim no 3× gain for C or D.

**1. Mid-scale LNG trains in number**
- **Cost:** 15% lower (est.), $7.5 to about $6.4/mmBtu. Basis: capex haircut to $700/t·yr against the claimed $450–500 (F174).
- **Time:** about 3 years to first train, against 4 years at Yamal T1 (F44).
- **Benchmark:** Yamal T1–3.
- **Scope:** Yamal and Gydan only, where Arc7 tonnage exists. Not Arctic LNG 2 until it has ships.
- **Physics:** colder ambient air raises refrigerant cycle efficiency (Carnot).
- **Suppliers:** UEC-Perm Motors PS-90GP-25 and Kuznetsov NK-36ST gas-turbine drives, already in serial use on gas pipelines; REP Holding compressors.
- **Risks:** low-temperature alloy piping (F183); maintenance across many trains.
- **Test:** two more 1 Mtpa trains, 2027–30.

**2. Underground ore pre-concentration (Norilsk disseminated ore)**
- **Cost:** 5–10% lower per tonne Ni-eq (est.), because waste no longer uses concentrator capacity.
- **Time:** sorter modules installed in about 2 years.
- **Recovery:** 1–3% of metal lost to rejected waste.
- **Benchmark:** Alrosa X-ray diamond sorting; magnetic cobbing at Kiruna.
- **Scope:** disseminated ores, Udokan, Baimskaya. Not massive sulphides, placers, coal or LNG.
- **Physics:** X-ray attenuation rises with atomic number, so Fe-Ni-Cu sulphides absorb far more than the silicate host rock.
- **Suppliers:** RADOS (Yekaterinburg), Bourevestnik (St Petersburg).
- **Test:** bulk-sample trial in 2027.

**3. Re-levelling foundations that accept thaw**
- **Infrastructure life:** set by steel, about 25 years (est.), not by bearing loss.
- **Capex:** about 3–5% higher on structures (est.).
- **Time:** saves about half a season, because there is no pile freeze-back wait.
- **Benchmark:** Norilsk fixed piles, with 35% of buildings deformed (F252).
- **Scope:** modules, pipe racks, conveyors, camps. Not mills, kilns, tailings dams or ice-rich ground that settles more than 1 m in total.
- **Physics:** settlement equals thaw strain times thaw depth. Measured movement is 60–180 mm/yr (F101), so a 300 mm jack stroke covers about 2 years between re-levels.
- **Suppliers:** Enerpred hydraulic jacks, Severstal and MMK steel.
- **Test:** twin pilots, re-levelling frame against conventional piles, at Norilsk and Salekhard, 2027–31, monitored by InSAR and survey.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Numbered-up mid-scale LNG trains
MECHANISM: Replicate the Arctic Cascade train built at 0.9 Mtpa about seven times instead of scaling up to 6.6 Mtpa. Each train is driven by 16–25 MW gas turbines already in serial production for Russian pipelines, and Arctic air does the precooling. The closest earlier attempt, Arctic LNG 2, stalled on scarce large turbines; this design avoids that by using drives already built in volume. The deciding quantity is installed capex per tonne-year together with train availability at −45 °C.
VALUE: It phases LNG capital and starts revenue sooner using only domestic drives.

IDEA 2
NAME: Underground X-ray ore pre-concentration
MECHANISM: Domestic X-ray transmission sorters at the shaft bottom reject waste lumps from disseminated Ni-Cu ore, and the rejected rock becomes stope backfill. The concentrator's fixed capacity then processes more metal per hour. The closest earlier practice, Alrosa's diamond sorting, has never been applied to sulphide ore here. The deciding quantity is the waste-rejection fraction achieved while losing less than 3% of the metal.
VALUE: It raises metal output per unit of existing concentrator capital.

IDEA 3
NAME: Re-levelling thaw-tolerant foundations
MECHANISM: Surface structures sit on spread footings or space frames with 300 mm hydraulic jacks and are re-levelled from survey data as the active layer moves. The ground is allowed to thaw instead of being kept frozen. The closest earlier attempts, fixed piles and thermosyphons, failed as winters warmed; this design carries no assumed ground-temperature margin. The deciding quantity is the differential settlement rate compared with jack stroke and re-levelling interval.
VALUE: It decouples infrastructure life from the rate of permafrost warming.
