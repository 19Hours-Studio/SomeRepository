FACT 12: Streletskiy and Grebenets (AGU 2015) give shares of deformed buildings. About 60% or more in Vorkuta and Dikson, nearly 40% in Yakutsk and Igarka, and about 35% in Norilsk, Talnakh and Mirny. Over 80% of ground dams with frozen cores were in poor condition. The causes were both climate and technogenic. | SOFT (2015 poster abstract) | https://agu.confex.com/agu/fm15/webprogram/Paper77610.html
FACT 13: An MChS Krasnoyarsk official (Tereshkov) wrote that almost 60% of Norilsk buildings were deformed. This is much higher than the 35% in Fact 12. | SOFT | https://www.newsweek.com/node/510283
FACT 14: Vorkuta buildings were mostly built by the "active" method (thawing soil before construction), which helps explain the high deformation. | SOFT | https://ges.rgo.ru/jour/article/view/2069
FACT 15: The Hjort et al. 2022 review (Nature Rev. Earth Environ., ~170 papers) found damaged shares of building stock from under 10% to 80%. Russia had the highest share. 30–50% of critical circumpolar infrastructure is at high risk by 2050. Russian road upkeep losses from permafrost for 2020–2050 are about US$7 bn (422 bn RUB) on the existing network. | HARD | https://www.oulu.fi/en/news/thaw-permafrost-has-vast-impact-built-environment ; https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/climate-crisis/2022/01/30-50-critical-northern-infrastructure-could-be-high-risk-2050-due-warming
FACT 16: The Hjort 2018 Nature Communications paper (vol. 9, 5147) maps infrastructure hazard under projected warming to 2050. Proven mitigations are convection embankments, thermosyphons and pile foundations. | HARD | https://permafrost.gi.alaska.edu/biblio/degrading-permafrost-puts-arctic-infrastructure-risk-mid-century
