=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Fuel on empty coal-barge legs
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 24 bladders do not fit in one layer in a 1,000 t hold, so each leg carries far less than 1,000 t | 42 t of diesel is 50 m³. A pillow tank holds about 70% of a box of the same size ([Interstate](https://store.interstateproducts.com/data-management-center/water-bladders/)), so at 1.2–1.5 m filled height each bladder covers 48–60 m², and 24 of them cover 1,140–1,430 m². A typical 1,000 t river-barge hold is about 550–980 m² (my assumption). That gives 290–710 t per leg (29–71%), so the plan needs 24–59 legs, not 17.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Arctic and winter diesel has a flash point of 30–40 °C, below 60 °C, so it is Class 3 dangerous goods (UN1202). Inland dangerous-goods rules (ADN-type) accept Class 3 liquids on dry-cargo vessels only in approved packagings or tank containers. I found no rule that accepts soft pillow tanks, or a coal hold as spill containment | I searched and found no permitting provision ([Mintrans rules](https://mintrans.gov.ru/file/393584)). Unless a rule is found, this blocks the idea.
DEFECT | MINOR | The baseline is a sunk-cost, 38–54-year-old tanker. It ignores the legal alternative on the same empty legs: domestic ISO tank containers | About 40 containers × 21 t ≈ 850 t per leg. At about 2M rub per container over 20 years (assumed), that is roughly 3,000 rub/t, so the bladder advantage is ≤1.4x, and that is before the regulatory risk.
DEFECT | MINOR | "Under 10% of the delivered price" holds only at the top of the range | The saving is 600–9,900 rub/t, or 0.5–11% of a delivered price of 90–120k rub/t.
SURVIVES | 17,000/42 = 405 bladder-trips. 17 × 24 × 42 = 17.1 kt. That is about 4 trips per bladder in a season, and 17–59 legs still fit within the 165 available, even if fuel only reaches the river mouth from late July.
RATIO | 1.02 | The river leg is 1.0–3.5x cheaper after the hold-area correction, but the whole delivered-diesel chain moves only 1–3%.
KNOWN | partly | NPF Politekhnika makes domestic PER soft fuel tanks ([rg.ru](https://rg.ru/2016/09/28/reg-dfo/npf-politehnika-pristupila-k-proizvodstvu-innovacionnyh-rezervuarov.html)). Tank containers on river barges are standard practice.

IDEA 2: Shaft-bottom XRT sorting, hoist-bound mines
DEFECT | MAJOR | The "Selebi-type" basis is one bulk test reported by the company in a press release, with no grades for the rejected waste. The 44%/4% "stringer" result has no source at all. One sample cannot put bounds on rejection or loss | [NexMetals, Jul 2025](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/260258): 15.2% rejected, 98% retained. If stringer rejection is 30% instead of 44%, the gain falls from ×1.364 to ×1.21.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The hoist-bound premise is assumed, not shown. The mine would have to mine, develop, ventilate and cool ×1.11–1.40 more ore at about 2 km depth. If ventilation, cooling or development is also a limit, the gain goes to zero | I found no Skalisty data showing that the skip, not ventilation, sets the limit.
DEFECT | MAJOR | "2–3 years" is optimistic. A sorting cavern with screen, wash, sorters and reject conveyors would sit at about 2 km in rockburst-prone ground. The only underground XRT precedent is a pilot in salt | [K+S Zielitz/TOMRA](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/11092025/tomra-mining-breaks-new-ground-in-underground-rock-salt-sorting-pilot-project-at-k-s-zielitz-mine/): a pilot since 2022, built to relieve a shaft bottleneck, and still "exploring ways to … increase throughput".
DEFECT | MINOR | The $5.0–8.3k vs $6.7–12.6k per t Ni-eq cannot be reproduced from the stated inputs | No capex, mining cost or Ni-eq grade is given.
SURVIVES | ×1.094 and ×1.364 hoisted metal reproduce, as do NPV ×1.083 and ×1.307 and 0.40 t of rejects per t hoisted. Backfill need is 0.43–0.50 t/t at 4.4 t/m³ ore, so the rejects fit. Sorter count comes out at 1.5–2.4 units at 150 t/h (5,000–6,500 h/yr).
RATIO | 1.04 | At Selebi-type results; the stringer case is unproven.
KNOWN | partly | K+S Zielitz underground XRT pilot (TOMRA, 2022–). Surface XRT rejects used as underground rockfill at Sotkamo ([Mining Magazine](https://www.miningmagazine.com/plant/news/1388148/sotkamo-starts-ore-sorting-trial)).
NEW FACT | The Selebi 15.2% rejection may be a share of the total feed, which would give ×1.156 and not ×1.094 | [TipRanks summary](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/nexmetals-reports-positive-ore-sorting-results)

IDEA 3: Hull-paced domestic Arctic Cascade trains
DEFECT | MAJOR | The EU facts are wrong, and the delivered cost leaves out the last leg. Zeebrugge transshipment has been banned since about March 2025. EU imports are banned from 25 Apr 2026 (short-term contracts) and 1 Jan 2027 (long-term). So all winter LNG out of Murmansk must reach Asian buyers on conventional carriers, a trip of about 11,000 nm via Suez | [Belga](https://www.belganewsagency.eu/eu-adopts-14th-sanctions-package-against-russia-targeting-lng) (9-month transition), [S&P](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/012626-eu-finalizes-ban-on-russian-gas-lng-by-2027)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Supply of Arc7 hulls is unproven. Only 5 Samsung-built hulls exist. The first was delivered on 24 Dec 2025, and the last two are "likely years away". Zvezda has not yet built a hull of its own, so "1–2 a year from Zvezda" is an assumption. Arctic LNG 2 also needs about 8.1 hulls first | [High North News](https://www.highnorthnews.com/en/russia-delivers-first-domestically-assembled-arc7-lng-carrier-after-years-sanctions-delays)

The $236 < $257 claim gives no series savings to offset this. Train 4's track record also argues against copying it | Mikhelson said Train 4 "works poorly" and had claims against "almost all suppliers", and Ob LNG dropped Arctic Cascade ([Barents Observer](https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/arctic-lng/2021/09/novatek-ceo-blames-bad-russian-quality-troubled-start-fourth-train)).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Buyers and sanctions are the real limit, not hulls. Arctic LNG 2's idle 13.2 Mtpa shows this, so added trains add capacity that may not sell | The idea itself says it is 1.5x worse than filling Arctic LNG 2.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 12-day winter round trip has no source. At 14–16 days a hull carries 1.26–1.41 Mt/y, so each train needs 0.64–0.71 hull (+16–30%) | Argus gives about 20 days for winter round trips to NW Europe ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news/2154877-nsr-to-stay-open-until-december-novatek)).

RATIO | 0.67 | Against the best current alternative, filling Arctic LNG 2 (by the idea's own 1.5x).
KNOWN | yes | Yamal Train 4 itself. The Arc7 shuttle to the Saam FSU at Ura Bay has run since Feb 2026 ([Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/shipping-news/russias-arc7-lng-carrier-alexey-kosygin-completes-first-arctic-lng-delivery/)).

IDEA 4: Pre-staged flood-crest barges
DEFECT | MAJOR | A loaded barge in a 2.5 m backwater freezes in and may ground. Stefan's law with a freezing index of 5,000–6,000 °C·d and α = 1.7–2.4 cm gives 1.2–1.9 m of ice. That leaves 0.6–1.3 m of water under the ice, less than or about equal to a 1.2–2.0 m loaded draft, before any winter drop in water level | Calculation (my estimate). Yakutian backwater practice freezes in empty fleet ([rg.ru 2024](https://rg.ru/2024/01/11/reg-dfo/soiuz-lda-i-stali-kak-vymorazhivaiut-i-remontiruiut-rechnye-suda.html)). I found no precedent for laden barges.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Ice in a backwater clears after the main channel does. If breaking out of it is delayed 5–10 days, the 12-day recession takes the same 35–62% the idea blames on late convoys | This follows from the idea's own recession numbers.
DEFECT | MINOR | It ignores winter roads, the usual competitor on one-trip-a-year tributaries, and the barge's lost autumn trips on the main river | No costing given.
DEFECT | MINOR | 35–62% does not reproduce. A linear 12-day recession gives 42–83% | 5/12 and 10/12.
SURVIVES | 4,700–11,300/3,100 = 1.5–3.6 is correct arithmetic.
RATIO | 1.03 | The river leg is a small share of the delivered cost of goods.
KNOWN | partly | Northern Delivery cargo that misses its window already winters at staging points by accident; this makes it deliberate.

IDEA 5: Level-crusher conveyor decline for Talnakh
DEFECT | FATAL | The geometry and energy figures contradict each other. 4.1 km × sin 14° = 992 m of lift, but the comparator is a 2.1 km shaft. A 2.1 km lift needs an 8.68 km decline (2.4–4.8 years to drive). Also, the 3.4 kWh/t given for the skip is below the 5.72 kWh/t physical minimum for a 2.1 km lift, so the comparator really lifts about 1 km | Against a 1 km shaft (4.6–8.2 years including equipping), the decline takes 2.1–3.3 years, a gain of 1.3–6.1 years, not 4–11.
DEFECT | FATAL | The value rests on hoisting being the limit, and the source points the other way. The Talnakh concentrator's expansion to 18 Mtpa by 2030–31 is described as letting it "take all ore from the Talnakh mines", which means moving ore away from the Norilsk concentrator (7.6 Mt in 2020), not new hoisted ore. The $1.5–3.5bn PV is unsupported | [www1.ru, 6 Mar 2026](https://www1.ru/news/2026/03/06/tretii-puskovoi-kompleks-talnaxskoi-obogatitelnoi-fabriki-v-norilske-postroiat-polnostiu-na-otecestvennom-oborudovanii.html), [AR2020](https://ar2020.nornickel.ru/pdf/ar/ru/business-overview_operational-performance_norilsk.pdf)
DEFECT | MINOR | Power is understated. Lift needs 4.33 MW and belt friction 1.0–1.3 MW; at 90% drive efficiency that is 5.95–6.3 MW, or 3.7–3.9 kWh/t. The belt therefore uses more energy than a 1 km skip (3.4) | Chuquicamata: 11,000 t/h, about 1 km rise, 58 MW installed ([TAKRAF](https://www.takraf.com/expertise/references/detail/takraf-conveyor-system/))

IDEA 6: Cold-margin triaged thermosyphon retrofit
DEFECT | MAJOR | The TAPS check does not validate M. The cooling loss on TAPS heat pipes came from non-condensable hydrogen blocking them (a hardware defect seen in infrared surveys in the early 1980s), not from a climate margin | [Sorensen et al. 2002](https://inl.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/thermal-performance-of-taps-heat-pipes-with-non-condensable-gas-b/), [ASCE 2021](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/9780784483589.031)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Norilsk, the only deployment site, passes by 2% (1.53 against 1.5). In my degree-day model, M = 1.48 at +3 K of winter warming and 1.37 at +4 K, so Norilsk fails if 2050 winters warm by more than about 2.9 K. Norilsk has warmed about 0.34 °C per decade since the mid-1970s, with winters warming fastest | [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2013/12/19/where-has-siberias-winter-gone), [Rough & Polished](https://rough-polished.expert/en/safety-and-ecology/136156.html)
DEFECT | MAJOR | "3× cheaper" is measured against a straw man. Nornickel has run an automated geotechnical monitoring system since 2021 (phase 1 cost 1.4bn rub), so triage is already current practice. The triage itself has no cost edge: $1.28M per structure triaged against $1.20M per structure for all 165. The saving is only the count, 50 against 165 | [ComNews](https://www.comnews.ru/digital-economy/content/219214/2022-03-11/2022-w10/nornikel-konsolidiruet-dannye-o-vechnoy-merzlote-norilske)
DEFECT | MINOR | "One winter" holds only for frozen but warm ground. Sensible cooling needs 0.17–0.39 GJ per unit, against 0.86–1.73 GJ of capacity per winter. For the worst-ranked structures, with 15% unfrozen water by volume, freezing needs 1.75–3.9 GJ, which is 1–4.5 winters | The effective cooling radius is about 1 m ([TPU 2024](https://izvestiya.tpu.ru/archive/article/download/4532/3313/18739)).
SURVIVES | $64M/8,500 = $7.5k per unit. 198/64 = 3.1. 196–392/64 = 3.1–6.1. $3–19 per t Ni-eq is consistent.
RATIO | 1.00 | Against existing practice of monitoring and triage, and 0.04–0.2% of full cost.
KNOWN | yes | Nornickel's Norilsk geotechnical monitoring plus thermostabiliser retrofits.
NEW FACT | In Salekhard, the part of the year when seasonal cooling units can work has shrunk by almost a month over 30 years | [geosib 2025](https://geosib.sgugit.ru/upload/geosibir/sborniki/2025/tom-2-3/193-200.pdf)

The decisive defects are in Ideas 5 and 6. Idea 5's decline only lifts about 1 km, half of the 2.1 km shaft it is meant to replace, and the Talnakh expansion it relies on is mostly re-routed ore. Idea 6's two validation cases are a hardware fault on TAPS and a 2% margin at Norilsk. Under the corrected ratios, no idea improves whole-chain cost by more than about 4%. The stated hold areas, ice-growth coefficients and container prices are my assumptions, and they are flagged where they are used.
