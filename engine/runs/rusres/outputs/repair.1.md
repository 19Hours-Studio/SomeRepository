=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Hull-paced domestic Arctic Cascade trains
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged: copy the domestic 0.9 Mtpa Arctic Cascade Train 4 many times instead of building 6.6 Mtpa trains that need imported or first-of-kind drives. In the repaired design, trains are added only at Sabetta and only as fast as Arc7 hulls arrive. Each train needs about 0.55 hull: a hull carries about 1.63 Mt/y when it runs 150 days a year east on the NSR and spends the winter shuttling 12-day round trips to the Saam/Ura FSU near Murmansk. In winter this shuttle replaces the 20-day Zeebrugge run, which the EU bans from 2027. With this matching, all added output can be shipped year-round, which fixes the kill metric. The trains have two roles. First, they are a domestic-drive hedge for the Nuovo Pignone strings on Trains 1–3: each 1% of availability lost there is 0.165 Mt/y. Second, they are the growth path once Arctic LNG 2's idle 13.2 Mtpa is full. That needs about 8 hulls, so it happens around 2030–33 at 1–2 hulls a year from Zvezda. No new trains go to Utrenneye before then.
VALUE: Full delivered cost is about $236/t, against $295/t for a Yamal T1–3-type train (1.25x) and $257/t for a large domestic-drive train (1.09x). First LNG is about 3 years after the decision for a series copy, against 4–4.5. But it is 1.5x worse than simply filling Arctic LNG 2's idle trains, which is why it is held back until those are full.

IDEA 2
NAME: Level-crusher conveyor decline for Talnakh
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged: crush ore at the level and carry it on a fixed belt, replacing truck haulage. The belt no longer stops at an existing shaft. It runs up a 14° decline all the way to surface, and serves as the hoisting route for the Talnakh concentrator expansion from 10 to 18 Mtpa in place of a new 2.1 km skip shaft. Nornickel's shafts advanced only 150–322 m in 2017, so sinking takes 6.5–14 years, plus about 1.5 years to equip. A 4.1 km decline (1,000 m lift) driven by drill-and-blast from 2–3 headings at 150 m/month takes about 1–2.3 years, plus about 1 year to install the belt. It is lifted by three flights of ST3150 belt (or two of ST5000) at 1,600 t/h, drawing 5.1 MW, or 3.2 kWh/t against 3.4 for a skip.
VALUE: Cost per tonne of ore is $2.9–4.0, against $3.6–5.3 for a new shaft. That is only about 1–2% of full cost per tonne of nickel-equivalent, so the gain is not cost. The gain is ore 4–11 years sooner for 8 Mt/y, worth about $1.5–3.5bn PV. That holds only if hoisting is what limits the expansion.

=== NOTES ===

**IDEA 1. Anchor kept: yes.** Series-built domestic 0.9 Mtpa Arctic Cascade trains, with no imported drives.

**Decisive numbers:**
- Plant capex:
  - Train 4 at $450–500 per t/y against about $1,100 for T1–3 (FACT 174, soft).
  - Annualised at 12% over 25 years: $64–77/t against $140/t.
- Arc7 hull price $315–330m (Kommersant, quoting Zvezda, Novatek and Minpromtorg).
- One hull carries 2.29 Mt/y on the Murmansk shuttle, 1.37 Mt/y on Zeebrugge runs, 0.69 Mt/y going east to Asia, and 1.63 Mt/y mixed. That works out to about $37/t of hull cost.
- Hull supply:
  - Russia has 2 Arc7 carriers (FACT 139), and the next two are slowed because GTT membranes are unavailable (FACT 347).
  - Arctic LNG 2 T1 and T2 are built and idle (FACTS 51, 283).

**Best-baseline comparison, $/t delivered** (plant + field + Arc7 + 60% sent on by conventional carrier from Murmansk):

| Option | $/t delivered |
|---|---|
| Fill idle Arctic LNG 2 trains (cheapest) | 155 |
| Small series trains | 236 |
| Large 6.6 Mtpa train with domestic drives (Kazankompressormash 112 MW, FACT 348; $800/t is my assumption) | 257 |
| T1–3 type | 295 |

Adding capacity in 0.9 Mtpa steps instead of 6.6 Mtpa saves about $4.7/t of capital that would otherwise sit idle while waiting for hulls.

**Hazards:**

**Deciding unknowns:**

- Zvezda's actual hull delivery rate.

**Closest existing work:** Yamal LNG Train 4 and Arctic Cascade Modified (3 Mtpa, 2023 patent). The repair adds pacing to hull supply, the Murmansk-shuttle logistics, and the hedge role.

**Verdict: NICHE.**

**IDEA 2. Anchor kept: yes.** Crush at the level and move ore by fixed belt instead of trucks; the belt now ends at surface instead of at a shaft.

**Decisive numbers:**
- Shaft advance in 2017 was 322 m for the skip-cage shaft and 152 m for the ventilation shaft, on shafts of about 2.1 km (Nornickel AR2017).
- The Nurkazgan crusher plus an 844 m, 1,000–1,200 t/h conveyor cascade costs 5.9bn tenge, about $12.6m or about $14.9k per metre including the crusher (Forbes.kz, Digitalbusiness.kz). This is a domestic-style price check.
- Domestic precedent: Oleniy Ruchey (FACT 355).
- Lift energy is set by physics (m·g·h); belt and skip are within 6% of each other.

**Best-baseline comparison:**
- New skip shaft (Norilsk practice): $3.6–5.3/t ore.
- Conveyor decline: $2.9–4.0/t ore.
- Cheapest option: spare capacity in existing shafts plus the original belt-to-shaft retrofit. The kill test caps that at ≤6% of full cost.
- Truck ramp haulage from 1 km: $6.75/t, 47 trucks, 19 ML/y of diesel, and 168 GWh/y of winter air heating. Nobody would do this, so it is not the comparison case.
- Per tonne of nickel-equivalent (about 84 t ore per t, my value estimate), the belt-versus-shaft difference is about $60–110, set against roughly $6,300 full cost.

**Consistency checks:**

| Limit | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Belt angle with −250 mm crushed ore | 14° used; limit 16–18° | OK |
| Belt rating | 1,600 t/h × 6,600 h = 10.6 Mt ≥ 8 Mt | OK |
| Flight tension at safety factor 7 | ≤469 m lift per flight on ST3150 | OK |
| Portal site | Valleys have 120–200 m of Quaternary cover (FACT 6) | Portal must sit on the basalt plateau |
| Upper decline in permafrost | Exhaust air would thaw it | Use it as the intake airway (heated to +2 °C) or line and insulate it |
| Depth of 1,700 m (Oktyabrsky rich ore) | 7 km decline, about 1.3 years from 3 headings | Still fast |

**Hazards:**
- Belt fire in a possibly gassy mine: use self-extinguishing belts.
- Transfer chambers in weak tuff.
- If existing shafts already have the capacity, all of the value disappears.
- LHDs are still needed between stopes and the crusher.

**Deciding unknown:** whether Talnakh hoisting capacity, rather than ore reserves or development, limits the 18 Mtpa feed in 2030–31.

**Cheapest experiment:**
- A hoisting-capacity audit against the 2030–31 feed plan.
- Drive a 500 m pilot section of the decline and measure advance per month over 6 months, with at least 2 headings, aiming for ≥150 m/month.

**Equipment, all domestic or unrestricted:**
- Steel-cord belts from Kurskrezinotekhnika or Chinese makers.
- Ruselprom motors.
- Uralmash crushers.
- Kopeysk roadheaders, or Chinese jumbos.
- The Nornickel–BelAZ LHDs (FACT 184).

**Closest existing work:** decline conveyors at Cadia and Carrapateena, Grosvenor's TBM-driven decline, Oleniy Ruchey and Nurkazgan. The repair adds the use of a conveyor decline instead of a 7–14-year Norilsk shaft, timed to the concentrator expansion, with the portal designed for permafrost. The mechanism itself is KNOWN.

**Verdict: NICHE** (wins on time, roughly neutral on cost).

**Assumptions to check:** these are not sourced and should be replaced with real figures: 12% cost of capital, shaft cost $40–70k/m, a $4.5/MMBtu LNG margin, the $800/t domestic large-train capex, and ore value and grades.
