=== ATTEMPTS ===

All figures come from the supplied facts. Items marked "derived" are my arithmetic, not sourced.

ATTEMPT 1: Pile foundations on frozen ground, designed to a fixed temperature coefficient (γt) | Russia-wide norms (SP 25.13330), 1970s onward | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Warming erodes the capacity the design assumed. Bearing capacity has fallen more than 40% at Nadym and about 20% at Salekhard, and is projected above 55% at Norilsk by the 2040s. A designer cannot change γt without a norm change. Amendment 3 only takes effect 14 Jun 2026. Reported deformed-building shares are 35% (Norilsk, Talnakh and Mirny) to about 60% (Vorkuta, Dikson). | F89, F92, F252, F267

ATTEMPT 2: Thermosyphons and cooling pipes (GET/VET; Trans-Alaska heat pipes; Qinghai–Tibet) | Chayanda; TAPS (1970s); Chinese railways | standard, niche in Russia | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: They work only in the cold season, and their cooling fades as the air freezing index falls. TAPS needed repairs: more than 124,000 pipes were installed, some lost performance to non-condensable gas, and 60 freestanding units were added at Lost Creek. On 14 Qinghai–Tibet embankments, cooling weakened and taliks re-formed. A coupled model gives up to 50% less settlement but strong lateral thermal gradients. Russian price and capacity data are missing. | F205, F320–322

ATTEMPT 3: Conservative mine-water and pit-stability design in permafrost with brines | Mir (Alrosa), dry conservation in the early 2000s | standard, failed | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The design ignored rock fracturing and the need for long-term pumping. The mine flooded in 2017, with 8–9 dead. Restoration is priced at RUB 73 bn and 6–8 years, against about $1.3 bn estimated earlier. Restart is unlikely before about 2030, and the mine held 68.6 Mct of reserves. | F272–274, F79

ATTEMPT 4: Fast-track, module-based mega-project (Yamal LNG), with ice-class shipping | Novatek, FID 2013, first cargo Dec 2017 | standard, best Russian benchmark | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It cost about $27bn, or about $1.6bn per mtpa (derived). It needed over 30,000 workers on site. Trains 1–3 rely on Western APCI-C3MR and Baker Hughes drives, and 15 Arc7 ships. Asia is reachable by NSR only about July to late November, so winter cargo is trans-shipped at Zeebrugge. The model also depends on Western spares. | F43–47, F138, F176, F300, F308

ATTEMPT 5: Replicating that model under sanctions (Arctic LNG 2) | Novatek, FID 2019 | dropped in part | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Planned at $21.3bn, it is now over $22bn. Baker Hughes supplied only 4 of the 20 turbines. Wison quit in June 2024. The GTE-170 is at "units" scale. Train 1 started Dec 2023 but sold nothing for lack of ice-class tankers. Output is about 3.6–4.4 Mt/y per train against 6.6 designed (Forbes estimate). Train 3 has slipped to about 2028. Cargoes sell at 30–40% discounts. Only 2 Arc7 carriers existed at end-2025. | F48, F50, F177–182, F209, F283, F139

ATTEMPT 6: Domestic liquefaction technology (Arctic Cascade) and domestic equipment | Yamal LNG Train 4, 0.9 mtpa | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It has run only at 0.9 mtpa, so it is unproven at the 6.6 mtpa scale. Unit capex was claimed at $450–500 per t/y against about $1,100 on Trains 1–3. Low-temperature alloy piping had to be replaced. Murmansk LNG (Arctic Mix) has no FID and has slipped to 2032. | F172–174, F183, F304

ATTEMPT 7: Rail and port to the deposit, funded by the miner (Elga) | Mechel, 321 km line, 2008–2011 | standard, financially costly | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The line cost 71–77 bn RUB and Mechel's debt reached 421 bn RUB. Design output was reached about 12 years after the rail opened: 4.3 Mt in 2019, against 30 Mt designed. The Elga port and Pacific railway (Nov 2024) are a second round of the same problem. The coal sector is loss-making: 66% of enterprises lose money, and rail tariffs rose about 100% in 2020–24. | F74–75, F162, F234, F286

ATTEMPT 8: Greenfield copper with its own infrastructure (Udokan, Baimskaya) | BMC/Usmanov; KAZ Minerals | standard, slow | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Udokan planned production for 2010–16 and got concentrate in Sept 2023. Cathode followed in Sept 2026, after a fire and about 3 years late. Capex rose from $2.1bn to $2.9bn (+38%). Baimskaya capex rose from $5.5bn to about $8bn, driven by external infrastructure, a revised tailings facility and a one-year delay; it comes to about $114 per annual tonne of ore (derived). The project sits in a 9-point seismic zone (Udokan) and on continuous permafrost 150–280 m thick (Baimskaya). | F71–73, F103, F124, F227–228, F277

ATTEMPT 9: Nuclear and floating power for remote mines | Akademik Lomonosov (Pevek); Baimskaya RITM-200S; Ust-Kuyga | niche, delayed | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Capex is about $4,000/kW against 4–5× lower for renewables (analyst figure). The Baimskaya units cost RUB 190 bn in 2021, are undisclosed now, and are dated 2027 or 2028–30. Ust-Kuyga has slipped from 2028 to 2030 with no capex published. Bilibino NPP is closing, and the Pevek–Bilibino line slipped. | F151–156, F309–310

ATTEMPT 10: Wind or solar plus diesel | Raglan, Diavik, Tiksi, Yakutia | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It covers only about 10% of mine power. Raglan's two 3 MW turbines offset about 4 million L of about 50 million L used per year. Diavik's 9.2 MW cost C$31M with an 8-year payback. Raglan modelling gives up to 64% diesel cut, but storage costs CAD 7,831/MWh. Diesel power in Taimyr costs over 25 RUB/kWh. | F157, F169–170, F312–315

ATTEMPT 11: Seasonal winter roads and short shipping windows | Tibbitt–Contwoyto; Alaska; Red Dog; Mary River; Pevek | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The window is shrinking and fixed. Tibbitt–Contwoyto averaged 65 days (47–78), projected at 49 days in the 2050s. Alaska's tundra season fell from about 200 days to about 130. Red Dog ships only mid-July to late October (122 days in 2019). Mary River ships mid-July to mid-October, and its Phase 2 was rejected in 2022. Pevek's window is 3–4 months. | F122, F148, F167–168, F293, F323

ATTEMPT 12: Nuclear icebreakers and NSR as a trunk route | Rosatom, 2018 decree | standard, below plan | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The decree targeted 80 Mt by 2024 and actual traffic was about 38 Mt (37.02 Mt in 2025). Transit is only about 9% of it. The 2025 transit season was about 4.5 months. Chukotka's handover slipped to 2027, and Lider is "by 2030". The EU bans services to icebreakers and LNG tankers from 1 Jan 2027. | F115, F132–133, F142–143, F215

ATTEMPT 13: Domestic substitution of mining fleets | Nornickel and BelAZ JV (Sept 2026); Chinese excavators | niche, early | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: BelAZ had never built underground trucks, and the 45 t truck was still on factory-ground tests in Oct 2026. The installed base is Western. Nornickel lost about half its suppliers, and its output dipped in 2023 during the equipment switch. Chinese trucks are rated only to −30 °C. Component quality is the main obstacle, and imports exceed 69% of the drilling-rig market. | F63–64, F184–190, F191, F208

ATTEMPT 14: Autonomous haulage | Zyfra and BelAZ pilots | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Claims are about +20% productivity, from developer data on non-Arctic sites. Satellite signal is lost in deep pits. There is no Arctic deployment count, and customers still ask for a cab. | F196–198, F338

ATTEMPT 15: Domestic geology software (Geomix) | Rostec, 2024–26 | niche | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The gap is in 2D/3D seismic and 3D mine modelling. Free licences went only to universities, and no commercial deployment at Nornickel or Polyus was found. | F192, F195

ATTEMPT 16: Dry-stack and frozen-core tailings | Meliadine, Keno Hill, Colomac, Mt Nansen | niche, mixed | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: Freeze–thaw changes density and permeability, and the coupled models are immature. Colomac seepage rose from 6 to about 300 gpm over a talik. A Nunavut frozen-core dam had a 7-year design life. Baimskaya's tailings redesign added capex. | F127–128, F332, F277

ATTEMPT 17: Tax relief and Arctic Zone regime (Arctic-Zone status, MET deductions) | Rosneft Vankor/Vostok; AZRF | standard | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: The 0% profit tax excludes extraction and LNG. Murmansk and Arkhangelsk residents have declared about 270 bn RUB each but invested only about 60 bn. Vankor's MET deduction was 102–125 bn RUB per year. Vostok Oil slipped from 2024 to 2026, with first export in Sept 2026, against a $157bn capex estimate. A windfall tax from 2027 hits metals. | F221, F230, F248–249, F280

ATTEMPT 18: Post-spill monitoring (Norilsk boreholes, sensors, InSAR) | Nornickel; Roshydromet | standard, new | WHY IT DID NOT SOLVE IT: It detects but does not fix. Roshydromet had 78 points against 140 planned. The Nornickel network is five 200 m boreholes. InSAR shows seasonal movement (up to 180 mm/yr), not structural settlement. Warming rates cannot be transferred between boreholes. The Ambarnaya tank collapse was officially blamed on weak piles and poor maintenance (ERM found piles set in permafrost, not bedrock), not on thaw. | F9, F101, F107, F257–258, F263, F325

LESSONS:
- No ground-cooling or pile fix has a measured lifetime against thaw. Cooling weakens as winters warm, and design norms lag the ground.
- The unit cost of a remote site is dominated by external infrastructure and power. Power and the transport window repeatedly slipped or went over budget, by 38% to 60% or more.
- Every domestic substitute for imported plant is at prototype or unit scale. Spares for the Western base remain the schedule risk.
- Seasonal windows and ice-class tonnage cap deliveries, and ice-class tonnage is the scarcest item (only 2 Arc7 carriers).
- Many failures were organisational (poor maintenance, immature design, ignored warnings), not physics.
- The evidence base is thin: many figures are press, vendor, secondary or conflicting, and few site-level permafrost data exist. This applies to the cost-per-unit comparisons too.
