# Task: one slice of a research dossier

QUESTION the dossier serves: Russia wants to develop and extract minerals and energy in its permafrost regions at the lowest full cost, in the shortest time from investment decision to first production, and with the highest effectiveness. Effectiveness means resource recovery, working time across the year, and how long infrastructure lasts on thawing ground. All three are measured per unit of product delivered to the buyer. The scope covers:
- Hard-rock and placer metals: Norilsk and Taimyr nickel, copper and platinum-group metals; gold in Yakutia, Magadan and Chukotka; Udokan and Baimskaya copper; Tomtor rare earths.
- Diamonds in Yakutia, plus the Arkhangelsk deposits (see CORRECTED).
- Coal in Taimyr, Elga and the rest of Yakutia.
- Oil and gas in Yamal, Gydan and Taimyr.
The whole chain counts: choosing and exploring deposits, building and powering sites, mining or drilling, processing, staffing, and delivering the product. The conditions are:
- Permafrost that is thawing under a warming climate.
- Extreme cold, with short building and shipping seasons.
- Long distances with no roads or railways.
- Ice seasons on the Northern Sea Route (NSR) and the rivers.
- A shortage of workers.
- Expensive energy at remote sites.
- Restricted access to imported mining, drilling and LNG equipment and software since 2022.
What changes to any stage, or to the order in which deposits are developed, would beat the best current practice in Russia and in comparable cold regions (northern Canada, Alaska, Greenland, northern Scandinavia)?
A good answer: 
- Each proposal states its effect, with an estimate and its basis, on three measures: (a) full cost per unit delivered (annualised capital plus operating plus logistics); (b) years from investment decision to first production; (c) effectiveness (recovery, days worked per year, infrastructure life on thawing ground).
- Each proposal is compared with a named benchmark, either Russian (e.g. Yamal LNG, Norilsk, Vankor/Vostok Oil, Kupol/Pavlovskoye, Nord/Elga) or foreign (e.g. Canadian Arctic mines such as Raglan and Diavik, Red Dog in Alaska, Kiruna/Aitik, Greenland).
- It names which deposits or stages it applies to and which it does not.
- It states what physical law or measured data it relies on.
- It lists the equipment or technology by its domestic maker or unrestricted supplier.
- It includes the main risks and how to test the idea within 3–7 years.
Constraints: 
- It must obey known physics.
- It must be deployable within 3–7 years.
- It may use only equipment and technology Russian operators can get from domestic makers or from suppliers not restricted for them.
- It must not depend on ways around sanctions.

YOUR SLICE: Newest developments (2023–2026) and leading practice in comparable cold regions | COVER: Technologies and methods that changed in the last three years in Russia and abroad and that could be deployed in 3–7 years: modular and gravity-based LNG construction (Novatek Utrenneye GBS), pre-assembled processing modules; dry-stack and frozen tailings; heap leach in deep cold; ore sorting and early-stage rejection; cold-climate battery-electric and trolley haulage; underground automation and remote operation centres; active foundation cooling (thermosiphons, vertical evaporative systems) and pile monitoring with fibre-optic or satellite (InSAR) methods; ground-based small reactors and microreactors for mines; wind-solar-battery hybrids at Arctic mines; nuclear and LNG-powered icebreakers and Arc7 fleet operations year-round; digital permafrost monitoring under the new Russian national system. Foreign leading practice with published capex, opex, recovery and uptime: Raglan (wind and storage, Tarquti), Diavik (solar and wind), Red Dog (seasonal shipping, port), Kiruna and Aitik (automation, electrified haulage, large-scale low-grade processing), Agnico Eagle Nunavut mines (Meliadine, Amaruq, Meadowbank), Greenland projects (Tanbreez, Amitsoq), Mary River (seasonal shipping), Alaska North Slope (gravel pads, ice roads, thermosiphon pipelines, Willow project) | ANGLES: Russian programme and pilot announcements 2023–2026 (Rosatom Yakutia SMR, Arctic LNG 2 GBS, Nornickel and Polyus decarbonisation and automation pilots, the national permafrost monitoring system of Roshydromet/MNR, Alrosa new underground methods) from company reports and specialised press; NI 43-101 technical reports and annual reports for Agnico Eagle, Rio Tinto Diavik, Glencore Raglan, Teck Red Dog, Baffinland Mary River, Boliden Aitik, LKAB Kiruna, with capex, opex, recovery and uptime figures; cold-region engineering research on thermosiphons, frozen tailings, and InSAR-based permafrost monitoring (Canadian Geotechnical Journal, Cold Regions Science and Technology, Криосфера Земли, Основания фундаменты и механика грунтов); Arctic renewable and nuclear microgrid case studies (NRCan, US DOE Alaska, Tarquti Energy, Rosatom, Chinese microreactors); Alaska North Slope oil practice (ConocoPhillips Willow, Alyeska thermosiphon data) and Hammerfest/Yamal LNG modular build schedules | LANGUAGES: English (primary for foreign practice and international research); Russian (Russian developments and journals); Swedish (LKAB, Boliden); French (Quebec/Raglan, Nunavik documents); Danish and Greenlandic (Greenland projects); Chinese (Chinese modular LNG, microreactor and mining-equipment developments)
