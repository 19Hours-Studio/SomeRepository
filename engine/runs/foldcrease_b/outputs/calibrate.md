**WEAKEST LINK:** Idea 1 depends on how much curvature a free-standing 20 µm panel (colour filter on encapsulation, on-cell touch) retains as set. The residual slope scales linearly with that set (set × π × D-ratio). To reach 0.3 mrad with 5 leaves, retained set must be about 2% or less, not the assumed 10%.

**FLIP TEST:** This is also the cheapest experiment. Fold bare 20 µm panel coupons, with no adhesive, at R = 2.5 mm:
- 2000 cycles at 60 °C and 90% relative humidity,
- plus 24 h held folded at 60 °C,
- then measure the retained curvature with a profilometer after 1 h of recovery.

How to read the result:
- **Set of 2% or less:** idea 1 rises to about 55–60% and "none" falls.
- **Set of 5% or more:** idea 1 cannot be invisible even with 5 leaves (≥0.8 mrad). It falls to about 10%, and "none" rises to about 75%.

A second cheap check is to thermally cycle a 7-film fluid sandwich from −20 to 60 °C and inspect it for bubbles. If bubbles appear, idea 1 falls just as far, whatever the set result.

**UNKNOWN:** Even if idea 1 works, three things remain unverified:
- Whether 0.3 mrad and about 1 µm are truly invisible under specular off-axis light and to a sliding finger or stylus. No published human-threshold data was cited.
- How the sealed fluid stack holds up over years of permeation, outgassing and drop shock.
