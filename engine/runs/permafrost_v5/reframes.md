**ASSUMPTIONS:**
- One method can serve oil, gas, metals, diamonds, coal and rare earths alike (contradicted: costs are driven by capital in Cu and Li, by tax in oil, by logistics in coal and by earthmoving in placers)
- Engineering alone can make delivered cost several times cheaper (contradicted: the ceilings allow less than 3x for A, B, D and E; oil is capped at 1.9x by MET+NDD)
- Reaching the resource and bringing it to market is the main cost (contradicted: outbound logistics is 0.2% for gold, 1.5% for lithium and 5% for copper; it dominates only for coal)
- Distance and remoteness are the binding constraints (contradicted: the season binds, with ice roads open 45-54 days, dredges 5-6 months and thaw 4-5 months)
- The relevant market is export (contradicted: settlement coal at about $275/t at the boiler is the only case with room)
- Big projects are the way to cut unit cost (contradicted: ships, rail and pipe need 4-22 Mt/yr to repay; the gap is a sub-1 Mt/yr unit)
- The idea must be untried (mostly contradicted: 21 of 23 attempt classes exist; only dredging a talik under lake ice and a barge wintering as a boilerhouse were not found)
- "A few years" is compatible with greenfield megaprojects (contradicted: Baimskaya needs $8.5bn plus state road, port and NPP)

**REFRAME 1:** How can the delivered cost of solid fuel or heat at isolated Yakutian and Arctic settlements fall 3x from about 23,000 rub/t at the boiler, using a winter-capable unit under 1 Mt/yr that has on-site storage, no thaw lead and no subsidy dependence? | CHANGES: commodity, market (local, not export), unit (rub per Gcal or t at the boiler) | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: high | WHY: the only archetype marked ROOM; outbound logistics is 85%, so a 10x cut of that one term gives 4.26x, against a 66.7x ceiling.

**REFRAME 2:** How can permafrost placer gold be mined through winter, replacing the summer-only stripping of frozen overburden (about 2/3 of the physical cost, about 11 l diesel/g), with a target of 1.5-2x lower cost per oz rather than 3x? | CHANGES: target (3x has NO ROOM; ceiling 4.5x, and 3x needs 10x cuts in five terms), sub-question (the season and earthmoving link) | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: margins per oz are large; one search gap remains, dredging a talik under lake ice.

**REFRAME 3:** How can the cost per tonne (or per delivered GJ) of moving Yakutian export coal to Pacific tidewater be cut 10x, together with processing and capital, at sub-rail volumes? | CHANGES: scope narrowed to the single dominant term (55%); unit allowed to be energy rather than tonnes | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: rated TIGHT, since 3x needs three terms each cut 10x; a 10x cut of outbound logistics alone gives 1.98x.

**REFRAME 4:** How can the Far-North capital premium per annual tonne of capacity (1.3-2x global for Cu, 2-3x construction cost) be removed for Cu-Au and lithium projects, with a 1.5-2x target? | CHANGES: target and lever (capex per unit of capacity, not extraction physics) | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: low | WHY: capital is 41-42% of cost, but even a 10x cut gives only about 1.6x.

**REFRAME 5:** How can small East-Siberian oil fields (about 0.2 Mt/yr) remove the ~300 km trucking leg to ESPO, accepting at most about 1.2x from engineering? | CHANGES: target, scope (one link) | IMPORTANCE: low | POSSIBILITY: low | WHY: fiscal take is 53% of cost, so 3x is impossible even if every physical term falls 10x.

**REFRAME 6:** Across commodities, how can a link that today runs 45-150 days a year (ice road, dredge, thaw, barge) be made to work year-round at unchanged unit cost, measured as cost per unit of final product? | CHANGES: sub-question (the seasonal window as the shared constraint) | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: the lessons show the season binds more than distance; this is useful only when tied to one product path, as in Reframes 1 and 2.
