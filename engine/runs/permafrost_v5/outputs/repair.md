All four anchors were kept. One idea works under conditions and three are niches; none is several times cheaper across the whole chain except Idea 1 at the right site. Two limits on the evidence:
- **Unsourced inputs:** the delivered price of imported coal (15–23k rub/t) and several unit costs (tanker day rate, equipment, ice road, boilerhouse capital) are my assumptions and are flagged as such in `NOTES.md`.
- **Sources read only through search summaries:** the proxy blocked direct downloads, so I never opened the primary papers.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Scrape-thawed river-bluff lignite
MECHANISM: Anchor: the river has already exposed the seam, so it is mined by natural thaw only, with no drilling, blasting or overburden stripping. Repair: scrape a 0.1 m thawed layer off the bluff face every 1–3 days. Each scrape restarts the thaw, so the face thaws 3–7 cm/day, or 2.7–5.6 m per 85-day season, against 0.9–1.35 m when the face is left alone (a 1951 placer-mining paper reports about 10 cm/day on a freshly exposed surface). The best regime is one bluff with a seam at least 6 m thick, supplying 20 kt/yr to several villages from about 850 m of face. It works only above high water, behind a berm and settling sump, and the whole face has to retreat together so that no frozen overhang forms.
VALUE: About 3,200 rub/t of lignite, or 1,390 rub per useful Gcal. That is 2.8–4.3x cheaper than imported hard coal at 3,850–5,900 rub/Gcal, but only 1.0–1.5x cheaper than a conventional micro-pit on the same deposit, and 1.3–2.0x if one village is served alone.

IDEA 2
NAME: Fuel on empty coal-barge legs
MECHANISM: Anchor: one Kolyma flow rides on the other flow's empty leg. The data reverse the direction. Only about 3 kt of dry cargo goes upstream against 165 kt of coal coming down, so at most 1.8% of the coal could ride on returning barges. The ~165 coal-barge legs going back upstream empty are the real spare capacity. About 17 kt/yr of Northern Delivery fuel would ride those legs in 42 t collapsible fuel bladders, with the coal hold acting as spill containment. That needs 17 of the 165 legs, about 100 bladders and 24 per shoal-limited 1,000 t leg.
VALUE: About 2,100 rub/t for the river leg against 2,700–12,000 rub/t for a dedicated 38–54-year-old tanker (1.3–5.7x). That is under 10% of the delivered diesel price. The real value is not having to replace the tanker fleet.

IDEA 3
NAME: Pre-staged flood-crest barges
MECHANISM: Anchor: a loaded barge waits out the ice drift in a sheltered backwater and leaves on the flood crest. Repair: load it in autumn while it is still afloat at a deep berth, so nothing is loaded through ice. It winters in a backwater at least 2.5 m deep, and it is used on a tributary that is navigable only on the spring flood, where there is just one trip a year anyway. With a modelled 12-day recession, a convoy arriving 5–10 days late loses 35–62% of its load. A barge pre-staged at the river mouth goes in full.
VALUE: About 3,100 rub/t against 4,700–11,300 rub/t on that leg today (1.5–3.6x). On the Kolyma main stem the gain is only about 14%.

IDEA 4
NAME: Self-heated barge boilerhouse
MECHANISM: Anchor: the coal barge is itself the village boilerhouse and coal yard, frozen in at the berth. Repair: drop the bubbler. A covered 1 m moat is kept open with the boiler's own spare low-grade heat. It needs about 8 kW against roughly 480 kW of flue loss from a 3 MW boiler (0.3%), or 36 kW if the moat is left uncovered. The hull should sit in a backwater, not in the main channel. It pays best where the shore boilerhouse has to be replaced anyway.
VALUE: A net saving of 0.8–3.4k rub/t out of 15–23k delivered, about 1.04–1.3x cheaper.

=== NOTES ===
**Idea 1:** anchor kept.
- **Numbers:**
  - Thaw rate comes from a heat-conduction model: coal conductivity 0.33–0.5 W/mK (Herrin & Deming 1996), 25–35% moisture assumed, surface heat capped at 110 W/m².
  - The thaw rate is cross-checked against the 4 inches/day placer figure.
  - Zyryanka-scale micro-pit literature gives 20–50 kt/yr.
- **Baselines:**
  - Imported delivered coal: 2.8–4.3x.
  - Conventional micro-pit on the same deposit: 1.0–1.5x.
- **Consistency:** the thaw window holds in 10 of 16 parameter cases. It fails at 35% moisture with 0.2 m layers. The overburden must be no more than the seam thickness. River turbidity and the freezing of wet stockpiled coal into lumps are not yet costed.
- **Hazards:** bluff collapse, fine coal washing into the river, self-heating of stockpiled lignite.
- **Deciding unknown:** the thaw rate of frozen lignite under repeated scraping. The test is one summer on a 10 × 3 m panel, scraping every 1, 2 and 4 days, about 30 cycles each. A desk count of seams near villages on geological maps should come with it.
- **Closest work:** natural layer-by-layer thaw stripping in placer mining, Alaska village-coal exploration, Yakutian micro-pit proposals.
- **Verdict:** WORKS, conditional on finding the sites.

**Idea 2:** anchor kept as a principle, with the direction reversed.
- **Numbers:** coal flow 160–175 kt/yr; upstream fuel 13 kt delivered = three quarters of the 2025 plan. Large dry-cargo ships have not entered the Kolyma since 2019.
- **Baselines:** a dedicated old tanker, or a new shallow tanker (about 2,700 rub/t in capital alone).
- **Consistency:** the load, the season (23 May–10 Oct) and the number of legs all fit.
- **Hazards and deciding unknown:** abrasion of the bladders and approval to carry dangerous cargo this way from the Russian River Register. That approval, plus the operator's real tanker costs, decides the idea. The test is a 5-bladder trial on 2 legs.
- **Verdict:** NICHE.

**Idea 3:** anchor kept.
- **Numbers:** the Kolyma peaks in June at 25,100 m³/s against a 2,220 m³/s mean. The tributary recession shape is assumed, and it decides the result.
- **Consistency:** a loaded hull frozen in needs a backwater at least 2.5 m deep at freeze-up.
- **Hazards:** losses during ice drift (the Lena sinkings and the Indigirka breakaway).
- **Experiment:** a desk study of 10 or more years of Roshydromet gauge records around ice-out, then one pre-staged barge for one season.
- **Verdict:** NICHE.

**Idea 4:** anchor kept, and the physics objection is removed.
- **Numbers:** open-water heat loss of 250 W/m² in calm air (polynya measurements); bottom water at 0.01–0.2 °C.
- **Hazards:**
  - Self-heating and CO in the hold.
  - The hull sinking at its berth during ice drift.
  - A flexible heat pipe to shore.
  - Its legal status as a hazardous facility on a vessel.
- **Deciding unknown:** avoided boilerhouse capital against the extra classification and running costs. The test is a quoted bill of materials plus a one-winter trial of a 20 m covered, heated trench.
- **Closest work:** Akademik Lomonosov and heat barges. No coal-fired, self-stored, frozen-in barge boilerhouse was found.
- **Verdict:** NICHE.
