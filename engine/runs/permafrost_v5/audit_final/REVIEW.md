=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Channel-Talik Seam Dredging
DEFECT | FATAL | The Kovin benchmark doesn't support the rate: a 150 kW cutter can't match a dredge with a 750 kW cutter, and scaled by cutter power it gives about 13 t/h, not 46–65 t/h. | Kovin produced 4,400,863 t over 66,056 h, which is 66.6 t/h ([Rudnik Kovin 2024](https://scindeks-clanci.ceon.rs/data/pdf/proc-0026/2024/proc-00262401206F.pdf)). Kovin I has a 750 kW cutter and 10,090 kW installed ([DredgePoint](https://dredgepoint.org/dredging-database/equipment/kovin-i)). That is 0.089 t/h per kW, so 150 kW gives 13 t/h. At that rate 10 kt takes 32 days of nonstop cutting, or 53 days at 60% utilisation.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The season doesn't fit: 4 bends at 8–25 days each is 32–100 days, but cutting is limited to the 61 days of June–July. | Even at the claimed rate it works out to 2–4 bends, and at 13 t/h it is about 1 bend. Sharing the dredge is what gives the 730–930 figure, so that figure falls.
DEFECT | MAJOR | June–July is when Yakutian rivers are in their spring and summer flood, so the thalweg seam is at its deepest and the current is fastest just when cutting is planned. | The design states no dredging-depth or current limit for the leased dredge.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Cutter-suction dredging produces a very dilute slurry, so the hopper barge has to decant 230–360 m³/h of water, and lignite fines leave with the overflow. Neither fines loss nor turbidity is in the cost. | Kovin's slurry is 85–90% water ([Kovin](https://www.gem.wiki/Kovin_Coal_Mine)). Cutting in the riverbed in June–July is also during fish spawning, which needs fisheries approval under the Russian Water Code.
DEFECT | MINOR | The comparison uses Gcal in the fuel, not useful heat. Wet lignite at 32% moisture burns less efficiently in small village grates than hard coal does. | 15,000/2,830 means 5.3 Gcal/t for imported coal against 3.67 Gcal/t for the dredged lignite. Its 32% moisture also freezes stockpiles into lumps that need crushing in winter.
SURVIVES | 2,700–4,300 rub/t ÷ 3.67 Gcal/t = 736–1,172 rub/Gcal, and the 3.0–3.9x ratio, both reproduce. 10 kt at 46–65 t/h is 6.4–9.1 days at 100% uptime.
RATIO | 0.9 | After the dredge-rate correction, roughly 1,600–2,000 rub/Gcal against a bank micro-pit's 1,000–2,200.
KNOWN | partly | Kovin mine in Serbia has dredged lignite underwater since 1989 at about 200 kt/yr ([GEM](https://www.gem.wiki/Kovin_Coal_Mine)).
NEW FACT | Kovin I installed power is 10,090 kW, with a 45–60 m dredging depth | [DredgePoint](https://dredgepoint.org/dredging-database/equipment/kovin-i)

IDEA 2: Barge-Mounted Bluff Auger Coal
DEFECT | FATAL | The 60 t/h rate and 62% recovery are assumed, and the later repair (Idea 8) of this same rig puts it at 21–38 t/h and 30–50% field recovery. | At 21.5 t/h and the same 56% utilisation the rig makes 28.7 kt/yr. Annual cost of 1,800 × 80 kt = 144 M rub then gives about 5,000 rub/t, against 2,800–3,700 for a pit.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The frame has to be unbolted, the barge moved and the frame re-bolted 4.2 times a day, to a new hole 2.14 m along the face. That stepping time isn't in the cycle. | 421 holes a year over 100 days. Each hole is 80 m, so the auger string also has to be pulled every 5.7 h.
DEFECT | MAJOR | There is no margin on stage: the frame has 3 m of travel and the stage drops 1–3 m in a campaign, before allowing for waves or wind setup. | 3 m of travel against a 3 m drop.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Augering at water level undercuts a 20–30 m frozen bluff, and the barge sits right under the face. Rockfall and block collapse are a crew hazard that isn't costed. | Webs are about 0.6 m against 1.53 m holes, so the tributary-area stress on the webs is about 3.5 times the overburden stress, and the webs thaw from the warm air in the holes.
DEFECT | MINOR | Frozen coal is stronger than the unfrozen coal behind the historical 21.5 t/h, which pushes the rate down further. | The rate isn't sourced for frozen coal.
SURVIVES | Hole size is consistent: 190 t per 80 m hole means a 1.53 m auger. 80 kt/yr at 60 t/h means 56% utilisation over the 100-day season.
RATIO | 0.6 | About 5,000 rub/t against 2,800–3,700 for a bank pit.
KNOWN | partly | Auger and highwall mining from a bench is standard. I found no floating version.

IDEA 3: Self-heated barge boilerhouse
DEFECT | FATAL | The gain is 1.04–1.3x, nowhere near the "several times cheaper" the brief requires. | 15/(15–0.8) = 1.06, and 15/(15–3.4) = 1.29.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The uncovered-moat heat is understated by 1.3–3x. | Open water in winter loses 250–575 W/m² ([Makinson Inlet](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/arctic/article/view/65922), Mertz). On about 190 m² of moat that is 48–110 kW, not 36 kW.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A backwater berth has to stay deeper than ice plus draft, and the heat-network link has to ride the spring stage rise and ice jams. If the barge fails, the village loses its only heat source in mid-winter. | Mean end-of-April ice is 1.42 m on the Kolyma, 1.52 m on the Yana and 1.67 m on the Indigirka ([AARI](https://old.aari.ru/resources/f0021/2016_spring.pdf)). With a 2 m draft that needs 3.4–3.7 m of water.
DEFECT | MINOR | A boilerhouse on a vessel has to be registered both with the River Register and as a hazardous production facility, and that cost isn't included. |
SURVIVES | 8 kW / 3 MW = 0.27%, and 480 kW is 16% flue loss, which is plausible.
RATIO | 1.05 | The idea's own savings, less the heat-network link.
KNOWN | partly | Floating power and heat plants exist, e.g. Akademik Lomonosov at Pevek, 70 MWe.

IDEA 4: Pre-staged flood-crest barges
DEFECT | MAJOR | 2.5 m of backwater is not enough to winter a loaded barge. | Mean ice is 1.42–1.67 m, plus about 1.3–2 m of loaded draft, which needs 2.7–3.7 m. The barge freezes to the bed or sits on it, and the winter drop in stage makes it worse.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A frozen-in loaded barge in a backwater at break-up is exposed to ice jams. The loss risk and insurance, plus the cost of capital in barge and cargo idle for 7–8 months, aren't costed. | No loss probability or capital charge is given.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 35–62% loss for a convoy 5–10 days late rests on a modelled 12-day recession, not a measured hydrograph. | Not sourced.
SURVIVES | The 1.5–3.6x arithmetic reproduces, and the idea concedes a 14% gain on the Kolyma main stem.
RATIO | 1.3 | After lost hulls, capital tied up and cutting the barge out of the ice.
KNOWN | partly | Wintering loaded vessels in backwaters for a spring high-water run is, as far as I know, a long-standing practice on Yakutia's small rivers. My search didn't confirm it.

IDEA 5: Wintering Coal CHP Barge
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is wrong: the barge runs only in winter, so the diesel plant, its staff and the backup boiler house all stay. The right comparison is fuel-only diesel cost, about 30–47 rub/kWh, not the full 45–88. | 0.33–0.36 l/kWh at 90–130 rub/l.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Round-the-clock crew for a coal boiler plus ORC isn't costed. | About 10 people at Arctic wages adds about 17 rub/kWh on 1.4 GWh a year.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 1,780 t runs a 2.34 MW fuel input for only 147–195 days, while the Arctic heating season is 270–300 days. | 1,780 t × 4,000–5,300 kcal/kg ÷ 2.34 MW.
DEFECT | MINOR | Rejecting heat at district-heating temperature lowers the electric efficiency of a 0.3 MWe ORC below 16%. | The efficiency is assumed, not sourced.
SURVIVES | These reproduce: fuel input 2.34 MW, reject heat 1.575 MW, avoided boiler fuel 2.42 MW (so net extra fuel ≈ 0), 12.7 rub/kWh, 381 m³ of ash at 15% ash and 0.7 t/m³, and capital cost of 13.8–24.7 rub/kWh.
RATIO | 1.0 | 31–49 rub/kWh with staff, against 30–47 rub/kWh fuel-only diesel.
KNOWN | partly | Thermal-oil ORC CHP is commercial (Turboden), and floating power plants exist (Akademik Lomonosov).

IDEA 6: Fuel on empty coal-barge legs
DEFECT | MAJOR | Sea tankers reach the Kolyma mouth only after the ice clears, in July–August, so the upstream fuel window is about 50–60 days of falling water. Each bladder needs about 4 round trips of 12–15 days. | 4 × 12–15 = 48–60 days. Zelyony Mys navigation averages 85 days ([Ruwiki](https://ru.ruwiki.ru/wiki/Зелёный_Мыс_(порт))).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Under the River Register, carrying oil in flexible tanks in a dry-cargo coal hold needs a fire and oil-cargo class. Coal residue can puncture the bladders, and partly filled bladders have a free-surface effect. None of this is costed. | No compliance cost is given.
DEFECT | MINOR | The best real competitor is the existing, already-depreciated tanker at the low end, 2,700 rub/t, not the 12,000 rub/t replacement figure. | 2,700/2,100 = 1.29.
SURVIVES | 3/165 = 1.8%, 17,000/42 = 405 bladder-trips = 17 legs × 24, and 24 × 42 = 1,008 t.
RATIO | 1.2 | Against the existing tanker, after bladder handling.
KNOWN | partly | Flexible fuel tanks rated to –60 °C are sold for northern supply ([bizon](https://bizon.ru/news/print/id/398842/format/smoothbox)).
NEW FACT | Zyryansky coal output was about 250 kt in 2021 | [GEM](https://www.gem.wiki/Ziryansky_Coal_Mine)

IDEA 7: Ice-Deck Borehole Talik Mining
DEFECT | FATAL | The 86–89% extraction, the 20–40 m³/h airlift and the 9 m jet reach in gravel up to 15 cm are all unproven. The only permafrost gold borehole-miner trial recovered 7 g in total, and clogging and boulders stopped it. | [USBM prototype](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/219378)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 57 t parked limit leaves no room for the surface load: about 30 m³ of wet tailings weighs 57 t, and each hole draws 2,200–2,500 m³ over 59–118 h. | Gold's formula reproduces, but stationary loads are only good for about 2 h before microcracking ([Barrette/TAC](https://tac-atc.ca/wp-content/uploads/barrette.pdf)).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The cost only works if the gravel holds 1.3–1.6 g of recovered gold per m³ of pay, and that grade is assumed. | Derived from 1,130–1,970 rub/m³ ÷ 3,000–4,800 rub/g, times 3.4–3.9.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A plume of mud under the lake ice in an oxygen-poor lake is a Water Code and fisheries showstopper. | Not addressed.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Coarse-gold nugget effect makes per-hole recovery far too variable to confirm 86–89% from a few test holes. | No sample size is proposed.
SURVIVES | Gold's formula numbers reproduce: 113 t short-term and 50 t at 1.2 m.
RATIO | 1.0 | If recovery halves, the cost rises to about 6,000–9,600 rub/g, against ≥8,800 for a pontoon dredge.
KNOWN | partly | USBM's 1997 borehole miner, and Arens et al. 2019 on borehole hydraulic mining of buried placers ([Gornyi Zhurnal](https://rudmet.ru/journal/1793/article/30642)).

IDEA 8: Frozen-bluff auger coal, shallow bunker hulls
DEFECT | FATAL | By the idea's own numbers it is 0.82x against the best competent alternative, a bank pit with the same hulls, so it loses money. | 0.67–0.98x, as stated in the text.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A hull wintering as a bunker on a shoaling river freezes to the bottom and is then hit by spring ice jams. | Ice 1.52–1.67 m plus 1.26 m draft needs 2.8–2.9 m of water, against 1.5–2.0 m low-water depth.
DEFECT | MINOR | The 5.9 days per hull doesn't match the reviewed rate: 21 t/h at 75% fills a hull in 5.3 days, and 5.9 days means 16–19 t/h. | 25 kt needs 74 of the 60–90 effective days.
DEFECT | MINOR | Frozen coal that thaws in the hold for 8–26 days and then refreezes sets into a mass that stokers have to dig out at –40 °C. | Not costed.
SURVIVES | 25/205 = 12%, and the 0.96 m rise means about 2,083 m² of waterplane, which is consistent with a 2,000 t hull drawing 1.26 m. The 0 °C crossing around 21 September is plausible for the lower Yana and Indigirka.
RATIO | 0.75 | The idea's 0.82, less the wintering risk.
KNOWN | partly | Same as Idea 2.

IDEA 9: Scrape-thawed river-bluff lignite
DEFECT | FATAL | "The whole face retreats together" means the overburden above the seam has to be thawed and removed as well, so there is stripping after all. | A 20–30 m bluff over a 6 m seam has 14–24 m of overburden, which is 2.3–4 m³ per m³ of coal, or 46–80 k m³ a year.
DEFECT | MAJOR | At the low end of the thaw range the face doesn't deliver 20 kt. | 850 × 6 × 2.7 × 1.25 = 17.2 kt. It needs ≥3.14 m of thaw a season.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Thawed overburden slumping down the face mixes waste into the coal, and coal exposed at the face is weathered, so its heating value drops. | Not in the cost.
DEFECT | MINOR | The thaw range doesn't reproduce. | 3–7 cm/day × 85 days = 2.55–5.95 m, not 2.7–5.6. The heat needed, about 72 W/m² for 5 cm/day, is plausible on a south-facing bluff.
SURVIVES | 3,200/1,390 = 2.30 useful Gcal/t, and 2.8–4.2x against imported coal.
RATIO | 0.8 | Against a micro-pit on the same deposit, once overburden retreat is counted.
KNOWN | partly | Thawing and scraping off thawed layers in successive passes is standard in Kolyma placer stripping, which is where the 1951 paper comes from.

**Ranking, strongest to weakest:** 6, 4, 3, 5, 7, 1, 9, 2, 8. None of the 9 is "several times" cheaper than the best current practice once corrected. The best is about 1.3x, and four come out below 1x.
