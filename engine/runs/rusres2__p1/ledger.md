KIND: cost

The shares below are my own estimates. No source publishes a cost split for these sites. The anchors are the facts quoted in each line. The annualisation assumes 14% and 25 years, which gives a capital recovery factor of about 0.145. The 14% is the key rate in Fact 229.

ARCHETYPE A: Baimskaya copper-gold. Concentrate goes by an 800 km road and the Nagleynyn/Pevek port, with four floating nuclear units for power. | cost: capex $8bn for 300 kt Cu and 490 koz Au a year (Facts 12, 73, 277).
- Annualised capex is about $1.16bn a year, or about $3,900/t Cu before any gold credit and before operating cost (my calculation).
- External infrastructure is about $0.28bn a year, or about $900/t Cu. It is the $0.6bn road and port plus power of about $1.3bn (318 MW at $4,000/kW, Facts 151–152).
- Operating cost per tonne is not published.
- Time: end-2027 first production, already slipped about a year. That is more than 6 years from the 2021 feasibility study, against 4–5 years at Natalka.
- Power: the units are due in 2028–30, so the 2026–30 gap is uncovered. The Chaun-Bilibino node has about 8 MW of winter spare, and demand is 251 MW (Facts 311, 352).
- Port: the navigation window is 3–5 months (Facts 147–148). Red Dog (Alaska) ships on a 122-day window and holds 950 kt of stockpile (Fact 167).
- Foundations: permafrost is 150–280 m thick, continuous, with thaw zones under rivers (Fact 103). Pile bearing capacity is falling (Facts 89–90).

LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes about 40% of the cost.

ARCHETYPE B: Natalka (Magadan), a large open-pit gold mine on the Kolyma hydro grid with a road. This is the benchmark. | cost: total cash cost (TCC) $490/oz in 2023, $452/oz in H2 2023 (Fact 66).
- During ramp-up, all-in sustaining cost (AISC) was $1,143/oz in Q3 2018 (Fact 69).
- Time: first dore in Dec 2017 at 50% of design rate, full rate in 2019 (Fact 287).
- Recovery: group recovery is about 82–83% (Fact 67). A flotation circuit was planned for 2025.
- Cost drivers: lower grade, and mineral extraction tax (MET) rising from 1.2% to 2.4% (Fact 66).
- Kupol/Dvoinoye could not be costed per ounce, because the ounce count is missing (Fact 70).

SHARES: processing 22% | labour 20% | capital 18% | inbound logistics 12% | energy 10% | outbound logistics 1% | other (MET, royalty) 17%

LARGEST: processing - a 10x cut of it removes about 20% of the cost.

ARCHETYPE C: Firm power for an off-grid Chukotka mine in 2026–30. Diesel, plus wind or hybrid where possible, until the nuclear units arrive. | cost: diesel at 10–40 RUB/kWh, over 25 RUB/kWh in Taimyr (Fact 157).
- Reference prices for alternatives: floating nuclear about $4,000/kW and about 2× diesel per kWh by one analyst (Fact 152).
- Microreactors: $0.25–0.54/kWh, and no built units exist (Fact 334).
- Wind covers only about 10% of mine power at Diavik and Raglan, at a 8-year payback (Facts 169–170).
- Russia's Tiksi wind-diesel plant ran at −42 °C (Fact 159).
- Bilibino stopped in Dec 2025, and Akademik Lomonosov covered 84% of node demand in early 2026 (Fact 354).
- Ust-Kuyga (Yakutia) moved to 2030 (Fact 156).

SHARES: energy (fuel) 50% | inbound logistics (fuel delivery) 20% | capital 12% | labour 5% | processing (maintenance) 3% | outbound logistics 0% | other 10%

LARGEST: energy - a 10x cut of it removes about 45% of the cost.

ARCHETYPE D: Tomtor rare earths (Rosneft), a pilot in 2027 with river access over about 400 km. | cost: not published. No capex or opex was found (Facts 81, 18).
- The ore is fine-grained with 50–60% slimes, so ordinary beneficiation does not work (Fact 16).
- The reported grade is 4.5–6.7% Nb2O5 and 10–12.5% rare-earth oxide, and the two sources conflict (Facts 17, 224).
- The licence runs to 2034 (Fact 18).

SHARES (guess): capital 40% | energy 15% | labour 10% | inbound logistics 10% | outbound logistics 10% | processing 10% | other 5%

LARGEST: capital - a 10x cut of it removes about 36% of the cost.
