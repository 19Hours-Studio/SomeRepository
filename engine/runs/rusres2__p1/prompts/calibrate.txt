Cases: Baimskaya copper-gold (Pevek/Nagleynyn route, Rosatom floating power units 2028–30); Chukotka and Magadan hard-rock and placer gold (Kupol, Natalka, Kolyma placers); Tomtor rare earths (Rosneft, pilot 2027, Anabar/Lena river access).
A good answer: (a) full cost per oz Au or per t Cu or rare-earth oxide delivered, including annualised external-infrastructure capex; (b) years from investment decision to first production, against Natalka (about 4–5 years, then 2 to full rate) and Baimskaya (end-2027 target, already slipped); (c) recovery, operating days per year, and life of tailings, dams and foundations in discontinuous or warm permafrost.

IDEA 1
NAME: Diesel-sized first line, nuclear expansion
MECHANISM: Anchor: a diesel-sized starter on leased domestic or unrestricted gensets comes first. The nuclear-sized expansion follows when the floating units deliver, and the gensets then move to other sites. Repaired design: the starter is no longer a separate 6 Mt/y plant. That version still fails: $8.3k/t Cu net against $2.0k/t for the full plan, and a $45m a year margin on $1.08bn of capex. Instead, line 1 of the Baimskaya plant now under construction (start planned for end-2028) runs at a throughput sized to 80 MW of leased gensets. It uses the SAG mill and one of two ball mills at a coarser grind, on the highest-grade benches. That gives 15 Mt/y of ore at about 0.51% Cu (the first-decade head implied by the feasibility study), 67 kt Cu and 98 koz Au a year. It burns 117 kt of diesel a year at $0.30/kWh, which takes 6 tanker calls into Pevek and needs 174k m³ of tankage. With fuel coming in and concentrate going out on the same trucks, that is about 7,000 loads a year: 20 a day on the all-season road, or 78 a day on a winter road alone. Net cash cost is $1.95k/t Cu after gold credit. Line 1 is also commissioned on diesel, so the ramp-up is done before the reactors arrive.
VALUE: Each year the floating units slip, the bridge earns about $0.52bn of cash margin ($0.29bn at Cu $8k / Au $2.8k) from a plant whose $8bn would otherwise cost about $1.2bn a year to carry idle. Waiting idle earns nothing in those years.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The diesel figure breaks the energy balance. 117 kt/y at 80 MW (700.8 GWh) implies 0.167 kg/kWh, which no diesel genset achieves; leased units run 0.20–0.25. Correcting it gives either 140–175 kt/y (+20–50% fuel, tankage, tanker calls and road loads) or only 53–67 MW average, which is about 11–14 Mt/y at the stated 46.7 kWh/t. (python)
- MAJOR: The road load count does not reproduce. 268 kt of concentrate at 25% Cu plus 117 kt of diesel over 7,000 loads means 55 t per load. Concentrate can't ride in fuel tankers unless a swap system (ISO tank plus concentrate box) is designed and costed. Separate loads come to about 10,600 a year, or 11,500 at 22% Cu. Consumables add about 50 kt (about 1,250 loads). On a winter road only, that is 118–140 loads/day, near the TCWR record rate. (python)
- MAJOR: Recovery is assumed. The text gets 87.6% Cu recovery (67/76.5 kt) from a coarser grind than design, on a SAG mill built for a 35 Mt/y line running at 15 Mt/y. Each 4 points of lost recovery removes about 3 kt Cu and about $30m/y of margin. The 0.51% head is not sourced. So the years of margin per slip could be 0–2, while gensets, about 174k m³ of tankage and its Arctic tank-farm capex (unpriced; my rough guess $150–250m) are paid up front. IDEA 2
NAME: Kupol winter-road toll hub
MECHANISM: Anchor: third-party high-grade lodes are developed as mine-only satellites. Their ore goes by winter road to the Kupol mill, which runs on its own diesel plant, so it needs none of the 8 MW the Chaun–Bilibino node has spare. Repaired design: haul radius ≤200 km, head grade ≥4 g/t, and at most 0.5 Mt per winter season per corridor. That cap comes from 12,500 loads in a 75-day season, about 167 a day, which matches the Tibbitt–Contwoyto record. Each satellite runs a domestic RADOS X-ray sorter and ships only the sorted fraction (assumed 65% of mass, 93% of the gold). The first target is Kekura's second-stage ore (7.5 g/t, 160 km west of Kupol). Kekura's feasibility study assumed grid power from Bilibino, which has now closed. Ore is stockpiled frozen at Kupol and milled all year. Haul costs $0.22/t-km, or $177/oz at 7.5 g/t. Avoiding a satellite mill, tailings dam and power plant saves $322/oz. The extra milling adds only about 1.7 MW and 3.4 kt a year of diesel at Kupol.
VALUE: Full cost is $863/oz delivered against $1,018/oz for a stand-alone Kekura-type mill (0.85×), and first gold comes about 2 years after decision against about 4 for the stand-alone mill and 4–5 for Natalka. At 300 km the advantage almost disappears ($20–45/oz).
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The first target is wrong. Kekura is not a greenfield site waiting on a mill or on Bilibino power: Highland Gold started its own 0.8 Mt/y mill there in March 2023 (about 85% design recovery), and a branch of the 235 km Bilibino–Peschanka line now powers it, replacing its diesel. So the $322/oz saving is mostly spent already, and the $1,018/oz stand-alone baseline does not exist. ([biznes-gazeta 29.03.2023](https://biznes-gazeta.ru/?id=location.view&obj=905697ad890a3743bf6bd5e1bb4996b8), [zolteh](https://zolteh.ru/news/fabrika_na_mestorozhdenii_kekura_na_chukotke_gotova_k_zapusku/), [prochukotku](https://prochukotku.ru/news/promyshlennost/mestorozhdenie_kekura_na_chukotke_poluchilo_elektroenergiyu_ot_lep/))
- MAJOR: The sorter step destroys value. Rejecting 7% of the gold costs about $50 per ROM tonne (0.07 × 0.241 oz/t × 0.85 recovery × $3.5k/oz). It saves only about $12/t of haul (0.35 × 160 km × $0.22) plus about $7/t of milling. At the stated 7.5 g/t, a single 7% loss is about $260/oz of revenue foregone, which is more than the $155/oz advantage claimed. (python recompute)
- MAJOR: The cap does not "match the Tibbitt–Contwoyto record". It is 14% more loads and 1.5× the tonnage. The 2007 record was 10,922 loads, 330 kt and about 4,783 t/day in 69 days. The idea needs 12,500 loads, 0.5 Mt and 6,667 t/day in 75 days. It also assumes 40 t loads against about 30–37 t on the TCWR (2018: 304,685 t in 8,300 loads, 36.7 t/load). And it runs overland through mountains, not mostly on lake ice. ([JVTC poster](https://jvtcwinterroad.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021-Winter-Road-Poster.pdf), [NNSL](https://nnsl.com/nwtnewsnorth/winter-road-haul-amounts-to-257176-tonnes-7260726))
- MAJOR: The 75-day window is assumed, not measured for a Kekura–Kupol route. The reference road averages 67 days. A warm winter in 2026 shut it early. At 67 days and 37 t loads, the corridor carries about 0.4 Mt, roughly 20% below the cap. ([Skillings](https://skillings.net/arctic-logistics-crisis-record-warmth-shuts-diamond-mine-ice-roads), [Wikipedia TCWR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibbitt_to_Contwoyto_Winter_Road))
- SURVIVES: 1.7 MW and 3.4 kt/y diesel: about 0.228 kg/kWh, about 30 kWh/t at 0.5 Mt. Arithmetic: 167 loads/day, 40 t/load, 0.863/1.018 = 0.85. Thawing frozen ore adds only about 0.7 MW thermal on average.
- RATIO: ≈0.85 | Against the best current practice, Kekura's own grid-fed mill whose capex is already spent (about $700–800/oz). The idea comes to about $890/oz: $863 plus the sorter loss. For a truly mill-less satellite under the tonnage cap it is at best about 0.95.
- KNOWN: partly | Kupol has milled Dvoinoye ore trucked about 100 km since 2013. Highland Gold has owned both Kupol and Kekura since 2022. | [Kinross 40-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/701818/000110465922041156/kgc-20211231xex99d1.htm), [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/business/833256)
