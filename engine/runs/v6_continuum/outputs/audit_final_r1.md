=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: N6 SRAM chiplets, package-wide tensor stages
DEFECT | MAJOR | The headline 1,198 tok/s/user rests on a 1 µs all-reduce across 32 dies on an organic substrate, and that figure has no source; with the claimed 4.7 layers per stage there are about 9.4 all-reduces per stage, so speed is very sensitive to it. | Recomputed: at 2 µs the result is 1,006 tok/s (799 at 50% MAC efficiency); at 3 µs it is 866 (709); at 5 µs, a typical small-message GPU-collective latency, it is 678 (578). Each all-reduce moves about 63 KB per die (2 tokens × 8,192 × 2 B, ring), and the largest organic multi-chiplet parts today (EPYC-class, about 17 chiplets) are not this fabric.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The claim of being 2.6–5.6× below Groq and Cerebras compares a 4.5-bit service with list prices for services that run at higher precision (Cerebras serves 70B at 16-bit, about 140 GB of weights against 39.7 GB here). It also leaves out the current best practice for SRAM decode, Nvidia's Groq 3 LPX rack: 256 × LP30, 500 MB and 150 TB/s per chip, 128 GB of SRAM per rack, shipping in H2 2026. | The weight-capacity gap is 3.6×, which takes most of the claimed advantage. One LPX rack holds the whole 67 GB working set that this design spreads over 544 dies in 17 packages.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Capacity, and therefore cost per token, is sized only for 32 users at 8K context. Llama-3.3-70B supports 128K, and KV cache is 92 KB per token at 4.5 bits. | Recomputed: 64 GB / 514 dies at 8K; 136 GB / 1,097 dies at 32K (2.1×); 426 GB / 3,431 dies at 128K (6.7×). $/Mtok scales almost linearly with die and package count.
DEFECT | MINOR | The "916 at 50% MAC" label implies that 1,198 is the 100% case, but 37.7 µs per stage already assumes 70% MAC efficiency. | Recomputed: 162.7 GFLOP/token → 26.4 µs per stage at 100%, 37.7 µs at 70%, 52.8 µs at 50%.
DEFECT | MINOR | The design has no ECC or redundancy budget for 0.54 Tb of SRAM in one pipeline, where a single upset or a single die failure stalls the whole model instance. | At 100–1,000 FIT/Mb that is one upset every 1.8–18 h. SECDED 72/64 would cost 12.5% of capacity, about 612 dies instead of 544.
SURVIVES | 124 MB per die (14.2 Mb/mm² is the 36 mm² Zen 4 V-Cache die; the Zen 3 die at 41 mm² would give 12.5 Mb/mm² and 109 MB). 22.7 TF per die. 67 GB in 544 dies across 17 stages. 1,198 and 916 tok/s reproduce at the stated inputs. Capex per GB of SRAM is $1.8–4.0k against $45–68k for CS-3 (≥11×), so "13×" holds.
RATIO | ≈0.35 | By the idea's own numbers it costs $0.14–0.23/Mtok against about $0.07 for GB300 at 50 tok/s (that GB300 figure is from the material and I have not verified it). Adding ECC and a realistic all-reduce makes it worse. At matched ~1,000 tok/s speed there is no verified cost baseline (LPX price unknown).
KNOWN | partly | d-Matrix Corsair is SRAM-first 6 nm chiplets; the vendor claims 30k tok/s per rack at 2 ms/token on Llama3-70B. Groq LPU/LP30 and the Nvidia LPX rack are the other close systems (d-Matrix; StorageReview / The Register, GTC 2026).
NEW FACT | Groq 3 LP30: 500 MB of SRAM and 150 TB/s per chip, LPX rack of 256 LPUs with 128 GB SRAM and 40 PB/s, shipping H2 2026 | storagereview.com/news/nvidia-groq-3-lpx-everything-we-know ; theregister.com/2026/03/19/nvidia_lpx_deep_dive/

IDEA 2: Cheap-node HBM decode tier
DEFECT | MAJOR | The formula applies the 2.8× package-price ratio to all 91% of non-energy TCO, but host, scale-up network, rack and facility capex do not shrink when the GPU package gets cheaper. | Model: Rubin all-in = $45k package + $20k system + $9.2k facility share + $10k energy (energy share 12%). Idea = $16k + the same rest. Ratio 1.53×, or 1.40× with $5k per unit of amortised NRE. That is below or at the 1.5× target, not 2.7×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Energy ÷2" has no basis. 3 PF of FP8 on N6 draws more than it would on N3, and the HBM power is unchanged. | 3 PF at 0.35–0.64 pJ/op is 1.05–1.92 kW for compute alone. With energy ratio 1.0 the stated formula already falls to 2.42×; at 0.8 it falls to 2.29×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | "3 PF saturates 22 TB/s" holds only for GQA at batch ≥38 tokens per weight read (FP4) or ≥68 (FP8). MLA models, the frontier MoE class, are compute-bound at long context. | 128K attention costs 1.11 TFLOP/token for Kimi K2 and 2.23 for DeepSeek-V3. The N6 part then reaches 56% and 28% of Rubin's (17.5 PF dense FP8) throughput, so cost per token is worse than Rubin for DeepSeek (2.8 × 0.28 ≈ 0.8) and only about 1.6× better for Kimi.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 3 PF FP8 on N6 needs about 3,000–4,000 mm² of logic, or 3.5–4.6 reticles, plus 8 HBM4 stacks. That means a large CoWoS-L interposer, so the part does not escape the CoWoS bottleneck, and "$2k packaging" has no source. | 3,000 / 0.755 TOPS/mm² (A100 density) = 3,974 mm²; 3,000 / 1.0 = 3,000 mm². Reticle limit is 858 mm².
DEFECT | MINOR | The baseline is Nvidia street price against an at-cost build. The fair at-cost comparators are hyperscaler ASICs (TPU, Trainium) or Rubin at volume discount, and the idea admits that most of its margin is markup avoided. | $/TB/s: $2.05k for Rubin street against $0.73k for the idea; the gap closes by whatever the buyer's discount is.
SURVIVES | 1/(0.91/2.8 + 0.09/2) = 2.71. The break-even package price of $30.7k and the ~1.9× margin reproduce at the stated inputs. Energy at about 9–12% of TCO is plausible.
RATIO | ≈1.3 | Whole-chain TCO with non-scaling system and facility costs plus NRE is about 1.4× for GQA, and below 1× for MLA at 128K; I weighted these to about 1.3.
KNOWN | partly | Disaggregated prefill/decode is standard practice (Splitwise, DistServe, Nvidia Dynamo). Nvidia Rubin CPX is the inverse specialisation (a cheap GDDR7 prefill part).
NEW FACT | Rubin TDP is reported as about 2.3 kW (not 1.8), with FP8 dense 17.5 PF and FP4 dense 35 PF | cudocompute.com/products/nvidia-vera-rubin ; gigabyte NVIDIA_2026_1H_V2.pdf (preliminary)
NEW FACT | HBM4 at roughly $500+ per stack, which would be about $4k per 288 GB instead of $12k (single blog, unverified) | techovedas.com/sk-hynix-hbm4-price-hike-is-nvidias-ai-strategy-at-risk/

IDEA 3: N6 SRAM-chiplet pipeline packages
DEFECT | FATAL | 112 GB over 16 × 32 = 512 dies needs 219 MB per 100 mm² die. That is 17.5 Mb/mm² over 100% of the die, leaving no logic area, and 17.5 is above the best measured density of 14.2. | At 14.2 Mb/mm² with 70% of the die as SRAM, 112 GB needs 901 dies (631 even if the die were all SRAM), which is 1.8× the silicon and package count.
DEFECT | FATAL | The value claim "$0.07/Mtok, GB300's cost at 20× the speed" is contradicted by the repaired version of the same design. | Idea 1, at measured density, gives $0.14–0.23/Mtok and states it is 2–4× more expensive than GB300.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Die-by-die layer pipelining halves per-user speed, as the repaired version itself found. | About 1,000 tok/s becomes about 500 tok/s/user.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The design fails its own deciding criterion of ≤$1.5k delivered system cost per GB of SRAM. | Recomputed capex (2× markup, $3–7.5k overhead per package) is $1.8–4.0k/GB.
SURVIVES | 8 KB of activations per token (d_model 8,192 × 1 B FP8). Silicon cost of about $0.13/MB ($10k wafer, about 600 good dies). N6 cost per SRAM bit is 0.39–0.41× that of N3, so "about half" is if anything conservative. Groq's 576-chip 14 nm deployment for 70B matches the public record.
RATIO | ≈0.35 | Corrected to Idea 1's measured-density economics: $0.14–0.23 against about $0.07/Mtok for GB300.
KNOWN | partly | Groq LPU (576 chips, 70B) and d-Matrix Corsair (6 nm SRAM chiplets) are the closest systems.

IDEA 4: Hybrid-bonded DRAM-on-logic decode package
DEFECT | MAJOR | The stated target workload is 1T-total / 32B-active MoE models, and that class is MLA (Kimi K2 matches it exactly; DeepSeek-V3 is close). The idea's own model gives only 1.08–1.29× on MLA. | Recomputed: Kimi K2 (64 heads) costs 1.11 TFLOP/token at 128K. The 2.2–2.7× figure applies only to GQA models outside the stated target.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Putting a ~500 W logic chiplet (1.25 W/mm²; 1.5 W/mm² counting DRAM) directly on DRAM is a thermal showstopper the idea does not mention. DRAM above 85 °C needs 2–4× refresh. | imec's IEDM 2025 3D HBM-on-GPU study measured 141.7 °C unmitigated. Getting to 70.8 °C needed base-die removal, die merging, GPU frequency scaling and double-sided cooling, and frequency scaling cuts the 20 PF.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The memory spec is far beyond any production part. | 160 GB over 16 dies is 80 Gb per die, against 24–32 Gb in HBM3E/HBM4 dies. 16 TB/s per stack is 6–8× HBM4. The closest silicon, d-Matrix Raptor (vendor-reported), reaches 100 TB/s with only 32 GB per card, so this design needs 5× that capacity at a similar footprint.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The tokens/J advantage rests on a self-assumed 3 pJ/bit for HBM, and it uses Rubin's TDP rather than its power during memory-bound decode. | With HBM at 2.0–2.5 pJ/bit and 150 FLOP/B, the tokens/J ratio is 1.5–1.7×; at 300 FLOP/B it is 1.3–1.45× (not 2.3–2.5×).
DEFECT | MAJOR | There is no cost figure, but energy is only about 9–12% of TCO (per Idea 2), and the custom stack yields poorly. | A 2.5× energy gain with equal capex gives ≤1.06× TCO. Composite wafer-on-wafer yield is about 34% (N2 400 mm² at 67%, four DRAM dies at 85% each, four bonds at 99% each).
DEFECT | MINOR | The power budget is already full before I/O is counted. | 1.6 kW FP8 + 410 W DRAM = 2.01 kW, with no allowance for scale-up SerDes or regulator losses. A 1T model needs ≥3.5 packages for weights alone.
SURVIVES | 64 TB/s × 8 × 0.8 pJ = 409.6 W. Bandwidth per kW is 2.62× Rubin at 1.8 kW (3.35× at the reported 2.3 kW TDP). The 1.1 TFLOP MLA figure reproduces for Kimi K2. 0.66–0.88 pJ/bit is a real published 3D-DRAM estimate. 0.08 pJ/FLOP for FP8 on N2 is plausible.
RATIO | ≈1.0 | The power-limited tokens/kW gain is about 1.1–1.3× on the MLA target and about 2.6× on GQA. Whole-chain cost is about 1.0× once capex and yield are counted (no cost was given).
KNOWN | partly | d-Matrix Raptor (Hot Chips 2026): 4 nm logic bonded face-to-face on custom DRAM at 36 µm pitch, >100 TB/s, 32 GB, 0.37 pJ/bit (vendor). Also arXiv 2603.04797 (3D-DRAM LLM accelerator co-design) and imec's HBM-on-GPU work.
