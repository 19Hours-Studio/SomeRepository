**Candidates (P that a typical strong answerer proposes it)**
1. MXFP4/NVFP4 block-scaled weights | P 0.60
2. 2:4 structured sparsity in the MAC array | P 0.40
3. FP8/FP4 KV cache | P 0.40
4. Table-based vector-quantized weights decoded at the SRAM port | P 0.15
5. Lossless entropy coding on top of FP4 | P 0.08
6. Low-rank KV latents with an outlier side-buffer, expanded at point of use | P 0.07
7. MoE experts stored as a shared base plus compressed per-expert deltas | P 0.05
8. Table-free trellis codes whose codebook is regenerated by a hash circuit | P 0.06

I develop 8, 6 and 7. Candidate 5 is dropped because its ceiling is only about 1.1–1.2× at FP4.

**Target and arithmetic**
- **Main target: Archetype C**, the only archetype marked ROOM. Silicon is 60% of its cost and is bought per SRAM byte.
  - A 70B model at FP8 is 70 GB. At 2 bits per weight it is 17.5 GB.
  - That fits on one WSE-3 (44 GB) with 26 GB left for KV. Groq 3 needs about 35 chips instead of 140.
  - A 4× silicon cut lowers total cost by 45%, which is **1.8×**. That is not 3×. Reaching 3× also needs the host and rack term cut.
- **Secondary target: Archetype A** at batch-1 decode on HBM. 22 TB/s ÷ 17.5 GB gives about 1,250 tok/s per user, against about 300 at FP8.
- **What this loses:**
  - Training gains little; weights must be re-encoded after training.
  - Large-batch prefill is compute-bound, so smaller weights do not speed it up.
- **Decoder throughput check:** 150 TB/s at 2 bits per weight is 6×10¹⁴ weights/s. At 0.1 pJ per decode that costs 60 W. At 1 pJ it costs 600 W, which kills the idea.
- **Software:** a PyTorch/XLA quantizer pass re-encodes trained checkpoints. Models need no retraining, except possibly fine-tuning at 2 bits.
- **Falsification test:** run Llama-3-70B through RTL plus a quantizer. The design is dead if either holds:
  - the accuracy drop exceeds 1 point (MMLU or GSM8K), or
  - decode logic exceeds 15% of the area of the MAC array.

**Facts vs assumptions**
- The QTIP and AQLM results at 2–3 bits are published software results.
- The decode energies and 2-bit accuracy figures given for this design are my estimates.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Hash-Regenerated Trellis Weight Decoder
MECHANISM: Weights are stored as about 2-bit trellis codes with per-block scales. A small hash circuit at each SRAM or HBM read port regenerates the codebook values, so no table is stored. The circuit emits FP8 operands straight into the MACs. The closest earlier attempt is QTIP-style decoding in software on GPUs, which steals ALU cycles at large batch; fixed logic at the port removes that cost. The deciding quantity is decode energy per weight, which must be ≤0.1 pJ.
VALUE: A 70B model needs 4× fewer SRAM bytes, so it fits on one wafer or about 35 LPUs, and batch-1 HBM decode is 4× faster.

IDEA 2
NAME: Latent KV With Outlier Sidecar
MECHANISM: Keys and values are cached as per-head low-rank latents at 3–4 bits, with per-block scales. The few outlier channels go to a small FP16 sidebuffer. Expansion happens inside the attention unit, so full-width KV never crosses memory. The closest earlier attempt is FP8 KV, which saves only 2× and still grows linearly with full width. The deciding quantity is accuracy retained at 128k context.
VALUE: KV bytes per token fall about 6–8×, so long-context serving no longer sets the chip count.

IDEA 3
NAME: Shared-Base Expert Delta Store
MECHANISM: Each MoE layer stores one dense base expert at 4 bits. Each expert is stored as a 1–2-bit sparse residual against that base. The router's top-k decision is sent ahead so that only the selected residuals are fetched and added at the MAC input. The closest earlier attempt is plain per-expert quantization, which still stores and streams every expert at full code width. The deciding quantity is the residual bits per weight needed to hold accuracy, which must be ≤1.5.
VALUE: The MoE capacity footprint and the bytes fetched per token fall about 2–3×, which cuts the package count per model.
