=== DIRECTIONS ===

DIRECTION 1: Separate memory capacity from memory bandwidth across tiers. Model weights and KV cache live in high-capacity memory that does not need CoWoS: LPDDR or CXL-attached DRAM, or high-bandwidth flash. Bandwidth for each token comes from a large on-die SRAM working set. Hardware schedulers prefetch the next layers or experts into that SRAM ahead of compute, so the wide external pipe is only needed for the working set. | AIMS AT: the accelerator-package share (about 38–45%), the sold-out HBM supply, and the capacity-vs-bandwidth trade-off shown by Groq at 230 MB and Cerebras at 44 GB | NOT: vertical 3D stacking (Direction 2), reducing the bytes that have to move (Direction 3), fabric-level pooling (Direction 4), or die/packaging economics (Direction 5)

DIRECTION 2: Bond memory directly onto logic in 3D, using wafer-on-wafer or die-on-wafer hybrid bonding of DRAM or SRAM layers above or below the compute. This gives TB/s per mm² of bandwidth and tens to hundreds of GB of capacity in one vertical stack, with no silicon interposer and no HBM PHY. | AIMS AT: the capacity-vs-bandwidth wall, the CoWoS interposer bottleneck, and batch-1 decode being limited by bandwidth (about 300 tok/s/user at 22 TB/s for a 70 GB model) | NOT: tiering to external commodity memory (Direction 1), compression (Direction 3), interconnect (Direction 4), or using many small mature dies (Direction 5)

DIRECTION 3: Cut the bytes moved and stored per token, using numerics and compression implemented in hardware. Options include inline decoders for codebook, vector-quantized or entropy-coded weights below 4 bits; structured-sparsity and MoE-aware gating that skips memory fetches; and compressed or quantized KV-cache formats that are decoded at the point of use. | AIMS AT: the memory-bandwidth roofline for decode, effective capacity per GB (fewer chips per model, which also attacks Archetype C's 60% silicon share), and KV-cache growth with context | NOT: adding memory supply through new tiers or stacking (Directions 1 and 2), changing the system topology (Direction 4), or changing manufacturing cost (Direction 5)

DIRECTION 4: Disaggregate at system scale with a new scale-up fabric. Co-packaged optics or a photonic interposer would carry a memory-semantic fabric, so that pools of compute and pools of memory scale independently. Prefill, decode, KV cache and experts would be placed across the fabric rather than tied to the HBM bundled with each package, which removes stranded capacity and much of the copper NVLink hierarchy. | AIMS AT: the scale-up and scale-out network share (about 18–23%), stranded per-package memory, and the rack power and cooling ceiling on copper NVL racks | NOT: on-package memory mechanisms (Directions 1 and 2), reducing data at the source (Direction 3), or die cost and yield (Direction 5)

DIRECTION 5: Trade silicon area for power and package cost. Use many small, high-yield chiplets, possibly on N3/N4 or older nodes, running at low voltage and moderate clocks. Mount them on organic or panel-level substrates rather than CoWoS, and rely on compiler-scheduled deterministic dataflow so that small dies keep high utilization without large cache or scheduler overhead. Packages would stay well under about 2.3 kW and would not need exotic cooling. | AIMS AT: capex per token, through cost per good mm², yield, and use of foundry capacity outside the CoWoS pool; power density per package; and facility build-out pace (facility share about 12–15%) | NOT: new memory technology or memory tiers (Directions 1 and 2), compression of data (Direction 3), or a new optical fabric (Direction 4)
