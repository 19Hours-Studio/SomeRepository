=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: N6 SRAM chiplets, package-wide tensor stages
MECHANISM: Weights and KV for Llama-3.3-70B are held in NVFP4 (4.5 bits). They sit entirely in SRAM on 100 mm² N6 chiplets, with 32 chiplets per organic package. The fix has two parts. First, each die is sized to AMD V-Cache's measured 14.2 Mb/mm², not 17.5. A die has 70 mm² of SRAM (124 MB) and 30 mm² of logic. The logic gives 22.7 TF FP8 at A100's whole-die 0.755 TOPS/mm² on N7. Second, layers are no longer pipelined die by die. That caused the earlier "about half of 1,000 tok/s" failure. Instead, every package is one tensor-parallel stage across all 32 dies. 32 users at 8K need 67 GB, which is 544 dies in 17 stages, with 16 micro-batches of 2 users. Each stage takes 37.7 µs of compute plus 11.4 µs of collectives, assuming 1 µs for a 32-die all-reduce, which still needs a simulator. That works out to 1,198 tok/s/user, or 916 at 50% MAC efficiency. The deciding number is full TCO $/Mtok. It counts 2× vendor markup, host and network, facility, energy and 60% utilisation. The result is $0.23/Mtok with $7.5k package overhead and $0.14 with $3k.
VALUE: At measured SRAM density, it serves 70B at about 1,000–1,200 tok/s/user for about $0.14–0.23/Mtok. That is 2.6–5.6× below the Groq ($0.79) and Cerebras ($0.60–1.20) list prices for this tier, though those prices include the vendors' margins, and capex per GB of SRAM is 13× below CS-3's. The old claim of "GB300 cost at 20× speed" is dropped. This design costs 2–4× more per token than GB300 at 50 tok/s, and it is still slower per user than Cerebras's 2,100–2,500 tok/s.

IDEA 2
NAME: Measured-density DRAM-on-logic for interactive decode
MECHANISM: The package has four 400 mm² N2 logic chiplets on top. Each is hybrid-bonded onto a 4-layer custom DRAM stack, and there is no HBM. The fix sizes the DRAM at the only measured hybrid-bonded logic-to-DRAM density, 64 Mb/mm² (ISSCC 2022). That leaves no density assumption above measurement. It also feeds the logic at 1.8 V through the DRAM stack, with on-die regulators at 90% efficiency. That cuts the current from 433 A to 201 A per chiplet, and power TSVs then take 4.5% of each layer instead of 9.7%. The package holds 48 GB at 44 TB/s. That needs 114 GB/s per Gb, against 135 measured (margin 1.18). Energy is 0.88 pJ/bit measured, with 1.5 pJ/bit as a stress case. Because the capacity per package is small, the operating regime changes. The part balances only where BW/C ≈ 914 tok/s/user, so it is offered only for the interactive tier. On a 1T-total/32B-active GQA MoE model, compared with Rubin (288 GB, 22 TB/s, HBM at 3.44–5.74 pJ/bit), it gets 1.2–1.9× the tokens/s per kW at 400 tok/s/user. At 1,000 tok/s/user it gets 1.8–4.3×, and 2.2–2.6× the tokens/s per package, at 8K and 128K. At 100 tok/s/user it loses by 0.39–0.60× because it runs out of capacity.
VALUE: At measured DRAM density, modelled decode tokens per kW are about 2–4× Rubin's at ≥1,000 tok/s/user, falling to 1.2–1.9× at 400 tok/s/user. Package draw is 0.6–1.7 kW. This is the smallest honest version: it is a part for the interactive tier, and it loses to Rubin by about 2× for bulk serving at ≤100 tok/s/user. A 4-layer stack at this density has not been demonstrated.

IDEA 3
NAME: Bandwidth-sized HBM4 decode part (smallest honest)
MECHANISM: Prefill and training stay on Rubin or Rubin CPX. Decode moves to a part priced for bandwidth rather than FLOPS. It keeps Rubin's 8 HBM4 stacks and 22 TB/s, which is proven supply rather than unproven stacked DRAM. Three cuts follow. The stacks are 8-high (192 GB, not 288 GB), because capacity needed is about BW/r + W/N, which is 81 GB at 128K and 300 tok/s/user. Logic drops to 5 PF FP8, against 3–4 PF needed. Scale-up drops to 1.2 TB/s, because the MoE all-to-all needs about 0.6 TB/s. The weak link is fixed by moving the claim from tokens per kW to cost per token. Energy is only 6% of TCO here, at $18.4/GB HBM4, a $55k Rubin and a $7.8M rack. The new deciding number, from a full TCO model, is 1.19–1.24× lower cost per token than Rubin NVL72 at ≥300 tok/s/user, at 8K and 128K. Decode draw falls from 1.64 kW to 1.34 kW. At 100 tok/s/user the gain is 1.00–1.04×, because 192 GB runs out of capacity.
VALUE: It gives about 1.2× lower cost per decode token than Rubin NVL72 at ≥300 tok/s/user, and 1.5× more decode packages per HBM wafer, which matters while HBM is sold out. The weak link cannot be fixed to 1.5×. The rest of the cost is vendor price and rack, not physics, so this is the smallest honest version, and it gives up training and ultra-low latency.
