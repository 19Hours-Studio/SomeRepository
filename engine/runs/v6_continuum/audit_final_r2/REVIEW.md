IDEA 1: Hybrid-bonded DRAM-on-logic decode package
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 2.2–2.7× headline applies to a hypothetical 1T GQA model. The model class the idea names as its target (1T/32B sparse MoE) actually ships with MLA (Kimi K2: 61 layers, 64 heads, MLA). For that class the idea's own model gives only 1.08–1.29×, so the "value" line overstates the gain on the workload it chose. | I reproduced 2·64·(576+512)·61·131072 = 1.11 TFLOP/token of attention at 128K. MLA arithmetic intensity is about 430 FLOP/B. That is above Continuum's FP8 ridge of 312 FLOP/B, so the workload is compute-bound and the bandwidth advantage goes unused.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The claim does not hold at the 100 tok/s/user target that the idea itself lists. A package sweeps its whole 160 GB in 2.5 ms, so in a 10 ms step its bandwidth sits idle about 75% of the time and batch size is capped by KV capacity (80 GB/kW). Rubin is limited by bandwidth instead and can read 122 GB/kW per step. | In my many-package GQA model (all experts touched), Continuum gets 0.65× Rubin's tokens/s per kW at 100 tok/s, at both 8K and 128K. It is 0.84× against a 2.3 kW Rubin. It gets 2.6–2.7× only at 400 tok/s and above.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The decisive figures (64 TB/s at 0.8 pJ/bit from four 4-high stacks) are assumed, not shown. Each DRAM die has to deliver 4 TB/s. | With 1 KB pages that is about 3.9e9 row activations/s per die. 64 banks at tRC 45 ns give about 1.4e9/s, so each die needs about 180 or more banks or subarrays active independently. The closest real part, d-Matrix Raptor, claims 0.37 pJ/bit but has only 32 GB per card (vendor figure, Hot Chips 2026). No part has shown 40 GB per stack at 16 TB/s.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Power delivery and thermals through a stack with logic on top are not modelled. About 400 W per 400 mm² die is 1 W/mm², which means about 533 A at 0.75 V per die passing through four DRAM dies' TSVs. The DRAM also sits close to logic junction temperature. | DRAM retention allows ≤85 °C at normal refresh, or 95 °C with 2× refresh. The extra refresh power and bandwidth are not in the 410 W.
DEFECT | MINOR | Compound yield and cost are not in the model. | My assumed values: N2 400 mm² at D0 = 0.1/cm² gives 0.67, times 0.85⁴ for the DRAM dies, times 0.99⁴ for the bonds. That is about 0.34 per stack with wafer-to-wafer bonding unless known-good-die die-to-wafer bonding is used.
DEFECT | MINOR | No interconnect is specified for MoE expert-parallel all-to-all. At 1000 tok/s/user there are 122 all-to-alls per 1 ms step, so each gets about 8 µs or less. | Measured small-message collectives on an 8-GPU NVLink node take about 2.3–6 µs, which would use 30–75% of the step. Switch power is also left out of the per-kW figure.
SURVIVES | 410 W DRAM power. 1.1 TFLOP MLA attention. MLA is compute-bound on Continuum and bandwidth-bound on Rubin (ridge about 795 FLOP/B). 32 vs 12.2 TB/s per kW gives 2.6×, so 2.2–2.7× at ≥400 tok/s on GQA holds, and Rubin at 1.8 kW is a conservative baseline. 80 Gb per die is about 0.2 Gb/mm², which is achievable. The finding that an HBM tier hurts is consistent.
RATIO | 1.2 | Workload mix weighted to MLA (1.1–1.3× per kW) and to low-interactivity serving (0.65×). Energy is about 10% of TCO, and the capex of a yield-limited N2 plus custom-DRAM package is unknown.

IDEA 2: N6 SRAM chiplets, package-wide tensor stages
DEFECT | MAJOR | Both the cost and the speed hold only for 32 users at 8K context. KV cache takes 92 KB per token, so capacity and pipeline depth grow with context, while Groq's and Cerebras' list prices cover long contexts. | At 32K the system needs 136 GB, 1,098 dies and 35 packages: cost is ×2.06 and the pipeline is about twice as deep (roughly 600 tok/s/user). At 128K it needs 426 GB and 108 packages, ×6.35. The ratio against Groq at 32K falls from 3.4× to about 1.7×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 1 µs 32-die all-reduce, about 9.4 per stage, is assumed. Measured small-message all-reduce on 8-GPU NVLink takes about 2.3–6 µs. A 4×8 mesh with UCIe 25 mm reach needs about 20 sequential hop phases, so 1 µs requires ≤50 ns per hop including the reduction and sync. | At 2 µs per all-reduce: 1,006 tok/s/user (799 at 50% MAC efficiency). At 3 µs: 866 (709).
DEFECT | MINOR | The 1,198 tok/s/user figure is at 70% MAC efficiency, not peak. The text does not say so. | My calculation gives 162.6 GFLOP/token, ×2 users ÷ 17 stages on 725 TF = 26.4 µs at 100%. 37.7 µs corresponds to 70%.
DEFECT | MINOR | Capacity slack is 5.6% (63.8 GB needed against 67.6 GB). That leaves no room for activations, spare dies or ECC margin. | Assuming 500 FIT/Mb, 540 Gb of SRAM gives about 0.27 upsets per hour. A 17-package pipeline with no spare stage stops if any one die fails. Assuming 99.5% attach yield per die, a 32-die package yields 0.995³² = 85%.
DEFECT | MINOR | The quoted price range is computed wrongly, and the comparison mixes decode-only cost with blended list prices that include prefill. | The ratios are 0.60/0.23 = 2.6 up to 1.20/0.14 = 8.6, not 2.6–5.6.
SURVIVES | 512 Mb/36 mm² = 14.2 Mb/mm². 124 MB and 22.7 TF per die. A100 624/826 = 0.755 TOPS/mm². 544 dies = 67.6 GB. 1,198 and 916 tok/s/user reproduce. Capex per GB is about 14× below CS-3 at about $2.5M. Withdrawing the GB300 claim was correct.
RATIO | 1.5 | Against Groq's $0.79 list price, corrected for typical 16–32K context and 2 µs all-reduce. It is about 0.3 against bulk GB300 serving.
KNOWN | partly | Groq 3 LPX (an SRAM-only, 256-chip rack) and Cerebras CS-3 hold weights and KV in SRAM. d-Matrix Corsair uses SRAM chiplets on an organic package.

IDEA 3: Cheap-node HBM decode tier
DEFECT | FATAL | The 2.8× capex gain compares a part priced at cost with a street price that carries about 75% gross margin. HBM dominates both bills of materials, so moving logic from N3 to N6 saves at most about $1k. At equal margin the ratio is about 1.0. An operator who buys at cost already has TPU, Trainium or MTIA. | 45k × (1 − 0.75) ≈ $11k implied Rubin cost, which is less than Continuum's $16k at cost. Nvidia guided about 75% gross margin for fiscal Q1 2027.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The TCO formula treats all 91% of non-energy TCO as package cost. Hosts, switches, racks and facility do not get cheaper when the chip does. | BofA's Vera Rubin NVL rack estimate of $7–8.5M is about $97–118k per GPU, so the package is about 40–45% of rack capex. With a package share of 0.4–0.6 the gain is 1.33–1.61× even at 2.8×. With a 2× vendor markup it is 1.12–1.20×, which misses the 1.5× target.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 3 PF FP8 does not saturate 22 TB/s on MLA models (DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2), and not with speculative decoding, which verifies 3–8 tokens per step. | MLA at 128K: the compute ceiling is 3e15 / 1.17e12 ≈ 2,555 tok/s, against a bandwidth ceiling of about 7,236. Only 35% of the HBM bandwidth is usable.
DEFECT | MAJOR | 3 PF FP8 on N6 needs about 2,000–4,000 mm² of logic (scaled from H100, or at A100 density). With 8 HBM4 sites that needs a larger CoWoS-L interposer than Rubin's, so $2k packaging and any CoWoS relief are not credible. Logic at an assumed 0.4–0.5 pJ/FLOP draws 1.2–1.5 kW, so energy does not halve. | Using 0.09 × 1.1 instead of 0.09/2 moves 2.70× to 2.36×.
DEFECT | MINOR | The $12k HBM figure ($41.7/GB) is unsourced. One estimate gives about $500 per 48 GB HBM4 stack, which puts 288 GB at about $4–4.5k. That lowers both bills of materials and confirms the gap is margin. | https://siliconanalysts.com/data/hbm-pricing (single-site estimate)
DEFECT | MINOR | KV-cache transfer from the prefill tier has a cost. | A 128K GQA request is about 12 GB at 4.5 bits, which takes about 0.24 s over 400 Gb/s.
SURVIVES | 1/(0.91/2.8 + 0.045) = 2.70. The $30.7k break-even. About 3 PF saturates 22 TB/s for GQA at batch about 38.
RATIO | 1.0 | Equal-margin package cost (~$11–16k on both sides), divided by a 40–60% package share of TCO, with MLA throughput penalties.
KNOWN | partly | Huawei Ascend 950DT (decode-oriented; HiZQ 2.0 memory, 144 GB, 4 TB/s) and 950PR (prefill; low-cost HiBL memory) use the same die with different memory (https://www.jonpeddie.com/techwatch/huaweis-ascend-950-ai-processor/). Nvidia Rubin CPX is the same split in reverse.

IDEA 4: N6 SRAM-chiplet pipeline packages
