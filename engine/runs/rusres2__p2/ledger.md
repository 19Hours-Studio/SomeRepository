ARCHETYPE B: Arctic LNG 2, Trains 1–2 at Utrenneye. Unlike the Yamal LNG trains, it uses licensed Linde MFC4 liquefaction, not Arctic Cascade or Mix (F171). Cargoes are sold at a discount to China via Ura Bay FSU trans-shipment. | cost about $970/t all-in (est.), made up of capital about $620/t at the actual 4 Mt/y, shipping about $100/t and a buyer discount of about $190/t | realised price about $390/t ($30m per cargo of about 77 kt; market above $44m, which is 30–40% higher). Capital alone exceeds the price.
- Utilisation: Forbes estimates 3.6–4.4 Mt/y, about 27–33% of the two-train design of 13.2 Mt/y (F182).
- Delay: Train 1 started in Dec 2023 and has been idle for long periods (F283). Train 3 has slipped to about 2028.
- Ships: Russia has 2 Arc7 LNG carriers and 21 are contracted. The lead Zvezda hull arrived in Dec 2025 (F139) and the next two are due late 2026, though GTT membranes are unavailable (F347).
- Power: the Power Machines GTE-170 is at the level of single units (F181).
- Fleet use: Alexey Kosygin delivers to Ura Bay, where conventional ships take over, because the Ob Gulf is Arc7-only in winter (F140). This is the winter-access limit.

LARGEST: capital, driven by utilisation. At the 19.8 Mt/y design the same capital is about $125/t, a 5x cut. Capital is 64% of cost, so a 5x cut would remove about 51% of it. The other half of the cost is the Arc7 hull gap (the only way to lift output) and the legal buyer (which sets the 20% discount).
