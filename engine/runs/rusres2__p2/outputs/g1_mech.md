No new searches were run. Distances and speeds are my estimates and marked "est.". Two caveats bound all three ideas:

- **Hulls.** Russia builds no conventional LNG carriers. Any leg beyond Murmansk needs lawfully chartered tonnage.
- **Buyers.** Chinese buyers of Arctic LNG 2 cargoes or Rosneft crude act lawfully under their own law but carry US secondary-sanction exposure. That exposure is the 30–40% discount. Domestic Russian buyers are the only unambiguous legal payers.

**Arctic LNG 2 arithmetic (est.).**

- **Cycle.** Utrenneye to Ura Bay is about 1,250 nm, with about 600 nm in ice at 9 kn and 650 nm open at 17 kn. That gives about 4.4 days each way, or about 11 days per round trip including berth time.
- **Per hull.** About 33 cargoes × 75 kt ≈ 2.5 Mt/y. A summer NSR voyage to China (about 40+ days) yields about 0.6 Mt per season per hull.
- **With 5 Zvezda hulls by 2027.** About 12.5 Mt/y. Capital falls from $620/t to about $200/t. All-in cost falls from about $970/t to about $490/t, a 2.0x gain within the 5.0x ceiling.

**Coal arithmetic.** Year-round mining at 5 Mt/y with a 150-day ship window needs a 2.9 Mt winter stockpile at Dikson and about 33 kt/day of loading.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Arc7 never leaves Barents
MECHANISM: Every Zvezda Arc7 carrier runs only the Utrenneye–Ura Bay leg, all year, and the Ura Bay floating storage unit hands cargo to lawfully chartered conventional carriers. Liquefaction trains are brought online one per about 2.5 Mt/y of shuttle capacity. Long-haul summer NSR sailings by Arc7 hulls are dropped because each one costs about 2 Mt/y of winter shuttling. The closest earlier attempt is the Alexey Kosygin's Ura Bay runs, which stopped short because hulls were still split between short shuttles and long-haul voyages; this rule removes the split. The deciding quantity is the winter round-trip time, which must stay at or below 11 days.
VALUE: Lifts output toward about 12.5 Mt/y from built trains by 2027 and roughly halves full cost per delivered tonne, to about $490/t.

IDEA 2
NAME: Winter coal stockpile, summer escort
MECHANISM: Syradasayskoye mines and hauls all year and banks about 2.9 Mt at Dikson. It ships only from July to November, using the lowest ice class that is lawfully available, at about 33 kt/day of loading. This frees nuclear icebreaker escort hours in winter and spring for the Ob Gulf and Bukhta Sever. The closest earlier attempt is the Red Dog and Mary River summer-only shipping model, which stopped at a capped mine rate; here the mine is uncapped and only shipping is seasonal. The deciding quantity is coking-strength (CSR) loss in a 7-month Arctic stockpile, which must be below the buyer's grade threshold.
VALUE: Cuts outbound logistics, about 25% of the coal cost, by using cheaper hulls and removes coal from the winter competition for icebreakers.

IDEA 3
NAME: Size Sever to tankers
MECHANISM: Payakha and Vankor volumes are allocated each month. Bukhta Sever receives only what the Arc7 tanker fleet can lift, about 2.1 Mt/y per 70 kt hull on a roughly 12-day Murmansk cycle. The surplus goes south through the existing Vankor–Purpe pipeline to domestic refineries, which are legal payers with no discount. The closest earlier attempt is the 30 Mt/y H2 2027 target, which assumes about 14–15 hulls that do not yet exist and sanctions-exposed buyers. The deciding quantity is spare southbound Transneft capacity in Mt/y.
VALUE: Turns stranded Arctic barrels into domestically sold tonnes now and brings the Arctic route to full output only as hulls arrive.
