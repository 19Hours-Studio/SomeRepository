Under these constraints, the LNG lever is moving more tonnes through the same few Arc7 hulls. The oil and coal levers avoid the bottleneck: pipeline headroom for oil, and stockpiling for coal. Figures marked "est." are my own arithmetic, not sourced. No new data was gathered.

**Deciding numbers**
- **Arctic LNG 2 shuttle:** An Arc7 run from Utrenneye to the Murmansk-area FSU and back takes about 13–14 days in winter ice (est., about 1,100 nm each way at about 8 kn). That gives about 26 loads × 77 kt ≈ 2.0 Mt per hull per year, versus about 1 Mt today.
  - 4 Russian hulls by end-2026 would deliver about 8 Mt/y, against 3.6–4.4 Mt/y now (F182).
  - Capital per tonne would fall from about $620 to about $310 (est.).
  - The weak link is the onward leg. Russia builds no conventional LNG carriers, so the winter onward hull is unsolved.
- **Dikson coal:** Shipping 5 Mt in a 135-day season (F115) needs about 37 kt/day of loading, which is at least 1,550 t/h around the clock. Year-round mining then needs a stockpile of about 3.6 Mt. Both figures are est.
- **Vostok Oil:** Whether the southern route works depends on spare capacity in the Vankor–Purpe pipeline as Vankor output declines. That figure is unsourced and has to be checked first.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Short-cycle Arc7 LNG shuttle
MECHANISM: Every Zvezda Arc7 hull runs only the ice leg, Utrenneye to the Ura Bay/Murmansk FSU, year-round. The exception is July–November, when hulls go east to China direct over the NSR if that beats two shuttle cycles. The closest earlier attempt is Alexey Kosygin's Ura Bay runs (F140), which stopped short because only one or two hulls existed and they were also used on long hauls. This idea makes the short cycle a fixed rule for each new hull, so each delivery adds about 2 Mt/y instead of about 1. The deciding quantity is the winter round-trip time; it must be 14 days or less.
VALUE: Built capacity in use rises from about 30% to about 60% with 4 hulls, and capital per tonne halves (est.), as long as a lawful non-ice onward hull exists.

IDEA 2
NAME: Vostok barrels south first
MECHANISM: Field sequencing sends early Payakha and Vankor-cluster oil into spare capacity in the Vankor–Purpe and Transneft pipelines as Vankor declines. Domestic refiners and the ESPO route buy it, which are buyers that can legally pay. Bukhta Sever ships only the volume above that headroom. The closest earlier attempt is Vankor's own southern link, which was not used for Vostok because the plan assumed Arc7 export. Here the 790 km northern pipe ramps up behind the southern one instead. The deciding quantity is spare pipeline capacity in Mt/y.
VALUE: First delivered barrels arrive sooner without waiting for about 15 Arc7 tankers (F146), and some volume avoids both the Arc7 tanker shortage and the SDN-driven buyer discount.

IDEA 3
NAME: Stockpile coal, ship open-season
MECHANISM: Syradasayskoye mines year-round into a Dikson stockpile of about 3.6 Mt. The coal ships only in the July–November open season, on lower-ice-class Russian bulkers escorted in convoys timed between LNG peaks, and goes to China, a lawful buyer paying 3% duty. The closest earlier attempts are Red Dog and Mary River (F167–168). Their limit was a fixed 90–120-day window; this idea fits within it by sizing loading at 1,550 t/h or more instead of stretching the season. The deciding quantity is loader rate × open-season days ≥ annual output.
VALUE: Winter ice-class hull demand for coal falls to zero, so Arc7 hulls and icebreakers stay free for LNG while coal still reaches its design 5 Mt/y.
