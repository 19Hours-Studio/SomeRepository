Deciding arithmetic (est., from the supplied facts): one Arc7 LNG carrier holds about 77 kt. Utrenneye to Ura Bay and back takes about 14 days in winter and about 8 in open water, assumed by scaling the 20-day Sabetta to Zeebrugge round trip.

- **Short shuttle:** about 15 winter trips plus 19 summer trips, so 34 × 77 kt ≈ **2.6 Mt/y per hull**.
- **Direct to Asia by the NSR:** about 40-day round trips in Jul–Nov only, so about 4 trips, or **0.3 Mt/y per hull**.
- **Check:** 2 hulls on the shuttle gives about 4–5 Mt/y. That matches the Forbes estimate of 3.6–4.4 Mt/y, so hulls, not trains, set Arctic LNG 2 output.

The 20% discount on Arctic LNG 2 cargoes stays in every case. The realistic gain is therefore about 2x, not 3x, which the TIGHT ceiling allows. Rosneft's SDN listing leaves the set of Vostok Oil buyers unresolved. No idea below fixes that.

=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Arc7 never leaves shuttle
MECHANISM: Each Russian-built Zvezda Arc7 hull runs only Utrenneye to Ura Bay/Murmansk, all year. Conventional tonnage serving non-sanctioning buyers carries the long leg, and Arc7 hulls never sail the NSR east leg to Asia. A rolling schedule recomputes each hull's cycle time from ice state each week and holds the gravity-based storage tanks near empty in summer, when turns are fastest. The closest earlier attempt is the Yamal LNG Zeebrugge model, which sent Arc7 hulls 20-day legs to Europe because hulls were plentiful there. With only 2 hulls, that long-leg habit costs about 8x tonnage per hull, so the shuttle rule reverses it. The deciding quantity is the winter round-trip time; it must stay at or below 14 days for the 2.6 Mt/y per hull to hold.
VALUE: With 5 hulls by 2028, Arctic LNG 2 reaches about 13 Mt/y, essentially the 13.2 Mt/y two-train design. Capital per tonne falls from about $620 to about $190, and delivered cost drops from about $970/t to roughly $540/t without any sanctions workaround.

IDEA 2
NAME: Coal summer-only, oil gets winter
MECHANISM: Syradasayskoye coal is stockpiled at Dikson through winter, about 3.2 Mt of the 5 Mt/y. All of it ships Jul–Nov on lower ice-class bulkers, some directly east to China, which charges a 3% duty. Every winter nuclear icebreaker hour in the shared Yenisei Gulf then goes to the Bukhta Sever Arc7 tanker convoy. The closest earlier attempts are Red Dog and Mary River, whose fixed 3–4 month windows capped output. Coal here does not need a year-round window, because the stockpile, unlike LNG with its boil-off, does not decay. The deciding quantity is summer loading and hull rate. About 38 kt/day over about 130 days needs roughly 6 hulls of about 70 kt.
VALUE: Coal gets a delivered route that uses no Arc7 hulls and no icebreaker hours, and Vostok Oil gets the winter escort capacity its 30 Mt/y target in H2 2027 needs.

IDEA 3
NAME: Freeze Train 3 until hulls
MECHANISM: Arctic LNG 2 Train 3 spending is gated on a hull ledger. Spending resumes only when (Arc7 hulls delivered × 2.6 Mt/y) exceeds the 13.2 Mt/y of Trains 1–2. With Zvezda delivering about 2 hulls/yr, that is about 2029–30. The GTE-170 units and the train module meanwhile go to hull-limited Trains 1–2 as spares. The closest earlier attempt is Arctic LNG 2 itself, which built trains ahead of ships and left Train 1 idle. This gate makes ship deliveries the trigger for train capex. The deciding quantity is the Zvezda delivery rate, which depends on finding a substitute for the unavailable GTT membranes.
VALUE: Avoids several years of idle-capital annuity, about $0.8bn per year on a Train 3 cost of about $7bn (est.), without losing any delivered tonne.
