=== REFRAMES ===

**ASSUMPTIONS:**
- The built capacity is Russian technology. (contradicted: Yamal Trains 1–3 run on APCI C3MR with Baker Hughes drives, and Arctic LNG 2 runs on licensed Linde MFC4. Only Train 4, at 0.9 Mt/y, uses Arctic Cascade.)
- Russian-built hulls can fill the Arc7 gap in time. (contradicted: GTT membranes are unavailable, and after the Dec 2025 lead hull only two more are due, in late 2026.)
- Plant capex is the lever that cuts cost per tonne. (contradicted: plant is sunk and idle. Cost per tonne falls only through utilisation, which hulls and buyers cap.)
- Pad and pipeline life on thawing ground is a major cost term. (contradicted: earlier runs put foundation and permafrost losses below 1–2% of delivered cost.)
- "A buyer that can legally pay" is a single fixed set. (contradicted: legality differs by product and jurisdiction. The EU bans LNG in 2027; Rosneft is under US SDN with secondary-sanction exposure; coal faces only a 3% Chinese duty.)
- Vostok Oil reaches 30 Mt/y in H2 2027. (unverified: it is at least 3 years late, and Carnegie says about 15 Arc7 tankers are needed for year-round export.)
- The four streams are independent. (contradicted: they share Kara Sea season, icebreakers and escort capacity.)
- The fleet count is "2 Arc7 LNG carriers". (conflicting: an earlier run counts 5. This needs sourcing.)

**REFRAME 1:** Given the Arc7 LNG carriers, Arc7 tankers and nuclear icebreaker hours actually available each year to 2028, which allocation across Arctic LNG 2, Yamal LNG, Vostok Oil and Dikson coal maximises legally sold tonnes per year and minimises marginal cost per added delivered tonne? The decision variables are shuttle length (Ob Gulf or Bukhta Sever to the trans-shipment point) and season split.
CHANGES: The unit becomes tonnes per hull-day on a shared fleet, and the four cases become one system. | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: high | WHY: Hulls are the stated binding constraint, and tonnes = hulls × round trips × cargo can be computed directly from the material.

**REFRAME 2:** Which product form and route reaches buyers who can pay without sanctions exposure after 1 Jan 2027, for each stream? Examples are Yamal LNG volume displaced from the EU, Arctic LNG 2 cargoes, Rosneft crude, and coal. What discount does each pay?
CHANGES: The scope is narrowed to the buyer set per product, and the discount is treated as a cost term (about 20% of Arctic LNG 2 cost). | IMPORTANCE: high | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: Buyer access sets both the discount and whether built capacity runs at all.

**REFRAME 3:** Report two metrics separately: marginal cash cost per added delivered tonne, which drives decisions, and full cost including idle sunk capex, which is what the asker wants reported. Ask which change moves full cost most through utilisation alone.
CHANGES: The unit is split. Sunk capex becomes a function of utilisation, not a lever. | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: high | WHY: At 27–33% utilisation, capex per tonne is about 5× the design-rate figure. Only throughput moves it.

**REFRAME 4:** Which domestic item gates each added cargo, and on what date: Zvezda hulls without GTT membranes, GTE-170 turbines, Kazanskompressormash 112 MW units, or Arctic Cascade/Mix scale-up beyond 0.9 Mt/y? Which streams need none of them?
CHANGES: The "Russian-only" constraint becomes a schedule of equipment gates. | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: Every domestic substitute is at unit or prototype scale, and this question exposes which streams depend on them.

**REFRAME 5:** How should Arctic LNG 2 Train 3, the Vostok Oil ramp and Syradasayskoye stage 2 be timed so that output is not produced ahead of the hulls and buyers needed to deliver it?
CHANGES: The target becomes field sequencing matched to hull deliveries. | IMPORTANCE: medium | POSSIBILITY: medium | WHY: It avoids adding more idle capacity of the kind behind today's high full cost.

**REFRAME 6:** Treat pad and pipeline life as a screening check, not a lever. Ask only whether any stream's life falls below its payback period.
CHANGES: Criterion (c) is cut back to a pass/fail check. | IMPORTANCE: low | POSSIBILITY: high | WHY: It frees effort for the terms that are actually binding.
