For Yamal, Gydan and Taimyr oil and gas, which changes to shipping, trans-shipment, product form or field sequencing would raise delivered tonnes per year from capacity already built or sanctioned, and cut full cost per tonne delivered to a buyer that can legally pay? The comparison is Yamal LNG, Arctic LNG 2 and Vostok Oil/Vankor. The answer must use only Russian-built hulls, liquefaction trains and equipment (Zvezda, Arctic Cascade/Mix, Kazanskompressormash, Kriogenmash, Power Machines) and must not depend on getting around sanctions.
Cases: Arctic LNG 2 trains 1–3 at Utrenneye; Yamal LNG; Murmansk/Ob LNG concepts; Vostok Oil (Payakha, Bukhta Sever terminal, 30 Mt/y target in H2 2027); Taimyr coking coal from Syradasayskoye via Dikson, which shares the Kara Sea route, icebreakers and season.
A good answer: (a) full cost per tonne delivered, including idle sunk capex, trans-shipment and buyer discount (Arctic LNG 2 cargoes sell 30–40% below market); (b) years to first delivered cargo or added cargo; (c) utilisation of built capacity, Kara Sea and Ob Gulf operating days per year for each hull class, and life of pads and pipelines on ice-rich Yamal ground.
The binding constraint to attack: Ice-class hulls (2 Arc7 LNG carriers available to Russia, 21 contracted), winter access to the Ob Gulf, which only Arc7 ships can reach, and legal access to buyers (EU LNG ban and EU tanker and icebreaker service bans from 2027; Rosneft on the US SDN list since Oct 2025).
This is part 2 of 3 of a larger question (context, not instructions): Russia wants to develop and extract minerals and energy in its permafrost regions at the lowest full cost, in the shortest time from investment decision to first production, and with the highest effectiveness. Effectiveness means resource recovery, working time across the year, and how long infrastructure lasts on thawing ground. All three are measured per unit of product delivered to the buyer. T

The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: Size Sever to hulls
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged: each month Bukhta Sever receives only what the Russian-built Arc hulls can lift, about 1.54 Mt/y per 69 kt hull on the 15.5-day Murmansk cycle (1.2 Mt/y if winter stretches the cycle to 20 days). The allocation is reversed. Vankor-cluster oil (about 11.6–12.3 Mt in 2026–27) stays on the existing Vankor–Purpe line. That line is rated 25 Mt/y and has 12.7–13.4 Mt/y spare. The oil goes to the export outlet it already sells to, not to domestic refineries, which are already crude-long. South is the cheaper margin: about $57/t to an Asian buyer via Transneft and Kozmino, against about $86/t north via Sever, Murmansk and Suez. The north carries only Payakha oil, and Payakha drilling is scheduled to Zvezda Arc hull deliveries, so no well is drilled whose oil cannot be lifted. Because of that, no surplus ever has to go south and the Payakha–Vankor segment never needs reverse flow.
VALUE: Delivered cost per tonne falls by about $29/t on every Vankor tonne kept south, and holding back 15–25 Mt/y of Payakha capacity by 2–3 years saves $2.4–5.8bn in present value. That is about $80–190 per tonne of the capacity deferred, against the published plan of 30 Mt/y in H2 2027, which needs about 19 hulls that do not exist.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The headline "$29/t on every Vankor tonne kept south" counts all 11.6–12.3 Mt/y of Vankor oil, but only oil the hulls could have lifted north was ever at risk of going north. That is 1.2–1.54 Mt/y per hull, and there is one Russian-built hull. (29 × 1.54 Mt ≈ $45m/y with the one existing hull (Valentin Pikul, 69 kt), against 29 × 12 Mt ≈ $350m/y implied. [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/russia-launches-massive-vostok-oil-project-marking-new-arctic-shipping-era/), [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/09/09/vostok-oils-first-tanker-cannot-conceal-rosneft-empty-promises-a93665))
- MAJOR: The baseline is Rosneft's announced plan, not current practice. Vankor oil already flows south on Vankor–Purpe, so for Vankor tonnes the "reversed allocation" is the status quo. The idea also ignores a cheaper competing allocation: put Vankor oil (no new wells) in the few hulls and defer all Payakha drilling. That saves more capex than deferring only part of Payakha. (The idea's own PV figures imply about $0.8bn per Mt/y of Payakha capacity at 12% (0.92 at 10%). Drilling 3 Mt/y to fill 2 hulls costs about $2.4bn, or about $88/t/y capital charge, against a $29/t saving. [Carnegie 2025](https://carnegieendowment.org/ru/russia-eurasia/politika/2025/07/russia-new-oil-project))
- MAJOR: The "15–25 Mt/y of Payakha deferred" counterfactual is inflated. Rosneft's 30 Mt target for H2 2027 includes about 12 Mt of Vankor-cluster oil, so Payakha's share is at most about 18 Mt/y. Payakha only reached pilot production in June 2026. (30 − 12 = 18 < 25. The $5.8bn upper bound therefore defers capacity that was never scheduled. [Payakha pilot](https://geoconversation.org/en/news/first-oil-at-the-peschany-block-vostok-oil-project-on-taimyr-moves-to-pilot-production/), [S&P](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/090626-russia-launches-major-vostok-oil-project-with-first-arctic-loading))
- MAJOR: Full cost per delivered tonne leaves out idle sunk capex, which the question requires. Taking Vankor oil out of the 790-km line (rated 100 Mt/y) and the Sever terminal puts the whole sunk charge on 1.2–3 Mt/y of Payakha oil. (Per-tonne capital charge rises about 5–9× (13.5–15 Mt/y down to 1.5–3 Mt/y throughput). This dwarfs the $29/t gain.)
- MAJOR: It is not shown that the line can run on Payakha oil alone. At hull-limited flow the oil moves very slowly in an Arctic pipe, with cooling and wax risk. The Vankor–Payakha segment would sit idle full of oil. (At 1.5–3 Mt/y in a 1.0–1.2 m line: 0.05–0.15 m/s, 32–93 days to cross 410 km, about 0.5–0.76 Mt line fill over 790 km. The pipe diameter is my assumption, not sourced.)
- MAJOR: The $57/t (south) and $86/t (north) figures are both assumed. The ESPO network tariff alone is about 3,373 RUB/t (2024 draft), roughly $40/t. Add Vankor–Purpe, Kozmino and freight to China, which rose after Rosneft was put on the SDN list. (South is plausibly $60–85/t and north $70–95/t, so the gap is $0–35/t and the sign is not certain. [Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/95327/))
- MAJOR: The cycle assumes an Arc hull, but the only Vostok Oil hull is Arc6, not Arc7. Winter Yenisei Gulf fast ice is the heavy-ice case, so the 20-day winter cycle (1.2 Mt/y) is unproven for this class. The first cargo shows the real limit at start-up was supply and the berth, not the hull. (Valentin Pikul is Arc6 with 69 kt deadweight. It sat 84 days at Sever's single loading berth before loading. [Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Vostok-Oil-One-Tanker-Four-Trillion-Rubles.amp.html))
- SURVIVES: Hull throughput: 365/15.5 × 69 kt × 0.95 = 1.54 Mt/y, and 20-day cycle = 1.20 Mt/y. Vankor–Purpe is rated 25 Mt/y, so spare = 12.7–13.4 Mt/y. 30/1.54 ≈ 19.5 hulls. Deferral PV is internally consistent at about $0.8bn per Mt/y.
- RATIO: 1.0 | Against current practice (Vankor oil already south, Payakha north as hulls allow) nothing changes. Against Rosneft's plan the gain is about $45m/y on about 1.5 Mt, which is under 1% of chain cost.
- KNOWN: yes | Vankor–Purpe is already the export route. Carnegie (2025) argues existing pipelines already export Vankor oil, so Sever adds little. [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/ru/russia-eurasia/politika/2025/07/russia-new-oil-project)

IDEA 2
NAME: Legality-split hull-day swing
MECHANISM: The anchor is unchanged: ice-class hulls stay on the short ice leg wherever a lawful onward hull exists, and train output is set by hull-days. The design now splits cargo by legal status. From 1 Jan 2027 the EU ban removes Zeebrugge and about 15 Mt/y of EU sales. Yamal LNG (not on the US SDN list) would then run its 15 existing Arc7s as a winter shuttle from Sabetta to a Kildin ship-to-ship (STS) transfer point near Murmansk, not the Saam FSU, whose owner is on the SDN list. Clean non-EU conventional hulls take the cargo on to Asia. In summer the fleet splits into 40 direct NSR-east cargoes plus 64 shuttle cargoes. Winter needs only 9.7–11.4 of the 15 hulls, so all 19.7 Mt/y is delivered, against 7.4 Mt/y if the Arc7s carry every cargo to Asia themselves. Arctic LNG 2 cargo has no lawful onward hull, so it moves only on Russian-built Zvezda Arc7s delivering straight to the Chinese buyer (0.49 Mt per hull per year). Train 1 runs at the rate those hulls allow, a 30% duty factor with 4 hulls, and Trains 2–3 become a spares pool. Train 3 is not commissioned.
VALUE: Yamal LNG's full cost falls from about $657/t to about $387/t delivered (ratio 0.59) against realised prices of $442–494/t. Arctic LNG 2 earns about $110–160/t in cash on each delivered tonne, but its full cost (about $1,216/t with 4 hulls) stays far above price until about 33 Zvezda hulls exist.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: Onward hulls: the plan needs about 40–50 conventional LNG carriers loading in Russian waters with no EU services at all. From 1 Jan 2027 the EU 20th package bans services to foreign-owned LNG tankers operating in Russia, which covers class, insurance and repair. None of those hulls are identified or costed, and none are Russian-built. (232 STS cargoes a year (168 winter + 64 summer). Round trip to China is 62 days via Suez or 79 via the Cape, so 39–50 carriers. [Latvian MFA](https://www.mfa.gov.lv/en/article/20th-package-eu-sanctions-against-russia), [Baltic Exchange](https://www.balticexchange.com/en/news-and-events/news/member-news/2026/EU-adopts-20th-sanctions-package-against-Russia.html))
- MAJOR: Arc7 fleet: "15 existing Arc7s" are Korean-built (DSME), which breaks the Russian-built-hulls rule. Five are run by Greek Dynagas, inside the EU ban. Denmark's Fayard is the last EU yard servicing them. With 10 hulls the margins disappear. (With 10 hulls: winter capacity 147–172 cargoes against 168 needed, and summer 1,530 hull-days against 2,096 needed. [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/eus-next-russia-sanctions-package-adrift-following-greek-veto-to-protect-arctic-lng-shipping-interests/), [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/07/16/eu-sanctions-on-russia-meet-resistance-from-greek-shipping-sector))
- MAJOR: STS throughput at Kildin is assumed, not demonstrated. Each LNG transfer takes 36–48 h, so winter needs 1.2–2.3 transfer pairs alongside at all times, in Barents winter weather. (Annual transfers needed are 232, about 4.2× the "over 55 reloadings" forecast for Kildin. A dual STS was reported as "uncommon" in March 2025. [Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/shipping-news/russia-begins-ship-to-ship-lng-transfers-ahead-of-european-unions-ban-on-transhipments/), [Worldports](https://www.worldports.org/?p=633741))
- MAJOR: The $657 → $387/t "ratio 0.59" is measured against a strawman: Arc7s carrying every cargo to Asia, giving 7.4 Mt/y. Novatek already delivers about 19.6–21 Mt/y using Kildin STS plus summer NSR-east cargoes. The idea's $387 also leaves out onward freight to Asia. (Onward freight is about $75–100/t, against about $35–50/t for an Arc7 round trip to Zeebrugge before the ban. Corrected cost is about $430–450/t, close to the $442–494 realised price. [Baird](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/tankers/yamal-lng-ramps-up-ship-to-ship-transfers-near-murmansk), [Energy Intel](https://www.energyintel.com/0000017c-bcfb-dd37-af7e-befb56280000))
- MAJOR: "Full cost covered at about 33 Zvezda hulls" contradicts the idea's own plan to leave Train 3 uncommissioned. Trains 1–2 can only fill about 27 hulls. At that maximum, full cost is still above the discounted price, and even more so once hull capital is added. (33 × 0.49 = 16.2 Mt/y > 13.2 Mt/y. At 13.2 Mt/y: about $325/t excluding hulls against about $304/t price (0.65 × $468). Hull capital adds $79–101/t at $350–450m per hull. Break-even is never reached.)
- MAJOR: Arctic LNG 2 direct-only shipping delivers less than current practice. Current practice is Russian-built Arc7s shuttling to the Saam FSU, then onward sanctioned hulls. Kosygin shuttles about 250 kt a month in winter. (2026 actual: at least 31 cargoes, 2.3 Mt to about Sep, ≈ 3.2 Mt/y annualised, against 1.96 Mt/y for the idea's 4 direct hulls. [The Insider](https://theins.press/en/news/288111), [Baird](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/tankers/gas/china-receives-first-gas-in-2026-from-russias-arctic-lng-2-project))
- MAJOR: Operating Train 1 at 30% duty is a hazard the idea does not mention. A train cannot run steadily at 30%, so it must run in on/off campaigns. One GBS stores roughly 5–6 days of output (my rough figure, not sourced), while hulls arrive every 13–20 days. That means repeated start-stop cycles on the coil-wound exchanger and the Western-made gas turbines, which get no OEM service. Each cycle brings flaring and freezing risk at the wells. (Storage about 103 kt ÷ 18 kt/d ≈ 5.7 days, giving about 18–26 cold cycles a year.)
- SURVIVES: 19.7 Mt ≈ 272 cargoes, so winter is about 168 cargoes. 9.7–11.4 hulls implies a 12.3–14.4-day Sabetta–Kildin cycle, which is plausible. Summer 40 NSR + 64 shuttle needs about 2,096 of 2,295 hull-days. 4 × 0.49 / 6.6 = 30% duty. 657/387 arithmetic is correct.
- RATIO: 1.0 | For Yamal this is Novatek's existing Kildin + NSR-east practice, with onward freight now included. Arctic LNG 2 direct-only shipping delivers about 40% less than 2026 practice.
- KNOWN: yes | Novatek has run Kildin STS since 2020 and scaled it up in 2024–25 after the EU transshipment ban. Kosygin has delivered Arctic LNG 2 cargo to Beihai. [Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/shipping-news/russia-begins-ship-to-ship-lng-transfers-ahead-of-european-unions-ban-on-transhipments/), [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/russian-lng-carrier-alexey-kosygin-makes-first-delivery-amid-looming-arctic-fleet-crunch/)
