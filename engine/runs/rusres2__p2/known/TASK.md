THE 4 IDEAS:
IDEA 1
NAME: Freeze Train 3 until hulls
MECHANISM: Arctic LNG 2 Train 3 spending is gated on a hull ledger. Spending resumes only when (Arc7 hulls delivered × 2.6 Mt/y) exceeds the 13.2 Mt/y of Trains 1–2. With Zvezda delivering about 2 hulls/yr, that is about 2029–30. The GTE-170 units and the train module meanwhile go to hull-limited Trains 1–2 as spares. The closest earlier attempt is Arctic LNG 2 itself, which built trains ahead of ships and left Train 1 idle. This gate makes ship deliveries the trigger for train capex. The deciding quantity is the Zvezda delivery rate, which depends on finding a substitute for the unavailable GTT membranes.
VALUE: Avoids several years of idle-capital annuity, about $0.8bn per year on a Train 3 cost of about $7bn (est.), without losing any delivered tonne.

IDEA 2
NAME: Swing trains to hull-days
MECHANISM: Put the four Zvezda Arc7 carriers (two in service, two due late 2026) on a fixed Utrenneye–Ura Bay ice-leg shuttle only. Sell the cargoes FOB at the Saam FSU, and run Trains 1–2 at the shuttle-limited rate from Jan to Jul. Run both trains at design rate in the ice-free Ob Gulf months, when buyer-chartered open-water hulls can load at the berth. Defer Train 3, and use its domestic modules and Kazanskompressormash and Power Machines units as spares to lift availability on Trains 1–2. The closest earlier attempts are Kosygin's ad-hoc Ura Bay runs and Yamal LNG's Zeebrugge trans-shipment, which used Arc7 hulls on long open-water legs. Here the hulls never leave the ice leg. The deciding quantity is the winter shuttle cycle: about 14 days, implied by 2 hulls × 26 trips × 77 kt ≈ 4 Mt/y.
VALUE: Output roughly doubles to 8–9 Mt/y within 0–1 year. Capital falls from about $620/t to about $300/t, which makes marginal cargoes cash-positive at the $390/t realised price. All-in cost stays above price because the $190/t discount remains.

IDEA 3
NAME: Short-cycle Arc7 LNG shuttle
MECHANISM: Every Zvezda Arc7 hull runs only the ice leg, Utrenneye to the Ura Bay/Murmansk FSU, year-round. The exception is July–November, when hulls go east to China direct over the NSR if that beats two shuttle cycles. The closest earlier attempt is Alexey Kosygin's Ura Bay runs (F140), which stopped short because only one or two hulls existed and they were also used on long hauls. This idea makes the short cycle a fixed rule for each new hull, so each delivery adds about 2 Mt/y instead of about 1. The deciding quantity is the winter round-trip time; it must be 14 days or less.
VALUE: Built capacity in use rises from about 30% to about 60% with 4 hulls, and capital per tonne halves (est.), as long as a lawful non-ice onward hull exists.

IDEA 4
NAME: Size Sever to tankers
MECHANISM: Payakha and Vankor volumes are allocated each month. Bukhta Sever receives only what the Arc7 tanker fleet can lift, about 2.1 Mt/y per 70 kt hull on a roughly 12-day Murmansk cycle. The surplus goes south through the existing Vankor–Purpe pipeline to domestic refineries, which are legal payers with no discount. The closest earlier attempt is the 30 Mt/y H2 2027 target, which assumes about 14–15 hulls that do not yet exist and sanctions-exposed buyers. The deciding quantity is spare southbound Transneft capacity in Mt/y.
VALUE: Turns stranded Arctic barrels into domestically sold tonnes now and brings the Arctic route to full output only as hulls arrive.
