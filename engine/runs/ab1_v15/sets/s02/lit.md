L01 | PARTIAL | closest work: Machine learning conservation laws from trajectories, Liu & Tegmark (2021) https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.126.180604 ; Hidden particles from conservation patterns in reaction data, Schulte (IJCAI 2009) https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/09/Papers/248.pdf (also ConservNet, Ha & Jeong 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04008) | New is the use of a debit/credit ledger over learned conserved quantities as the per-step state tracker for language and reasoning, with the imbalance as the training signal and hidden holders spawned only if they later balance other transfers. Learned conserved quantities and hidden-particle inference from imbalance both already exist, in physics settings.
L02 | PARTIAL | closest work: Statistical Ranking and Combinatorial Hodge Theory, Jiang, Lim, Yao & Ye (2011) https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.1067 ; Sheaf Cohomology of Linear Predictive Coding Networks, Seely (2025/26) https://proceedings.mlr.press/v282/seely26a.html (also Topological Obstructions to Autoencoding, Batson et al. 2021, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.08380) | New is closing the loop: using the curl and harmonic parts of the cycle residuals of learned maps to decide between deleting or rewiring edges and creating a context variable that splits the domain into charts with transition maps. Hodge decomposition of cyclic inconsistency, cohomology of learned restriction maps and multi-chart atlases all exist separately, but I found no work that chains them into one repair loop.
L03 | PARTIAL | closest work: Ensemble Kalman Inversion: a derivative-free technique for machine learning tasks, Kovachki & Stuart (2019) https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03620 ; Low-rank extended Kalman filtering for online learning of neural networks from streaming data, Chang et al. (2023) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19535 (also LoKO, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11551) | New is the combination of an ensemble of about 64 copies of a parameter subset, a gain taken from the parameter-prediction cross-covariance, a concept-space localization taper, and re-spreading of the ensemble, applied to fact and correction updates. EnKF-style training and Kalman online learning of networks exist, but I found no Kalman-style knowledge editing for LLMs; current editing work, such as LyapLock and spectral collapse papers, uses preservation penalties or orthogonality constraints.
L04 | PARTIAL | closest work: Baarda's reliability theory (w-test, redundancy numbers), reviewed in A half century of Baarda's concept of reliability https://agrimensura.florianopolis.ifsc.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/A-half-century-of-Baarda-s-concept-of-reliability-a-review-new-perspectives-and-applications.pdf ; NCV: Node-Wise Consistency Verification for Error Localization in LLM Reasoning (2025) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02816 (also Graph of Verification, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12509) | New is importing least-squares adjustment, w-test blunder localization and redundancy numbers as the checking machinery for multi-route derivations of reasoning claims. The statistics are textbook geodesy, and LLM step-level checks exist, but I found none that use misclosure adjustment or redundancy numbers. Note that the w-test detects one outlier more reliably than it identifies it, and it degrades with several errors or correlated observations, which LLM errors usually are.
L05 | PARTIAL | closest work: Kinetic proofreading, Hopfield (1974) and Ninio (1975) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_proofreading ; Solving a Million-Step LLM Task with Zero Errors (MAKER), Meyerson et al. (2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09030v1 | New is the LLM instantiation: staged re-encoding (paraphrase, dropout, abstraction) with irreversible discard of low-margin steps, and choosing n by how much downstream work depends on the step. The ε^n scaling from sequential irreversible discard stages is exactly kinetic proofreading, and MAKER already gets exponentially falling error per step from voting. The independence assumption behind ε^n is doubtful for LLMs because the Graduated Dissent preprint and the MAKER red-flagging results both point to correlated errors.
L06 | PARTIAL | closest work: Uniqueness of Low-Rank Matrix Completion by Rigidity Theory, Singer & Cucuringu (2010) https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3846 ; Embeddability and Stresses of Graphs, Connelly and others, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411009 (rigidity-matrix null space and stress space) | New is applying rigidity analysis to concept embeddings built from learned facts, then turning each floppy mode into a latent variable or query and each self-stress into a node split. The rigidity-matrix test for under- and over-determination is established in matrix completion and sensor-network localization. I found no application to concept or knowledge-graph embeddings; the knowledge-graph results were only a spring-model embedding paper and a loose Ricci-flow mention. Splitting a conflicted concept into senses is also known from multi-sense embeddings, which I did not search.
NOTE: Every idea except possibly L01 imports an established method from another field (geodesy, kinetic proofreading, data assimilation, rigidity theory, Hodge/sheaf theory, physics conservation laws), and I found no exact prior for any of them as an LLM mechanism. All six are therefore PARTIAL, with the novelty in the transplant and the repair loop, not in the core mathematics. L05 overlaps most with existing work (kinetic proofreading plus MAKER), while L02 and L06 are the least anticipated as AI mechanisms. L03 and L04 name unverified efficacy assumptions that would decide whether they work: a localization taper that actually blocks interference between facts, and error independence in LLM verification. Results came from 20 short web searches, 2-3 per idea, so absence of a hit is weak evidence.
