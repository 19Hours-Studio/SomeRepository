=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Single-use ice barges on spring flood
DEFECT | FATAL | The hull melts before the cargo can be moved to a ship. The 7 cm/day melt rate assumes 0.5 °C water, but the lower Lena averages about 2.7 °C in June, and seagoing ships only reach the delta after it breaks up at the end of June. So the hull has to sit in warming water for weeks after a 7–17 day trip. | Weeks–Campbell melt formula, 60 m hull, water flowing past at 0.1–0.3 m/s, ΔT 2.7 °C: 0.11–0.26 m/day. Over 17 days that is 1.9–4.4 m, more than the 1.5 m wall. At ΔT 6–10 °C in July it is 0.24–0.98 m/day. June mean 2.7 °C at the outlet ([Liu et al.](https://iahs.info/uploads/dms/16848.16-71-76-346-29_Baozhong_Liu-H02Corr.pdf)); delta breakup at end of June ([Runeberg](https://runeberg.org/jssiberia/0082.html))
DEFECT | FATAL | Ice or pykrete is too weak to survive the bending loads a loaded river hull meets when it grounds or rides on a jam or floe. | U-section with walls and floor 1.5 m thick: section modulus about 15 m³. Grounded at midship (WL/4): 773 MN·m, which is about 51 MPa. Ends resting on a shoal (WL/8): about 25 MPa. Even uneven still-water loading gives about 1.3 MPa. Ice flexural strength is about 0.5–1 MPa and pykrete about 5 MPa, and both are weaker near 0 °C.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The creep protection depends on keeping pykrete below −16 °C, which cannot happen in May–July. The text's own figure of 24 days for warmth to soak through means the whole wall is near 0 °C by the end of the trip. | t = L²/α = 1.5²/1.2e-6 s ≈ 21.7 days (reproduces). The −16 °C condition fails from day 0 at the wetted faces.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A 2,740 t hull cannot be built on top of the river ice sheet. It has to be cut into the sheet and float, so the site needs at least about 1.8 m of liquid water under 1.5–2 m of ice. That means 3.5–4 m of winter depth at the mine bank, and this is not checked. | Gold's formula P = A·h², h = 150 cm, A = 3.5–7: 79–158 t allowed against a 2,740 t hull. Empty-hull draft is 2,740/1,500 m² ≈ 1.83 m.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is wrong. The $29/t covers only the hull and tug and leaves out transshipment at the mouth, waiting time and the risk of losing cargo. It is compared with a 2010 Lena average freight of unknown distance. It also ignores the railhead now at Nizhny Bestyakh, the obvious route south for Yakutsk-area bulk. | $29/t × 2,500 t = $72.5k per hull, about $24/m³ of ice including the tug. That figure is assumed, not sourced. Losing one 2,500 t concentrate load wipes out the saving on many hulls.
DEFECT | MINOR | The drift-speed figures contradict each other. | 1–2.5 m/s = 86–216 km/day (which gives the stated 7–17 days). The stated 160–290 km/day would need 1.85–3.36 m/s.
SURVIVES | Ice volume 2,988 m³, displacement 5,250 t, payload 2,510 t, payload/ice 0.92 against 0.09 for a solid slab, and a 22-day build at 0.2 m/day all reproduce.
RATIO | <1 | The hull does not survive to transshipment, so no product is delivered.
KNOWN | partly | Project Habakkuk built and tested a 1,000 t refrigerated pykrete prototype at Patricia Lake in 1943 (Wikipedia, "Project Habakkuk"). Siberian rivers also have a history of one-way flood-season craft (kayuk/pauzok, [ESBE](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/%D0%AD%D0%A1%D0%91%D0%95/%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%8E%D0%BA)). I did not confirm that pauzki were scrapped after one trip.
NEW FACT | Mean water temperature at the Lena outlet is 2.7 °C in June. The June–September mean is 9.7 °C at Kyusyur. | [Liu et al.](https://iahs.info/uploads/dms/16848.16-71-76-346-29_Baozhong_Liu-H02Corr.pdf), [Fofonova et al., HESS preprint](https://hess.copernicus.org/preprints/hess-2016-254/hess-2016-254.pdf)

IDEA 2: Winter-cold salt removal for lithium brines
DEFECT | FATAL | The chemistry is wrong. Kovykta brines are saturated magnesium–calcium chloride brines at a 35–40 °C reservoir temperature. Cooling them precipitates antarcticite (CaCl₂·6H₂O) and tachhydrite, not mainly sodium and potassium salts. The patented freezing method is for sodium/potassium salts in carbonate-type brines. | Sergeeva et al. 2025: antarcticite and tachhydrite are the main phases, and precipitation is negligible only above 55 °C ([PMI](https://pmi.spmi.ru/pmi/article/view/16629?setLocale=en_US)). Antarcticite grows up to 86% in the wellbore ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0742046326700053)).
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Won't freeze at −40 °C" does not mean "stays liquid". The brine turns into a salt-hydrate slush that blocks sprays and ponds. The enrichment is small, and lithium is lost in the liquor trapped in the crystals. | Lever rule, 37 wt% CaCl₂ cooled to −40 °C (liquidus about 31%): about 30% of the mass crystallises. Lithium enrichment is only about 1.3x. With 10–20% liquor trapped in the cake, 3–6% of the lithium is lost.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The waste is huge and the process only runs in winter. | 20 kt/yr LCE at 0.4 g/L Li and 80% recovery needs about 11.7 Mm³, or about 16 Mt, of brine a year. That gives about 4.9 Mt/yr of hexahydrate to dispose of on taiga. About 7 months of brine output must be stored for the 5-month cold season.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is a straw man. Nobody evaporates these brines at 2.26 GJ/t. Current practice is direct lithium extraction (DLE) with sorbents on raw brine, and Gazprom is building exactly that pilot at Kovykta. Mechanical vapour recompression (MVR) also needs about 10x less energy than the latent heat. | [Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/88591/)
DEFECT | MINOR | "Up to 600 mg/L Li" is not sourced. I could not find a published Kovykta lithium assay. | none found
SURVIVES | The lab chilling test is cheap and valid for measuring equilibrium enrichment. It does not show what happens with spraying or pond kinetics.
RATIO | about 1.0 | DLE flow drops about 30%, offset by about 5 Mt/yr of waste salt handling and seasonal brine storage.
KNOWN | partly | Natural winter-cold crystallisation (mirabilite, Na/K salts) is used on Chinese and Tibetan salt lakes ([Frontiers, Zabuye](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.865158/text)). Freeze-enrichment of lithium is patented ([US9932241](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9932241)).
NEW FACT | Kovykta lithium is classed as hard-to-recover reserves. Modelled output is 4.2–31.2 t Li per well. | [PMI 2025](https://pmi.spmi.ru/pmi/article/view/16629?setLocale=en_US)

IDEA 3: Self-heated barge boilerhouse
DEFECT | MAJOR | The saving ignores the cost of tying up a barge for a whole year. A coal barge that would otherwise make several legs a season becomes a stationary coal store. | 3 MW boiler at 75% efficiency burns about 0.65 t/h, about 1,900 t a season. If the barge loses about 5 legs × 1,000 t × about 2,100 rub/t (Idea 5's rate), that is about 10.5M rub/yr, or about 5,500 rub/t of coal burned. That is more than the 0.8–3.4k rub/t saving.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The uncovered 36 kW figure only holds for about 75 m² of open water, roughly the moat around a hull 25×12 m. A barge holding 1,900 t needs a perimeter of about 170 m, which means about 80 kW. | Open water loses about 480 W/m² at −30 °C with 5 m/s wind ([Makinson Inlet](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/arctic/article/view/65922)), and 250–575 W/m² measured in polynyas ([Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/product/E2DC0D1793BEF5CECAC951D5D90E7384/core-reader)). Even 80 kW is still under 3% of output, so the heat itself is not a showstopper.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The shore links are not costed. The heat main and power links to shore must cope with the river level rising and falling by several metres between winter and the spring flood, and with breakup ice. A boiler on a vessel also counts as a hazardous production facility and needs River Register classification. | no costing given
DEFECT | MINOR | The "0.3%" compares 8 kW with the 3 MW output, not with the 480 kW flue loss. | 8/3000 = 0.27%; 8/480 = 1.7%
SURVIVES | 15–23k rub/t with a 0.8–3.4k saving gives 1.04–1.29x (reproduces). Wintering a barge in a backwater (zaton) is routine practice.
RATIO | about 1.0 | The 1.04–1.3x is cancelled by the barge's lost capacity unless the hull is already surplus.
KNOWN | partly | Floating power and heat plants exist (Akademik Lomonosov, floating diesel plants in Yakutia). I did not find a coal-barge boilerhouse.

IDEA 4: Kite-towed sled trains, no roads
DEFECT | FATAL | The operating window excludes the named sites. The idea itself says friction rises sharply below −30 °C, and the Taymyr coast averages −31.2 °C in January, with polar night from November to January. The Anabar diamond mines are inland, which the idea itself calls the calm zone. | Khatanga January mean −31.2 °C ([kupi](https://www.kupi.com/en-ae/explore/russian-federation/khatanga/weather)). Steel runners drag hard on granular snow at about −36 °C ([J. Glaciol. 1988](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/product/BD1C7C2AE10781B76B2B96F26FFA9F34/core-reader)).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The 32 t pull is a single press quote and a peak at ideal wind. SkySails' own figures elsewhere give 16 t for 320 m². Average pull, limited by wind direction relative to the route, is a fraction of peak. | With 16 t and μ = 0.15–0.25: 64–107 t gross, about 45–75 t payload, not 320–640 t ([ing.dk](https://ing.dk/artikel/kaempedrage-paa-320-kvadratmeter-skal-traekke-fragtskib), [The Local](https://www.thelocal.de/20110415/34411)).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Scaling up from the Windsled is about 30x in kite area and 100–300x in load. Gust and braking control of a 300+ t train on sea ice is not addressed. Kite force scales with wind speed squared, so a 1.4x gust doubles the pull. | The Windsled's largest kite is 150 m² and its maximum cargo is 2 t, not 2–3 t ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WindSled), [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/now-this-is-kiting/))
DEFECT | MAJOR | With tractor backup still needed, the only real saving is fuel. Crews, tractors and sleds stay, and kite capital cost is added. | Fuel is typically 30–40% of truck cost (assumed). Upper bound about 1.4x before kite cost.
SURVIVES | 32/0.1 = 320 t and 32/0.05 = 640 t (arithmetic only).
RATIO | about 1.0 (0.8–1.2) | Fuel saving only, with backup tractors, kite capital cost and payload at least 4x lower.
KNOWN | partly | Inuit WindSled, which carries cargo and runs science platforms in Antarctica and Greenland ([windsled.org](https://windsled.org/concept/)).
NEW FACT | About 480–528 kt a season moves on Yakutian winter roads, and the plan was only 55–72% met. | [EastRussia](https://www.eastrussia.ru/news/plan-gruzoperevozok-po-avtozimnikam-vypolnili-bolee-chem-na-70-v-yakutii/)

IDEA 5: Fuel on empty coal-barge legs
DEFECT | MAJOR | Under ADN rules, a 42 t (about 50 m³) bladder is far beyond the 3 m³ limit for a packaged container. In practice this is bulk oil in an unclassed dry-cargo barge, and an open coal hold is not a certified containment. | IBC limit 3 m³, against 24 bladders × 50 m³ ≈ 1,200 m³ per leg.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The whole-chain gain is small. The text says the river leg is under 10% of the delivered price, so a saving of 0.6–9.9k rub/t on a delivered price of at least 27–120k rub/t is only 1.02–1.09x. | arithmetic
DEFECT | MINOR | Bladder capital, wear from coal residue, pumping at both ends and the timing of legs are not costed. Only legs after the sea tanker reaches the lower Kolyma, in the late-summer shoal season, can be used. | Roughly 100 × 0.3–0.6M rub bladders over 5–7 years adds about 300–700 rub/t (assumed price).
SURVIVES | 3/165 = 1.8%, 17 legs × 24 × 42 t = 17.1 kt, and 2,700/2,100 to 12,000/2,100 = 1.3–5.7x on the river leg all reproduce. Bladders rated to −60 °C exist ([bizon](https://bizon.ru/news/print/id/398842/format/smoothbox)).
RATIO | about 1.05 | Whole delivered chain, before the added bladder cost.
KNOWN | partly | Polymer fuel bladders are already used for Arctic fuel stores in the Northern Delivery ([runeft](https://runeft.ru/upload/iblock/f72/f727753cec5a70f565c35ec6818fe6c5.pdf)). Carrying them on barge backhauls is not documented.

IDEA 6: Pre-staged flood-crest barges
DEFECT | MAJOR | Holding the cargo for 7–8 months between autumn loading and the spring flood costs about as much as the saving. | 80k rub/t fuel × about 15% interest × 0.67 yr ≈ 8k rub/t, against a 1.6–8.2k rub/t leg saving.
DEFECT | MAJOR | A 2.5 m backwater is too shallow for a loaded barge under 1.5–2 m of ice with a winter level drop. The hull freezes in or grounds, and ice pressure on a loaded hull is not addressed. | 2.5 − 1.8 m ice − 0.5 m drawdown ≈ 0.2 m of water left under the ice, less than a loaded draft of about 1.2–1.6 m (draft assumed).
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline assumes a convoy that arrives 5–10 days late. A competent operator already winters vessels at tributary mouths and leaves on the first high water, so the 1.5–3.6x compares against bad practice. | The "12-day recession" model is not sourced. A linear recession gives 42–83% lost, not 35–62%.
SURVIVES | 4,700/3,100 to 11,300/3,100 = 1.5–3.6x reproduces as leg arithmetic, as does the roughly 14% main-stem gain.
RATIO | about 1.05 | Whole chain after cargo holding cost and against a well-timed convoy.
KNOWN | yes | Wintering loaded or ready vessels in sheltered spots (zaton backwaters) to catch the spring high water is standard Northern Delivery practice on small Yakutian rivers. I did not find a source document for it.
