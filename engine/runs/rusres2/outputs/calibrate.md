=== CALIBRATION ===

P | 5 | 20% | The most corrected and integrated candidate. Its oil and gas part honestly reproduces current practice. It still carries three unanswered MAJORs: the 2028 tank-farm timing, the unbuilt road (about 10,500 loads a year) and an energy balance that runs at a 93% load factor. Its Norilsk part relies on grey imports, which the task forbids, so only the copper bridge counts, and that is worth about 1.02× at best.

P | 3 | 7% | It avoids the all-season road. However, Pevek-plus-mine storage needs twice the tankage, Kupol's winter road gives about 95 days rather than 130, and 69 loads a day is 3.6× Kupol's record. Together these turn the expected value to about +$16m, or negative at low prices.

P | 2 | 4% | Superseded by IDEA 5. Output is overstated by about 23%, the concentrate back-haul on fuel tankers is impossible, and the timing defect remains.

P | 1 | 4% | Rosneft already does this: the first Bukhta Sever cargo was a Vankor–Payakha blend. Running Payakha oil alone at 1.5–3% of trunk design raises wax and minimum-throughput risk. Ratio 1.00.

P | 4 | 3% | It depends on grey imports, which breaks the constraints. The Poisson pooling figures do not reproduce, and the 6.3 t per LHD-hour figure contradicts hoist-limited mines.

P | none | 62% | Every candidate's ratio after review is 1.00–1.005, which is not a material gain over best practice. Stages with larger possible gains were never tested as candidates: thaw-resilient foundations, processing order and Tomtor or diamond sequencing.

WEAKEST LINK: The copper bridge in IDEA 5 needs 173k m³ of fuel tankage on thermosyphon piles. That tankage would have to be built, frozen in and filled before the last Pevek ship of 2028. Piles set in summer 2028 do not freeze until the following winter, which pushes the bridge to 2030, when the floating units are due anyway.

FLIP TEST: Two observations matter by Q4 2027: how many km of the 800 km Baimskaya–Nagleynyn road are open, and the mooring-trial status of FNPP units 1–2.
- If the road is at 600 km or less, or units 1–2 are on schedule, IDEA 5 drops below 8% and "none" rises above 75%.
- If the road is complete and the units have slipped at least a year, IDEA 5 rises to about 40%.

UNKNOWN: Even if IDEA 5 is right, the size of the gain is unknown. It depends on the real slip in the floating units, on whether domestic Kolomna or UDMZ gensets can be delivered at 80 MW scale, and on Pevek's real spare tankage. No public data pins down any of these.
