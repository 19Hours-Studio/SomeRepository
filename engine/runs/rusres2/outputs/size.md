SIZE: split

WHY: The listed resources are limited by different things. Remote north-east metals are limited by power, access and external infrastructure capital before first production. Arctic hydrocarbons are limited by ice-class hulls and sanctioned buyers, not plant cost (Arctic LNG 2 is about 13 Mt/y idle). Norilsk is a producing brownfield limited by processing capital, equipment substitution, declining grades and the life of old structures. One mechanism cannot move all three, and past runs show a single answer for everything shifts the whole chain by only 1–6%.

PART 1: For greenfield metal deposits in north-east Russia that have no grid or road, which changes to power supply, construction method, access logistics or development order would cut full cost per unit delivered and the years from investment decision to first metal? The comparison is Kupol/Dvoinoye, Natalka, Baimskaya's 2020 plan, Red Dog and Meliadine. The answer must use only domestic or unrestricted equipment, and it must work through the period when Bilibino has closed and the floating units are not yet running (2026–2030). | CASES: Baimskaya copper-gold (Pevek/Nagleynyn route, Rosatom floating power units 2028–30); Chukotka and Magadan hard-rock and placer gold (Kupol, Natalka, Kolyma placers); Tomtor rare earths (Rosneft, pilot 2027, Anabar/Lena river access). | MEASURE: (a) full cost per oz Au or per t Cu or rare-earth oxide delivered, including annualised external-infrastructure capex; (b) years from investment decision to first production, against Natalka (about 4–5 years, then 2 to full rate) and Baimskaya (end-2027 target, already slipped); (c) recovery, operating days per year, and life of tailings, dams and foundations in discontinuous or warm permafrost. | BINDING: Firm power in the 2026–2030 gap (about 8 MW of winter spare in Chukotka), the capex of external road and port (Baimskaya rose from $5.5bn to $8bn, about $600m of it road and port), and short seasonal supply windows (winter roads, Pevek's 4–5-month navigation).

PART 2: For Yamal, Gydan and Taimyr oil and gas, which changes to shipping, trans-shipment, product form or field sequencing would raise delivered tonnes per year from capacity already built or sanctioned, and cut full cost per tonne delivered to a buyer that can legally pay? The comparison is Yamal LNG, Arctic LNG 2 and Vostok Oil/Vankor. The answer must use only Russian-built hulls, liquefaction trains and equipment (Zvezda, Arctic Cascade/Mix, Kazanskompressormash, Kriogenmash, Power Machines) and must not depend on getting around sanctions. | CASES: Arctic LNG 2 trains 1–3 at Utrenneye; Yamal LNG; Murmansk/Ob LNG concepts; Vostok Oil (Payakha, Bukhta Sever terminal, 30 Mt/y target in H2 2027); Taimyr coking coal from Syradasayskoye via Dikson, which shares the Kara Sea route, icebreakers and season. | MEASURE: (a) full cost per tonne delivered, including idle sunk capex, trans-shipment and buyer discount (Arctic LNG 2 cargoes sell 30–40% below market); (b) years to first delivered cargo or added cargo; (c) utilisation of built capacity, Kara Sea and Ob Gulf operating days per year for each hull class, and life of pads and pipelines on ice-rich Yamal ground. | BINDING: Ice-class hulls (2 Arc7 LNG carriers available to Russia, 21 contracted), winter access to the Ob Gulf, which only Arc7 ships can reach, and legal access to buyers (EU LNG ban and EU tanker and icebreaker service bans from 2027; Rosneft on the US SDN list since Oct 2025).

PART 3: For the producing Norilsk/Taimyr nickel-copper-PGM complex, which changes to mining or development order, processing of lower-grade and disseminated ore, equipment substitution, or foundation design and monitoring would cut full cost per tonne of nickel-equivalent delivered and raise recovery and structure life? The comparison is the current Norilsk practice (Talnakh concentrator expansion, Sulphur Programme), Raglan and Kiruna/Aitik. It must not overstate thaw where engineering faults (Ambarnaya, the 2021 concentrator collapse) were the cause. | CASES: Talnakh/Oktyabrskoye rich and cuprous ore; Norilsk-1 disseminated ore; Maslovskoye (low grade, rich in PGM); Nadezhda and Talnakh processing; Dudinka-Murmansk delivery; ageing industrial and residential structures on Norilsk ground. | MEASURE: (a) full cost per tonne of nickel-equivalent delivered, where capital is about 37% of the total and foundation losses about 1–2%; (b) years from investment decision to added output or capacity, against Talnakh modernisation (47bn RUB for +34%) and the Sulphur Programme's slip of at least 2 years; (c) recovery for each ore type, plant availability, and design life of foundations against the 1 cm/yr deepening of thaw and Nornickel's thermometric borehole forecasts to 2050. | BINDING: Sustaining and processing capital with foreign contractors and equipment gone (Sulphur Programme cost +40–70%), falling grade as Maslovskoye and disseminated ore come in, and the life of structures where maintenance and design faults count as much as thaw.

LEFT OUT:
- **Yakutia diamonds and Arkhangelsk (Grib, Lomonosov):** limited by the G7/EU ban and a falling market. Alrosa is curtailing output (25–26 Mct guidance in 2026), so adding output has negative value, and Arkhangelsk is probably not permafrost.
- **Elga and other Yakutia coal:** already rail- and port-connected (about 35 Mt in 2025). Its limits are China's import duties, rail tariffs and price, not cold-region engineering.
- **Udokan:** on the BAM railway and already in production. Its remaining issues (hydrometallurgy ramp-up after the fire, a seismic rating of 9 or more) are specific to the plant, not to remote permafrost logistics.
- **Polyus Krasnoyarsk and Irkutsk assets (Olimpiada, Sukhoi Log):** not in the listed permafrost scope.

INTERFACES:
- **Icebreakers:** the four project 22220 icebreakers in service, with Chukotka due in 2027, serve Part 2 (Ob Gulf, Kara Sea), Part 3 (year-round Dudinka) and Part 1 (Pevek in the eastern sector).
- **Shipyards:** Zvezda builds Arc7 LNG and oil carriers (Part 2) and would also build any ice-class bulk or concentrate carriers (Parts 1 and 3). Baltiysky Zavod builds both the 22220 icebreakers and Baimskaya's RITM-200S floating power units, so Parts 1 and 2 compete directly for its slots.
- **Rosatom:** it is the single northern-delivery operator, the NSR operator and the Part 1 power supplier at the same time.
- **Capital:** all parts draw on state-backed project finance at a 14% key rate (VEB, Gazprombank, Sber). The windfall tax hits Parts 1 and 3, not oil, gas or coal, and Arctic Zone tax breaks apply to Parts 1 and 3 but not LNG.
- **Domestic equipment makers:** Power Machines gas turbines (GTE-170) and compressor and cryogenic makers serve Part 2 power and LNG, while heavy mining trucks and excavators (BelAZ, Chinese suppliers) serve Parts 1 and 3. Serial output is small, measured in single units.
- **Workers:** all parts draw on the same shrinking rotation-worker pool on northern allowances (Magadan's population is falling), and Nornickel's wage level (2.1 times the national average) sets the price the other parts must match.
- **Permafrost standards:** SP 25.13330 Amendment 3 (June 2026), GET/VET thermosiphons and monitoring under Decree 854 apply to Yamal pads (Part 2), Norilsk (Part 3) and NE tailings (Part 1). A validated design method developed for one transfers to the others.
