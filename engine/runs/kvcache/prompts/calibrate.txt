Which KV-cache design (storage layout, compression, eviction, quantization, offloading or reuse) can be swapped into an existing pretrained transformer LLM with no retraining and no fine-tuning, so that the same model gives the same outputs with a smaller, faster or cheaper cache? The asker wants to take any model and replace one component with another. This design should be the best across memory, speed and quality. "Best" is not defined. The answer should say which metric or workload it is best for, and where it stops working. Plausible workloads are long-context single requests, multi-user serving, multi-turn chat and prefix reuse. The asker did not give a model family, hardware, context length or serving stack. The answer may be a ranking of existing approaches, a new combination of them, or a new idea. Each claim must be checkable against published benchmarks or an experiment that can be run.
A good answer: 
- It names the criteria that define "best" (memory saved, throughput or latency, accuracy loss, calibration cost) and the workload each applies to.
- It checks "no training, any model" against published evidence: which models and architectures it was tested on, and what happens on an architecture it was not tested on.
- It gives numbers (compression ratio, accuracy change on benchmarks such as RULER, LongBench or multi-turn tasks, throughput) with sources.
- It states the conditions under which it fails, including a compression level, a task type and a model type.
- If it proposes something new, it gives a test that could show the idea is wrong.
Constraints: 
- No retraining and no fine-tuning of the model weights.
- It should be near plug-and-play and work on "any" model. See ASSUMPTIONS on whether that is possible.

The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: FLOP-per-byte bounded-tier admission
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: score cached prefixes by (expected reuses × recompute FLOPs) ÷ bytes, with exact reuse only. The repair applies the score only where capacity or writes are actually bounded, since the replay confirmed it gives 0% at ≥25M tokens. The first place is the HBM-only prefix cache, as leaf-only eviction with a 180 s reuse half-life. On replayed Mooncake traces at 0.5–1M tokens it saves 43–75% more prefill FLOPs than LRU for Llama-8B and 156–207% more for DeepSeek-V3 (MLA), and it is never more than 5% worse at any capacity. The second place is SSD admission under an endurance budget. There the score must cover the whole prefix chain, because per-block admission keeps deep blocks whose ancestors are missing and gets about zero value. For DeepSeek-V3 it keeps 37–95% more SSD-hit FLOPs per byte written than random admission at a 10–35% write budget. In $ form the score gives the threshold directly: admit if P(reuse) ≥ $wear/byte ÷ $recompute/byte, which is 0.04 for DSv3 and 0.21 for 8B.
VALUE: For MLA-class models in a capacity-short or write-capped last tier it doubles or triples useful hits per byte. For dense 8B models, or any tier above about 5M tokens, it is worth ≤3% of node cost, because write-all SSD wear costs only $17 per day against $576 per day for the GPU node.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: "Write-all SSD wear costs $17/day" fits DeepSeek-V3, but for dense 8B it is about 9× too low. That reverses the "≤3% of node cost for dense 8B" conclusion. (8 GPUs × ~31K tok/s × 128 KiB = 32.8 GB/s, which is 2.83 PB/day. At $0.057/TB written (1 DWPD, 7.68 TB, $800) that is about $161/day, or 28% of the $576/day node. It is also about 92 DWPD on 8×3.84 TB, wearing the drives out in about 20 days. DSv3 comes to about $15/day, which matches the claim.)
- MAJOR: The baselines are LRU and random admission, the weakest available. Cost × frequency ÷ size is GDSF (Cherkasova 1998), and FLOP-saved-per-byte prefix eviction and admission is Marconi. Second-hit admission (HiCache, and Idea 1's own rule) is the real SSD competitor. (Marconi (MLSys'25) weighs compute saved against memory and reports up to 34.4× hit rate (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19379). The 43–207% and 37–95% gains are never measured against these.)
- SURVIVES: MLA at 70,272 B/token BF16 has about 8.6× the FLOP/byte of Llama-8B, so MLA does benefit much more from cost-aware admission. A 0.5–1M-token per-DP-rank HBM cache is realistic for DSv3 on 8×H200. 17/576 = 2.95%. Whole-chain scoring is needed for prefix trees.
- RATIO: 1.05 | Most of the gain over LRU is already captured by GDSF/Marconi-style cost-aware eviction and second-hit admission. What remains is the MLA last-tier write-cap case.
- KNOWN: yes | Marconi (MLSys 2025), GDSF web caching, SGLang RadixAttention leaf-only LRU eviction, HiCache selective write-through.

IDEA 2
NAME: Audited exact-prefix agentic KV tier
MECHANISM: The anchor stays: KV is reused only on an exact token-prefix match and kept in the deployment's own KV dtype, so the result is lossless by construction. In the repaired design, the CPU DRAM of all 8 GPUs in a node is pooled into one cache (about 1.6 TB). NVMe is written only for blocks that have already been hit twice. Each block gets a hash, and a 1% sample of cache hits is recomputed from scratch. That sample is checked against the server's own run-to-run noise. This replaces the claim that outputs are bit-identical, which would need batch-invariant kernels that cost 34–56% of throughput. The design is aimed at agentic or long multi-turn sessions whose working set is 4–40× larger than GPU memory. For a 30–60K-token context on Llama-3.1-8B, loading a 95% hit takes 0.15–0.30 s, against 1.4–3.8 s of prefill. That gives 2.04–2.19× less GPU time per request than prefix caching on the GPU alone. PCIe is busy 11–13% of the time and the audit adds about 1.4%.
VALUE: In agentic or multi-turn serving where the working set is larger than GPU memory, it needs about 2.0–2.2× less GPU time per request than vLLM prefix caching on the GPU alone. On chat traces like Mooncake the gain is only about 1.17–1.24×: $228 → $196 per 1M requests.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The claimed 95% hit at 0.15–0.30 s cannot come from DRAM over the stated workload. HBM plus DRAM holds only part of the working set, so most hits must come from NVMe. NVMe loads are 4× slower and saturate the drives at the high end. (At 128 KiB/token: node HBM KV ≈416 GB + 1.6 TB DRAM ≈2.0 TB. A working set of 4–40× of 640 GB is 2.6–25.6 TB, so only 79%→8% fits. A 3.7–7.5 GB load at about 6.25 GB/s of NVMe per GPU (8 drives ≈50 GB/node) takes 0.60–1.20 s, not 0.15–0.30 s. At 3–8 req/s per node that needs 11–60 GB/s of NVMe reads against about 50 GB/s available.)
- MAJOR: The baseline is a straw man. A competent operator already runs a CPU/SSD KV tier with hot-only write-back, so the 2.04–2.19× is mostly the gain of any offload tier, not of this design. (SGLang HiCache `write_through_selective` writes back only data whose access count passes a threshold (https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/hicache_design.html). LMCache, Dynamo KVBM and Mooncake Store do the same job. Against these, the design adds a 1.4% audit cost: about 0.99×.)
- MAJOR: The twice-hit NVMe rule makes the first reuse after a DRAM eviction always miss. When the working set is 13× or more larger than DRAM, most blocks are evicted before their second hit, so the 95% hit rate is assumed, not derived. (At 40× only 7.9% of the working set fits in HBM plus DRAM. No trace or replay is given for the hit rate.)
- SURVIVES: KV size 128 KiB/token, 3.74–7.47 GB at 95% hit, 0.149–0.299 s at 25 GB/s, prefill 1.44–3.81 s at about 500 TFLOP/s effective, 11–13% PCIe duty and the ~1.4% audit cost all reproduce. Exact-prefix reuse in the native dtype is lossless up to kernel noise.
- RATIO: 0.99 | Against LMCache/HiCache-class offload with selective write-back, the only addition is the 1% recompute audit.
- KNOWN: yes | SGLang HiCache (GPU/host/L3 tiers with write_through_selective), LMCache, CachedAttention/AttentionStore (ATC'24, multi-turn), Mooncake Store, NVIDIA Dynamo KVBM.

IDEA 3
NAME: Two-arm probe-gated FP8 layers
MECHANISM: The anchor stays: a cheap per-model probe compares BF16 KV with FP8 KV and picks which layers keep BF16. Everything else uses the stock vLLM FP8 kernels and the `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` flag. The repaired probe runs three arms over 100 prompts of 32K tokens each, plus 512 decode tokens per prompt: BF16, the stock FP8 kernel, and a simulated FP8 rounding with BF16 attention. If the stock kernel fails but the simulated rounding does not, the problem is the kernel (as in vLLM's 128K NIAH drop from 91% to 13%). The answer is then to fix the kernel, not to gate layers. Gating is used only when the error sits in a few layers. NVIDIA's docs trace Qwen2.5-7B's FP8 loss to very large values in its last layer, and R1-Distill-Qwen-7B uses the same architecture. With 1 of 28 layers in BF16, R1-Distill gets 1.93× the KV capacity and decode tokens/s of BF16 at 32K. That is 96.5% of what FP8 everywhere would give. The probe costs about 9 GPU-minutes (about $0.38).
VALUE: On models where FP8 fails, it gives about 1.9× the throughput per request of the usual BF16 fallback, for $0.38 of probing. On models where FP8 already works, it changes nothing and only certifies FP8. On Gemma-3-1B it drops to 1.0–1.5×.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: The kernel failure the probe is designed to diagnose cannot appear in a 32K probe, and it is already fixed upstream. The third arm therefore separates kernel from rounding for a failure mode it never runs into. (vLLM's 91%→13% drop was at 128K NIAH, caused by FA3 FP32 accumulation loss on Hopper. Two-level accumulation fixed it (13%→89%) (https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-04-22-fp8-kvcache, https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2048796304508330462).)
- MAJOR: Carrying the "last layer" diagnosis over to R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is an assumption. That model is a fine-tune of Qwen2.5-Math-7B, not Qwen2.5-7B, so same architecture does not mean the same outlier layers. Separate evidence puts the outliers in almost every layer of Qwen2.5-7B, which would break "1 of 28 layers". (DeepSeek-R1 model card lists the base as Qwen2.5-Math-7B. A 2026 arXiv KV-quantization study reports K max/median >250× and the threshold exceeded in ≥95% of Qwen2.5-7B layers (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27646). I could not locate the cited NVIDIA doc. TRT-LLM #4218 only shows a ModelOpt "Large KV activation" warning with no layer attribution (https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/issues/4218).)
- MAJOR: 100 prompts cannot certify FP8 on a task-accuracy metric. (The 95% CI half-width is ±5.9 points at 90% accuracy and ±9.8 points at 50%, so a 3–5 point FP8 loss goes undetected.)
- MAJOR: The baseline is a straw man. With the fixed kernel and calibrated per-head scales, a competent operator runs FP8 on every layer, and this design is then 3.4% worse. (1.931/2.0 = 0.966. The vLLM blog reports ≤0.7 points of impact for FP8 KV on the models it tested, and llm-compressor per-head calibration is the documented route.)
- SURVIVES: Capacity 56/29 = 1.931×, 96.55% of FP8-all, and decode tokens/s ≈1.93× at 32K on H100 (28.6 vs 55.2 sequences, step ≈20.6 ms, bandwidth-bound) all reproduce. Probe cost is about 7.1 GPU-min of prefill plus decode ≈ 9 min at $2.53/GPU-h, which holds. `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` exists and accepts layer indices (https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache/).
- RATIO: 1.0 | Against fixed-kernel, calibrated FP8 on all layers it ranges from 0.97 (FP8 works) to 1.93 (rare models with a true few-layer failure). No such model has been verified.
- KNOWN: yes | vLLM's `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` (April 2026, aimed at sliding-window layers), ModelOpt and llm-compressor KV calibration, and mixed-precision KV work such as KVTuner.

IDEA 4
NAME: Turn-granular reversible KV eviction
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: a query-agnostic score in the style of KVzip decides which ~30% of the FP8 KV stays on the GPU, and the evicted pages stay in CPU memory where they can be recalled, not deleted. The repair moves recall from every decode step to once per user turn. When a new message arrives, its query vectors are scored against page summaries (min/max keys, about 1% of HBM) and the top 10% of offloaded pages are fetched in one ~8 ms burst that overlaps the message's prefill. A per-step Quest-style "valve" is capped at ≤0.36% of the context per step. This cuts PCIe load from 25.8× the step time (the killed design) to 0.08×. At 32K context that is 64 vs 26 active users per H100 and 2.49× decode tokens/s over FP8-full. Turn-start reload from CPU drops from 84 to 34 ms. The binding quantity is the residency-scoring cost: the design only pays if scoring costs ≤0.29× prefill on trace-average turns (7.6K new tokens), or the turns are short follow-ups on a shared context. So the residency score has to be cheap (an observation window or KVzip's static head-level score), not KVzip's full context-reconstruction pass.
VALUE: In multi-turn shared-context chat (≈500 new tokens per turn) it costs 0.49–0.61× the GPU-seconds per request of FP8-full; on document-heavy turns with full KVzip scoring it costs 1.5–2.2× and loses.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The design is lossy eviction, so it fails the question's hard requirement that the same model gives the same outputs. At 30% residency, any token outside the turn-level recall changes outputs. (It keeps 30% plus 10% recalled and drops 60% of the KV from attention.)
- MAJOR: The per-step valve cap does not hold PCIe at 0.08×. At the cap it nearly saturates PCIe, so 0.08× requires average valve use of about 9% of the cap, which is unsourced. (At 64 users × 0.36% × 2.147 GB = 0.49 GB/step, against a step of 21.2 ms at 25.6 GB/s, PCIe load is 0.91× the step time. Per user it would be 0.014×.)
- MAJOR: Quality at 30% residency with a cheap score is assumed. KVzip's near-lossless 3–4× was measured with its expensive context-reconstruction score, which the design rules out. Query-agnostic or observation-window scores are known to degrade on multi-turn and multi-query tasks. (No RULER, LongBench or SCBench number is given for the cheap-score configuration.)
- MAJOR: The baseline is wrong. The competitor is not FP8-full but existing recallable or sparse systems. (ArkVale (NeurIPS'24, page digests plus CPU recall) reports up to 4.6× batch throughput (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/cd4b49379efac6e84186a3ffce108c37-Paper-Conference.pdf). ShadowKV reports about 3× on Llama-3.1-8B. Both exceed the claimed 2.49×.)
- SURVIVES: FP8 32K = 2.147 GB/user. 26 users full vs 64 users at 40% residency reproduces. Reload 84→34 ms reproduces. The killed design's ~25×-step PCIe load reproduces (25.3×).
- RATIO: 0.9 | Against ArkVale/ShadowKV-class recallable sparse attention, which publishes ≥2.5–4.6× over full KV, and the design is also lossy.
- KNOWN: yes | ArkVale (recallable eviction, bounding-volume page digests), Quest (min/max page summaries), ShadowKV, OmniKV, OPKV (MLSys 2026, recallable sparsity in paged KV).
