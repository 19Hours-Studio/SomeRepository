IDEA 1: Audited exact-prefix agentic KV tier
DEFECT | MAJOR | The claimed 95% hit at 0.15–0.30 s cannot come from DRAM over the stated workload. HBM plus DRAM holds only part of the working set, so most hits must come from NVMe. NVMe loads are 4× slower and saturate the drives at the high end. | At 128 KiB/token: node HBM KV ≈416 GB + 1.6 TB DRAM ≈2.0 TB. A working set of 4–40× of 640 GB is 2.6–25.6 TB, so only 79%→8% fits. A 3.7–7.5 GB load at about 6.25 GB/s of NVMe per GPU (8 drives ≈50 GB/node) takes 0.60–1.20 s, not 0.15–0.30 s. At 3–8 req/s per node that needs 11–60 GB/s of NVMe reads against about 50 GB/s available.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is a straw man. A competent operator already runs a CPU/SSD KV tier with hot-only write-back, so the 2.04–2.19× is mostly the gain of any offload tier, not of this design. | SGLang HiCache `write_through_selective` writes back only data whose access count passes a threshold (https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/hicache_design.html). LMCache, Dynamo KVBM and Mooncake Store do the same job. Against these, the design adds a 1.4% audit cost: about 0.99×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The twice-hit NVMe rule makes the first reuse after a DRAM eviction always miss. When the working set is 13× or more larger than DRAM, most blocks are evicted before their second hit, so the 95% hit rate is assumed, not derived. | At 40× only 7.9% of the working set fits in HBM plus DRAM. No trace or replay is given for the hit rate.
DEFECT | MINOR | The Mooncake number does not reproduce. | 228/196 = 1.163×, not 1.17–1.24×.
DEFECT | MINOR | The batch-invariance cost range does not match either source, and a 2026 alternative makes determinism pay-per-use. | SGLang reports 34.35% on average (24–46%) and says TML's approach costs 61.5% (https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/). TML's own 26 s→42/55 s timings mean a 38–53% loss, not "56%". LLM-42's overhead scales with the fraction of traffic that needs determinism (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17768).
DEFECT | MINOR | A 1% audit is weak at catching rare corruption, and "run-to-run noise" depends on batch shape, so it needs its own reference distribution for each configuration. | At a 1e-3 bad-hit rate, 95% detection takes about 3.0e5 hits. At 1e-4 it takes about 3.0e6.
DEFECT | MINOR | Pooling DRAM across sockets puts half of all loads over UPI, and Gen5-rate loads from all 8 GPUs come close to the host memory bandwidth. | 8 × ~55 GB/s ≈ 440 GB/s against 614 GB/s theoretical DDR5-4800 across 2×8 channels.
SURVIVES | KV size 128 KiB/token, 3.74–7.47 GB at 95% hit, 0.149–0.299 s at 25 GB/s, prefill 1.44–3.81 s at about 500 TFLOP/s effective, 11–13% PCIe duty and the ~1.4% audit cost all reproduce. Exact-prefix reuse in the native dtype is lossless up to kernel noise.
RATIO | 0.99 | Against LMCache/HiCache-class offload with selective write-back, the only addition is the 1% recompute audit.
KNOWN | yes | SGLang HiCache (GPU/host/L3 tiers with write_through_selective), LMCache, CachedAttention/AttentionStore (ATC'24, multi-turn), Mooncake Store, NVIDIA Dynamo KVBM.
NEW FACT | SGLang's batch-invariant mode costs 34.35% on average (range 24.4–46.0%). TML's original costs 61.5%. | https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/

IDEA 2: Two-arm probe-gated FP8 layers
DEFECT | MAJOR | The kernel failure the probe is designed to diagnose cannot appear in a 32K probe, and it is already fixed upstream. The third arm therefore separates kernel from rounding for a failure mode it never runs into. | vLLM's 91%→13% drop was at 128K NIAH, caused by FA3 FP32 accumulation loss on Hopper. Two-level accumulation fixed it (13%→89%) (https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-04-22-fp8-kvcache, https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2048796304508330462).
DEFECT | MAJOR | Carrying the "last layer" diagnosis over to R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is an assumption. That model is a fine-tune of Qwen2.5-Math-7B, not Qwen2.5-7B, so same architecture does not mean the same outlier layers. Separate evidence puts the outliers in almost every layer of Qwen2.5-7B, which would break "1 of 28 layers". | DeepSeek-R1 model card lists the base as Qwen2.5-Math-7B. A 2026 arXiv KV-quantization study reports K max/median >250× and the threshold exceeded in ≥95% of Qwen2.5-7B layers (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27646). I could not locate the cited NVIDIA doc. TRT-LLM #4218 only shows a ModelOpt "Large KV activation" warning with no layer attribution (https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/issues/4218).
DEFECT | MAJOR | 100 prompts cannot certify FP8 on a task-accuracy metric. | The 95% CI half-width is ±5.9 points at 90% accuracy and ±9.8 points at 50%, so a 3–5 point FP8 loss goes undetected.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is a straw man. With the fixed kernel and calibrated per-head scales, a competent operator runs FP8 on every layer, and this design is then 3.4% worse. | 1.931/2.0 = 0.966. The vLLM blog reports ≤0.7 points of impact for FP8 KV on the models it tested, and llm-compressor per-head calibration is the documented route.
DEFECT | MINOR | 512 decode tokens per prompt does not cover how a reasoning distill is actually used, where the KV is mostly generated tokens (thousands to tens of thousands). Error that builds up over long generations is not probed. | 512 tokens is about 1.6% of a 32K context.
DEFECT | MINOR | The name says "two-arm" but the design runs three arms. | Text: "three arms".
SURVIVES | Capacity 56/29 = 1.931×, 96.55% of FP8-all, and decode tokens/s ≈1.93× at 32K on H100 (28.6 vs 55.2 sequences, step ≈20.6 ms, bandwidth-bound) all reproduce. Probe cost is about 7.1 GPU-min of prefill plus decode ≈ 9 min at $2.53/GPU-h, which holds. `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` exists and accepts layer indices (https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache/).
RATIO | 1.0 | Against fixed-kernel, calibrated FP8 on all layers it ranges from 0.97 (FP8 works) to 1.93 (rare models with a true few-layer failure). No such model has been verified.
KNOWN | yes | vLLM's `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` (April 2026, aimed at sliding-window layers), ModelOpt and llm-compressor KV calibration, and mixed-precision KV work such as KVTuner.
NEW FACT | The FA3 FP8 long-context bug only shows once the contraction length reaches about 100K or more. B200 did not need the fix. | vLLM blog (https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-04-22-fp8-kvcache) and secondary summaries.

IDEA 3: Turn-granular reversible KV eviction
DEFECT | FATAL | The design is lossy eviction, so it fails the question's hard requirement that the same model gives the same outputs. At 30% residency, any token outside the turn-level recall changes outputs. | It keeps 30% plus 10% recalled and drops 60% of the KV from attention.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The per-step valve cap does not hold PCIe at 0.08×. At the cap it nearly saturates PCIe, so 0.08× requires average valve use of about 9% of the cap, which is unsourced. | At 64 users × 0.36% × 2.147 GB = 0.49 GB/step, against a step of 21.2 ms at 25.6 GB/s, PCIe load is 0.91× the step time. Per user it would be 0.014×.
DEFECT | MAJOR | Quality at 30% residency with a cheap score is assumed. KVzip's near-lossless 3–4× was measured with its expensive context-reconstruction score, which the design rules out. Query-agnostic or observation-window scores are known to degrade on multi-turn and multi-query tasks. | No RULER, LongBench or SCBench number is given for the cheap-score configuration.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baseline is wrong. The competitor is not FP8-full but existing recallable or sparse systems. | ArkVale (NeurIPS'24, page digests plus CPU recall) reports up to 4.6× batch throughput (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/cd4b49379efac6e84186a3ffce108c37-Paper-Conference.pdf). ShadowKV reports about 3× on Llama-3.1-8B. Both exceed the claimed 2.49×.
DEFECT | MINOR | "Trace-average turns (7.6K new tokens)" is Mooncake's average total input length (7,590), not new tokens per turn, so the 0.29× scoring-cost threshold uses the wrong workload number. | Mooncake trace statistics: average input 7,590 tokens, including the cached prefix.
DEFECT | MINOR | The burst size is ambiguous: 10% of context and 10% of offloaded pages give different numbers. | 10% of context takes 8.4 ms and is what the 64-user count needs. 10% of offloaded pages takes 5.9 ms.
SURVIVES | FP8 32K = 2.147 GB/user. 26 users full vs 64 users at 40% residency reproduces. Reload 84→34 ms reproduces. The killed design's ~25×-step PCIe load reproduces (25.3×).
RATIO | 0.9 | Against ArkVale/ShadowKV-class recallable sparse attention, which publishes ≥2.5–4.6× over full KV, and the design is also lossy.
KNOWN | yes | ArkVale (recallable eviction, bounding-volume page digests), Quest (min/max page summaries), ShadowKV, OmniKV, OPKV (MLSys 2026, recallable sparsity in paged KV).
NEW FACT | A 2026 analysis argues ArkVale-style digests are loose bounds under RoPE. | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03949

IDEA 4: FLOP-per-byte bounded-tier admission
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Write-all SSD wear costs $17/day" fits DeepSeek-V3, but for dense 8B it is about 9× too low. That reverses the "≤3% of node cost for dense 8B" conclusion. | 8 GPUs × ~31K tok/s × 128 KiB = 32.8 GB/s, which is 2.83 PB/day. At $0.057/TB written (1 DWPD, 7.68 TB, $800) that is about $161/day, or 28% of the $576/day node. It is also about 92 DWPD on 8×3.84 TB, wearing the drives out in about 20 days. DSv3 comes to about $15/day, which matches the claim.
DEFECT | MAJOR | The baselines are LRU and random admission, the weakest available. Cost × frequency ÷ size is GDSF (Cherkasova 1998), and FLOP-saved-per-byte prefix eviction and admission is Marconi. Second-hit admission (HiCache, and Idea 1's own rule) is the real SSD competitor. | Marconi (MLSys'25) weighs compute saved against memory and reports up to 34.4× hit rate (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19379). The 43–207% and 37–95% gains are never measured against these.
DEFECT | MINOR | The DSv3/8B threshold ratio does not match the FLOP/byte ratio, which implies an unstated 1.6× MFU difference. | FLOP/byte is 1.05e6 vs 1.22e5, a ratio of 8.6×. The thresholds 0.21/0.04 give 5.25×.
DEFECT | MINOR | The threshold leaves out SSD read bandwidth. For dense 8B, an SSD hit is barely faster than recomputing. | About 6.25 GB/s of NVMe per GPU against a recompute-equivalent rate of about 4.1 GB/s per GPU at short context.
DEFECT | MINOR | "Never more than 5% worse at any capacity" rests on replays of a few one-hour Mooncake traces, with no confidence interval and no other trace family. | The Mooncake trace has about 23.6K requests.
SURVIVES | MLA at 70,272 B/token BF16 has about 8.6× the FLOP/byte of Llama-8B, so MLA does benefit much more from cost-aware admission. A 0.5–1M-token per-DP-rank HBM cache is realistic for DSv3 on 8×H200. 17/576 = 2.95%. Whole-chain scoring is needed for prefix trees.
RATIO | 1.05 | Most of the gain over LRU is already captured by GDSF/Marconi-style cost-aware eviction and second-hit admission. What remains is the MLA last-tier write-cap case.
KNOWN | yes | Marconi (MLSys 2025), GDSF web caching, SGLang RadixAttention leaf-only LRU eviction, HiCache selective write-through.
NEW FACT | vLLM measured on H100 / Llama-3.1-8B that FP8 KV roughly halves the decode ITL slope, with break-even near 7K tokens. On sliding-window gpt-oss-20b it gives about 96% of the BF16 slope (little gain). | https://vllm-project.github.io/2026/04/22/fp8-kvcache.html
