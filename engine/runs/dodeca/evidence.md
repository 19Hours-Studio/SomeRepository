GAPS:
- Use-wear, residue and lipid, wax or fibre analysis (SEM, microscopy) of knobs, hole rims and interiors would separate tool, vessel and symbol; LVR-LandesMuseum Bonn, Gallo-Romeins Museum Tongeren and Jublains hold the pieces.
- Per-object tables (all 12 hole diameters, wall thickness, mass, alloy, CT scans of cores) would test whether hole sizes encode a system; Guggenberger's list nos. 1-134 and Nouwen 1993 hold the raw data.
- No casting workshop, mould or core debris is known; production site and date are unresolved, so the Gallic-Empire link is untested.
- Grave and hoard associations (Gellep 3040 at Krefeld, Bachem, Saint-Parize) need full inventories to show which objects travel with them.
- Reason for Mediterranean absence and Slovak imports remains unexplained; Hitchens pXRF data could show regional alloy groups.

FACT 55: dice evidence (checked sources): every known Roman 12-faced or polyhedral die carries letters, numerals or names, including the Geneva die (silver over lead, 35 mm, 297 g, twelve zodiac names, wear on edges and faces; its publisher says it is not directly related to the hollow series: Cervi-Brunier 1985, pp. 154-155); 12-faced dice are known from Crete, Patrae (a rock-crystal one in a rich grave with two silver astragals), Antioch and Egypt (Chaniotis 2006, pp. 207-210); the Latin Sortes Sangallenses take a number from I to XII; Roman dice towers (Richborough, Dorchester: stair plates about 70 x 45 mm) are too small for a 40-100 mm knobbed shell (Cobbett 2020, Lucerna 58). | HARD | tests/h1_practice/RESULT.md (sections 0, 1, 5)
FACT 56: throw simulation (MuJoCo, validated: a fair cube gives 1/6 and a regular dodecahedron 1/12 per face) of the two scans with their real hollow-shell mass distribution, on cloth, wood, board and earth: both are loaded dice. On Kleiner Feldberg the centre of mass lies 1.9 mm off centre toward the smallest hole, and faces come up with frequencies from 0.045 to 0.159 (fair 0.083; Hunt 0.06-0.12); the face that lands down follows the centre-of-mass offset (Spearman 0.82, p = 0.001). The unringed pole faces come up less than a fair share (0.11-0.15 vs 0.167). Every throw ends with one face clearly uppermost, resting on 3-4 knobs with the top face tilted 1.6-2 degrees. All 20 knobs are struck, unevenly: 2.8-8.5% of the contact impulse per knob, in an order that is the same on every surface. | HARD (simulation, 2 objects; contact parameters assumed) | tests/sim_throw/RESULT.md, throw.py, bias_check.py
