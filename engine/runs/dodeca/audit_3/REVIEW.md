=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Festival ember carrier
DEFECT | FATAL | A coal small enough to enter one unringed opening can fall out through the equally large opposite twin, or through ringed holes of the same size, so the shell cannot hold a coal while being "turned" to set vents. | FACT 3 (the larger unringed holes are an opposite pair); FACT 31 (Jublains ovals 26x21.5 and 25.5x21.5 mm, ringed holes up to 22 mm)
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Turning a vent windward" cannot select anything, because all 12 holes are open at once (Jublains-size holes on a 60-75 mm shell leave 14-23% of the surface open, by my calculation). | FACT 1; FACT 31; my geometry calc
DEFECT | MAJOR | Knobs cannot be "cooler grips": a 3-5 mm copper-alloy knob acts as a pin fin whose tip stays within 0.3% of the shell's excess temperature, and a 10-20 W ember brings an 81 g shell to +30 K in about 1-2 minutes. | FACT 1; FACT 6-8; my fin and heating calc
DEFECT | MAJOR | The rite is not attested for Roman Gaul: hearth-quenching comes from Keating (1634), whom Hutton suspects of pseudo-history, and Coligny attests a Samonios month or feast but no rekindling of hearths. | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlachtga; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny_calendar
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Christianisation ended it" clashes with the explanation's own analogue, a hearth-relighting custom that survived as Christian-era folk practice in Ireland and Scotland, so the 4th-century end of the objects is left unexplained. | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltane; FACT 13
DEFECT | MAJOR | A yearly rite performed by every household predicts thousands of finds rather than about 130, and the fragments from Germanic settlements in Slovakia lie outside any Gaulish-calendar land. | FACT 9; FACT 12
DEFECT | MAJOR | The rite does not need a regular dodecahedron, triple rings or variable opposite pairs; a pierced pot would serve, so the distinctive form stays unexplained. | FACT 1; FACT 3; FACT 4
DEFECT | MINOR | The soot and heat-tint test is weak, because cleaning of mostly antiquarian finds and corrosion remove soot, and brief yearly contact may leave no tint, so a negative result is easy to explain away. | FACT 16; FACT 20
FIT | 9 of 16 | It covers the civil contexts, low wear and north-western core, but the hole geometry contradicts it and it relies on a rite that is not attested.
SURVIVES | Annual, brief use fits the low wear and the household and villa contexts (Norton Disney), and the interior-residue test can be done on museum pieces.

IDEA 2: Staff finial on tapering shaft
DEFECT | MAJOR | A decorative finial gives no reason for the ten ringed "windows" to vary within one piece (Jublains 10.5-22 mm, with repeated sizes). | FACT 2; FACT 31
DEFECT | MAJOR | Unequal pairs are not "necessary": a tapering or straight shaft seats in any two coaxial holes, equal or not, so FACT 4 is only accommodated, and the refutation clause "no single tapering rod" can never be met. | FACT 4; FACT 31 (Jublains pair near-equal, 26x21.5 vs 25.5x21.5 mm); geometry
DEFECT | MAJOR | "An unofficial regional badge that escaped texts and art" is ad hoc: north-western monuments do depict insignia staffs yet no relief shows this head, and the Gallic-Empire map and the absence around the Mediterranean are asserted rather than derived. | FACT 18; FACT 37 (Friedberg beneficiarius altar); FACT 10; FACT 11
DEFECT | MAJOR | The remaining test, abrasion or organic staining at the unringed rims, is blunted by the idea's own premise of occasional, wear-free carrying and by corrosion masking fine abrasion. | FACT 20; idea text
DEFECT | MINOR | Corpus specialists read the less-round unringed pair as a casting feature, so the idea needs production openings to have been reused as the mount. | FACT 33 (Guggenberger & Leach 2025)
DEFECT | MINOR | "Knobs shield the head when grounded" cannot apply to a head at the top of a staff, which leaves the knobs as ornament only. | FACT 1
DEFECT | MINOR | It does not address the 2nd-4th-century date range or the end of use. | FACT 13
FIT | 9 of 16 | It explains the unringed axis, the low wear and the range of contexts, but not the hole variation within each piece, the distribution map or the absence from art.
SURVIVES | It gives the opposite unringed pair a concrete role that fits the two-hole axis of the Arloff icosahedron (FACT 32), the Gellep rod and deposition in graves, hoards and rivers, and a CT comparison of unringed and ringed rims is feasible.

IDEA 3: Coursing hunter's personal whistle
DEFECT | MAJOR | The acoustics are unshown and doubtful: with 11 holes open (14-23% of the surface), my lumped estimate gives a radiation Q of about 1, against about 100 or more for a one-hole vessel flute, and ka of about 1.3 lies outside the Helmholtz regime, so "one note fixed by volume and hole area" is unsupported and I found no published blowing trial. | FACT 3; FACT 31; my Helmholtz/radiation-Q calc
DEFECT | MAJOR | Since pitch depends only on total hole area, the mechanism makes the within-piece hole differences, the pairing, the triple rings and the knobs irrelevant, and a one-hole bone call would do (Roman bone animal calls are known in the north-west). | FACT 1; FACT 2; FACT 3; FACT 31; EXARC 2022/1 (Tamboer, Velsen)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The distribution argument fails: Arrian, a Bithynian Greek, coursed with his own Celtic hound Horme, and a 4th-century El Djem mosaic shows hounds chasing hares, both in regions with no finds. | FACT 11; Arrian, Cynegeticus (Dansey translation, Gutenberg 78013); penelope.uchicago.edu/encyclopaedia_romana/miscellanea/canes/coursing.html
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test cannot refute, because rim-blown vessels sound across rounded lips and corrosion alters edges, and the decisive test (whether a replica speaks with all holes open) is not proposed. | EXARC 2022/1 (rim-blown vessel flutes); FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | A hunter's call does not predict women's graves, sacred deposits or hoards. | FACT 14; FACT 17 (soft)
FIT | 7 of 16 | It fits the size spread, the lack of a shared scheme and the low wear, but none of the shell's distinctive features follows from it and its own map argument is contradicted.
SURVIVES | A continuous size spread with no shared hole scheme fits an individual signalling device, and low wear fits blowing.

IDEA 4: Sanctuary-theatre chorus pitch-giver
DEFECT | MAJOR | It shares the unshown acoustics: with every other hole open the shell is a very low-Q radiator (Q about 1 by my estimate), so a stable reference pitch is doubtful and no trial is cited. | FACT 3; FACT 31; my Helmholtz/radiation-Q calc
DEFECT | MAJOR | Jublains shows a planned, symmetric layout (17/17 and 22/22 mm, pole averages 17.5 vs 17.4 mm) rather than ad hoc reaming, and since only total open area sets pitch, tuning needs one hole adjusted, not ten graded ones. | FACT 31
DEFECT | MAJOR | The map is not derived: finds at Limes forts, in Pannonia and Serbia and in Germanic Slovakia lie outside the Gallo-British theatre-sanctuary zone, and military contexts reach about a fifth. | FACT 12; FACT 14; FACT 15 (soft)
DEFECT | MAJOR | No evidence ties choruses or pitch-givers to Gallo-Roman sanctuary theatres, and the Mediterranean, which had theatre choruses everywhere, yields no finds, so the link to this building type is simply assumed. | FACT 11; FACT 18
DEFECT | MAJOR | The knobs and the regular twelve-faced form have no acoustic or practical role. | FACT 1
DEFECT | MAJOR | The reaming-mark test cannot discriminate, because finishing after casting (lathe or other shaping) leaves tool marks anyway and corrosion can erase them, while the acoustic claim goes untested. | Dragonidesova 2025 (sav.sk); FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | Context dating cannot resolve an end tied to the temple closures of 391-392. | FACT 13
FIT | 11 of 16 | Tuning explains the hole variation within each piece and the missing shared scheme, but not the form, the knobs, the eastern and military finds, or the symmetry at Jublains.
SURVIVES | The tuning reason for unequal holes within a piece and the absent shared scheme holds, and the Besançon theatre find (FACT 15) and the civil contexts fit.

IDEA 5: Touch-coded casting lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its cultural anchor misfires: Tacitus' lots are marked slips scattered on the cloth, of which three are then picked up and read by their signs, not by how they land, and dodecahedra are almost absent from free Germania, where that practice was. | Tacitus, Germania 10 ("ter singulos tollit"); FACT 36; FACT 12
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Not Mediterranean dice culture" is wrong and leaves the map unexplained: the north-west had full Roman dice culture, and Italy had lot oracles (the Praeneste sortes) yet no finds. | FACT 35; FACT 11; Cicero, De Divinatione 2.85-86
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test contradicts the mechanism: if landing on cloth "wears little", fresh knob tips are what the idea itself predicts, so they cannot refute it, and corrosion masks polish anyway. | FACT 20; idea text
DEFECT | MINOR | Near-identical holes (Jublains 17/17, 22/22, 21/21.5 mm) cannot be told apart by finger, and all ten ringed faces carry the same rings, so the "code" is coarser than claimed. | FACT 31; FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | It does not say why a lot needs a hollow cast bronze shell when slips or dice would do, or why use is confined to the 2nd-4th centuries. | FACT 1; FACT 13
FIT | 12 of 19 | It is physically coherent for the knobs, holes, lack of letters and low wear, but the sources it invokes contradict its distribution argument.
SURVIVES | The knobs (resting geometry, FACT 34), the hole variation within each piece, the missing shared scheme, the absence of inscriptions, the low wear and the civil and grave contexts all follow from one mechanism.

IDEA 6: Bright-or-dark day-luck lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | The throw does not fix the reading: an all-cloth view needs a bottom hole of at least the top hole x (1+h/D), e.g. 20.4 mm under a 17 mm top hole (h = 60 mm) from 30 cm away but 17.7 mm from 1.5 m, and a 5 cm head offset at 60 cm shifts the view by about 5 mm, so the reader's posture decides pairs that differ by 1-3 mm. | FACT 5; FACT 34; my viewing-geometry calc
DEFECT | MAJOR | The calendar link goes beyond the evidence: Gaulish calendars are known only from Coligny and Villards-d'Héria in eastern Gaul, yet Britain holds about 25% of the finds and others come from Pannonia, Serbia and Germanic Slovakia. | FACT 10; FACT 12; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny_calendar
DEFECT | MAJOR | The defining decoration of the commonest type, triple rings on ten holes against a plain pair, plays no part in a bright/dark reading. | FACT 3; FACT 25 (soft)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The refutation is unlikely to occur and does not discriminate: pairs are already reported to range from near-identical to very different, and any hand-finished casting gives unequal pairs, so passing the test confirms nothing. | FACT 2; FACT 4
DEFECT | MINOR | It misstates its source: at Coligny, MAT/ANM mark months, while the day-level good/not-good marks are MD/AMB. | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny_calendar
DEFECT | MINOR | In type 2a the distinctive larger unringed pair is near-equal at Jublains (26x21.5 vs 25.5x21.5 mm), so the most distinctive axis voids the throw. | FACT 31
FIT | 17 of 19 | It covers the most hard facts with one physical mechanism and fails only on the rings and the eastern and Germanic finds (Britain rests on an assumption), but its reading rule is ill-defined.
SURVIVES | It uses the real resting geometry (FACT 34) to make variable opposite pairs and the absence of letters functional, it explains the knobs, the missing shared scheme and the low wear, and the pair-measurement test is feasible on existing pieces.
