=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Bronze serpent's-egg talisman
DEFECT | MAJOR | Hole diameters within each object are graded and all different, but an urchin's ten zones are alike; "smiths copied freely" covers overall size, not graded holes on one piece. | FACT 2; Jublains ten ringed holes 10.5-22 mm (Sparavigna 2012, arXiv 1204.6497)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The pair it reads as mouth and anus is explained by casting: those holes are less round and often larger "because of the production process", so the mapping gives symbolic meaning to a manufacturing feature. | Cambridge 2025 core reader (the commonest type, there called "1a"); Arloff icosahedron has holes in only two opposite faces (Wikipedia)
DEFECT | MAJOR | It needs a Druidic charm tradition to survive unseen for 100-300 years after Rome suppressed the Druids. | Pliny NH 30.13 (Tiberius); Suetonius, Claudius 25; FACT 13 (2nd-4th c.)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The model chain is unsupported: Pliny describes a cartilage crust with polyp-sucker cups (no holes, no pentagons); the urchin reading is Lhuyd's early-modern guess, against the glass-bead "adder stone" reading; and Micraster, the chalk urchin used in burials, has its anus on the rear face, not opposite the mouth. | Pliny NH 29.52-54 (maryjones.us/jce/druidegg.html); NHM Echinoid Directory; Dunstable burial "mainly Micraster"
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test is weak: no direction is set for the difference, "copied freely" can absorb a null result, the per-object tables are unpublished, and the one published table already gives the refuting null. | Jublains: the five ringed holes round one oval pole average 17.5 mm, round the other 17.4 mm (my calc from Sparavigna 2012); GAPS
DEFECT | MINOR | Finds from Pannonia, Serbia, Germanic Slovakia and Limes forts lie outside any attested Druidic area. | FACT 12, FACT 15
FIT | 9 of 13 | It explains the 10+2 layout, rings, knobs, size spread, Gallo-British concentration, contexts, silence, no letters and low wear; it does not explain graded holes (2), opposite-pair variation (4), the eastern finds (12) or the dates (13).
SURVIVES | Seen along a face axis, a dodecahedron really does have a 5+5 belt between two poles; "apple-sized" fits 4-11 cm; and a private charm fits low wear and silence.

IDEA 2: Wax-forming tool
DEFECT | MAJOR | It gives no role to the triple engraved rings, the unringed pair or the closed twelve-faced shell; a flat perforated plate with feet would form the same bosses. | FACT 1, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | Wax tablets and seals were used across the whole empire, and in Britain bronze seal boxes already standardised wax seals over cords in the 2nd-3rd c., so the tool is redundant and its absence from the Mediterranean is unexplained. | FACT 11; London Museum seal-box records; Winchester seal box (Hampshire Cultural Trust)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The proposed tests cannot refute it: replica trials show only possibility (Lamb's first 3D-printed trials were inconsistent), wax products seldom survive to be matched, and only residue analysis of uncleaned interiors could return a negative. | FACT 28; Lamb, EXARC 2026/2
DEFECT | MINOR | The "single wax-bearing specimen" it claims to explain cannot be located and may be casting residue. | FACT 21
DEFECT | MINOR | "Standardising" sits badly with the lack of any shared hole or size scheme: each tool's bosses fit no common standard. | FACT 2, FACT 5
DEFECT | MINOR | Rich graves of both sexes, hoards, sacred deposits and the Gellep rod are unexplained for a craft tool. | FACT 14, FACT 15, FACT 17
FIT | 6 of 13 | It explains graded apertures, no module, low wear, no letters, silence and civil findspots; it does not explain the form, rings, opposite pairs or distribution.
SURVIVES | Graded apertures with knob spacers did form repeatable bosses in replica trials, and soft wax would leave the bronze unworn.

IDEA 3: Axle-spun lot, size-coded holes
DEFECT | FATAL | Its own refutation criterion is already met: on the only published per-hole table, ringed holes coincide at 17/17 mm and 22/22 mm (also 21/21.5 mm), so there are not ten distinguishable diameters. | Jublains, Sparavigna 2012 arXiv 1204.6497 (the same figures nunc.ch gives for Newhall)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its first falsifier does not follow from the mechanism, since a rod thinner than the smaller hole spins in unequal bearings; the decisive check (bearing-rim polish against ringed rims) is not proposed; and "soft rods, occasional use" is assumed only to fit the low wear. | FACT 20; FACT 4
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Occasional private Gallo-British use" restates the distribution without explaining it: 70% of finds are from France and Germany, there are finds in Pannonia, Serbia and Germanic Slovakia, and none in Italy or the Mediterranean. | FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 12
DEFECT | MINOR | It works only for pieces with an unringed axis pair; that type is by far the commonest but not universal. | Cambridge 2025; FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | The Gellep bone lay beside the object, not through it, and was too decayed to lift, so it cannot confirm an axle. | FACT 17; nunc.ch; livescience
FIT | 7 of 13 | It explains the knobs, within-object variation, no shared scheme, the unringed pair, no module, civil contexts, silence and no letters; it does not explain wear, opposite-pair data or distribution.
SURVIVES | Bearings on the near-equal oval pair (Jublains 26x21.5 vs 25.5x21.5 mm) on a ~3 cm rod like the Gellep bone are physically workable, and the 5+5 belt gives ten faces readable round the axis.

IDEA 4: Staff finial on tapering shaft
DEFECT | MAJOR | It gives no mechanism for ten "windows" of graded, all-different diameters, nor for the dodecahedral form, rings or knobs beyond ornament: this is the status/decoration defect. | FACT 2, FACT 3; Jublains 10.5-22 mm
DEFECT | MAJOR | A staff grounded on its knobs predicts knob abrasion, but the pieces are reported unworn, so it needs the unsupported "carried occasionally". | FACT 20; Hitchens (Washington Post/Boston Globe 2024); Norton Disney unworn
DEFECT | MAJOR | Insignia staffs are shown on north-western provincial monuments (the beneficiarius staff on the Friedberg altar), so total absence from art needs the extra "unofficial badge" assumption. | FACT 18; mithraeum.eu/monument/1480
DEFECT | MINOR | "Regional badge" names no office that men, women, civilians and soldiers would share, nor why it stops at the Alps. | FACT 11, FACT 14, FACT 15
DEFECT | MINOR | It wrongly claims unequal pairs are necessary: the unringed pair can be near-equal, which fits a straight shaft too. | Jublains 26x21.5 vs 25.5x21.5 mm; FACT 4
DEFECT | MINOR | The "no single tapering rod" arm of the test can never fail, because opposite faces are always coaxial; the rim comparison is confounded because the unringed holes are less-finished production openings. | Cambridge 2025; geometry
FIT | 6 of 13 | It explains the unringed axis, unequal pairs, no module, light weight, grave/hoard/river deposition and no scheme; it does not explain graded windows, wear, distribution or art silence.
SURVIVES | Mounting on a shaft through the unringed pair is supported by Cambridge 2025's own handle suggestion, the Gellep bone (~3 cm, reported to match the largest pair) and the Arloff icosahedron, which is holed only in two opposite faces; the CT/microscopy abrasion test is feasible in Bonn and Tongeren.

IDEA 5: Coincidence rangefinder
DEFECT | FATAL | As stated (eye at the small hole, target fills a 17-40 mm far hole 40-100 mm away), a 1.8 m man fills the hole at only 1.8-10.6 m; in Sparavigna's coincidence variant, the near-equal Jublains pairs need the eye 1.0-2.2 m away and the usable pairs reach only 14-23 m, ranges where pacing is easier. | FACT 2, FACT 5; my calc R = H*h/D; Sparavigna 2012
DEFECT | MAJOR | The military link rests on a minority of finds: over half are civil and under a fifth military, and women's graves and hoards occur. | FACT 14, FACT 15
DEFECT | MAJOR | An army or surveying instrument would be expected wherever the army and surveyors worked (Italy, Africa, the East) and in the technical writers who describe the dioptra; it is absent from both. | FACT 11, FACT 18
DEFECT | MAJOR | With no scale or markings and no shared hole scheme or size module, every piece would need its own memorised range table. | FACT 2, FACT 5, FACT 19, FACT 27
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test cannot refute it: with target size free, any hole ratio maps to some plausible range, and no accuracy trial has been run. | FACT 27; arXiv 1204.6497
DEFECT | MAJOR | It does not explain the engraved rings or the irregular unringed pair. | FACT 3
DEFECT | MINOR | "Rest flat on its knobs" gives no horizontal sight-line: resting on any face puts every hole axis at 90 or 26.6 degrees to the horizontal. | my calc (dihedral 116.57 deg)
FIT | 3 of 12 | It explains unequal opposite pairs, within-object variation and low wear; it is contrary to or silent on the rest.
SURVIVES | Opposite holes do form coaxial sight-pairs of differing diameter, so similar-triangle sighting is geometrically possible.

IDEA 6: Touch-coded casting lot
DEFECT | MAJOR | On the one measured piece, ringed holes repeat in size (17/17, 22/22, 21/21.5 mm), so faces cannot all be told apart by size or ring; the code collapses and "explains why holes differ" fails there. | Jublains table, Sparavigna 2012
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Tacitus parallel works against it: Germanic lots were wooden slips "marked with signs" and read by those marks in free Germania, where dodecahedra are almost absent, while ~90% of dodecahedra lie inside the Gallic Empire. | Tacitus, Germania 10; FACT 10, FACT 12
DEFECT | MINOR | The north-western provinces had full Roman dice culture, down to a 4th-c. fair-throw dice tower, so "not Mediterranean dice culture" is false and an unlettered lot is left unmotivated. | Vettweiss-Froitzheim dice tower (Bonn); Museum of London dice
DEFECT | MINOR | It says the pieces wear little, yet its test needs polished knob tips, and Hitchens reports no tool-like wear on the many pieces she examined. | FACT 20; Washington Post/Boston Globe 2024
DEFECT | MINOR | It leaves the Gellep rod association unexplained. | FACT 17
FIT | 10 of 13 | It explains the knobs, graded holes, no shared scheme, ringed vs unringed, no module, light weight, Slovak finds, contexts, silence and no letters; it does not explain the opposite-pair data or the Gallic rather than Germanic concentration.
SURVIVES | Tumbling on the knobs to one face-up, with no need for fairness or letters, is mechanically sound; the knob-tip vs recess microscopy test is feasible on museum pieces and has a clear refuting result (a positive could also come from handling).

IDEA 7: Farmer's sowing-date instrument
DEFECT | MAJOR | Wagemans's test (his own bronze copy) shows only that a designed replica can work; run on a real piece, Jublains's six pairs at its own 48.25 N mark 26 Aug-21 Sep (first light) or 20-31 Oct (full disc), and each date moves about four weeks between Arles and Corbridge, so some pair hits some "window" almost anywhere and a hit proves nothing. | my calc (axis 26.565 deg, B about 50 mm, Sparavigna 2012 holes); FACT 2, FACT 5
DEFECT | MAJOR | With no markings and no shared module or hole scheme, the owner must know which pair suits his latitude and date; "pairs = dates or latitudes" turns the variation into free parameters instead of explaining it. | FACT 2, FACT 5, FACT 19
DEFECT | MAJOR | Timing winter-cereal sowing mattered equally in Italy, Spain and Africa, where agronomists give calendar and star rules with no such device, so the Mediterranean absence needs an extra assumption; forts, rich graves and hoards are also unexplained. | FACT 11, FACT 14, FACT 15, FACT 18; Pliny NH 18
DEFECT | MAJOR | It does not explain the triple rings or the irregular unringed pair; the date geometry comes from the solid itself (the 26.6-degree axis), so the decoration plays no part. | FACT 3; Cambridge 2025
DEFECT | MINOR | "Aimed at the sun" gives no date information, because light passes whenever the piece is aimed; it works only set level on its knobs, facing due south at true noon, and the mechanism never says how the meridian and noon were found. | romandodecahedron.com/the-hypothesis; geometry
FIT | 6 of 12 | It explains knobs as feet, unequal pairs, graded holes, a loose northern/civil fit and low wear; it does not explain the rings, module, distribution, silence or lack of letters.
SURVIVES | Resting on its knobs, every piece has the same 26.565-degree pair axis, so dated noon alignments are geometrically real and give plausible autumn dates for Jublains.
