THE 7 EXPLANATIONS:
IDEA 1
NAME: Bronze serpent's-egg talisman
MECHANISM: Pliny (NH 29.52) describes the anguinum, a Gaulish Druid badge, apple-sized with a cupped crust, carried to win lawsuits, often identified as a fossil sea urchin. The dodecahedron copies that urchin in bronze: pentagons give its five-fold symmetry, ten ringed holes its ten zones of ringed tubercles, the larger unringed opposite pair its mouth and anus, knobs its spine bosses. Smiths copied freely, so sizes vary. A private charm of a suppressed priesthood stays unworn, unlettered, unpictured and concentrated in Gaul, Britain and the Rhineland, ending in houses, graves, rivers and hoards.
VALUE: It explains the ten-plus-two layout, rings, knobs and Gallo-British distribution together, which rivals treat separately. Test: in Guggenberger's per-object tables, the five ringed holes around one unringed pole should differ systematically from the five around the other (urchin underside versus top); no difference refutes it.

IDEA 2
NAME: Wax-forming tool
MECHANISM: Warm wax pressed into the hollow body through the holes forms discrete, repeatable wax elements, such as bullae securing cords or marking documents. Corner knobs act as spacers during compression and aid removal; aperture size controls thickness and the raised central boss. The tool turns a flexible craft action into a standard one.
VALUE: It explains knobs, variable holes and the single wax-bearing specimen. Lamb's plastic trials produced repeatable forms; the proposed test is bronze replicas, wax-residue analysis and matching ancient sealings.

IDEA 3
NAME: Axle-spun lot, size-coded holes
MECHANISM: A household oracle was threaded on an upright wooden or bone rod through the larger unringed opposite pair, which were its bearings. It was spun and read from whichever ringed hole stopped facing the consultant. The answer was that hole's size, so each object needs ten distinguishable diameters, never a scheme shared with others, and no letters. Rings frame the readable apertures; knobs give spinning grip and keep rims off surfaces. Soft rods and occasional private Gallo-British use leave bronze unworn and suit settlement finds and textual silence; the Gellep bone rod is its axle.
VALUE: It alone makes within-object variation, no cross-object scheme, no inscriptions and the unringed pair all necessary. In Guggenberger's per-object measurements, if type-2a unringed pairs are often unequal or ringed holes on one object coincide within 0.5 mm, it is refuted.

IDEA 4
NAME: Staff finial on tapering shaft
MECHANISM: A light insignia head was slid onto a staff through the larger unringed opposite pair and wedged where the taper met that pair's smaller hole, so opposite pairs may differ widely. Ringed holes faced outward as decorated windows; knobs were ornamental terminals shielding the head when grounded. Each head fitted its own staff, so no size scheme was needed. Carried occasionally, it stayed unworn, followed its holder into graves (the Gellep rod), hoards or rivers, and as an unofficial regional badge escaped texts and art.
VALUE: It makes the unringed axis and unequal opposite pairs necessary, which symbolic and instrument readings leave unexplained. Under CT and microscopy, type-2a unringed pairs showing no more rim abrasion, organic traces or staining than ringed holes, or admitting no single tapering rod, refute it.

IDEA 5
NAME: Coincidence rangefinder
MECHANISM: Looking through a face's small hole at a standard-sized object (a man, a standard, a pole) until it exactly fills the large opposite hole gives distance by proportion. The faces form pairs of differing diameters, giving a range of calibrated distances; knobs let it rest flat or be gripped. Finds at forts link it to military engineering or surveying, like the dioptra.
VALUE: It explains the opposite-hole size contrast and finds in military camps. Sparavigna's test is measuring hole-pair ratios against plausible target sizes and ranges on real objects.

IDEA 6
NAME: Touch-coded casting lot
MECHANISM: Tossed onto cloth, it tumbles on its knobs and settles with one face up. That face is read by its hole (size, ringed or not; the large unringed pair a distinct outcome), legible by eye or finger, so no letters were needed, and since each owner knew their own piece, neither a shared scheme nor fairness mattered. A light shell landing on cloth wears little. It belongs to north-western lot-casting (Tacitus describes Germanic lots thrown onto a white cloth), not Mediterranean dice culture, fitting distribution, civil and grave contexts and textual silence.
VALUE: It explains why holes differ within each object yet share no scheme between objects, and why nothing is inscribed. On 10+ pieces, microscope knob tips against protected recesses: repeated tumbling polishes tips, so tips as fresh as recesses refute it.

IDEA 7
NAME: Farmer's sowing-date instrument
MECHANISM: Aimed at the sun and steadied on its knobs, opposite holes of different diameters act as apertures whose sunlight geometry marks the equinoxes and the autumn window for sowing winter wheat. Different hole pairs correspond to different dates or latitudes, which is why dimensions vary. It needed no markings, being a calendar-by-light.
VALUE: It explains twelve differently sized holes, northern farming provinces and low wear (used a few days a year). Wagemans tested a bronze copy over several years at different latitudes.
