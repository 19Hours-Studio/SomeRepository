=== REVIEW ===
IDEA 1: Platonic cosmic receptacle
DEFECT | MAJOR | It gives no mechanism for the distinctive physical features: the hole in every face, the different diameters within each object, the pattern of ten ringed and two unringed holes, and the knobs are all dismissed as "decorative or ritual variation", so the reading would fit any dodecahedral object. | FACT 2, FACT 3; FACT 29 (Guggenberger & Leach 2025 argue from texts and distribution, not from the objects)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The Druid-Pythagoras link needs Druidic learning to have survived 150-300 years after Rome suppressed the order (Tiberius per Pliny NH 30.13, Claudius per Suetonius Claud. 25), because the objects date to the 2nd-4th c. AD. | FACT 13; Suetonius, Claudius 25 (cts.perseids.org); Pliny NH 30.13
DEFECT | MAJOR | A Platonic-Pythagorean emblem would be expected where Platonism flourished, in the Greek and Italian Mediterranean, yet none comes from there; "Celtic provincial origin" is an added assumption and does not cover the Pannonian, Serbian and Slovak finds. | FACT 11, FACT 12
DEFECT | MAJOR | The proposed test (better-contexted finds, evidence of mounting) names no result that would refute the symbolic reading; only the sceptre sub-claim could fail. | The idea's own test; FACT 16
DEFECT | MINOR | Staff mounting rests on a single grave with a bone rod lying beside the object, out of about 130 pieces that are mostly found without fittings. | FACT 17 (SOFT), FACT 9, FACT 15
FIT | 5 of 15 | The 15 hard facts used for all seven ideas are FACT 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 20, 23 and 24; this idea covers the absence facts (no size standard, no texts or inscriptions, little wear) and the Gaul/Britain focus, and none of the physical features except the 12-pentagon shape.
SURVIVES | It is the only reading that explains why the shape is a dodecahedron at all, and the hollow, knobbed Arloff icosahedron (another Platonic solid; Wikipedia) fits it; it is consistent with the textual silence, the low wear and the lack of a standard size.

IDEA 2: Hand-tuned bronze vessel whistle
DEFECT | MAJOR | Many holes are too wide for a fingertip or thumb to close (the smallest hole reaches 28 mm and the largest 40 mm; Newhall's ringed holes go up to 22 mm and its ovals are 21x26 mm), so any scale would have to be fixed with wax, and wax is reported in only one unlocated piece, which sceptics read as casting residue. | FACT 2, FACT 3, FACT 21
DEFECT | MAJOR | The decisive test cannot fail: blowing across one rim of any sealed cavity gives a Helmholtz tone (my estimate for Newhall: volume about 142 cm3, one 21x26 mm oval open, about 660 Hz), so a tone proves nothing; the tests that would separate a whistle from other uses (finger polish on hole rims, whether 17-40 mm holes can be stopped by hand) are not proposed. | python Helmholtz calculation; FACT 2, FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | It does not explain why an instrument would be confined to the north-western provinces and the Gallic Empire area, absent around the Mediterranean, or limited to the 2nd-4th c.; "Gallo-British" is assumed rather than derived. | FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13
DEFECT | MINOR | The mouth hole has to be one of the irregular, sometimes oval unringed pair that Guggenberger & Leach 2025 treat as production holes, so the least-finished rim would be the sounding edge. | FACT 3; Cambridge 2025 (abstract and secondary summaries)
DEFECT | MINOR | Engraved rings have no acoustic or sealing role, so calling the ring-marked holes "stops" does not explain the rings. | FACT 3, FACT 25
DEFECT | MINOR | Roman musical instruments are often shown in art, yet no image of this one exists. | FACT 18
FIT | 7 of 15 | It gives working roles to the hollow body, the holes, their differing sizes, the knobs, the unringed pair and the Kortessem repair, and offers nothing for the distribution or the date.
SURVIVES | The acoustics are coherent: graded open area does change Helmholtz pitch, individual tuning does make a shared hole standard unnecessary, and the test is cheap to run on a replica or on Norton Disney.

IDEA 3: Founder's hollow-casting masterpiece
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Each founder set his own core and wax" explains variation between objects but not why the twelve holes differ within every single object, which is the most distinctive measured fact. | FACT 2
DEFECT | MAJOR | Holding and removing the core does not need a hole in every face: the Arloff icosahedron in Bonn is a hollow cast bronze with corner knobs but has holes in only two opposite faces, so the ten ringed holes are not a casting requirement. | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_dodecahedron (Arloff icosahedron; SOFT, single object)
DEFECT | MAJOR | Feeders are cut off after casting, but the knobs survive as finished spheres on every piece, no sprue stubs are reported, and the irregular knobs point to modelling in the wax rather than to feeding. | FACT 1; Wikipedia ("balls not perfectly regular ... made without the use of a mould")
DEFECT | MAJOR | Radiography can refute only the core-axis detail: Guggenberger & Leach 2025 already accept a production origin for the unringed pair inside a symbolic reading, so a pass gives no support to the "proof piece" function, and no result is named that would refute that function. | Cambridge 2025; FACT 29
DEFECT | MAJOR | Mediterranean founders routinely made much harder hollow castings, so a proof piece found only in the north-west, clustered in the Gallic Empire area and limited to the 2nd-4th c., needs an extra assumption; "a north-western casting tradition" restates the map instead of explaining it. | FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13
DEFECT | MINOR | The "closed polyhedron" is roughly one-fifth open (my estimate for Newhall: about 3,000 of 14,500 mm2), which gives twelve core supports and avoids the hard part of closed-core casting; only the 0.6-1.5 mm walls are demanding. | python area calculation on FACT 3; FACT 7
FIT | 7 of 15 | It explains the unringed pair, the thin walls, the lack of a size standard, the valued depositions and the textual silence, but not the within-object hole differences, the ten ringed holes, the knobs, the map or the date.
SURVIVES | Reading the larger irregular unringed pair as production openings agrees with Guggenberger & Leach 2025, a high-lead alloy (Norton Disney 18-27% Pb) suits thin-wall casting, and radiography or CT is feasible on the Bonn and Tongeren pieces.

IDEA 4: Travelling founders' trade sample
DEFECT | MAJOR | As in Idea 3, variation from one team to the next does not explain why the holes differ within each object. | FACT 2
DEFECT | MAJOR | The same production account of the ten ringed holes and the knobs fails: the Arloff icosahedron shows that two opposite holes were enough for a hollow knobbed casting, and the knobs are finished spheres, not trimmed feeders. | Wikipedia (Arloff icosahedron; irregular hand-modelled knobs); FACT 1
DEFECT | MAJOR | Itinerant founders carrying dodecahedra are not attested, "fixed Mediterranean workshops needed none" is an ad hoc patch for the gap around the Mediterranean, and nothing explains the Gallic-Empire cluster or the 2nd-4th c. limit. | FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13; ledger GAP 3 (no workshop, mould or core debris known)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test is weak: pXRF on corroded leaded bronze is unstable (the single Norton Disney piece gave both 18% and 27% Pb), core clay may not survive, and the predicted "few groups spread over hundreds of km" fits central production of an object with any function just as well. | FACT 8; FACT 30 (no results yet)
DEFECT | MINOR | The finds beyond the frontier are fragments, which fits the trade in scrap bronze into Germanic territory better than whole samples carried there by hired teams. | FACT 12 (Dragonidesova 2025: three fragments)
DEFECT | MINOR | The civil majority, the military minority and the losses in rivers and pits match any portable Roman bronze, so they are not specific support. | FACT 14, FACT 15
FIT | 7 of 15 | It explains the unringed pair, the thin walls, size variation between objects, the mixed contexts and the textual silence, but not the within-object hole differences, the map or the date.
SURVIVES | It states a concrete result that would refute it (alloy and clay matching the local findspot each time), and its production reading of the unringed pair holds.

IDEA 5: Gallo-Roman divination and magic
DEFECT | MAJOR | It has no mechanism for the distinctive features: twelve faces are simply the geometry of a dodecahedron, and nothing explains the holes, their different sizes within each object, the ringed and unringed pattern, the knobs or the thin hollow wall. | FACT 1, FACT 2, FACT 3
DEFECT | MAJOR | Its own analogues work against it: the 4th-c. Geneva die is solid lead cased in silver and inscribed with zodiac names, and the Sortes Sangallenses is a late-7th-c. manuscript written in Italy, whereas no bronze dodecahedron bears any sign and none comes from Italy. | FACT 19, FACT 11; averweij.web.cern.ch/zodiac.htm; CLA 7 961 (papyri-prod.lib.duke.edu/dclp/67105)
DEFECT | MAJOR | The test cannot refute the main claim: an absence of fibre rules out only the Spindle variant, and the deposition data already exist (over half civil, about 8.5% sacred, about 4% hoards) without any named pattern that would count against magic. | FACT 14, FACT 22
DEFECT | MINOR | "Ritual destruction" fits poorly: the Kortessem piece was mended, which points to further use, the "deliberate" reading is SOFT, and Norton Disney is complete and unworn. | FACT 23, FACT 24, FACT 20
DEFECT | MINOR | "Unofficial, therefore unrecorded" is weak, because private lot oracles were written down, including the Sortes texts it cites. | FACT 18; Sortes Sangallenses and Sortes Astrampsychi (Wikipedia, BMCR 2010.08.43)
FIT | 3 of 15 | It fits only the absence facts (no size standard, textual silence, little wear).
SURVIVES | The textual silence and low wear are compatible with it, and fibre microscopy on knobs and hole rims is feasible and could falsify the thread variant.

IDEA 6: Fumigation cage for aromatics
DEFECT | MAJOR | It leaves the commonest type's pattern unexplained: the rings are "ornament", and the opposite pair of large, unringed, sometimes oval holes gets no role beyond being "the largest hole". | FACT 3, FACT 25
DEFECT | MAJOR | Burning incense was common all around the Mediterranean, so a form confined to the north-western provinces, clustered in the Gallic Empire area and dated to the 2nd-4th c., is asserted as "a regional form" rather than derived. | FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13
DEFECT | MINOR | The knobs cannot be "cooler grips": a solid bronze knob a few mm across on a 0.6-1.5 mm shell reaches the shell's temperature within seconds (diffusion time L^2/alpha is about 2 s for 5 mm, with alpha about 1.5e-5 m2/s). | python thermal estimate; FACT 7
DEFECT | MINOR | No soot, char or heat tint has been reported on any piece, although no residue survey exists; the only residue claim is wax in one unlocated piece, which sceptics read as casting residue. | FACT 20, FACT 21
DEFECT | MINOR | Many pieces are cleaned antiquarian finds, so clean interiors could be blamed on conservation; to be decisive the test has to use excavated, unconserved pieces such as Norton Disney and Kortessem. | FACT 16, FACT 23, FACT 24
FIT | 8 of 15 | It gives roles to the hollow body, the holes, their within-object differences, the knobs, the thin walls and the mixed domestic, bath, theatre and grave contexts, and nothing to the ring pattern, the map or the date.
SURVIVES | Its test is the most decisive and feasible of the seven: the rough, unfinished interiors (Dragonidesova 2025) would trap residue, and soot, heat tint and GC-MS resin markers are standard checks with a clear result that would refute it.

IDEA 7: Hole-signature membership token
DEFECT | MAJOR | Checking diameters against a pegged gauge means seating pegs in the rims again and again, which should polish them, yet the pieces rarely show wear, and "shown rather than used" contradicts the checking function the idea relies on. | FACT 20
DEFECT | MAJOR | "Never repeat between objects" is asserted, not observed: the evidence only says there is no shared scheme, no per-object diameter tables are published, and the proposed test does not check uniqueness. | FACT 2; ledger GAP 2
DEFECT | MAJOR | The roundness test cannot separate this idea from others: any deliberate grading of holes (whistle, burner, tool) produces steps between holes (Newhall: ten holes of 10.5-22 mm, mean step about 1.3 mm) far larger than the scatter within one finished round hole, so it would pass without testing the signature claim. | FACT 3; python step calculation
DEFECT | MAJOR | The association, its counterpart gauges and its Gallo-British membership are unattested additions, and they restate the map and date instead of deriving them. | FACT 10, FACT 13, FACT 18
DEFECT | MINOR | The hollow thin-walled casting and the knobs get only "imitation costly" as a reason, and the related hollow knobbed Arloff icosahedron has just two holes, too few to carry a signature. | FACT 1, FACT 7; Wikipedia (Arloff icosahedron)
DEFECT | MINOR | The finds beyond the frontier are fragments, which fits scrap metal better than tokens that followed their owners. | FACT 12 (Dragonidesova 2025)
FIT | 6 of 15 | It explains the within-object variation with no shared scheme, the unringed pair as a reference axis, the uninscribed faces and the mixed deposition, but not the knobs, the hollow body, the low wear, the map or the date.
SURVIVES | It is the only reading in which the absence of a shared hole scheme follows as a design requirement, and its uniqueness claim can be checked against Guggenberger's list nos. 1-134.
