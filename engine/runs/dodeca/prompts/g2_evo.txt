What were the Roman dodecahedra? These are small hollow bronze objects with twelve pentagonal faces, a round hole of differing diameter in each face and knobs at the corners, found in the north-western provinces of the Roman Empire and dated to roughly the 2nd-4th centuries AD; no ancient text mentions them. Find the explanation of what they were and how they were used that best accounts for ALL the physical and archaeological evidence, and say how it can be tested.

LESSONS:
MAJORITY VIEW: no consensus (50+ proposals); specialists distrust everyday-tool readings and lean, unproven, to a non-utilitarian symbolic or ritual Gallo-Roman object.
Must fit about 130 objects, 4-11 cm, 35 g to 1 kg, twelve holes of 6-40 mm, corner knobs, rough interiors, no inscriptions.
Must explain little or no wear and wax in one specimen: light, sporadic or non-mechanical use.
Must explain distribution (about 70% Gaul/Germania, 20% Britain, none in Italy, Spain, Africa, East; a few beyond the Empire), AD 200-400 dating, and silence of texts and images.
Contexts are mixed (settlements over half, military under a fifth, sacred 8.5%, graves 7%, hoards and rivers 4% each): valued, sometimes deliberately deposited, not army-specific.
It must be testable through wear, residue, size-pattern or context evidence.
=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Platonic cosmic receptacle
MECHANISM: The dodecahedron, the Platonic solid tied to the cosmos, was made in Gaul and Britain as a material "receptacle" in Pythagorean-Platonic spirit, a philosophy Druids were said to admire. The twelve pentagonal faces embody the universe; holes and knobs are decorative or ritual variation, hence no standard sizes. A rod-shaped bone object beside the Gellep grave specimen suggests staff mounting, like a scepter of spiritual authority. Absence from Italy follows from its Celtic provincial origin.
VALUE: It explains the geography, lack of wear, lack of standardisation and silence of texts. Proponents propose better-contexted finds and evidence of mounting.
IDEA 2
NAME: Gallo-Roman divination and magic
MECHANISM: A private, non-official object of sorcery or fortune-telling, absent from literature and art because unofficial. Twelve faces echo zodiac or twelve-oracle systems like the Sortes Sangallenses and the zodiac-named Geneva die. In the Spindle-of-Necessity version, thread was wound across knobs and through holes to cast or read fate. Breakage and repair of finds such as Kortessem may mark ritual destruction.
VALUE: It explains absence from texts, lack of wear and the Celtic-area distribution. Its test is thread or fibre traces on knobs and holes, and deposition patterns showing deliberate burial or breakage.
IDEA 3
NAME: Farmer's sowing-date instrument
MECHANISM: Aimed at the sun and steadied on its knobs, opposite holes of different diameters act as apertures whose sunlight geometry marks the equinoxes and the autumn window for sowing winter wheat. Different hole pairs correspond to different dates or latitudes, which is why dimensions vary. It needed no markings, being a calendar-by-light.
VALUE: It explains twelve differently sized holes, northern farming provinces and low wear (used a few days a year). Wagemans tested a bronze copy over several years at different latitudes.
IDEA 4
NAME: Wax-forming tool
MECHANISM: Warm wax pressed into the hollow body through the holes forms discrete, repeatable wax elements, such as bullae securing cords or marking documents. Corner knobs act as spacers during compression and aid removal; aperture size controls thickness and the raised central boss. The tool turns a flexible craft action into a standard one.
VALUE: It explains knobs, variable holes and the single wax-bearing specimen. Lamb's plastic trials produced repeatable forms; the proposed test is bronze replicas, wax-residue analysis and matching ancient sealings.
IDEA 5
NAME: Coincidence rangefinder
MECHANISM: Looking through a face's small hole at a standard-sized object (a man, a standard, a pole) until it exactly fills the large opposite hole gives distance by proportion. The faces form pairs of differing diameters, giving a range of calibrated distances; knobs let it rest flat or be gripped. Finds at forts link it to military engineering or surveying, like the dioptra.
VALUE: It explains the opposite-hole size contrast and finds in military camps. Sparavigna's test is measuring hole-pair ratios against plausible target sizes and ranges on real objects.

DESIGN 1
NAME: Touch-coded casting lot
MECHANISM: Tossed onto cloth, it tumbles on its knobs and settles with one face up. That face is read by its hole (size, ringed or not; the large unringed pair a distinct outcome), legible by eye or finger, so no letters were needed, and since each owner knew their own piece, neither a shared scheme nor fairness mattered. A light shell landing on cloth wears little. It belongs to north-western lot-casting (Tacitus describes Germanic lots thrown onto a white cloth), not Mediterranean dice culture, fitting distribution, civil and grave contexts and textual silence.
VALUE: It explains why holes differ within each object yet share no scheme between objects, and why nothing is inscribed. On 10+ pieces, microscope knob tips against protected recesses: repeated tumbling polishes tips, so tips as fresh as recesses refute it.
REVIEW FINDINGS:
- MAJOR: On the one measured piece, ringed holes repeat in size (17/17, 22/22, 21/21.5 mm), so faces cannot all be told apart by size or ring; the code collapses and "explains why holes differ" fails there. (Jublains table, Sparavigna 2012)
- MAJOR: The Tacitus parallel works against it: Germanic lots were wooden slips "marked with signs" and read by those marks in free Germania, where dodecahedra are almost absent, while ~90% of dodecahedra lie inside the Gallic Empire. (Tacitus, Germania 10; FACT 10, FACT 12)

DESIGN 2
NAME: Fumigation cage for aromatics
MECHANISM: Resin or herbs on a small ember went in through the largest hole; then it sat on a smaller-holed face, ember over that hole. Knobs lift it to admit air while upper holes vent smoke, and turning it to another face changes intake and smoulder rate, so holes needed only to differ within a piece; rings are ornament. The thin shell heats fast, knobs give cooler grips, use causes no wear, and the wax specimen is residue. Domestic, bath, theatre and funerary fumigation fits the mixed contexts, a regional form of a common rite, unnamed in texts.
VALUE: It explains knobs as stand-offs, the perforated hollow and the wax specimen together, which symbolic and instrument readings do not. Clean interiors on 10+ pieces, with no soot, charred resin or heat tint and no resin markers by GC-MS, refute it.
REVIEW FINDINGS:
- MAJOR: It leaves the commonest type's pattern unexplained: the rings are "ornament", and the opposite pair of large, unringed, sometimes oval holes gets no role beyond being "the largest hole". (FACT 3, FACT 25)
- MAJOR: Burning incense was common all around the Mediterranean, so a form confined to the north-western provinces, clustered in the Gallic Empire area and dated to the 2nd-4th c., is asserted as "a regional form" rather than derived. (FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13)

DESIGN 3
NAME: Hand-tuned bronze vessel whistle
MECHANISM: Each piece was a Gallo-British edge-blown vessel flute: the player blew across one larger unringed hole, its opposite twin was a thumb vent, and the ring-marked holes were finger or wax stops. Pitch depends on open hole area against cavity volume, so graded holes give a fuller scale, and continuously varying bodies forced individual tuning, which rules out any shared hole standard. Knobs let it be held and set down without blocking holes. Wax, used to join and tune ancient pan-pipes, explains the German residue; lips and fingers leave bronze unworn; and the Kortessem repair restores airtightness.
VALUE: It turns differing holes, continuous sizes, the unringed pair and the wax trace into requirements. Sound the intact Norton Disney piece with an artificial-mouth air jet across an unringed rim, other holes film-sealed: if no stable tone results, it is refuted.
REVIEW FINDINGS:
- MAJOR: Many holes are too wide for a fingertip or thumb to close (the smallest hole reaches 28 mm and the largest 40 mm; Newhall's ringed holes go up to 22 mm and its ovals are 21x26 mm), so any scale would have to be fixed with wax, and wax is reported in only one unlocated piece, which sceptics read as casting residue. (FACT 2, FACT 3, FACT 21)
- MAJOR: The decisive test cannot fail: blowing across one rim of any sealed cavity gives a Helmholtz tone (my estimate for Newhall: volume about 142 cm3, one 21x26 mm oval open, about 660 Hz), so a tone proves nothing; the tests that would separate a whistle from other uses (finger polish on hole rims, whether 17-40 mm holes can be stopped by hand) are not proposed. (python Helmholtz calculation; FACT 2, FACT 20)
- MAJOR: It does not explain why an instrument would be confined to the north-western provinces and the Gallic Empire area, absent around the Mediterranean, or limited to the 2nd-4th c.; "Gallo-British" is assumed rather than derived. (FACT 10, FACT 11, FACT 13)

DESIGN 4
NAME: Staff finial on tapering shaft
MECHANISM: A light insignia head was slid onto a staff through the larger unringed opposite pair and wedged where the taper met that pair's smaller hole, so opposite pairs may differ widely. Ringed holes faced outward as decorated windows; knobs were ornamental terminals shielding the head when grounded. Each head fitted its own staff, so no size scheme was needed. Carried occasionally, it stayed unworn, followed its holder into graves (the Gellep rod), hoards or rivers, and as an unofficial regional badge escaped texts and art.
VALUE: It makes the unringed axis and unequal opposite pairs necessary, which symbolic and instrument readings leave unexplained. Under CT and microscopy, type-2a unringed pairs showing no more rim abrasion, organic traces or staining than ringed holes, or admitting no single tapering rod, refute it.
REVIEW FINDINGS:
- MAJOR: It gives no mechanism for ten "windows" of graded, all-different diameters, nor for the dodecahedral form, rings or knobs beyond ornament: this is the status/decoration defect. (FACT 2, FACT 3; Jublains 10.5-22 mm)
- MAJOR: A staff grounded on its knobs predicts knob abrasion, but the pieces are reported unworn, so it needs the unsupported "carried occasionally". (FACT 20; Hitchens (Washington Post/Boston Globe 2024); Norton Disney unworn)
- MAJOR: Insignia staffs are shown on north-western provincial monuments (the beneficiarius staff on the Friedberg altar), so total absence from art needs the extra "unofficial badge" assumption. (FACT 18; mithraeum.eu/monument/1480)
