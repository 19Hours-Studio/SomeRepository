=== IDEAS ===
IDEA 1
NAME: Bronze head on a rod
MECHANISM: The hollow bronze head was slid onto a wooden or bone rod through the two largest holes, the unringed opposite pair, which always lie on one axis; the thickest rod passes there (up to 22.6 mm on the Kleiner Feldberg piece) and a slightly tapering rod seats in both holes at once. On a vertical rod the viewer saw two crowns of five ringed faces and the 20 knobs; the rings and the differing sizes of the ten other holes were decoration, which is why their sizes form a continuum with no shared scheme. It was carried by men and women, mostly in civil settlements, rarely handled, and put into rich graves and hoards; at Krefeld-Gellep its position in a mid-4th-century woman's grave suggested a bone staff or handle. The twelve faces may stand for Plato's figure of the cosmos (Timaeus 55c), an argument from texts, not objects; why it occurs only in the north-west in the 2nd-4th centuries is not explained.
VALUE: It explains the axial unringed pair of the largest holes, the rubbed-looking rims of that pair (Duval 1981 on the Vienne piece; the roughest rims on the Kleiner Feldberg scan, but the smoothest on the Hunt scan), the unworn knobs and the continuum of hole sizes, which a lot with a hole code does not. Decisive test: stereo microscope on the unringed rims of the Kleiner Feldberg and Hunt pieces; wear striations along the axis with clean ringed rims support it, rims showing only casting or file marks refute it.
