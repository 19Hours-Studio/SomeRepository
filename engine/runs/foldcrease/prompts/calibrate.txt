Propose an architecture for a foldable smartphone (the whole stack that determines how the screen folds, including the display, its supporting layers and the hinge) in which the crease in the folding display is physically absent. It must be invisible to the eye and must stay invisible for at least 2000 open/close cycles. The crease must not be hidden by a matte or anti-glare coating or by any other surface treatment that only reduces its visibility. In current inward-folding phones, such as the Galaxy Z Fold 7, the display stack bends around a small radius. A permanent bend or dent forms along the fold line. It is reported to be about 0.7 mm deep, although no rigorous public measurement exists. Industry reliability tests run to 200,000 or more folds, but they check for breakage, not for crease formation. Apple officially launched its foldable, the iPhone Duo, on 9 September 2026 (on sale since 23 October 2026). Apple's hardware executives say a matte nano-texture coating minimizes the crease's visibility and that the top cover layer can be peeled off and replaced (about $19 with AppleCare); Apple does not claim the crease is gone, and hands-on reports differ on how visible it is. That matte, masking approach is what the asker rejects. The task is to find a design in which the crease is eliminated, not just reduced.
A good answer: 
- The answer is a complete architecture covering the display stack layers, the support structure and the hinge. Each layer's role is stated, along with the mechanism by which the crease cannot form or cannot be seen.
- It states how "invisible" would be measured. Examples are residual crease depth in µm and angle in degrees, optical reflection and off-axis visibility, and finger-touch detectability. It gives a target value for each measure.
- It states how persistence over 2000 cycles would be tested, and which conditions (temperature, humidity, time held folded) could break it.
- It states the main physical failure modes, such as plastic creep or permanent deformation of the polymer layers, adhesive creep, and glass fatigue. It also gives the trade-offs against thickness, mass, cost, display brightness and optical quality, touch and stylus feel, and durability.
- It distinguishes what is established by data from what is speculation. It names the cheapest experiment that would test the central claim.
- It is compared honestly with the Apple/Samsung approach that has been reported, and with the Oppo Find N6 approach.
Constraints: 
- No matte or anti-glare masking coating as the means of hiding the crease.
- The crease must be actually invisible, not just less visible.
- It must stay invisible for at least 2000 open/close cycles.
- It must be a folding smartphone.

The final candidates with their blind reviews (data):
IDEA 1
NAME: Glass-tendon tensioned clothoid hinge
MECHANISM: The anchor is kept: when the phone opens, the panel is put under in-plane tension that pulls any residual curvature flat. The tension now goes through the 45 µm UTG alone. Titanium rails grip the glass's two outer edges, and a force-controlled flexure spring, latched by a cam in the last few degrees of opening, sets the load at T ≈ 2000 N/m. That is 44 MPa in the glass, 0.063% strain and 300 N in total. A soft silicone optical gel (G ≈ 2 kPa) replaces the acrylic OCA under the glass. Through the acrylic OCA only 30% of the tension reached the fold, at about 48 kPa sustained shear. Through the gel 97% reaches it, at 1.9 kPa. The hinge path changes from a sharp teardrop to a teardrop with R = 3 mm and clothoid curvature ramps of at least 2 mm. Without the ramps, features narrower than √(D/T) ≈ 0.57 mm cannot be pulled flat. In a beam-column model with plate contact, calibrated so that today's creases come out at 60–150 µm, the result is a crease ≤2.1 µm deep with slope ≤0.82 mrad. The targets are ≤5 µm and ≤1 mrad. This holds for any untensioned crease up to about 180 µm.
VALUE: The cost is an estimated $13–50 per phone that meets the spec, about 16–72% of a $70–80 hinge. The reported baselines (Fold 7, Apple/Samsung Display drilled plate, Oppo N6) deliver essentially no phones that meet a ≤5 µm / ≤1 mrad spec, so no meaningful cost ratio exists.
BLIND REVIEW:
- MAJOR: √(D/T) = 0.57 mm uses a D close to UTG alone, but the set sits in the panel, back film and support plate, whose stiffness adds through the gel. Tripling D breaks the target. (Implied D = 6.5e-4 against 5.6e-4 for 45 µm UTG. Over a 13.4 mm arc+ramp, 180 µm leaves 3.2 µm / 0.74 mrad at D = 6.5e-4, but 9.1 µm / 2.1 mrad at 3D.)
- MAJOR: The model is calibrated to 60–150 µm creases and then extrapolated 70× down. The ≤180 µm envelope is below the only Fold 7 figure in circulation, and the gel thickness that must take the depth difference is not stated. (The task's ~0.7 mm figure (unconfirmed) is 3.9× the envelope. A 25–50 µm gel would need a 3.6–7.2× thickness change.)
- MAJOR: The tension acts only at full flat, so partial-angle use shows the raw crease. (—)
- SURVIVES: 44.4 MPa, 0.063%, 300 N, $13–50 = 16–71% of a $70–80 hinge, folded bending stress 525 MPa at R = 3 mm (in line with current practice), and gel cavitation (~5 kPa) is well above the ~0.25 kPa needed to hold the panel flat.
- RATIO: ≈0.98 | +$13–50 on $1,999. The "no meaningful ratio" claim fails because Oppo claims "no visible crease" after 600k folds (TÜV-tested, depth −82% vs N5), which is unverified but not zero.
- KNOWN: yes | Apple EP 4738056 A1 and Huawei-style US 20240069604 (more outward force when flat to speed crease recovery). https://patents.justia.com/patent/20240069604

IDEA 2
NAME: Per-layer sub-creep strain budget
MECHANISM: The anchor is kept: every polymer layer in the bend zone stays below its measured no-set strain, so no crease forms in the first place. The budget is now set per layer, not for the whole stack. With soft interlayers, each layer bends about its own mid-plane, so its strain is t_i/2R. The kill test's "60 µm stack at R = 5 mm = 0.6%" therefore does not apply. The thickest polymer layer is capped at 25 µm: the PET protector is removed, the UTG is hard-coated, and the back film is 25 µm CPI. The OCA is a low-relaxation silicone, because OCA slip (πd/2) does not shrink with radius. The locked shear drops from 92 kPa (acrylic) to 6 kPa. The polymers' share of bending stiffness falls from 16.9% to 2.4%. The hinge sets R ≥ 4.2 mm, which gives 0.30% per layer and about a 10.2 mm spine at the hinge.
VALUE: Any crease that remains is elastic and recovers, so flatness can be certified. The estimated cost is $7–33 per phone that meets the spec, about 8–47% of the hinge cost, plus about 1.3 mm more thickness than the 8.9 mm Fold 7.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The "measured no-set strain" is cited but never sourced, and below yield the set curvature fraction does not fall with strain, so a 0.30% strain cap does not make the residual curvature zero. The stack's residual curvature is roughly the polymer stiffness share times the polymer set fraction. (2.4% × (30–50% set, assumed) = 0.7–1.2% of 1/R, which gives 37–62 µm free-standing depth at R = 4.2 mm. That is roughly the 60 µm level a 17-prototype study graded only "good", not invisible.)
- MAJOR: "Elastic and recovers" leaves out the recovery time. Viscoelastic recovery runs in log time and is comparable to the hold time, so after an overnight fold the crease is present at opening. (Supporting NEW fact, not relied on: Choi et al. (2021) report residual strain in CPI decaying ∝ log t, with a critical radius ≈5 mm.)
- SURVIVES: 25 µm/(2×4.2 mm) = 0.298%; per-layer bending about each layer's own mid-plane is valid with soft interlayers; a polymer share of 2.4% is plausible (25 µm CPI alone ≈ 4.7% of a 30 µm UTG pair).
- RATIO: ≈0.99 | +$7–33 on $1,999, with no evidence of spec-meeting yield.
- KNOWN: partly | Low-strain CPI and large-R waterdrop hinges are standard practice; the "R above the critical residual-strain radius" idea is in Choi et al. 2021. https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202102455512209.pdf

IDEA 3
NAME: Floating glass on fluid
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: an index-matched liquid film, not an adhesive, couples the ultra-thin glass (UTG) to the panel where it bends. Because a liquid has no shape memory, the visible surface sits on a layer that cannot take a set. The repair fixes the flaw the kill test found: wherever the liquid strip met bonded adhesive, the layers had to slip 157 µm past each other, a shear strain of 6.3 against the 2.5–4 that folding adhesives are designed for. So the fluid (20 µm of phenyl-silicone, refractive index 1.51 ±0.02, viscosity 0.05–0.1 Pa·s) now covers the whole display, and the 149 µm of slip at each edge runs out at the perimeter. There it is taken by a pinned meniscus plus a 5 µm polyimide loop seal, which pushes back with at most 0.1 N/m; the glass would buckle at 0.34 N/m. The rest of the stack: 30 µm UTG with no polymer film on top (a bonded 5 µm hardcoat would print a 10 µm crease); a polarizer-free colour-filter OLED panel with touch built in; adhesive plus a 30 µm laser-patterned titanium plate; and a waterdrop hinge with a 3 mm minimum radius, so the glass and panel strain is 0.50% instead of 2.5% for a bonded stack. With the glass free to slip, any set in the layers below is levelled out by fluid flow in 5–10 s. Gravity, heat and stylus effects all stay inside their limits (a gravity tilt of at most 0.27 mrad, a 1.2 µm dome over 140 mm across 60 K, interface reflectance below 10⁻⁴). An earlier round with spacer pillars and a slight vacuum failed: the glass could then sag 360 µm into any dip and the crease printed straight through, so that version was dropped.
VALUE: It removes the layer that creeps from under the viewed surface for an estimated +$2–6 per phone, against roughly +$5–15 (my estimate) for the Apple/Samsung patterned plate or Oppo's ~20-pass printed hinge. On paper it is the only one of these aimed at zero crease rather than a smaller one, so cost per crease-free phone delivered is finite against undefined for the baselines.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: A free fluid film under 30 µm glass has no long-wavelength restoring force. With the phone upright the fluid drains to the bottom, the top region goes dry and atmospheric pressure presses the glass onto the panel, so the panel's crease prints through: the same failure as the dropped vacuum version. (Hydrostatic Δp over 150 mm = 1.6 kPa, against glass stiffness Dk⁴ = 32 Pa/m at λ = 300 mm, so the glass is not rigid and the "0.27 mrad tilt" (= 40 µm/150 mm, a rigid-plate full-drain wedge) is a lower bound. Drain time over 75 mm is 2.9 h at 0.05 Pa·s and 5.8 h at 0.1 Pa·s, i.e. overnight on a vertical stand.)
- MAJOR: After every opening the crease is visible until the fluid levels, and the levelling time grows with viscosity and feature width. This breaks "invisible" at the moment of use. (Thin-film plate model τ = 12μ/(h³Dk⁶): λ = 10 mm gives 7–15 s at 0.05–0.1 Pa·s, 59 s at 0.4 Pa·s (cold; the viscosity factor is assumed), and 470–940 s for a 20 mm-wide set.)
- MAJOR: The 20 µm gap is smaller than any plausible panel set at R = 3 mm. Where the set is a ridge, the glass rides on it; only valleys can be filled. (w₀ = fπ²R/8 = 74 µm at f = 2% and 296 µm at f = 8%, against a 20 µm gap.)
- MAJOR: Finger or stylus holds squeeze the film within about a second and leave a dimple that recovers slowly; "stylus within limits" has no number. (Rigid-disk Stefan bound: a 1 N press over 10 mm halves the gap in 0.55 s at 0.05 Pa·s.)
- SURVIVES: 0.34 N/m buckling (L ≈ 69 mm pinned), slip πd = 149 µm with d ≈ 47 µm, interface reflectance 4.3e-5 at Δn = 0.02, glass bending strain 0.50% (350 MPa), and the thermal dome (1.1 µm of fluid expansion over 60 K).
- RATIO: ≈1.0 nominal (+$2–6 on $1,999), but effectively <1 | Drain and print-through mean it does not deliver crease-free units, so cost per spec-meeting phone is undefined for the idea too.
- KNOWN: partly | Index-matched fill polymers in UFG recesses (TDCommons disclosure) and Apple's liquid-deposited polymer in a glass groove. I found no free-liquid full-area layer. https://www.tdcommons.org/dpubs_series/8090 , https://m.gsmarena.com/apple_patents_a_foldable_display_with_no_creases_for_iphone_and_ipad-amp-61917.php

IDEA 4
NAME: Spectrum-matched drumhead hinge
MECHANISM: Anchor kept: on opening, a hinge cam puts in-plane tension on the display and pulls any set flat over a flat plate. That tension works as a long-wavelength filter: the residual is w = w₀·k²/(k² + T/D), so it beats wide sets and loses to narrow ones. I reproduced the kill result (a 20 µm × 3 mm set leaves 3.3 µm and 2.3 mrad at 600 N/m; flattening it needs 3.9 kN/m). At today's 1.5 mm radius, 9.6–56 kN/m would be needed, which no phone can take. So the repair moves the design to where the anchor is strong. A thick hinge spine with a 4–5 mm bend radius spreads the set over a ~9 mm width. At the design tension of 1000 N/m (150 N total), the residual is ≤1 µm and ≤0.5 mrad as long as the set stays below 2.1–3.7% of the fold curvature. Clamping every layer together at the panel ends means the tension never passes through the adhesive as shear: shear in the viewing area drops from the kill's 25 kPa to 9.4 kPa, against a 10.5 kPa creep-test stress. A titanium frame and a 1 mm-stroke constant-force spring keep the tension within ±2% from −20 to +40 °C (±8% with aluminium). Glass strain from the tension is only 0.04%, and the cam releases the tension before folding starts.
VALUE: It turns any set below about 2.5% of the fold curvature into an invisible one, adding about +$8–20 per phone (cam, springs, titanium frame) and about 3 mm of spine thickness. Today's phones sit near 8% by my rough estimate, and the best current alternatives reduce the crease rather than remove it.
BLIND REVIEW:
- FATAL: The value claim needs the set to fall from the author's own "today ≈8%" to below 2.1–3.7% just because R grows from 1.5 to 4.5 mm, but no data shows that, and in linear viscoelasticity the set fraction does not depend on strain. (My code, with the implied D = 2.44e-4 N·m, half-wave over the arc πR and w₀ = fπ²R/8: at R = 4.5 mm, f = 8% and T = 1000 N/m the residual is 5.3 µm and 1.2 mrad, which is 5× the ≤1 µm / ≤0.5 mrad target. Moving to R = 4.5 mm also raises the untensioned w₀ from 148 to 444 µm at the same f.)
- MAJOR: The adhesive creep margin is 1.12 (9.4 kPa against a 10.5 kPa test stress), with no test temperature or hold time given; at 40–60 °C in a pocket the creep rate of acrylic OCA rises by orders of magnitude. (10.5/9.4 = 1.117.)
- MAJOR: The tension works only at full flat. At any partial angle (the cam releases "before folding starts"), and at flex-mode angles, the untensioned set is visible: 148 µm at R = 1.5 mm or 444 µm at R = 4.5 mm if f = 8%. (My w₀ calculation.)
- MAJOR: The thickness cost is understated. A bulb of 2R = 8–10 mm plus two display stacks and two cover walls gives a spine of about 11–12 mm, against the Fold 7's 8.9 mm folded, which is the market's reference point. (2×4.5 mm + 2×0.3 mm + 2×0.6 mm ≈ 10.8 mm minimum.)
- SURVIVES: Glass strain 0.048% (rounded to 0.04%), 150 N total, the 9.6–56 kN/m requirement at R = 1.5 mm (I get 16 kN/m at f = 8%), and the Ti thermal case: a 49 µm mismatch over 60 K against 71 µm of elastic elongation can be absorbed by a constant-force spring.
- RATIO: ≈0.99 | $1,999 phone against +$8–20 per phone. Neither side has a verified crease-free unit, so the "undefined" baseline is rhetoric. Against Oppo's claimed result (82% depth reduction, "no visible crease" after 600k folds, a manufacturer claim) it is a cost add for an unproven gain.
- KNOWN: yes | Apple EP 4738056 A1 (tension assembly in the flat state) and Huawei-style US 12541230 (tension assembly pushes brackets apart to flatten the display); LG US 20240215189 (magnetic tension). https://www.goveda.com/patent/EP-4738056-A1 , https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12541230
