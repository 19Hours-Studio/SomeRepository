P | 1 | 34% | It is the only candidate without a FATAL review. But all three MAJORs are unanswered: the stiffness D was underestimated, the model was extrapolated 70× below its calibration, and the crease is raw at partial angles. It is also known prior art (Apple EP 4738056, US 20240069604).

P | 3 | 8% | FATAL: it depends on the set fraction falling below about 2.5% as the radius grows, and no data supports that. At f = 8% the residual is 5× its target. The OCA creep margin of 1.12 is also thin.

P | 2 | 6% | FATAL: a cap on strain does not make the residual set zero. The estimated free-standing crease is still 37–62 µm, and recovering from an overnight hold takes log-time.

P | 4 | 3% | FATAL: the fluid drains when the phone stands upright and the panel crease prints through. The 20 µm gap is also smaller than any plausible set.

P | none | 49% | No candidate shows a verified crease-free unit. The tensioning family can only fix the fully flat state. Oppo's unverified "no visible crease" claim may already match these designs.

WEAKEST LINK: The bending stiffness D of the layers that actually carry the set (panel, back film and support plate, coupled through the 2 kPa gel), compared with 45 µm UTG alone. If D is about 3× the UTG value, the minimum flattenable feature width √(D/T) grows. A 180 µm set then leaves about 9 µm and 2.1 mrad, which misses the ≤5 µm / ≤1 mrad target.

FLIP TEST: The cheapest experiment is a bench test, with no hinge build.
- Take a real panel stack and laminate 45 µm UTG onto it with the gel.
- Fold it 2000 times at R = 3 mm, including 12 h holds at 60 °C and 90% RH.
- Clamp it flat on titanium rails at 2000 N/m.
- Map the fold with white-light interferometry and deflectometry, and also measure the untensioned set amplitude.

It flips if the residual is above 5 µm or 1 mrad, or if the untensioned set exceeds about 180 µm. Idea 1 would then fall below 10% and "none" would lead.

UNKNOWN: Even if the flat state is perfect, it is not known whether a crease visible at partial and flex-mode angles meets "invisible". Also unknown are the long-term behaviour of the gel (pump-out, outgassing, cavitation at cold temperatures) and the fatigue of the 300 N rail grip on the glass edges.
