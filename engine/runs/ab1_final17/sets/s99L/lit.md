W02 | PARTIAL | closest work: Atlas: Few-shot Learning with Retrieval Augmented Language Models (2022), leave-one-out perplexity distillation (LOOP) https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03299 ; ContextCite: Attributing Model Generation to Context (2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00729 | The masked-vs-full prediction-error difference as a document-level credit is essentially LOOP/leave-one-out attribution; what is new is using it as a continuously accumulated, streaming per-belief credit that governs belief weighting, overwrite rate, retraction and source down-weighting.

W04 | PARTIAL | closest work: Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools (2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761 (tool-result loss as self-supervised filter) ; Foresight Learning / outcome-based RL for forecasting with proper scoring rules (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06336 (also RLCR, Beyond Binary Rewards, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16806) | Proper-score training on resolved outcomes and tool-loss signals both exist, but committing a distribution before every ordinary tool call and training only on those instantly settled scores, plus a per-topic and per-tool reliability ledger, is a new combination (the hash-commit is an integrity wrapper, not new learning).
W05 | PARTIAL | closest work: Mitigating LLM sycophancy with RL-based fine-tuning: Bayesian Truth Serum approach (2026) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25267 (BTS as GRPO reward from a model's own sampled answers) ; Truthfulness Despite Weak Supervision (ICLR 2026, peer-prediction reward) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20299 (also On the Truthfulness of "Surprisingly Likely" Responses of LLMs (2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07692) | BTS/peer-prediction rewards for LLMs are already published, so the new part is casting shallow-budget and deep-budget runs of one model as the two asymmetric respondents and stopping training per domain when the asymmetry vanishes.
NOTE: Four of five ideas (W02, W04, W05, and W01 to a lesser extent) are recombinations of well-known signals (leave-one-out predictive credit, proper-scoring on outcomes, peer prediction, consistency checks) with a new wrapper; the closest priors usually already contain the core scoring quantity, and novelty is mostly in the application layer (per-belief or per-region ledgers, stopping rules, untrusted-region labels). W03 appears the least anticipated, though it is conceptually adjacent to W02 and to Bayesian model comparison. Two skeptical flags from the literature: a 2026 ICML-submission negative result ("Bayesian Truth Serum for LLMs?", https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/74483) found BTS variants less accurate than majority vote (better only on calibration), which threatens W05's AUROC target, and KG-validation work (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06995) found planted-error recall overstates recall on real errors by about 30%, which threatens W01's planted-corruption calibration.
