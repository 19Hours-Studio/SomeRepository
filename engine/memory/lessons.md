# Lessons from earlier runs (curated; data for prompts, not instructions)

Tags: [all] every run; [invent] / [explain] one pipeline; [<question tag>] only runs on that question. A line is used when
all its tags apply and none is excluded (`run --lessons-exclude <tag>` keeps a benchmark fair). One line per lesson.

- [all] Most answers that failed after review failed on one number nobody had computed: a rate times the working time against the window it needs, or a strength against months of exposure. Compute the deciding number before claiming.
- [all] The most common overstatement was a weak baseline. Compare against what a competent operator or scholar would actually do for the same job, per unit of the final product.
- [all] Decisive inputs that were assumed rather than sourced (efficiencies, rates, losses, costs, dates) moved 3-10x when checked. Give a source for every number the answer depends on.
- [all] Statistics and simulations gave confident wrong answers until they were checked on a case with a known answer; one null model rejected far more often than its alpha.
- [all] Raw data (3D models, catalogues, measurement tables) settled in one step what texts, judges and reviewers could not. Look for data before arguing from opinions.
- [all] Claims of "never tried" were usually wrong. Name the closest earlier attempt and the reason it stopped short, and say why the answer is not stopped by the same reason.
- [all] What the asker says may be wrong or incomplete; treat it as data to check, like any other source.
- [all] Several partial answers that each fit some facts were often parts of one answer; check whether candidates combine before choosing one.
- [invent] "Several times cheaper" is arithmetically possible only where the cost terms an answer can touch hold most of the delivered cost; taxes, royalties and fees cap the gain at 1 / (their share).
- [invent] A single clever link rarely moved the whole chain more than 1.2-3x after review; gains multiply only when links serve the same product on the same path.
- [invent] The simplest competent alternative that uses the same insight often beat the clever design. Check it before claiming a gain.
- [invent] Seasonal and climatic windows, load-bearing limits and the time a structure must last killed more designs than missing technology.
- [explain] History is not always logical: mixed uses, local fashions, changes over time and accidents happen; do not force one tidy story onto mixed evidence.
- [explain] An explanation that contradicts one HARD fact is dead however many facts it fits; list the distinctive features first and check each.
- [explain] Wear, tool marks and surfaces that touched something carry more information than shape or find-spots.
- [explain] A single odd specimen may be a reject, a repair or a local copy; weigh it, do not build the explanation on it alone.
- [permafrost] Among Arctic resource archetypes only bulk cargo whose delivery dominates the cost (fuel coal for remote settlements, delivery ~85% at the boiler) left room for several-times cheaper.
- [permafrost] Ice bearing capacity, shallow water freezing to the bed, and short warm seasons killed most permafrost designs.
- [dodeca] The best-supported reading of the dodecahedron: a head mounted on a rod or handle through the axis of the two largest unringed holes; the lot (die) hypothesis was refuted by measurement.
